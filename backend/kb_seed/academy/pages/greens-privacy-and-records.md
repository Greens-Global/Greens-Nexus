## The Rule

Everything about a tenant is private: their name, address, phone, balance, unit number, gate code, and even the fact that they rent from Greens. You discuss an account only with the tenant, after you confirm who they are, and with people the tenant listed on the account.

A spouse, parent, roommate, ex, landlord, process server or private investigator who is not on the account gets nothing. Say: "I'm not able to share whether that person is a customer."

## Police, Warrants and Subpoenas

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

- **Search warrant:** ask to see it; check that it names Greens and the unit or tenant; note the officer's name, agency, badge and warrant number; get a copy. Open the gate. Do not argue, help search or tip off the tenant. Call your manager and write an incident report.
- **No warrant:** politely say Greens requires a warrant to open a unit and legal process for records, and call your manager.
- **Subpoena for records:** do not ignore it. Note the deadline and send it to your manager the same day. Keep the records it asks for, camera footage included.
- **"Give us a list of all tenants":** never. Your manager handles it.

## Collections Messages

Never put a balance or lien detail in a text, an email or a voicemail unless Greens' policy says so, and never in a public reply (a review, social media). California's Rosenthal Act (Civil Code 1788 et seq.) and Texas Finance Code ch. 392 forbid harassing or misleading a person about a debt.

## Key Points

- Confirm identity before you discuss any account.
- Never confirm that someone rents from Greens to a person who is not on the account.
- Warrants and subpoenas go to your manager the same day.
- Notes are facts, written as if the tenant will read them.
