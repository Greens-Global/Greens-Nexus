## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Choose correctly between a waive, a credit and a refund.
- Waive all or part of a specific charge, with a real reason.
- Explain what a credit would do and why Greens does not use credits today.
- Know when a refund is right and what happens to the balance when you refund.
- Check your limits before you promise anything.

## Before You Start

- Waiving and crediting need the **"Credits and Waives"** permission in Cubby. Cubby does not ask anyone to approve a waive once you have it.
- **Greens' policy decides whether you should**, not the software. Your limits, and who approves above them, are on Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds. Read it before you waive anything.
- A tenant in the lien process: waive nothing without your manager.
- Where a button's exact name is not given, the button name in Greens' Cubby is on the screenshot.

## The Three Tools

| Tool | What it does | Balance | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Waive** | Cancels all or part of one specific charge | Drops now | A late fee on a first-time late payment; a fee posted by mistake |
| **Credit** | Holds goodwill money on the lease for the next recurring bill | Unchanged today | A make-good applied to next month. Not in use at Greens |
| **Refund** | Sends money back to the card or account it came from | Goes **up** unless the charge is also waived | An overpayment; a payment taken twice |

Since Greens moved to Cubby, staff waived 185 fees totaling $12,904.41 and 42 rent charges totaling $3,307.76, against about $25,800 of fees charged; there were 32 card refunds; and there were no credits (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Fee waives are about half of fees charged, which is why the policy matters.

## What Can and Cannot Be Waived

- Rent, fees, damage, coverage and deposit charges can be waived, in full or in part.
- Tax on a waived charge is waived in the same proportion, automatically.
- Taxes on their own, and waives, cannot be waived.
- If a waive takes the lease below zero (the charge was already paid), the extra becomes **prepaid** money on the lease.
- Every waive needs a **reason**.

## Steps: Waive a Charge

1. Open the lease and the ledger.
2. Find the **specific charge** to waive, for example "CA Late Fee 12/11, $24.00."
3. Check Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds: is this inside your limit? Has this tenant had a waive recently? If not inside your limit, stop and ask your manager.
4. Choose to waive the charge (the button name in Greens' Cubby is on the screenshot).
5. Enter the amount: the whole charge, or part of it.
6. Enter a **real reason**: "First late payment in two years, goodwill," or "Fee posted in error, tenant paid 12/09." Never "customer asked."
7. Save. Check the ledger shows the waive against that charge and the balance dropped by the amount.
8. Tell the tenant the new balance and take the payment if they are paying.

## Steps: Issue a Credit

Greens does not use credits today, and credits must be switched on per site. If your manager ever asks you to issue one:

1. Open the lease.
2. Choose to issue a credit (the button name in Greens' Cubby is on the screenshot).
3. Enter the amount and the reason.
4. Tell the tenant it is used on the **next** recurring bill, in this order: rent, then coverage, then automatic fees such as late fees. It is never used on a charge added by hand, and it does not lower today's balance.

## Steps: Refund a Payment

1. Confirm with Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds who may approve the refund. Get the approval first if it is not you.
2. Open the payment on the ledger.
3. Choose to refund it, in full or in part (the button name in Greens' Cubby is on the screenshot).
4. If the refunded payment paid a charge the tenant should **not** owe, also waive that charge, or the balance comes back as owed.
5. Tell the tenant a card refund can take several days to show on their statement.
6. Note what you did and why.

Screenshot: [Greens to add] (refunding a payment)

> [!WARNING]
> Never refund a payment for a charge the tenant still owes. The refund puts the charge back on their balance, and the delinquency steps start again.

## Waive or Credit: Which One?

| The tenant says | The right tool |
|---|---|
| "I paid on the 10th, why is there a late fee?" (the payment shows 12/10) | Check the dates; if the fee posted in error, **waive** it |
| "It's my first time late, can you take the fee off?" | **Waive** it, if inside your limit |
| "I was charged twice." | **Refund** the second payment; nothing to waive |
| "The gate was broken all week, can I get something?" | Ask your manager. A waive of part of next month's rent, if approved |
| "I prepaid and then moved out early." | Ask your manager: the unused prepaid is handled at move-out (Cubby How-To: Move-Out) |

## Example

**Valley Center.** A tenant renting a unit at a made-up $129.00 calls on 12/12. Autopay failed because her card expired; the late fee of $20.00 posted on the 11th (Greens' rule for rent of $100 or more: the greater of $20.00 or 15%, From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). She has never been late before. You check Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds: a first late fee is inside your limit. You waive the $20.00 with the reason "First late payment, card expired, goodwill." The balance drops from $149.00 to $129.00. She gives you a new card, you take the $129.00 and set the new card on autopay. You check her gate code works again.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Promising a waive before you know you may give it.** Say "Let me check," not "Sure."
2. **Waiving the wrong charge**, such as rent instead of the fee.
3. **Writing "customer asked"** as the reason.
4. **Refunding without waiving** a charge that should not be owed, so the balance comes back.
5. **Issuing a credit** when a waive was meant.
6. **Waiving for yourself, family or friends.** Never.

## Key Points

- A waive cancels a specific charge now and needs a real reason.
- A credit waits for the next recurring bill; Greens does not use credits today.
- A refund sends money back and puts the charge back as owed unless it is also waived.
- The Credits and Waives permission lets you; Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds says whether you should.
- In the lien process, waive nothing without your manager.
