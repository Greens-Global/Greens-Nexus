A Valley Center tenant calls on the 12th: her card expired, a $20 late fee posted, and she has never been late before. "Can you take that off?" The answer is "Let me check," then the right tool, on the right charge, with a reason anyone can read later.

## Before You Start

- The permissions are "Can waive charges," "Can issue credit" and "Can reverse payments" (refunds). Cubby asks nobody to approve a waive once you have the permission.
- **Greens' policy decides whether you should.** Your limits and who approves above them are on Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds. Read it before you waive anything.
- A tenant in the lien process: waive nothing without your manager.

## The Three Tools

| Tool | What it does | Balance | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Waive** | Cancels all or part of one charge | Drops now | A first late fee; a fee posted by mistake |
| **Credit** | Holds goodwill money for the next recurring bill | Unchanged today | A make-good on next month, only if your manager asks |
| **Refund** | Sends money back to the card or account | Goes **up**, unless the charge is also waived | An overpayment; a payment taken twice |

## What Can Be Waived

- Rent, fee, damage, coverage and deposit charges, in full or in part.
- Tax on a waived charge is waived in proportion, automatically.
- Tax on its own, and a waive, cannot be waived.
- A waive that takes the lease below zero turns the extra into prepaid money.
- Every waive needs a **reason**.

## Steps: Waive a Charge


1. Open the lease and its **Ledger**.
2. Find the **specific charge**, such as "CA Late Fee 12/11, $20.00."
3. Check Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds: inside your limit? A recent waive for this tenant? If not inside your limit, stop and ask your manager.
4. Choose to waive that charge and enter the amount, all or part.
5. Enter a **real reason**: "First late payment in two years, card expired, goodwill" or "Fee posted in error, tenant paid 12/09." Never "customer asked."
6. Save. Check the waive shows against that charge and the balance dropped by the amount.
7. Tell the tenant the new balance and take the payment if they are paying.

## Steps: Issue a Credit

Greens' ledgers show no credits so far; issue one only when your manager asks. Open the lease, choose to give the tenant a credit, and enter the amount and reason. Tell the tenant it is used on the **next** recurring bill (rent, then coverage, then automatic fees), never on a charge added by hand, and does not lower today's balance.

## Steps: Refund a Payment

1. Check Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds for who approves the refund; get the approval first if it is not you.
2. Open the payment on the ledger and choose to refund it, in full or in part. A payment that covered rent and merchandise together can only be refunded in full.
3. If the payment paid a charge the tenant should **not** owe, waive that charge too, or it comes back as owed.
4. Tell the tenant a card refund can take several days to reach their statement.
5. Note what you did and why.

> [!WARNING]
> Never refund a payment for a charge the tenant still owes. The refund puts the charge back on their balance.

## Which Tool?

| The tenant says | The right tool |
|---|---|
| "I paid on the 10th, why is there a late fee?" (the payment shows 12/10) | The fee posted in error: **waive** it |
| "It's my first time late, can you take the fee off?" | **Waive**, if inside your limit |
| "I was charged twice." | **Refund** the second payment |
| "The gate was broken all week, can I get something?" | Ask your manager |
| "I prepaid and then moved out early." | Ask your manager: unused prepaid is settled at move-out |

## At Greens

**Valley Center.** The caller from the opening: rent $129.00, CA Late Fee $20.00, balance $149.00, first time late. The policy puts a first late fee inside your limit. You waive the $20.00 with the reason "First late payment, card expired, goodwill." The balance drops to $129.00. She gives you a new card; you take the $129.00, set the new card for AutoPay and make sure her gate code works.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Promising a waive before you know you may.** Say "Let me check," not "Sure."
2. **Waiving the wrong charge**, such as rent instead of the fee.
3. **"Customer asked" as the reason.**
4. **Refunding without waiving** a charge that should not be owed.
5. **Waiving for yourself, family or friends.** Never.

## Key Points

- A waive cancels one charge now and needs a real reason.
- A credit waits for the next recurring bill; issue one only when your manager asks.
- A refund reopens the charge it paid unless that charge is also waived.
- The permission lets you; Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds says whether you should.
