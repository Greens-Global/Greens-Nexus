## Three Different Tools

| Tool | What it does on the ledger | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Waive | Cancels all or part of one charge now. The balance drops at once. Needs a reason | A late fee on a first-time late payment, a fee posted by mistake |
| Credit | Holds goodwill money on the lease. It does not lower today's balance; Cubby uses it on the next rent, then protection, then automatic fees | A make-good for a problem, applied to next month |
| Refund | Sends money back to the card or bank account it came from | An overpayment, a charge taken twice |

A refund puts the money back on the lease as owed unless the charge is also waived. Never refund a charge the tenant still owes.

## Example

An Escondido tenant at $129 a month pays on the 12th and asks you to remove the $20.00 late fee that posted on the 11th (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026: the late fee for rent of $100 or more is the greater of $20.00 or 15%). It is their first late payment in two years. If the fee is within your waiver limit, waive the $20.00 with the reason "first late payment, goodwill." The balance drops to $129 and they pay it. If it is above your limit, tell them you will ask your manager today and call them back.

## Rules That Always Apply

- Every waive and refund carries a real reason in Cubby. "Customer asked" is not a reason.
- Never waive or refund on your own account or for family or friends.
- Never promise a waive before you know you may give it.
- A tenant in the lien process: waive nothing without your manager.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## Key Points

- Waive cancels a charge now; a credit waits for the next bill; a refund sends money back.
- Greens waived about $12,900 of fees and $3,300 of rent since 09/2026 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Stay inside your waiver limit and write a real reason every time.
