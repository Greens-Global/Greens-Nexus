A Temecula tenant bought a longer trailer and wants to move from an open 10x20 to a covered space. She is staying with Greens, so this is a **unit transfer**, not a move-out and a new rental. Done as a transfer, her balance, history and AutoPay come with her.

## Before You Start

- A transfer is for a tenant who stays but moves to a different unit or space at the same site.
- Never close the old lease and rent a new one by hand to "transfer" someone: the balance is left behind and the reports count a move-out and a move-in that never happened.
- Read the charges Cubby shows for the transfer month before you confirm. If anything looks off, ask your manager.

## What a Transfer Does

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Lease A on the old unit | Lease A ends, marked as transferred |
| | Lease B starts on the new unit, same tenant |
| Any balance or prepaid on Lease A | Moves to Lease B as a transfer line, such as "(B011 to F035)" |
| The old unit | Vacant, or unrentable with a reason if it needs work |

## Steps: Before

1. Open the current lease. Check the balance and paid-through date.
2. If the tenant owes money, ask your manager whether the transfer should wait until it is paid.
3. Find the new unit (Cubby How-To: Find an Open Unit). For a vehicle, check the fit again: length plus a margin, and height for covered spaces.
4. Tell the tenant the new rate and that their bill on the 1st will change.

1. On the current lease, start a **unit transfer**.
2. Choose the new unit and the transfer date.
3. Read the charges Cubby shows for the new lease and the old unit's month; check them against what you told the tenant.
4. Take any payment due and confirm.
5. Open the new lease: the balance moved across and the paid-through date is right.

A custom rate on the new unit needs the "Can set custom rate" permission, and moving prepaid money needs "Can transfer prepaid balance." If a step is missing for you, ask your manager.

## Steps: After

1. **Coverage:** the right plan or the tenant's own policy, updated for the new space.
2. **Documents:** sign the new lease if Cubby asks; vehicle plate, state and type on it.
3. **Gate code:** works for the tenant (Cubby How-To: Gate Code).
4. **Lock:** a Greens lock moves to the new unit; no tenant lock left on the old one.
5. **AutoPay:** still on.
6. **The old unit:** walk it. Clean and empty, leave it rentable; if not, mark it unrentable with the reason.
7. **Note** on the new lease: "Transferred from 10x20 space 214 on 11/12/2026, wanted covered."

## Transfer or Something Else?

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Same tenant, different unit, same site | Unit transfer |
| Same tenant adds a second unit and keeps the first | A new rental for the second unit |
| Someone else, even family, takes over | Move-out, then a new rental for the new person |
| The tenant moves to another Greens site | Ask your manager |

## At Greens

**Temecula.** The trailer is 28 ft hitch to bumper; with a margin she needs a covered space at least 31 ft long, so the covered 12x35 fits. She owes nothing. You start the unit transfer, choose the 12x35, read the charges for the rest of the month, take the payment and confirm. The new lease shows a zero balance, her own policy, her vehicle details, AutoPay on and a working code. The old 10x20 is clean, so it stays rentable.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Moving out and moving in by hand** instead of transferring.
2. **Skipping the fit check** for a vehicle.
3. **Forgetting the old unit**: a lock left on, or a dirty unit left rentable.
4. **Promising a price** for the transfer month before reading Cubby's charges.

## Key Points

- A unit transfer ends the old lease, starts a new one and moves the balance across.
- Never fake a transfer with a move-out and a new rental.
- Check the fit, the rate and Cubby's charges before you confirm.
- After: coverage, documents, gate code, lock, AutoPay, the old unit, a note.
