## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Run an opening routine in Cubby in about fifteen minutes.
- Run a closing routine that makes the cash and checks match Cubby.
- Open the Rent Roll, Aged Receivables, General Ledger and Merchant Services Disbursements reports and say what each is for.
- Spot the problems each report shows.

## Before You Start

- Cubby is cloud software and does much of its work by itself. There is **no "end of day" close** in Cubby like some older storage systems have. The routine below is a **proposed routine based on industry practice**. Greens' own open and close checklist for your site comes from your manager, and wins where it differs.
- Reports can be run on demand or scheduled. Which reports you may open depends on your role.
- Report numbers are business records. Do not send them outside Greens.
- Where a button's exact name is not given, the button name in Greens' Cubby is on the screenshot.

## The Opening Routine

1. **Sign in** and check the site picker shows your site.
2. **Read the Home page counts**: tasks due, document issues, liens to initiate, overlocks needed.
3. **Open today's task list**, oldest first. Plan the collection calls first.
4. **Check today's move-ins and move-outs**: reservations converting today and scheduled move-outs. Make sure the units are ready.
5. **Check failed autopays** on the 1st and 2nd of the month: they become the "2nd" tasks.
6. **Check overlocks needed** and plan the walk (Cubby How-To: Overlock and Lockout).
7. **Walk the site** with the walkthrough on your phone: lock check, vacant units, the vehicle lot. Compare what you see with Cubby: a vacant unit with a lock on it, or a leased unit standing open, is something to fix today.
8. **Count the cash drawer** start amount.

## The Closing Routine

1. **Payments taken today**: open today's payments and compare them with the cash and checks in the drawer. Cash and check payments at the counter are entered in Cubby as point-of-sale payments.
2. **Make up the deposit** as your manager has shown you, and record it.
3. **Tasks**: every task due today is completed with a note, or reassigned with a reason.
4. **Leads**: every lead you spoke to today has a status and, if needed, a follow-up task.
5. **Move-ins today**: each new lease has no document left Incomplete, coverage set, autopay offered, gate code working.
6. **Notes**: anything the next shift must know is in a note.
7. **Sign out.**

> [!TIP]
> If the drawer and Cubby do not match, do not "fix" it by changing a payment. Count again, check for a payment on the wrong lease or a check entered as cash, and tell your manager before you leave.

## The Reports

| Report | What it shows | Who uses it | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rent Roll** | Every unit, its tenant and rent, occupied or vacant | Managers, accounting | Weekly and month end |
| **Aged Receivables** | Each tenant's open charges, sorted by age into 0-30, 30-60, 60-90, 90-120 and over 120 days | Managers, collections | Weekly |
| **General Ledger, Cash** | Money actually received, by account, for a period | Accounting | Month end |
| **General Ledger, Accrual** | Money earned and charged, by account, whether or not received | Accounting | Month end |
| **Merchant Services Disbursements** | What the card processor paid out to Greens' bank, and when | Accounting | When deposits are reconciled |

Cubby also has a **Management Summary** that managers use for occupancy and the month at a glance.

### Rent Roll

Use it to check that Cubby matches the site. Every leased unit should have a tenant and a rent; every vacant unit should be rentable or carry a reason. When you walk the site, the Rent Roll is the list you walk against.

What to look for: a unit leased in Cubby that you found empty and open; a unit vacant in Cubby with a lock on it; a rent that looks wrong for the size.

### Aged Receivables

Use it to see who owes what and how old it is. Each open charge sits in a bucket by its age. One tenant can be in two buckets.

What to look for: money in 30-60 and older, which is close to or in the lien process; a tenant you thought had paid.

Example: a tenant paid through 09/30 has, on 11/02, an October rent of $130.00 in 30-60 and a November rent of $130.00 plus fees in 0-30 (the worked example in the Ledger Worked Examples lesson).

### General Ledger, Cash and Accrual

The General Ledger lists every posting by account. The **cash** version counts money when it is received. The **accrual** version counts money when it is charged or earned. A month with many unpaid rents shows more on accrual than on cash. Accounting uses both; you rarely need them at the counter.

### Merchant Services Disbursements

Card payments are not in Greens' bank the minute they are taken. The card processor pays them out in batches. This report shows each payout. Accounting matches it to the bank. If a tenant asks why a refund has not reached their card, the timing of payouts and refunds is part of the answer.

## Steps: Run a Report

1. Open Reporting (the button name in Greens' Cubby is on the screenshot).
2. Choose the report.
3. Choose the site and the date or period.
4. Run it. Read it on screen or export it as your manager prefers.
5. To get it regularly, schedule it if your role allows.

## Example

**Valley Center, Friday close.** Cubby shows $412.00 in cash payments and $260.00 in checks taken today; the drawer holds $412.00 cash and two checks totaling $260.00. They match. You make up the deposit. Every task is complete with a note. One move-in today still shows Signature Needed: you text the tenant the signing link and leave a note for the morning shift. Monday morning, your manager runs the Aged Receivables: three tenants have charges in 30-60. You check each against the liens to initiate count on the Home page.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Skipping the walk** and trusting the screen.
2. **Leaving a drawer difference unexplained.**
3. **Changing a payment to make the drawer match.**
4. **Leaving a move-in with documents Incomplete** at close.
5. **Sending a report outside Greens.**

## Key Points

- Open: Home page counts, tasks, today's move-ins and move-outs, failed autopays, overlocks, the site walk.
- Close: payments versus the drawer, the deposit, tasks completed with notes, leads followed up, move-ins complete.
- The routine here is proposed from industry practice; your site's checklist from your manager wins.
- Rent Roll checks the site; Aged Receivables shows who owes and how old; the General Ledger (cash and accrual) and Merchant Services Disbursements are for accounting.
