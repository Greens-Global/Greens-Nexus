## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Add a note to the right record: a contact, a lease, a unit, a lead, a task or a pricing group.
- Pin a note on a contact so everyone sees it on the lease.
- Write a note the next person can act on.
- Keep private and sensitive information out of notes.

## Before You Start

- Every note in Cubby is saved under the name of the person who wrote it, with the date and time.
- Notes are the memory of the site. The person on the next shift, your manager and, one day, a lawyer or a judge may read them. Write every note as if the tenant will read it too.
- Where a button's exact name is not given, the button name in Greens' Cubby is on the screenshot.

## Where a Note Goes

| Attach the note to | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact** | It is about the person, whatever unit | "Prefers text, works nights" |
| **Lease** | It is about this rental | "Autopay moved to new card 11/12/2026" |
| **Unit** | It is about the space itself | "Door sticks, reported to maintenance" |
| **Lead** | It is about a person who has not rented | "Wants covered 12x35 from 12/01, call back Thursday" |
| **Task** | It records what you did on a task | "Called, voicemail, payment link sent" |
| **Pricing group** | It is about a whole unit type | "All 10x30 spaces in row C are gravel" |

### The Pinned Note

A contact can carry one **pinned note**, which shows on the lease where everyone sees it. Use it for what every staff member must know before they act:

- "Authorized: daughter may access the unit and pay. ID checked 11/04/2026."
- "Speaks Spanish; prefers calls after 5:00 PM."
- "Payment arrangement in place, see exemption."

Keep a pinned note short and current. Update it when it stops being true.

## What Makes a Good Note

A good note answers four questions: **what happened, what was said or agreed, what happens next, and by when.**

| Weak note | Good note |
|---|---|
| "Called." | "Called 11/06 10:15 AM about $134 past due. Tenant said card expired; will pay by Friday 11/08. Payment link sent by text." |
| "Customer upset." | "Tenant upset about late fee posted 11/11. Explained it posts after 10 days unpaid. Asked manager about a waive; will call back today by 3:00 PM." |
| "Boat." | "Stores 23 ft wake boat, 31 ft on trailer tongue out. Tower folds. Backs in alone." |

Rules:

1. **Facts, not opinions.** Write "Tenant raised voice and hung up," not "Tenant is rude."
2. **Dates and times** in MM/DD/YYYY and 12-hour time.
3. **Numbers exactly**: amounts, unit numbers, promised dates.
4. **What you promised**, so the next person keeps the promise.
5. **One note per event.** Do not edit an old note to add a new event.

## What Never Goes in a Note

- Full card numbers, bank account numbers or security codes.
- A Social Security number or a full ID number.
- Medical details, or why someone is in the hospital.
- Gate codes.
- Guesses about a tenant's private life.
- Anything you would not want read aloud to the tenant.

If you need to record that you saw a document (military orders, a bankruptcy notice, a death certificate), write that you saw it and where it is kept, not what is in it. Privacy rules are on Greens Privacy and Records.

## Notes and Tasks

When you complete a task, the note on the task is your record that the work was done. A task completed with no note tells the next person nothing. See Cubby How-To: Complete a Task.

## Example

**Murrieta.** A tenant's son calls to pay his father's past-due balance and asks for the gate code. You check the contact: the pinned note says "Authorized: son may pay. NOT authorized for access." You take the payment and, politely, do not give the code. You add a lease note: "11/14/2026 2:20 PM: son paid $184.00 by card for father. Asked for gate code; not authorized per pinned note; told him the tenant can call us." The next person who gets the same call knows exactly what happened.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Notes that say nothing**, such as "called."
2. **The wrong record**: a note about the person on one lease, where a staff member on another lease never sees it.
3. **Out-of-date pinned notes.**
4. **Sensitive data in a note.**
5. **Opinions about the tenant.**

## Key Points

- Put the note on the record it is about: contact, lease, unit, lead, task or pricing group.
- One pinned note per contact, shown on the lease, short and current.
- Good notes say what happened, what was agreed, what is next and by when.
- Never put card numbers, ID numbers, medical details or gate codes in a note.
- Write every note as if the tenant will read it.
