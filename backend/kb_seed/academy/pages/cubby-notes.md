A man calls Murrieta to pay his father's past-due balance and asks for the gate code. You have never spoken to either of them. The pinned note on the father's contact tells you in one line what you may and may not do. That is what a note is for.

## Before You Start

- Every note is saved under your name with the date and time.
- Notes are the site's memory. The next shift, your manager and one day a lawyer or a judge may read them. Write every note as if the tenant will read it too.

## Where a Note Goes

| Attach the note to | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact** | It is about the person, whatever unit | "Prefers text, works nights" |
| **Lease** | It is about this rental | "AutoPay moved to new card 11/12/2026" |
| **Unit** | It is about the space | "Door sticks, reported to maintenance" |
| **Lead** | Someone who has not rented | "Wants covered 12x35 from 12/01, call back Thursday" |
| **Task** | What you did on a task | "Called, voicemail, payment link sent" |
| **Pricing group** | A whole unit type | "All 10x30 spaces in row C are gravel" |

### The Pinned Note

A contact can carry one **pinned note**, shown where everyone sees it before they act:

- "Authorized: daughter may access the unit and pay. ID checked 11/04/2026."
- "Speaks Spanish; prefers calls after 5:00 PM."
- "Payment arrangement in place, see exemption."

To pin one, open the contact, write or open the note and pin it; then open one of the contact's leases and check it shows. Keep it short and update it the day it stops being true.

## What Makes a Good Note

A good note says **what happened, what was said or agreed, what happens next, and by when.**

| Weak note | Good note |
|---|---|
| "Called." | "Called 11/06 10:15 AM about $134 past due. Card expired; will pay by Friday 11/08. Payment link sent by text." |
| "Customer upset." | "Upset about late fee posted 11/11. Explained it posts after 10 days unpaid. Asked manager about a waive; will call back today by 3:00 PM." |
| "Boat." | "Stores 23 ft wake boat, 31 ft on trailer, tongue out. Tower folds. Backs in alone." |

1. **Facts, not opinions.** "Tenant raised voice and hung up," not "Tenant is rude."
2. **Dates and times** as MM/DD/YYYY and 12-hour time.
3. **Exact numbers**: amounts, unit numbers, promised dates.
4. **What you promised**, so the next person keeps it.
5. **One note per event.** Never edit an old note to add a new event.

## What Never Goes in a Note

- Card numbers, bank account numbers or security codes, whole or in part beyond the last four.
- A Social Security number or a full ID number.
- Medical details.
- Gate codes.
- Guesses about a tenant's private life.

For a document you saw (military orders, a bankruptcy notice, a death certificate), write that you saw it and where it is kept, not what it says. The privacy rules are on Greens Privacy and Records.

## At Greens

**Murrieta.** The son from the opening. The pinned note on his father's contact says "Authorized: son may pay. NOT authorized for access." You take the payment and politely do not give the code. You add a lease note: "11/14/2026 2:20 PM: son paid $184.00 by card for father. Asked for gate code; not authorized per pinned note; told him the tenant can call us." The next person who gets the same call knows exactly what happened.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Notes that say nothing**, such as "called."
2. **The wrong record**: a note about the person left on one lease, where staff on another lease never see it.
3. **A pinned note that is out of date.**
4. **Sensitive data in a note.**

## Key Points

- Put the note on the record it is about: contact, lease, unit, lead, task or pricing group.
- One pinned note per contact, short and current.
- A good note says what happened, what was agreed, what is next and by when.
- Never card numbers, ID numbers, medical details or gate codes.
