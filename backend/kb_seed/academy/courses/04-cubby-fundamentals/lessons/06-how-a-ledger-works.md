---
title: How a Ledger Works
summary: The tenant ledger in plain words - charges, payments, waives, credits, prepaid, deposits, refunds, write-offs and transfers - and what each does to what the tenant owes.
---

A Valley Center tenant leans on the counter: "I paid on Tuesday, so why does it say I owe $38?" You open the lease, scroll to the **Ledger** section, and in a minute you can show her exactly which charge is still open. This lesson teaches you to read that ledger; the next one teaches the dates on it.

## One Side Is All You Need

Cubby keeps a double-entry ledger: every transaction is written twice, once for what the tenant owes and once for where it lands in Greens' books. At the counter only the first side matters, **what this tenant owes Greens**. Think of it as a bank statement in reverse: charges push the balance up; payments, waives and write-offs bring it down.

## The Pieces

| Item | What it does to what the tenant owes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Charge** | Adds to it | December rent posts on 12/01 |
| **Payment** | Takes away from it | The tenant pays by card |
| **Waive** | Cancels all or part of one charge, now | You waive a late fee |
| **Credit** | Nothing today; it is used on the next recurring bill | A goodwill credit for next month |
| **Prepaid** | Money paid ahead, used as charges post | The tenant pays three months ahead |
| **Refund** | Sends money back to the card or account | A double payment is returned |
| **Write-off** | Clears a balance that will not be collected | A balance left after a move-out |

### Charges

Rent and coverage post by themselves on the 1st, and so do automatic fees such as "CA Late Fee" and "CA Preliminary Lien Notice." Other charges are added by hand with **Add Manual Charge**, only for a real sale or a fee your manager approves. A **discount** posts as its own line under the rent ("First 2 Months 50% Off New Tenants Only," for example), so the rent on the ledger never looks lower. Greens' fee names and amounts are on Greens Fees and Charges.

### Payments

Tenants pay by card, bank account (ACH), cash, check and other methods. Money already on the lease can pay a charge too: when the 1st's rent is paid from prepaid money, it shows as a payment from prepaid.

A **declined card posts nothing**. If a tenant says "I paid" and the ledger shows nothing, the card was most likely declined.

### Waives

A **waive** cancels all or part of a **specific charge**, now, and always needs a reason. Rent, fee, damage, coverage and deposit charges can be waived; tax on a waived charge is waived in proportion. If a waive takes the lease below zero, the extra becomes prepaid money. The permission is "Can waive charges"; Greens' limits are on Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds.

### Credits

A **credit** is goodwill money held on the lease. It does **not** lower today's balance; Cubby uses it on the next recurring charges, rent first, then coverage, then automatic fees, and never on a charge added by hand. Greens' ledgers show no credits so far. A tenant who is "owed something" almost always needs a waive of one charge, not a credit.

### Prepaid

**Prepaid** is money paid when nothing is owed. Cubby uses it as each new charge posts. Prepaid money does **not** move the paid-through date until the charges it pays have posted (see Paid-Through Date and Days Past Due).

### Deposits

New Greens move-ins pay no deposit. A deposit line comes only on some leases brought over from the old system. At the end it is returned or kept, and Cubby records which.

### Refunds

A refund sends money back to the card or account it came from; a card refund can take several days to reach the tenant's statement. Refunding a payment reopens the charge it paid, so the tenant owes it again unless that charge is also waived. Never refund a payment for a charge the tenant still owes.

### Failed, Returned and Disputed Payments

- A **bank payment that comes back** is marked failed, which **reopens the charges** it had paid. The balance goes back up.
- A **card dispute** (chargeback) shows as a disputed payment. Tell your manager the same day.
- A **pending** payment has an unknown outcome. **Never run it again**: you could charge the tenant twice.

### Write-Offs and Transfers

A **write-off** clears a balance Greens will not collect, recorded as bad debt. It is a manager decision ("Can write off move-out balance"). A **transfer** line moves a balance between leases, as in a unit transfer ("B011 to F035"), or carries an opening balance from the old system.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Refunding a payment the tenant still owes.** The charge comes back.
2. **Running a pending payment again.** It can double-charge.
3. **Giving a credit when a waive was meant.** A credit does not lower today's balance.
4. **Waiving without a real reason.** "Customer asked" is not a reason.

## Key Points

- Charges push the balance up; payments, waives and write-offs bring it down.
- A waive cancels one charge now; a credit waits for the next recurring bill.
- A declined card posts nothing; a returned bank payment reopens what it paid.
- A refund reopens the charge unless that charge is also waived.
- Never run a pending payment twice.
