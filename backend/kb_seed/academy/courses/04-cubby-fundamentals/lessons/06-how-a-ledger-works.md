- Explain double entry in plain words, and why you only need to look at one side of it.
- Tell apart a charge, a payment, a waive, a credit, prepaid money, a deposit, a refund, a write-off and a transfer.
- Predict how Cubby will apply a payment when several charges are open.
- Find a tenant's paid-through date and work out how many days past due they are.
- Read a ledger line by line and say what the balance is and why.
- Know which ledger actions need the Credits and Waives permission and which need your manager.

## Why the Ledger Matters

Most hard conversations at a storage counter are about money: "Why is my bill higher?", "I paid, why am I late?", "Why do I owe a fee?" Every one of those questions is answered by the ledger. If you can read it, you can answer the tenant calmly and correctly in a minute. If you cannot, you guess, and a wrong guess about money becomes a complaint, a refund or a lost tenant.

The ledger is also where mistakes are hardest to undo. A wrong waive, a refund on a charge the tenant still owes or a payment put on the wrong lease changes real money and real reports. This lesson gives you the ideas; the next lesson walks through eight worked examples with the arithmetic.

## Double Entry, in Plain Words

Cubby keeps a **double-entry ledger**. Every transaction is written down twice, once on each side: money the tenant owes on one side, and where it went in Greens' books on the other. A rent charge, for example, adds to what the tenant owes **and** to Greens' rent income. A payment takes away from what the tenant owes **and** adds to Greens' card or cash payments.

You do not need the accounting side to serve a tenant. From the tenant's point of view only one side matters: **Accounts Receivable**, which is simply "what this tenant owes Greens." Everything below is about that side.

> [!TIP]
> Think of the ledger as a bank statement in reverse. Charges push the balance up. Payments, waives and write-offs bring it down.

## The Building Blocks

Cubby's ledger search returns four kinds of entry: **charges**, **payments**, **transfers** and **write-offs**. It also keeps four running balances on every lease: **total due**, **deposit**, **prepaid** and **credit**. Here is what each piece means.

| Item | What it does to what the tenant owes | Plain example |
|---|---|---|
| **Charge** | Adds to it | December rent $129 posts on 12/01 |
| **Payment** | Takes away from it | The tenant pays $129 by card |
| **Waive** | Cancels all or part of one charge, now | You waive a $20 late fee |
| **Credit** | Does **not** change today's balance; is used on the next recurring bill | A $10 goodwill credit, used on next month's rent |
| **Prepaid** | Money paid ahead, held until charges post | The tenant pays three months ahead |
| **Deposit** | Money held, not used for rent, returned or kept at the end | A deposit carried over from the old system |
| **Refund** | Sends money back to the card or account it came from | A double payment is returned |
| **Write-off** | Clears a balance that will not be collected | A balance left after a move-out |
| **Transfer** | Moves a balance from one lease to another | A tenant moves to a bigger unit |

### Charges

A **charge** is anything the tenant owes. Cubby sorts charges into categories: rent, coverage (insurance or protection), fees, retail sales, damage, auction, tax, deposit and a few internal ones. Rent and coverage post by themselves on the 1st. Many fees post by themselves too, such as the late fee and the lien notice fees. Some you post by hand, such as a retail sale or a fee your manager approves.

**Discounts** post as their own line that takes away from the rent, not as a lower rent. A tenant on a 50% off promotion sees a full rent charge and a discount line beside it.

### Payments

A **payment** reduces what the tenant owes. Tenants pay by credit card, bank account (ACH), cash, check, money order and other methods. Cubby also has three "internal" payment methods that use money already on the lease: **prepaid**, **credit** and **deposit**. When Cubby uses a tenant's prepaid money to pay rent on the 1st, it shows up as a payment from prepaid.

A **declined card posts nothing** to the ledger. If the tenant says "I paid" and you see nothing, the most likely reason is that the card was declined.

### Waives

A **waive** cancels all or part of a **specific charge**, now. The balance drops at once. Cubby keeps a record that it was waived (as waived rent, waived fees or waived damage) against that charge, and a waive always needs a **reason**.

- Rent, fee, damage, coverage and deposit charges can be waived.
- Tax on a waived charge is waived in proportion.
- Taxes and waives themselves cannot be waived.
- If a waive leaves the lease below zero, the extra becomes prepaid money.

At Greens, waives are common: 185 fee waives totaling $12,904.41 and 42 rent waives totaling $3,307.76 since Greens moved to Cubby (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). That is why Greens has a waiver policy with limits (**Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds**).

### Credits

A **credit** is goodwill money held on the lease. It does **not** reduce today's balance. Instead Cubby uses it on the next recurring charges, in this order: rent, then coverage, then automatic fees such as late fees. It is never used on charges added by hand. Credits must be switched on per site.

Greens does not use credits today (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). You still need to know the difference, because a tenant who is "owed something" usually needs a waive of a specific charge, not a credit.

| Question | Waive | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| When does the balance drop? | Now | When the next recurring charge posts |
| Is it tied to a charge? | Yes, a specific charge | No, it waits on the lease |
| Used on a hand-added fee? | Can cancel it directly | Never |
| In use at Greens? | Yes | No |

### Prepaid

**Prepaid** is money the tenant paid ahead of any charge. If a tenant pays $400 and owes nothing, the whole $400 becomes prepaid. Cubby applies it automatically as each new charge posts.

One rule surprises people: **prepaid money does not move the paid-through date.** A tenant who prepays three months is still paid through the end of this month until next month's charges actually post and the prepaid pays them. The paid-through date then moves one month at a time.

### Deposits

A **deposit** is money Greens holds, such as a security deposit. It is not used for rent while the lease runs. At the end it is either returned to the tenant or kept (retained), and Cubby records which.

New Greens move-ins are not charged a deposit. Only some leases brought over from the old system at Fairfield carry one (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). If you see a deposit on a Fairfield parking lease, it came with the lease when Greens moved to Cubby.

### Refunds

A **refund** sends money back to the card or bank account it came from. A card refund can take a few days to show on the tenant's statement.

Here is the trap: a refund puts the money back on the lease **as owed**, unless the charge it paid is also waived. Refunding a payment for rent the tenant still owes leaves them owing that rent again. Never refund a charge the tenant still owes, and check **Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds** for who may approve a refund.

### Failed Payments, Returns and Disputes

- A **bank payment that comes back** (a returned or "NSF" payment) is marked failed in Cubby, which **reopens the charges** it had paid. The balance goes back up.
- A **card dispute** (a chargeback) shows as a disputed payment. Tell your manager the same day.
- A payment shown as **pending** has an unknown outcome. **Do not run it again**: you could charge the tenant twice. Wait, check again, and ask your manager if it stays pending.

### Write-Offs

A **write-off** clears a balance Greens will not collect, such as a small amount left after a move-out or a balance after an auction. It is recorded as bad debt with an explanation. Greens wrote off 6 balances totaling $3,264.51 since the move to Cubby (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Writing off is a manager decision.

### Transfers

A **transfer** moves a balance from one lease to another. You will see it when a tenant moves from one unit to another (the old lease's balance moves to the new lease), and on older leases as "imported" or "old system" balances that came over when Greens moved to Cubby. Those opening lines are normal.

## Oldest Charge First

When a payment comes in and several charges are open, Cubby decides which charge it pays. The rule is **oldest charge first**. A payment pays off October's rent before November's, and last month's late fee before this month's rent.

When several charges posted **at the same time** (rent and coverage both post on the 1st), Cubby pays them in this order:

1. Prepaid
2. Deposit
3. Rent
4. State tax
5. Local tax
6. Insurance (coverage)

So rent is paid before coverage. If a tenant owes $129 rent and $9 coverage and pays $100, the $100 goes to rent, leaving $29 of rent and $9 of coverage open.

> [!WARNING]
> Because payments go to the oldest charge first, a partial payment does not stop the delinquency steps. A tenant who pays "most of it" can still get the next fee or notice. Tell them the full amount that stops the process, and see **Greens Delinquency Timeline**.

## The Paid-Through Date

The **paid-through date** is the last day the lease is paid up to. It is the most useful single date on a lease.

- At a first-of-month site like every Greens site, a tenant who is current is paid through the **last day of this month**.
- When the tenant pays a whole month's charges, the paid-through date moves to the end of that month.
- A partial payment does not move it to the end of a month that is not fully paid. (How Cubby shows it on a partly paid month: check on a real lease, or ask your manager.)
- Prepaid money moves it only as each month's charges post and get paid.

The tenant is **late from** the day after the paid-through date.

## Days Past Due

Cubby counts how late a lease is from the paid-through date, with one day of grace:

**Days past due = today minus the paid-through date, minus 1.**

| Paid-through date | Today | Today minus paid-through | Days past due |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10/31 | 11/01 | 1 | 0 (rent is due today) |
| 10/31 | 11/06 | 6 | 5 |
| 10/31 | 11/11 | 11 | 10 |
| 10/31 | 11/15 | 15 | 14 |
| 09/30 | 11/04 | 35 | 34 |

Look at the 11/11 row. Greens' late fee posts at 12:00 AM on the 11th, after 10 full days unpaid (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). California allows a late fee only once rent is at least 10 days past due and the rental agreement says so. On the 15th the tenant has been unpaid 14 days, which is when California allows the preliminary lien notice, and that is the day Greens' CA Preliminary Lien Notice posts.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## Aging

**Aging** sorts what a tenant owes by how old it is. Cubby's **Aged Receivables** report puts each open charge into a bucket by its age: 0-30, 30-60, 60-90, 90-120 and over 120 days. A tenant can have money in two buckets at once: an old October charge in 30-60 and a new November charge in 0-30.

Collections views look at the whole lease instead, by days since the tenant went late. Both are useful: the per-charge view tells accounting how old the money is; the per-lease view tells you who to call first.

## Late Fees on the Ledger

The late fee is a charge like any other. Cubby posts it by itself as "CA Late Fee" on the 11th. Greens' amounts follow the California maximum for the tenant's rent (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026):

| Monthly rent | Late fee |
|---|---|
| $60 or less | $10.00 |
| Over $60 and under $100 | $15.00 |
| $100 or more | The greater of $20.00 or 15% of the monthly rent |

So rent of $129 gets a $20.00 fee (15% would be $19.35, less than $20.00), and rent of $200 gets $30.00 (15% of $200). Mailbox and commercial leases have their own late fees. The full list is on **Greens Fees and Charges**. You never type a late fee by hand.

## The Admin Fee

Every new storage or parking lease is charged a one-time **Administration Fee of $25.00** at move-in; a mailbox is charged a **Mailbox Administration Fee of $10.00** instead (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). It is a fee charge on the first day of the ledger, beside the first rent.

## Reading a Ledger, Line by Line

Here is a made-up Valley Center lease (rent $129, protection $9) for one month, as you would read it top to bottom:

| Date | Line | Charge | Payment or other | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12/01 | Rent | $129.00 | | $129.00 |
| 12/01 | Coverage | $9.00 | | $138.00 |
| 12/01 | Autopay declined | | (nothing posts) | $138.00 |
| 12/04 | Payment, card | | $100.00 | $38.00 |
| 12/11 | CA Late Fee | $20.00 | | $58.00 |
| 12/12 | Waive of late fee, reason "first late payment" | | $20.00 | $38.00 |
| 12/12 | Payment, card | | $38.00 | $0.00 |

Reading it:

- On 12/04 the $100 paid the rent first ($100 of $129), leaving $29 rent and $9 coverage open.
- The tenant was still unpaid on the 11th, so the late fee posted. A partial payment does not stop it.
- On 12/12 the fee was waived with a reason, and the tenant paid the last $38. The paid-through date moves to 12/31.

## Who Does What on the Ledger

| Action | Who can do it |
|---|---|
| Take a payment | Anyone whose role takes payments |
| Waive a charge or issue a credit | Staff with the **Credits and Waives** permission, inside Greens' waiver limits |
| Refund | As **Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds** says |
| Write off a balance | Your manager |
| Add a charge by hand | Only for a real sale or a fee your manager approves |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Saying "you're paid up" after a partial payment.** Check what is still open.
2. **Refunding a payment the tenant still owes.** The charge comes back.
3. **Running a pending payment again.** It can double-charge the tenant.
4. **Treating prepaid money as moving the paid-through date.** It moves only as charges post.
5. **Giving a credit when a waive was meant.** A credit does not lower today's balance, and Greens does not use credits.
6. **Waiving without a real reason.** "Customer asked" is not a reason.

## Key Points

- Charges push the balance up; payments, waives and write-offs bring it down.
- A waive cancels a specific charge now; a credit waits for the next recurring bill.
- Prepaid money is used as charges post and does not move the paid-through date until then.
- Payments go to the oldest charge first; at the same time, rent before coverage.
- Days past due = today minus the paid-through date, minus 1.
- Greens' late fee posts on the 11th at the California maximum for the rent; the admin fee is $25.00 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
