A Valley Center tenant emails that she is moving out at the end of the month. You could process it and move on. Instead you call her the next morning, live. Her mother moved into assisted living and the furniture was sold: there is nothing to save, and you thank her, confirm the steps and ask for a review. Selling gets the attention in training, but most of your calls are tenants, and keeping a good tenant is worth as much as finding a new one.

## Account Calls

### Confirm Who You Are Talking To

Before you say a balance, a unit number, a due date or a gate code, confirm the caller is the tenant or someone listed on the account, the way Greens Privacy and Records describes. An alternate contact receives notices but does not get account details unless they are also listed as authorized.

> [!NOTE]
> "I'm happy to help. I'm not able to discuss an account until I can confirm it's yours. If the tenant calls or comes in, I can help right away."

### Payments

- Take the payment the tenant's way: card at the counter, a payment link or the tenant portal.
- After every manual payment, **offer autopay**: "Would you like this to pay on the 1st automatically? Then you're far less likely to see a late fee."
- **The failed autopay call** that Cubby raises early in the month is a service call, not a collections call: "Your card on file didn't go through, probably an expired card. Can I help you update it so you don't get a late fee?"

### "Why Is This on My Bill?"

Open the ledger in Cubby with the tenant and explain each line. Most "wrong bill" calls are a prorated first month, a late fee, or a protection charge they forgot they chose (Cubby Fundamentals covers reading the ledger). If a charge is not on Greens Fees and Charges, do not guess: "Let me check with my manager and call you back by 3:00 PM."

## Move-Out Notices and the Save Call

When a tenant gives notice, **call them, live, within 24 hours.** Not an automated message, not an email. The call has two goals: understand why, and help if you can.

> [!NOTE]
> "Thanks for letting us know. Can I ask what's changing? ... If it helps, we have a 5x10 that would hold what you're keeping."

| What they say | A save that might fit |
|---|---|
| "I don't need this much space" | A smaller unit |
| "The rate went up too much" | A smaller unit; a concession only if your manager authorizes it (Why Did My Rent Go Up?) |
| "I had a problem here" | Fix the problem, apologize, and ask whether that changes things |
| "I'm moving out of the area" | No save. Thank them |
| "I finally have room at home" | No save. That is a happy ending |

### Most Move-Outs Are Happy Endings

Most people leave because their life change is over: the house closed, the remodel is done. Do not push a save on someone whose story just ended well.

> [!NOTE]
> "We've loved having you. Is there anything we could have done better?"

Then:

1. **Confirm the steps:** the date, the unit empty and broom-clean, their lock removed. Schedule the move-out in Cubby.
2. **Thank them** by name.
3. **Ask for a review** the same neutral way you ask everyone (Reviews and Reputation).
4. **Ask for a referral:** "If you know anyone who needs storage, we'd love to help them."

## When a Tenant Has a Loss

A leak, a break-in, pests. Difficult Situations covers the incident itself. For the claim:

1. **Check what they have** on the lease in Cubby: Greens' plan or their own policy.
2. **Explain the path, not the outcome.** "You'll want photos and a list of what was damaged, and a police report number if anything was stolen." The claim steps are on Greens Tenant Protection.
3. **Never promise the loss is covered.** The claims administrator, or their own insurer, decides.
4. **Never admit Greens is at fault**, and never blame the tenant.
5. **Follow up.** Silence after a loss is what turns a tenant into a bad review.

## At Greens

**Fairfield.** A trailer owner gives notice because he is selling the trailer, and mentions a boat next spring. You thank him, say you would love to have the boat, and note it in Cubby.

**Murrieta.** A motorhome owner gives notice because "the gate is always acting up." You apologize, report it, and call back the next day: it is fixed. He cancels the move-out.

**Escondido.** A tenant calls about a charge she does not recognize. You open the ledger together: it is the protection plan she chose at move-in. You show her where it is on her agreement.

## Key Points

- Confirm who you are talking to before any account detail.
- Offer autopay after every manual payment; the failed autopay call is a service call.
- Call within 24 hours of a move-out notice, live. Ask why; save only when it fits.
- Most move-outs are happy endings: thank them, confirm the steps, ask for a review and a referral.
- On a loss: explain the claim path, never promise coverage, never admit fault, and follow up.
