## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Handle an account call: confirm who you are talking to, then help.
- Turn a payment call into a chance to offer autopay.
- Have a rate-increase conversation that is honest, calm and offers real options.
- Call a tenant within 24 hours of a move-out notice, learn why, and offer a save only when it fits.
- Make a move-out a happy ending: thanks, a review and a referral.
- Point a tenant with a loss to the protection or insurance claim path without promising anything.

## Most of Your Calls Are Tenants

One vendor estimates that up to 80% of a storage site's calls come from existing tenants. That figure is not well sourced, but it matches what every site manager sees. Selling gets the attention in training, but **keeping a tenant is worth as much as finding a new one**, and costs less. A tenant who stays an extra six months at Valley Center's median enclosed street rate of $129 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026) is $774 in rent you did not have to win from scratch.

## Account Calls

### Confirm Who You Are Talking To

Before you say a balance, a unit number, a due date or a gate code, confirm the caller is the tenant or someone listed on the account, the way Greens requires (Greens Privacy and Records). An alternate contact receives lien notices but is not entitled to account details unless they are also listed as authorized.

> [!NOTE]
> "I'm happy to help. So I can talk about the account, can you confirm [the details Greens requires]?"

If they cannot, be kind and firm: "I'm sorry, I'm not able to discuss an account until I can confirm it's yours. If the tenant calls or comes in, I can help right away."

### Payments

- Take the payment the tenant's way: card at the counter, a payment link, the tenant portal (Cubby Fundamentals, Take a Payment).
- After every payment by phone or at the counter, **offer autopay**: "Would you like me to set this up so it pays on the 1st automatically? Then you never have to think about it, and you're far less likely to see a late fee."
- **The failed autopay call on the 2nd** (Cubby's "2nd - Call Failed Autopay Tenants" task, From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026) is a service call, not a collections call: "Hi, it's Lee at Greens Storage Fairfield. Your card on file didn't go through on the 1st, probably an expired card. Can I help you update it so you don't get a late fee?" The Collections With Respect lesson covers what changes once rent is actually late.

### "Why Is This On My Bill?"

Open the ledger in Cubby with the tenant. Explain each line. If a charge is not on Greens Fees and Charges, do not guess: "Let me check that with my manager and call you back by 3:00 PM." Most "wrong bill" calls are a prorated first month, a late fee, or a protection charge they forgot they chose. Cubby Fundamentals, the How a Ledger Works and Ledger Worked Examples lessons cover reading the ledger.

## Rate-Increase Conversations

Rate increases after a low move-in rate were the number one complaint in Modern Storage Media's 2018 list of the industry's top ten customer complaints. You cannot make an increase pleasant. You can make it honest and fair.

### Before an Increase Goes Out

- **It started at move-in.** The tenant who heard "rates are reviewed over time, and you'll get written notice" (the Move-In Done Right lesson) is far less angry than one who heard nothing.
- **Know the reasons.** Ask your manager why rates are changing: taxes, insurance, improvements, the market. You cannot answer confidently with reasons you do not know.
- **Flag the tenants who should not get a surprise.** If a tenant recently had a leak, a break-in or a hardship, tell your manager before the increase goes out.

### When the Tenant Calls

Use Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix (the The Greens Service Standard lesson).

1. **Hear.** Let them finish. They may have a lot to say.
2. **Own.** Tocarro Williams' wording (2022): "I understand this increase is hard to hear. Here's what's driving it, and I'm glad to walk through your options."
3. **Give the reasons**, honestly.
4. **Compare to the street rate, only if it is true.** "Your new rate is still below what a new customer pays for the same unit today." Check Cubby first. One operator says they never raise existing tenants to or above the current street rate; whether that is true for a tenant at Greens is a fact you check, not a promise you make.
5. **Offer the real options** within your authority:
   - a smaller unit that holds what they are keeping;
   - autopay, so at least the late fees never happen;
   - a concession for a long-time, on-time tenant, **only if your manager authorizes it**.
6. **Do not get defensive, and do not promise what you cannot deliver.** "I'll get it reversed" is never yours to say.
7. **Escalate with a time if they ask:** "I'll share this with my manager and you'll hear back within 24 hours."

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

How much notice a tenant gets before a rate change comes from the rental agreement and state law. We have not confirmed a California or Texas statute that sets one period for storage. Never tell a tenant a notice period you have not read in their agreement or confirmed with your manager.

## Move-Out Notices and the Save Call

When a tenant gives notice, **call them, live, within 24 hours** (Inside Self-Storage tenant retention guidance). Not an automated message, not an email. The call has two goals: understand why, and help if you can.

> [!NOTE]
> "Thanks for letting us know. Can I ask what's changing?
>
> ...If it helps, we have a 5x10 for [rent] that would hold what you're keeping. Or if it's the rate, let me see what I can do."

### When a Save Fits

| What they say | A save that might fit |
|---|---|
| "I don't need this much space anymore" | A smaller unit at the right rate |
| "The rate went up too much" | A smaller unit; a concession your manager authorizes; autopay with a late-fee waiver within your authority |
| "I had a problem here" (a leak, a gate issue, a rude interaction) | Fix the problem, apologize, and ask whether that changes things |
| "I'm moving out of the area" | No save. Thank them. Mention any Greens site near where they are going. |
| "I finally have room at home" | No save. That is a happy ending. |

### Most Move-Outs Are Happy Endings

One storage operator's October 2025 move-out report showed "no longer needed" as by far the top reason, with a single move-out for dissatisfaction. That is typical: **most people leave because their life change is over.** The house closed, the divorce is final, the remodel is done. Do not push a save on someone whose story just had a happy ending.

If they are leaving anyway:

> [!NOTE]
> "We've loved having you. Is there anything we could have done better?"

Then:

1. **Confirm the move-out steps:** the date, the unit empty and broom-clean, their lock removed. Schedule the move-out in Cubby (Cubby Fundamentals, Move-Outs).
2. **Thank them**, by name.
3. **Ask for a review** the same neutral way you ask everyone (the Reviews and Reputation lesson).
4. **Ask for a referral:** "If you know anyone who needs storage, we'd love to help them."

## Protection Claims

When a tenant has a loss (a leak, a break-in, pests), they will ask what happens next. The Difficult Situations lesson covers the incident itself. For the claim:

1. **Check what they have**: Greens' plan or their own policy (it is on the lease in Cubby).
2. **Explain the path, not the outcome.** "Here's how a claim works: [the claim steps on Greens Tenant Protection]. You'll want photos and a list of what was damaged, and a police report number if anything was stolen."
3. **Never promise the loss is covered.** The claims administrator, or their own insurer, decides.
4. **Never admit Greens is at fault**, and never blame the tenant.
5. **Follow up** to see how it is going. Silence after a loss is what turns a tenant into a bad review.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A tenant of four years calls, upset: "You raised me again!" You let her finish. You say you understand it is hard to hear, explain what is driving it, and check Cubby: her new rate is still below the street rate for her unit, so you tell her that. You offer autopay and ask whether a smaller unit would work. She stays.

**Valley Center.** A tenant emails a move-out notice for the end of the month. You call the next morning. Her mother moved into assisted living and the furniture was sold. There is no save. You thank her, confirm the move-out date and steps, and ask for a review.

**Fairfield.** A trailer owner gives notice because he is selling the trailer. He mentions he is buying a boat next spring. You thank him and tell him you would love to have the boat. Note it in Cubby.

**Murrieta.** A motorhome owner gives notice because "the gate is always acting up." You apologize, report the gate issue to your manager, and call back the next day: it has been fixed. He cancels the move-out.

**Temecula.** A tenant asks you to "just remove" the rate increase because he has been there five years. A concession is above your authority. You tell him you will ask your manager today and call him by 4:00 PM, and you do.

**A Texas site.** A tenant with a water-damaged box asks if "your insurance" will pay. You explain the claim path for her own policy, help her photograph the damage, and do not promise any outcome.

## Key Points

- Confirm who you are talking to before you share any account detail (Greens Privacy and Records).
- Offer autopay after every manual payment. The failed autopay call on the 2nd is a service call.
- Rate increases: hear them out, give the real reasons, compare to the street rate only if true, offer options within your authority, never promise a reversal. Notice periods come from the agreement; never guess one.
- Call within 24 hours of a move-out notice, live. Ask why. Offer a save only when it fits.
- Most move-outs are happy endings. Thank them, confirm broom-clean and lock removed, and ask for a review and a referral.
- On a loss: explain the claim path, never promise coverage, never admit fault, and follow up.
