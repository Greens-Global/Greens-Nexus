## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Confirm a move-in the day before.
- Explain the lease terms that cause most later complaints, in plain words, before the tenant signs.
- Offer protection honestly and record the tenant's choice.
- Give a move-in orientation: gate, lock, payment, rules and how to reach you.
- Follow up after the move-in so the tenant stays, pays and tells their friends.

## Most Complaints Start at Move-In

Look at the industry's top complaints: rate increases after a low first month, late fees, lien fees, "nobody told me." A 2018 Modern Storage Media list of the top ten customer complaints put rent increases and expiring specials at number one. Almost every one of those complaints traces back to a move-in where something was not explained.

The move-in is your chance to prevent next year's angry phone call. Cubby Fundamentals (The Move-In) taught you the clicks. This lesson is the conversation that goes with them.

## The Day Before: Confirm

Garrett Byrd's follow-up guidance (2021) is simple: **the day before the move-in, confirm the arrival time.**

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi Karen, it's Daniel at Greens Storage Escondido. Just confirming your 10x20 for tomorrow. Are you still planning on about 10:00 AM? ... Great. Bring a photo ID and the card you'd like to use. If you'd like a lock or boxes, I'll have them ready."

For a vehicle customer, add: "Please bring the registration and your insurance card for the trailer." RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals, Paperwork and Move-In lists what to collect.

The confirmation call also catches the customer whose plans changed. Better to know today than to hold a unit for someone who is not coming.

## Completing the Lease

Cubby's move-in flow drives the order: unit, Preview Charges, coverage, payment, autopay, documents, gate code and lock (Cubby Fundamentals, The Move-In). Your job alongside the clicks:

1. **Verify identity.** Photo ID. Make sure the name on the ID matches the lease.
2. **Collect contact details.** Name, phone, email, address, and an **alternate contact**. Explain why: "This is someone we can reach if we ever can't reach you. We won't share your account with them."
3. **Ask about military status**, and record it. Self-Storage Fundamentals, Lines You Never Cross explains why.
4. **Walk through the terms below** and have the tenant initial where the agreement asks.
5. **Show them the move-in total** from Preview Charges, so the first charge is never a surprise.

## The Terms That Cause Most Complaints

Explain each of these in plain words. Do not read the agreement aloud; point to each section and say what it means.

| Term | What to say at a Greens California site |
|---|---|
| **Due date** | "Rent is due on the 1st of every month." (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026) |
| **Late fee** | "If rent isn't paid by the end of the 10th, a late fee posts on the 11th." Show them the amount for their rent on Greens Fees and Charges. (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026) |
| **If rent goes unpaid** | "If rent stays unpaid, access is suspended and the lien process starts, which can end in a sale. Here's the timeline in your agreement." Point to it; Greens Delinquency Timeline has the days. |
| **Rates can change** | "Rates are reviewed over time, and you'll always get written notice before a change." If there is a move-in special, say exactly when it ends. |
| **Prohibited items and uses** | "No flammables, food or anything living, and nobody can stay or live in a unit, even for a night." |
| **Access** | "The gate is open [gate hours]; the office is open [office hours]." |
| **Who can get in** | "Whoever has your gate code and key can come and go. If you give them to someone, they have access until you change your code or your lock." |
| **Trash** | "Please take out whatever you bring in. Anything left at the dumpster costs everyone." |
| **Move-out** | "When you're ready to move out, let us know, empty the unit, sweep it, and take your lock." |
| **Protection** | Below. |

For a Texas site, the due date, late fee and lien steps come from that site's agreement. Until the Texas sites are in Cubby, ask your Area Manager what to say.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

Explain the lien process accurately and the same way to everyone. Never soften it into "we'd never actually sell your stuff," and never make it sound like a threat. It is a fact in the agreement, said kindly.

### The Gate Code and Key Line

That "whoever has your gate code and key" line is worth saying every single time. A storage law firm (Greenberger and Brewer, 2014) described a case where a divorcing tenant had listed her husband on an "authorized access" list and forgot to remove him; he demanded the lock be cut and called the police. Greens' rule is simple and protects everyone: **the tenant controls access with their code and their key.** The Difficult Situations lesson covers what to do when someone else asks to get in.

## Offering Protection Honestly

Self-Storage Fundamentals, Tenant Protection and Insurance covered how protection works. The move-in version:

1. **Kill the myth.** "Our property insurance doesn't cover what's inside your unit. Most people don't know that."
2. **Tell a real story, not a disclosure.** "A common problem at storage sites is water: a roof leak or a neighbor's unit. Protection is what helps if that happens."
3. **Give the choice.** Greens' plan, $2,500 or $5,000 of coverage, $14.00 or $25.00 a month for a new move-in (From greensstorage.com, 11/2026), or their own policy if it covers storage.
4. **Record the choice in Cubby.** Plan, own policy with the policy number and expiration date, or (rarely) none. Industry guidance is to have the tenant initial or sign the choice, so it is on record.

> [!NOTE]
> "Do you have homeowner's or renter's insurance? Many policies cover things in storage, but check the off-premises limit. If yours does, I'll just need the policy number and the expiration date. If not, our plan starts at $9 a month for $2,500 of coverage."

**Never** call Greens' plan "insurance," never say it is required unless the agreement says so, and never promise a loss will be covered: the claims administrator decides. Vehicle tenants carry their own vehicle insurance; only one vehicle lease is on a Greens plan (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

## The Orientation

Before they leave the counter:

- [ ] **Gate code** set in Cubby, written down for them privately, and tried at the keypad if they are moving in now
- [ ] **Lock** on the unit, theirs or one they bought, and they know Greens does not keep a key
- [ ] **Autopay** offered: "This way you never have to think about it, and you'll never get a late fee." If they say yes, turn it on in Cubby after the payment succeeds
- [ ] **How to pay** otherwise: the tenant portal, a payment link, or the office (Cubby Fundamentals, Take a Payment)
- [ ] **Hours**, gate and office, and how to reach the office
- [ ] **Where to load**, the carts, the dumpster rule
- [ ] **Their unit number**, said out loud and written down

Then the second mile (the Greens Service Standard lesson): carry the lock and boxes to the car, or walk them to the unit.

## After the Move-In

| When | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| That day | A thank-you: a handwritten card or a short text | DeFazio, 2026 |
| A few days later | A call: "How did the move go? Everything working with your code?" | DeFazio, 2026 |
| Once they are settled | Ask for a review, the same neutral way you ask everyone (the Reviews and Reputation lesson) | |
| Any time | Mention the referral program, if Greens runs one | Jordheim, 2012 |

The call a few days later catches problems while they are small: a code that does not work at the exit gate, a latch that sticks, a question about the first bill.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** You confirm Karen's move-in the day before. She says the closing moved to Monday. You move the reservation and thank her for telling you. On Monday, the unit is ready and swept.

**Valley Center.** A first-time renter is about to sign. You point to the due date, the late fee day, the rate-change notice and the lien section, and explain each in one sentence. She initials them. A year later, her rate changes and she is not surprised.

**Temecula.** A boat owner says his homeowner's policy "covers everything." You ask him to check that it covers the boat in storage, and you record the policy number and expiration date in Cubby. Cubby will raise a reminder task if proof is missing or expired.

**Fairfield.** A trailer owner moves in. You set his gate code, then walk with him to the keypad so he can try it on the way out. Two days later, you call: "Everything working with the gate?" It is.

**Murrieta.** A couple moving in their motorhome mentions they will "probably stay in it one night before the trip." You explain kindly that nobody may stay overnight on the lot, and why: it is a safety rule for everyone.

**A Texas site.** A new tenant asks when rent is due and when a late fee applies. You do not guess from the California rules. You read it from the site's agreement and check with your Area Manager.

## Key Points

- Confirm every move-in the day before (Byrd, 2021).
- Verify photo ID, collect an alternate contact, and ask about military status.
- Explain the terms that cause complaints before they sign: due on the 1st, late fee on the 11th, what happens if rent goes unpaid, rates can change, prohibited items and no living in units, access hours, the gate code and key rule, trash, and move-out (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026, for the California days).
- Offer protection with a real story, record the choice in Cubby, and never call the plan insurance or promise coverage.
- Orientation: gate code, lock, autopay, how to pay, hours, where to load, the unit number.
- Thank them the same day, call a few days later, and ask for a review once they are settled.
