## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Greet a walk-in within seconds and pick the right conversation for them.
- Prepare a unit or space before the tour.
- Give the five-stop tour: before, at the gate, on the way, at the unit, and the close at the unit.
- Stay safe on every tour: never enter a unit with a prospect.
- Turn "I'll think about it" at the counter into a written quote, a hold and a set follow-up.

## Walk-Ins Are Your Best Customers

Someone who drove to your site is much closer to renting than someone who called. Mixon's mystery-shop standard (2020) is to **close 90% of walk-ins who come in to rent.** Garrett Byrd (2021) claims a customer who visits will rent "nine times out of 10" when the presentation is handled well. That is a trainer's claim, not a study, but every site manager knows the feeling: if they came in, you should not let them leave without a rental, a reservation or a firm plan.

### Why Walk-Ins Leave Without Renting

Eric Mees reviewed walk-in mystery shops in 2020 (Inside Self-Storage) and found the same failures again and again:

| Failure | The fix |
|---|---|
| No urgency | Tie it to their date: "Your move is Saturday. Let's make sure this unit is yours." |
| Assuming the customer knows storage | Explain the gate, the lock and the paperwork before they ask |
| No stated difference from competitors | Say your site's one clear thing (the The Nine Parts of a Sales Call lesson) |
| No written quote | Hand-write a quote sheet for the units you showed, with the site's phone number and your name |
| No contact capture | Ask for name, phone and email so you can send the quote and hold the unit |

## The Greeting

1. **Stand up.** Mystery shoppers grade it.
2. **Greet within seconds**, even if you are on the phone: a smile and a raised finger, "I'll be right with you."
3. **Introduce yourself** and ask their name.
4. **Ask if they called before.** It decides where you start. If they spoke with you yesterday, you do not repeat discovery; you pick up where you left off.

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi, welcome to Greens Storage Valley Center. I'm Maria. What's your name? ... Nice to meet you, David. Did you call us earlier, or is this your first time talking with us?"

Then do discovery (the The Nine Parts of a Sales Call lesson, part 3) at the counter. Recommend a size (the Recommending a Size lesson). Then offer to show it.

> [!NOTE]
> "The best way to know it fits is to see it. Do you have ten minutes? I'll show you the 10x15 I have in mind."

## The Tour Route

### 1. Before You Walk Out

- Pick the route and the unit before you stand up. Know the unit number.
- Make sure the unit is clean, swept and lit. Your morning walk (Self-Storage Fundamentals, Running the Site: Open to Close) should already have done this for your show units.
- Bring a cart if the unit is near one. Bring a disc lock to show.
- Take your phone. Tell a coworker where you are going, or follow your site's check-in routine if you are alone (the Difficult Situations lesson).

### 2. At the Gate

- Have the prospect **practice the keypad** with a demo or show code, if your site has one. A first-timer who has pressed the buttons once is not nervous on move-in day.
- Point out the security they can see: the gate, cameras, lighting, the on-site office.
- Say the gate hours and the office hours.

> [!NOTE]
> "Every tenant gets their own code, so we always know who's on site. You'd use it right here, coming and going. The gate's open [gate hours] every day."

### 3. On the Way

Point out what they will use, tied to what they told you:

- Carts and the elevator (if your site has one)
- Where to park the truck for loading
- The dumpster and the rule: take out what you bring in
- For vehicle customers: the wash bay or dump station if your site has one, the turning room, and where the pull-through rows are

### 4. At the Unit

- Repeat the unit number and size.
- Open the door and **let them look in from the doorway, then step in themselves.**
- Show how the latch closes and let them try it. Show the difference between a disc lock and a padlock (Self-Storage Fundamentals, Access, Locks and Security).
- Place their things out loud (the Recommending a Size lesson): "Your sofa goes on end along this wall..."
- If you are showing two sizes, show the smaller first, then the one you recommend.

### 5. Close at the Unit

Ask right there, while they are picturing their things inside.

> [!NOTE]
> "This unit will work great for you. Let's go back to the office and take care of the paperwork."

Or, more directly (Jordheim):

> [!NOTE]
> "Would you like to rent this one today?"

## Safety on Every Tour

> [!SAFETY]
> Never enter a unit with a prospect or a tenant. Stay in the doorway. Let them step in while you stand outside the door.

Older sales advice says to step into the unit first so the customer follows you. **Greens does not do that.** Storage operators list "never enter a unit with a tenant or prospect" as safety rule one (Inside Self-Storage operator roundup, 12/31/2025). A closed unit is a box with one door. Stand in the doorway, hand them the tape measure, and talk them through it from there.

More tour safety:

- **Never say you are alone.** "My coworker's in the office" or "My manager will be right out."
- **Keep your phone in your hand.**
- **Trust your instincts.** You may end a tour at any time, or offer a video tour instead of walking the property. A short video or photos of the exact unit, sent by text, is a fine substitute.
- **Do not tour after dark alone.** Offer a time the next day.

The Difficult Situations lesson and Self-Storage Fundamentals, Safety and Emergencies cover working alone in more depth.

## Touring a Vehicle Lot

Vehicle customers want to see the space, the access and the neighbors.

1. Walk the route they will drive: the gate, the turn, the aisle.
2. Show the space and its markings. Tell them the length and the margin you allowed (RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals, Measuring for the Right Fit).
3. For covered spaces, point to the clear height posted for that row, or tell them you will measure it for them. Never guess a clearance.
4. Point out lighting and cameras on the lot.
5. Close at the space: "This one fits with room to spare. Want me to put your name on it?"

## When They Are Not Ready

Not every walk-in rents on the spot. Do not let them leave with nothing.

1. **Ask what is holding them back.** "Is it the size, the price, or the timing?" The Objections, Honestly lesson covers each answer.
2. **Write a quote sheet:** the unit numbers and sizes you showed, the rent from Cubby, any special and when it ends, the $25 admin fee (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026), the protection options, your name and the site's phone number.
3. **Capture their details** so you can follow up: "Can I get your email so I can send this to you, along with the unit details?"
4. **Offer a free hold.** "I can reserve it for you with no charge. If you change your mind, just let me know." Greens charges no reservation fee (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
5. **Ask whether they want a heads-up** if their size runs low.
6. **Set a specific follow-up.** "Can I call you Thursday at 5:00 PM?" Put it in Cubby as a task.

Then follow up as promised.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A couple walks in while you are on the phone. You smile, hold up a finger, finish the call in 30 seconds, and stand up. They are moving from a three-bedroom house. You show a 10x15 first, then the 10x20 you recommend. At the 10x20, they picture the dining table along the back wall. You close at the unit.

**Valley Center.** A woman wants to see a drive-up 10x10, and she has never rented storage. At the gate, you let her practice the keypad. At the unit, you show her how a disc lock works. She rents, and says the gate practice is what made her feel ready.

**Murrieta.** A fifth-wheel owner wants to see the pull-through rows. You walk the route he will drive, show the space, and tell him its length and the margin you allowed. He asks to reserve it for the 1st.

**Temecula.** A prospect insists you step inside the far unit with him to measure. You stay in the doorway and hand him the tape: "I'll call out the numbers from here." He measures; you write them down.

**Fairfield.** A walk-in loves the space but says he needs to check his budget. You write a quote sheet, get his email, offer a free hold, and set a call for Friday at 10:00 AM. On Friday at 10:00 AM, you call.

**A Texas site.** You are alone on site late in the afternoon and a prospect wants to see a unit at the back of the property. You do not feel right about it. You offer to text him a video of the unit now, and a tour tomorrow morning when your coworker is in.

## Key Points

- Walk-ins are the easiest customers to close. The mystery-shop standard is 90% of walk-ins who come to rent (Mixon, 2020).
- Stand up, greet within seconds, and ask whether they called before.
- The tour: prepare the unit, practice the keypad at the gate, point out what they will use on the way, let them try the latch at the unit, and close at the unit.
- Never enter a unit with a prospect. Stay in the doorway. Never say you are alone. You may offer a video tour instead.
- If they are not ready: ask what is holding them back, write a quote sheet, capture their contact details, offer a free hold, and set a specific follow-up time.
