A couple walks into Valley Center while you are on the phone. They drove here, which means they are much closer to renting than any caller. If they leave with nothing, that is almost always something we did: no urgency, nothing in writing, no way to reach them. This lesson walks you from the greeting to the close at the unit, and keeps you safe the whole way.

## The Greeting

1. **Stand up.**
2. **Greet within seconds**, even on the phone: a smile and a raised finger, "I'll be right with you."
3. **Introduce yourself** and ask their name.
4. **Ask if they called before.** If they spoke with you yesterday, pick up where you left off instead of starting over.

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi, welcome to Greens Storage Valley Center. I'm Maria. What's your name? ... Nice to meet you, David. Did you call us earlier, or is this the first time we're talking?"

Then do discovery at the counter (The Nine Parts of a Sales Call), recommend a size (Recommending a Size) and offer to show it: "The best way to know it fits is to see it. Do you have ten minutes?"

**1. Before you walk out.** Know the unit number. The unit is swept and lit (your morning walk should have done this). Bring a disc lock to show. Take your phone, and tell a coworker where you are going.

**2. At the gate.** Let a first-timer practice the keypad with a demo code if your site has one. Point out the security they can see: the gate, cameras, lighting. Say the gate and office hours. "Every tenant gets their own code, so we always know who's on site."

**3. On the way.** Point out what they will use, tied to what they told you: carts, where to park the truck, the dumpster rule (take out what you bring in).

**4. At the unit.** Repeat the unit number and size. Open the door and let them step in while you stay in the doorway. Let them try the latch. Show a disc lock next to a padlock. Place their things out loud: "Your sofa goes on end along this wall..." Showing two sizes? The smaller first.

**5. Close at the unit**, while they are picturing their things inside: "This one will work great for you. Shall we go take care of the paperwork?"

## Safety on Every Tour

> [!SAFETY]
> Never enter a unit with a prospect or a tenant. Stay in the doorway. Let them step in while you stand outside.

A closed unit is a box with one door. Hand them the tape measure and call out the numbers from the doorway.

- **Never say you are alone.** "My coworker's in the office."
- **Keep your phone in your hand.**
- **Trust your instincts.** You may end a tour at any time, or offer a video of the exact unit by text instead.
- **No tours after dark alone.** Offer a time the next day.

Self-Storage Fundamentals, Safety and Emergencies covers working alone.

## Touring a Vehicle Lot

1. Walk the route they will drive: the gate, the turn, the aisle.
2. Show the space and tell them its length and the margin you allowed (RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals, Measuring for the Right Fit).
3. For covered spaces, point to the posted clear height or offer to measure. Never guess a clearance.
4. Close at the space: "This one fits with room to spare. Want me to put your name on it?"

## When They Are Not Ready

Do not let them leave with nothing.

1. **Ask what is holding them back.** "Is it the size, the price or the timing?" (Objections, Honestly.)
2. **Write a quote sheet:** the units you showed, the rent from Cubby, any special and when it ends, the admin fee, the protection options, your name and the site's phone.
3. **Capture their email** so you can send it.
4. **Offer a free hold.** Greens charges no reservation fee.
5. **Set a specific follow-up** ("Can I call you Thursday at 5:00 PM?") and put it in Cubby as a task. Then call.

## At Greens

**Escondido.** A couple moving from a three-bedroom house sees a 10x15 first, then the 10x20 you recommend. At the 10x20 they picture the dining table along the back wall. You close at the unit.

**Fairfield.** A walk-in loves the space but wants to check his budget. Quote sheet, email, free hold, a call set for Friday at 10:00 AM. On Friday at 10:00 AM, you call.

## Key Points

- Walk-ins are the easiest customers to close; aim to close nearly every one who came to rent.
- Stand up, greet within seconds, and ask whether they called before.
- Prepare the unit, practice the keypad, show what they will use, and close at the unit.
- Never enter a unit with anyone; never say you are alone.
- Not ready: what is holding you back, a quote sheet, an email, a free hold and a set time.
