## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Name the nine parts of a sales call in order.
- Open every call with the property name, your name and an offer to help.
- Ask the four discovery questions and the follow-ups that matter at Greens.
- Build value by tying features to what the caller said, before you give the price.
- Quote price, fees and protection accurately, then ask for the rental.
- Capture name, phone, email, move date and the unit quoted on every call.

## Scripts Set the Tone, Not the Words

The scripts in this lesson are assembled from the trade sources behind this course (Ballard and Maxwell 2011, Weinman 2010, Mixon 2020, DeFazio 2026 and the Inside Self-Storage script articles) and adapted for Greens. Learn the **structure** by heart. Use the words as a starting point and make them yours. The lesson Who Walks Through Our Door told you callers can tell within seconds whether you sound scripted. A script you read sounds like a script. A structure you know lets you listen.

You will not always go through all nine in order. A caller may raise an objection at part 2 or want the price in the first sentence. That is fine. Know where you are, answer what they asked, and steer back.

## Part 1: The Greeting

1. Pick up by the third ring, smiling.
2. Say the company and property name.
3. Say your name.
4. Offer to help.

> [!NOTE]
> "Thank you for calling Greens Storage Valley Center, this is Maria. How can I help you today?"

That is it. No long speech, no "Greens Storage where we treat you like family, how may I direct your call." Short, warm, clear.

## Part 2: Find Out Why They Called

Answer what they asked first. Then ask one question that opens the conversation. The lesson Why the Phone Wins or Loses Rentals gave you the hours, price and directions versions. The general form:

> [!NOTE]
> "Sure, I can help you with that. Are you looking at renting, or is this about an account you already have with us?"

If it is a tenant, help them (the Existing Tenants: Payments, Rate Increases and Move-Outs lesson). If it is a shopper, go to part 3.

## Part 3: Discovery

These four questions come from Ballard and Maxwell's 2011 storage sales training, and they still hold up.

| Question | Why you ask it |
|---|---|
| "Have you ever rented storage before?" | A first-timer needs you to explain the gate, locks and paperwork. An experienced renter needs less, and may be comparing you to their old site. |
| "What are you planning to store?" | Decides the size and type. Also screens for things that cannot be stored (the Recommending a Size lesson) and spots business users and vehicles. |
| "When do you need to move in?" | Creates honest urgency and tells you which unit to hold. |
| "How long do you think you'll need it?" | Shapes the unit choice (ground floor for frequent access) and what you say about rates over time. |

Add these at Greens:

- **"Is any of it sensitive to heat?"** Wood furniture, electronics, photos, documents, instruments and mattresses (the Recommending a Size lesson).
- **For vehicles:** "What's the overall length, including the tongue or hitch? And the height?" and "How often do you take it out?" (RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals, Measuring for the Right Fit).
- **Get the name and number early.** "Who am I speaking with? And in case we get cut off, what's the best number for you?" Asked early and naturally, almost nobody says no.

> [!NOTE]
> "Happy to help you find the right space. Have you rented storage before?
>
> ...What are you planning to store? Is that from an apartment or a house, and about how many rooms?
>
> ...Anything big, like a sectional, a piano or a king bed?
>
> ...When are you moving?
>
> ...And how long do you think you'll need it?"

**Listen to the answers and write them down.** Every answer is something you will use in parts 4 to 7.

## Part 4: Recommend

**Never ask "What size do you need?"** Most callers have no idea, and the question hands them a decision they cannot make. You are the expert. Ask what they are storing, then recommend a size and say why. The Recommending a Size lesson is all about this.

> [!NOTE]
> "From what you've told me, a two-bedroom with a sectional and about 30 boxes, I'd put you in a 10x15. That gives you room for everything plus a walkway, so you can get to the boxes you'll need before spring."

## Part 5: Build Value

Value is a feature tied to something the caller said. Pick two or three that matter to **this** caller, not a list of everything the site has.

| Feature | Tied to what they said |
|---|---|
| Drive-up unit | "You mentioned you're doing this yourself with a rental truck. With a drive-up unit you back right up to the door." |
| Climate control | "You said the dresser was your mom's and it's solid wood. Our climate-controlled units keep it out of the summer heat." |
| Gate and cameras | "You said you'll be away for work a lot. Every tenant has their own gate code, and the site has cameras." |
| Pull-through space | "You said backing the fifth wheel in is the hard part. We have pull-through spaces, so you drive straight in and straight out." |
| Covered parking | "You said the boat's gelcoat took a beating last summer. Our covered spaces keep the sun off it." |

Then say **one clear thing that makes this site different**. A 2020 walk-in shopping report (Eric Mees, Inside Self-Storage) found that "no stated difference from competitors" was one of the most common reasons shoppers left. Know your site's one thing. Your Site Profile in RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals is a good place to start.

> [!NOTE]
> "Since your movers come on the 15th and the new place isn't ready until spring, I'd put you in a 10x15 on the ground floor. It's drive-up, so you can back the truck right to the door, and with a sofa, a queen bed and about 30 boxes you'll still have a walkway. You mentioned your mom's dresser. If it's solid wood, our climate-controlled building keeps it out of the summer heat."

## Part 6: Price

Give the price **after** value, never before (unless they asked for it first, in which case give a range and then go to discovery). Say every part of it up front. Surprise fees are one of the industry's top complaints.

1. **Monthly rent** for the unit you recommended. Read it from Cubby; never quote from memory.
2. **Any special**, exactly as Cubby shows it, and **when it ends**. If there is no special, say nothing about specials.
3. **The admin fee:** $25.00 at every Greens site (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). A mailbox rental at Valley Center has been charged a $10.00 Mailbox Administration Fee instead (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
4. **Protection:** every tenant has Greens' protection plan or their own policy. Greens' plan levels run from $9.00 to $25.00 a month (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026; details on Greens Tenant Protection). Never call the plan "insurance."
5. **A lock**, if they need one.
6. **Rates can change.** Say it plainly: "Like every storage company, our rates are reviewed over time. You'll always get written notice before a change."

Greens charges no reservation fee and no deposit on new move-ins (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Say so: it is a reason to reserve now.

> [!NOTE]
> "That 10x15 is [rent] a month. There's a one-time $25 admin fee, and you'll choose protection for your things: our plans start at $9 a month, or you can use your own insurance if it covers storage. There's no deposit and no charge to reserve it."

> [!TIP]
> Because Greens bills on the 1st with jump day 26, the first payment depends on the move-in date. Do not guess it. Run Preview Charges in Cubby (Cubby Fundamentals, The Move-In) and quote that number.

## Part 7: Close

Ask. Most storage staff never do (the Why the Phone Wins or Loses Rentals lesson: only 25.4% in the 2004 IntelliShop study). Three closes that work:

**The assumptive close.** Act as if they have decided, and ask about the details.

> [!NOTE]
> "Great, let's get you set up in the 10x15. When do you want to move in? Does Saturday work?"

**The option close.** Offer two choices, both of which are a yes. A trade tip is to offer one unit below and one above the target.

> [!NOTE]
> "I have a 10x15 on the ground floor at [rent], or a 10x20 at [rent] if you'd like extra room. Which one feels right?"

**The reservation close.** For someone not ready to rent today.

> [!NOTE]
> "I can reserve it right now, with no charge, so it's held for the 15th. What's the best email for the confirmation?"

**The visit close.** If they want to see it first, set a time, not "come by anytime."

> [!NOTE]
> "I'd love to show it to you. I have openings at 11:00 AM or 3:00 PM tomorrow. Which is better?"

## Part 8: Objections

Most calls have one. "I need to check with my husband." "The place on the highway is cheaper." "I'll think about it." The Objections, Honestly lesson covers them all. The short rule: **listen, find out what is underneath, answer honestly, then ask again.**

## Part 9: Capture and Follow Up

Before you hang up, you have:

- [ ] Name
- [ ] Phone
- [ ] Email
- [ ] Move-in date
- [ ] The unit and price you quoted
- [ ] How they heard about Greens (the lead source in Cubby)
- [ ] A set time for the next contact

Put all of it in Cubby as a lead, or as a reservation if they reserved (Cubby Fundamentals, Leads and Reservations). Then close with the property name and thanks.

> [!NOTE]
> "Let me check whether a ground-floor unit opens up before the 15th and call you back by 3:00 PM. Is this the best number? ... Thank you for calling Greens Storage Valley Center, Mrs. Lopez. Talk to you at 3:00 PM."

Then call at 3:00 PM.

## A Full Call, Start to Finish

**Escondido, a Tuesday morning.**

1. **Greeting.** "Thank you for calling Greens Storage Escondido, this is Daniel. How can I help you?"
2. **Why.** "How much is a 10x10?" Daniel gives the range, then: "What are you planning to store? I want to make sure a 10x10 is even right."
3. **Discovery.** First time renting. A three-bedroom house; selling it, moving into an apartment next month. A piano and a dining set. "Probably six months." Daniel gets her name, Karen, and her number.
4. **Recommend.** "A three-bedroom with a piano won't fit in a 10x10. I'd put you in a 10x20."
5. **Value.** "Pianos and wood dining sets don't like the heat. We have climate-controlled units here, and a 10x20 drive-up if you'd rather back the truck up. Let's talk about which."
6. **Price.** She picks climate control. Daniel quotes the rent from Cubby, the $25 admin fee, and the protection options, and says there is no deposit or reservation charge.
7. **Close.** "Your closing is on the 28th. Want me to reserve it for the 27th so you're not rushing?"
8. **Objection.** "I need to talk to my son first." Daniel offers to email the quote and hold the unit, and asks when they will both be free.
9. **Capture.** Email, move date, unit and price go into the Cubby lead with a follow-up task for Thursday at 6:00 PM, when Karen and her son will be together. Daniel thanks her using the property name. On Thursday at 6:00 PM, he calls.

## Greens Scenarios

**Fairfield.** A caller opens with "Do you have parking for a 26-foot trailer?" You do not stop at "yes." You ask the overall length with the tongue, how often they tow it, and their name and number. Then you recommend a space size with a margin (RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals, Measuring for the Right Fit) and ask to reserve it.

**Murrieta.** A fifth-wheel owner wants "something easy to get in and out of." Your value statement is the pull-through space. Your close: "I can hold a pull-through for you today. Want me to?"

**Temecula.** A boat owner asks the price first. You give the range for open and covered spaces, then ask the boat's length on the trailer and how often he goes out.

**A Texas site.** A caller asks whether you have "storage." You give the greeting, find out she is moving from a two-bedroom apartment in three weeks, and recommend a 10x15 with a reason. She reserves it.

## Key Points

- The nine parts: greeting, why they called, discovery, recommend, build value, price, close, objections, capture and follow up.
- Learn the structure, not the words. Answer what they asked, then steer back.
- Discovery: rented before? What are you storing? When? How long? Plus heat-sensitive items, vehicle length and height, and the name and number early.
- Never ask "What size do you need?" Recommend, and say why.
- Value is a feature tied to their words. Say one clear thing that makes this site different.
- Price after value, all of it up front: rent from Cubby, any special and its end date, the $25 admin fee, protection from $9 a month, a lock, and that rates can change (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Ask for the rental, the reservation, or a set visit time. Capture everything in Cubby and call back exactly when you said.
