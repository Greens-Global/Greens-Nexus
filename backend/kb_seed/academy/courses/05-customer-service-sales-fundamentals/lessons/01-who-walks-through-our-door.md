The phone rings at Escondido. A woman apologizes for "rambling" before she has said much at all: she is emptying her late father's condo and the buyer closes in two weeks. She does not need a pitch. She needs someone calm who knows what to do next. By the end of this lesson you will know who is on the other end of most of our calls, and how to set the right tone in the first minute.

## Something Changed in Their Life

You met the reasons people rent in Self-Storage Fundamentals, Why People Rent: a move, a death, a divorce, downsizing, a remodel, a home that is simply too full. The pattern to remember is this: **most people who call you are in the middle of a change**, and some of them are going through the hardest month of their year.

What that means for how you sound:

- **They are stressed.** Many are mid-move, mid-divorce or grieving. A calm, unhurried voice is a relief.
- **They are new to storage.** Most shoppers have never rented a unit. They do not know how a gate keypad works, what a disc lock is or what size they need. Do not use words they have not heard.
- **They can tell if you care.** Callers decide in the first few seconds whether you sound scripted or interested.
- **Their things are their history.** To you it is a 10x10. To them it is their mother's dresser.
- **They have read the bad stories.** Rate increases after a cheap first month, surprise fees and lien sales are the complaints people hear about storage. Your job is to show Greens is different.

> [!TIP]
> You never need to pry about why someone is renting. "Is this for a move, or just making some room?" lets them share as much as they want.

## They Shop, Then They Call

Customers look at prices, photos and reviews online before they call, and most have a few sites in mind. So the call is not the start of their decision; it is near the end. They already have prices in their head. What they are testing now is you: are you helpful, honest and easy to deal with?

## What Decides the Choice

Price matters, and you will learn to talk about it honestly. But price is rarely the only reason. Location, security, how clean the site looks, the hours and how the customer was treated decide most close calls. **People rent from the person who helped them, not from the cheapest sign on the road.**

## The First Good Conversation Wins

Many shoppers stop shopping as soon as someone helps them well. If you are the first site they call and you help them, they often never call the next one. If you are the third, you need to be clearly better than the first two.

What this means every day:

- **Answer the phone.** A missed call is a rental handed to the next site on the list.
- **Help first, quote second.** A price is easy to compare. Help is not.
- **Make the next step easy.** A free reservation, a set time to visit, a text with the unit and the price.

## They Stay Longer Than They Think

Almost every caller says they need storage for "a month or two." Most stay far longer; Greens' average stay is on Greens by the Numbers. So the person who "only needs it till spring" may be with you for years. Treat every caller as the start of a long relationship.

## Vehicle Customers Are Different

At Fairfield, Murrieta and Temecula, nearly every customer is parking an RV, a boat or a trailer. RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals, Selling Vehicle Storage covers that sale in depth. The short version:

| Household customer | RV, boat and trailer customer |
|---|---|
| Often renting for the first time | Often has rented vehicle storage before |
| Tied to a life change | Tied to an HOA rule, a city rule or no room at home |
| Thinks the stay is short | Rents year after year |
| Asks "what size do I need?" | Needs the right length, height and turning room |

Vehicle customers notice whether you know their world. Saying "fifth wheel" and "tongue length" correctly, and knowing whether your site has pull-through or covered spaces, builds trust fast.

## At Greens

**Escondido.** The woman emptying her father's condo apologizes again. You tell her there is nothing to apologize for, say you are sorry about her father, and ask what furniture there is.

**Fairfield.** A caller says he has "a 30-foot travel trailer and the HOA is on me." You ask the overall length with the tongue and how often he takes it out. He relaxes: you know trailers.

**Valley Center.** A young couple moving in together has never rented storage. You explain how the gate works before they ask.

## Key Points

- Most callers are in the middle of a change. Be calm, patient and clear.
- Most shoppers are new to storage and have already looked online. The call tests you, not only the price.
- The first good conversation usually wins. Answer, help, then make the next step easy.
- Customers stay longer than they expect; see Greens by the Numbers.
- Vehicle customers rent year after year and trust staff who know vehicles.
