## You Already Know the Product. Now Meet the Person.

This is course 5 of the Greens Storage Academy. You already know the product (Self-Storage Fundamentals), the lot (RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals) and the software (Cubby Fundamentals). This course is about the person on the other end of the phone or the other side of the counter: what they need, how to help them choose, and how to keep them happy for the year or more they will be with us.

Everything in this course comes back to one idea: **people rent from the person who helped them, not from the cheapest sign on the road.** Price matters, and you will learn to talk about it honestly. But the rental is usually won or lost in the conversation.

## Something Changed in Their Life

Self-Storage Fundamentals lesson 2 covered the 4 Ds (Death, Divorce, Downsizing, Dislocation) and the other reasons people rent. Here is what those reasons look like in the numbers, with the year each was measured:

| Finding | Source and year |
|---|---|
| Moving was the top reason for renting, at 37%; lack of space at home was 22% | StorageCafe survey of 1,700+ U.S. respondents |
| Moving drove 40% of bookings; downsizing drove 18% | Storable Tenant Insights, Spring 2024 |
| 37% of Americans were thinking about or planning a move in the next 6 to 12 months, up from 25% | Storable Outlook, 2025 |
| 26.5% of storage users cite at least one serious disruption, such as divorce, eviction, death or money trouble | HireAHelper storage statistics |

You do not need to memorize the percentages. Remember the pattern: **most people who call you are in the middle of a change**, and about one in four is going through something hard.

### What They Feel

Think about the last time you moved. Now add a divorce, a parent's funeral, or a house that will not close on time. That is who is calling.

- **They are stressed.** Many are mid-move, mid-divorce, grieving, or emptying a parent's home. A calm, organized voice is a relief.
- **They are new to storage.** Most shoppers have never rented a unit. They do not know how a gate keypad works, what a disc lock is, or what size they need. Do not assume they know the words you use every day.
- **They can tell if you care.** A 2026 trade column by a storage operator (Sarah Beth DeFazio, Universal Storage Group, 06/16/2026) put it bluntly: callers can tell within about 10 seconds whether you sound scripted or caring.
- **Their things are their history.** To you it is a 10x10. To them it is their mother's dresser, their kids' baby clothes, the tools their father left them.
- **They have read the bad reviews.** Rate increases after a low move-in price, surprise fees and lien sales are the industry's top complaints. Assume the customer has seen a bad story about storage before they called you. Your job is to prove Greens is different.

> [!TIP]
> You never need to ask why someone is renting in a way that pries. "Is this for a move, or just making some room?" gives them an easy way to share as much as they want.

## They Shop, Then They Call

Customers do their homework before you hear from them. They look at prices online, read reviews, look at photos on Google, and then call to check. The 2026 DeFazio column says they have usually compared about three places before they call.

That means two things:

1. **The call is not the start of the decision; it is the end of it.** They already have prices in their head. What they are testing now is you: are you helpful, honest and easy to deal with?
2. **Your online face matters.** Photos, reviews and the price on the website set expectations. If the website price and your quote do not match, you lose trust instantly (lesson 11 covers what to do).

## What Drives the Choice

| What customers say matters most | Source and year |
|---|---|
| Price was the top single factor, at 28%; location was 17%; 54% chose a climate-controlled unit | StorageCafe survey of 1,430 renters, 2026 |
| Price was the top single factor, at 27%, with security close behind | StorageCafe survey of 2,824 renters, 2025 |
| When people could pick several reasons: price 83%, convenient location 68% | Storable Outlook, 2025 |

Price leads. But look closer: in every survey, most people did **not** pick price as the top reason. Location, security, access, cleanliness and how they were treated decide most close calls. Nobody has measured "the office was clean and the person was kind" in a survey, but every operator you will ever meet treats it as part of security and trust.

> [!NOTE]
> Climate control shows up big in the 2026 numbers. At Greens, Escondido is where the climate-controlled units are: 549 of its 717 enclosed units carry the Climate Controlled feature (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Lesson 5 covers when to recommend it.

## The First Good Conversation Wins

Here is the most useful idea in this lesson. Two call-center and marketing vendors have reported, citing the Self Storage Association's Demand Studies, that **57% to 58% of people rent from the first storage facility they contact** (58% citing the 2023 study; 57% citing the 2025 study). We could not check the original studies, which are for members only, so treat the exact number as reported, not proven.

The lesson holds either way: **the first good conversation usually wins the rental.** If you are the first site they call and you help them, most of them stop shopping. If you are the third site they call, you need to be clearly better than the first two.

What this means for you every day:

- **Answer the phone.** A missed call is a rental handed to the next site on the list.
- **Help first, quote second.** A price is easy to compare. Help is not.
- **Make the next step easy.** A free reservation, a set time to visit, a text with the unit and the price.

## How Long They Stay

Customers usually think they will need storage for "a month or two." They are almost always wrong. The Self Storage Association's 2023 Demand Study (as reported in industry press) put the average stay at about 20 months, up from 16.5 in 2020. Self-Storage Fundamentals lesson 2 showed what that means in dollars: one Valley Center tenant at the site's median enclosed street rate can be worth more than $2,500 over a typical stay.

So the person on the phone who "only needs it till spring" may be a tenant for two years. Treat every caller like a long relationship, because most of them will be.

## RV, Boat and Trailer Customers Are Different

About 38% of Greens' active spaces are vehicle parking: 1,038 parking spaces against 1,687 enclosed units (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). At Fairfield, Murrieta and Temecula, nearly every customer is a vehicle customer. RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals lesson 12 covered selling vehicle storage in depth. The short version for this course:

| Household storage customer | RV, boat and trailer customer |
|---|---|
| Often renting for the first time | Often has rented vehicle storage before |
| Usually tied to a life change | Usually tied to an HOA rule, a city rule or no room at home |
| Thinks the stay is short | Rents year after year, often seasonally |
| Price is the top factor | Less price-driven; many rent for emotional reasons, the vehicle is their weekends and vacations |
| Asks "what size do I need?" | Needs the right length, height and turning room |
| Cares about clean, safe and close | Cares about security, access hours, and amenities like wash and dump stations where a site has them |

Vehicle customers notice whether you know their world. Saying "fifth wheel" or "tongue length" correctly, asking how often they take it out, and knowing whether your site has pull-through spaces (Murrieta has 36 and Temecula 28, From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026) builds trust fast. The trade press reports the same thing from reviews: vehicle customers praise staff who are knowledgeable and who keep them informed.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A woman calls about storing furniture from her late father's condo. She apologizes for "rambling." You tell her there is nothing to apologize for, that you are sorry about her father, and that you will make this part easy. Then you ask what furniture there is. She did not need a sales pitch; she needed someone calm.

**Fairfield.** A caller says he has "a 30-foot travel trailer and the HOA is on me." You ask its overall length with the tongue, and how often he takes it out. He relaxes, because you clearly know trailers.

**Valley Center.** A young couple moving in together has "two of everything." It is their first time renting storage. You explain how the gate works before they ask, because you know they do not know.

**Temecula.** A boat owner says another site is cheaper. You do not argue price first. You ask how often he takes the boat to the lake and whether he wants covered parking, which Temecula has (27 covered spaces, From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Lesson 11 covers the rest of that conversation.

**Murrieta.** A retired couple with a 38-foot motorhome asks about a pull-through space because backing in is hard for them. You know Murrieta has pull-through spaces and say so in the first minute.

**A Texas site.** A family is between houses while their new home is built. They expect four months. You recommend the right size for the whole house anyway, knowing builds run late and stays run long.

## Key Points

- Most people who call are in the middle of a change, and about one in four is going through something hard (HireAHelper). Be calm, patient and clear.
- Most shoppers are new to storage. Do not assume they know the words or how anything works.
- Customers research online and compare about three sites before they call (DeFazio, 2026). The call tests you, not just the price.
- Price leads in surveys (StorageCafe 2025 and 2026), but location, security, cleanliness and how you treat people decide close calls.
- Vendors citing SSA Demand Studies (2023, 2025) report that 57% to 58% rent from the first site they contact. The first good conversation usually wins.
- RV, boat and trailer customers rent year after year, care about security and access, and trust staff who know vehicles.
