## What You'll Be Able to Do

- De-escalate an angry customer step by step, and set a limit when you need to.
- Respond to a break-in report: secure, report, preserve, explain, follow up.
- Handle damage from a leak, pests or heat with empathy and without admitting fault.
- Help a locked-out tenant, but only after you confirm who they are.
- Say no, kindly, to anyone asking for access to someone else's unit.
- Guide a family after a tenant's death, and stay neutral in a domestic dispute.
- Respond to signs that someone is living in a unit, without ever approaching alone.
- Keep yourself safe when you are the only one on site.

## Angry Customers: De-Escalation

Most anger at a storage counter is not about you. It is a code that failed after a long drive, a fee they did not expect, or a bad week landing on the nearest person. These steps are from Tocarro Williams (Inside Self-Storage, 05/2022), combined with the Greens recovery method from lesson 2.

1. **Stay calm and lower your voice.** People match the volume around them. Let them finish.
2. **Empathize and play it back.** "So you came to move your things and your code didn't work, after a long drive. I'd be upset too."
3. **Apologize for the experience**, not for legal fault. "I'm sorry this happened" is always fine.
4. **Solve what you can, within your authority.** Explain policy using the signed agreement: "Your agreement covers this. Let's look at that section together."
5. **Set a limit if you need to.** "I'm happy to keep talking, but I can't continue if we move to yelling or insults."
6. **Escalate with a time.** "I'm going to share this with my manager and you'll hear back within 24 hours." Give your manager's name and number if they ask.
7. **Thank them and follow up.** Then Fix: log it in Cubby and change whatever caused it.
8. **Threats, harassment or any safety concern: call 911.** Do not argue.

> [!SAFETY]
> If you ever feel unsafe, you do not have to finish the conversation. Step back, keep the counter between you, and call 911. A customer complaint can wait. Your safety cannot.

## A Break-In or Theft Report

A tenant calls: "My lock is cut and my tools are gone!" What you do, compiled from operator guidance in the trade press:

1. **Empathy first.** They may have lost something irreplaceable. "I'm so sorry. Let's take care of this together."
2. **Secure the unit** with a facility lock.
3. **Ask the tenant to file a police report** and get the report number.
4. **Write an incident report** (Self-Storage Fundamentals lesson 14 covers Nexus Forms) and **tell your manager**.
5. **Preserve camera footage and gate logs.** Do not promise what they show, and do not share footage with the tenant; footage goes to the police and follows Greens' policy.
6. **Check neighboring units** for cut locks, and notify those tenants.
7. **Explain the claim path** for their protection plan or their own insurance: the claim, the police report number, and their own list and photos of what was taken (lesson 12).
8. **Do not admit liability, and do not speculate.** Facilities generally do not cover tenants' losses, and the agreement says so; say it gently if it comes up.
9. **Do not blame the tenant.** "You should have bought a better lock" is never okay.
10. **Keep them updated.** A news story about break-ins at another operator's sites showed what tenants hated most: nobody followed up.

Any public review about the incident gets no details of the incident or the investigation (lesson 15).

## Damage: Leaks, Pests and Heat

A tenant's boxes are wet, or there are droppings in the unit, or a candle collection melted. They want Greens to pay.

- **Show empathy without admitting liability.** "I'm really sorry. That's awful to find." Not "This is our fault and we'll cover it."
- **Document it.** Photos of the unit and the damage. Report it to maintenance and your manager.
- **Explain the agreement and the claim path**: Greens' plan or their own policy (lesson 12). Never promise coverage.
- **For a leak:** offer to move them to a dry unit, with a concession only if your manager authorizes it. Get the leak fixed.
- **For pests:** explain the treatment schedule and commit to handling it.
- **For heat:** this is why lesson 5 asks about heat-sensitive items before move-in. Do not say "you should have rented climate control."

## Lockouts, Lost Keys and Gate Trouble

**Verify identity first, every time.** Before you reset a code or help anyone into a unit, check photo ID against the lease. A gate code is a password.

| Situation | What you do |
|---|---|
| Forgot the gate code | Verify photo ID against the lease, then give or reset the code in Cubby. Never read a code to an unverified caller. |
| Lost the key to their own lock | Cut the lock only for the verified tenant, following Greens' steps and any fee, and record it in Cubby. |
| Code stopped working, account past due | Explain kindly that access is suspended and the amount needed to restore it (lesson 13). No lecture, no threat. |
| Code does not work, account current | Most gate failures are user error. Walk them through the keypad step by step. Check the code in Cubby, and check the gate hours. |
| Someone stuck inside after hours | Make sure they can get out, then help with the code. |

## "Let Me Into That Unit"

Someone who is not the tenant asks you to open a unit, cut a lock, or tell them whether someone rents there.

**The answer is no.** Only the tenant, or someone who has the tenant's code and key, gets in. Greens is not a gatekeeper between tenants and other people, and never cuts a lock for anyone but the verified tenant.

> [!NOTE]
> "I'm really sorry, I can tell this is hard. I'm not able to say whether any particular person rents here, and I can't give anyone access to a unit except the tenant. If you believe some of the property is yours, the path is through the court; a court order is what would let us act."

- **Do not confirm or deny** that the person even rents from Greens (Greens Privacy and Records).
- **Refer them to civil court** for a court order.
- **If they threaten you or anyone, call the police.**
- **Document it** in Cubby and tell your manager.

Remember the move-in line from lesson 9: "Whoever has your gate code and key can come and go." That rule is what makes this answer simple.

**The police** get cooperation, under Greens' rules: a warrant to open a unit, legal process for records, and your manager called right away. Greens Privacy and Records has the steps.

## A Family After a Tenant's Death

Self-Storage Fundamentals lesson 11 covered the documents. The conversation:

> [!NOTE]
> "I'm so sorry for your loss. I want to help you get his things. To protect his belongings, we need a few documents first: the death certificate, and the court paper or affidavit that shows who is handling his estate. Once we have those, I'll walk you through everything."

- **Lead with condolence.** Then the documents.
- **Do not discuss the account or give access** until your manager has reviewed the documents.
- **Ask your manager about pausing late fees** while the family gathers the paperwork, if Greens' policy allows it.
- **Give the next steps in writing**, so the family is not trying to remember them through grief.
- **Tell your manager the same day**, especially if the account is past due: a lien on a deceased tenant's unit needs care.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

California (Probate Code 13100) and Texas each have a small-estate affidavit procedure. Whether Greens accepts one, and which documents it needs, is your manager's call with counsel.

## Domestic Disputes

Two people who rented together are separating, or one partner shows up asking about the other's unit.

- **Never take sides.** Follow the agreement and the code and key rule.
- **Joint tenants:** get consent from both for any change to the account. Keep both people's contact details current.
- **Never tell one person about the other's unit, visits or account.** Not when they last came, not whether they still rent.
- **If anyone is in danger, or someone presents a protective order:** call the police and your manager.

## Someone Living in a Unit

People living in storage units is a real problem: no ventilation, no water, no bathroom, code violations, and danger to the person and everyone around them. The agreement prohibits it (lesson 9).

**Warning signs:**

- the same car parked overnight, night after night;
- people walking the property after hours;
- a unit door partly open from the inside;
- extension cords running into a unit;
- the same gate code used very late and very early.

> [!SAFETY]
> Never approach alone. If you suspect someone is living in a unit, do not knock, do not open the door and do not confront them. Call your manager. Where there is any risk, call the police and plan a contact with backup.

**What you do:**

1. **Document:** dates, times, what you saw, camera footage, gate logs and keypad activity.
2. **Tell your manager** the same day. Greens follows the agreement's default process and state law.
3. **Be firm and respectful** in any contact your manager plans. Treat the person with dignity. They are likely in a very hard place.
4. **Offer a local services card** (shelters, 211) if Greens provides one.

Prevention is in the move-in: state the rule plainly, and enforce loitering rules every day.

## Safety When You Work Alone

Many Greens shifts are one person on a big property. The trade press has documented the risk: a storage manager in Seattle was killed on duty. Self-Storage Fundamentals lesson 14 covers working alone; the habits that matter most with customers:

- **Never enter a unit with a tenant or prospect.** Stay in the doorway (lesson 8).
- **Never say you are alone.** "My manager's in the back." "My coworker will be right out."
- **Keep the counter between you** and visitors. Know where the exits and any panic button are.
- **Keep little cash** in the office.
- **Check that cameras record and alarms work** as part of your open routine.
- **Check in** with your manager or a coworker at set times, by phone or text, if your site has that routine.
- **Trust your instincts.** You may end a tour or decline to walk the property, and offer a video tour instead.
- **In a robbery, comply. Do not resist.** Afterward, write down descriptions and call 911.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A tenant arrives shouting that his gate code failed after a two-hour drive. You lower your voice, let him finish, and play it back. You verify his ID, check Cubby: the code is fine, but he was entering it at the exit keypad. You walk him through it. You thank him, and note that the gate signs need to be clearer.

**Valley Center.** A woman says her ex-husband rents a unit with "half my furniture" and wants the lock cut. You do not confirm whether he rents there. You explain kindly that only the tenant controls access and that a court order is the path. You note it in Cubby and tell your manager.

**Fairfield.** A tenant reports that his trailer's lock was cut. You secure the space, ask him to call the police for a report number, write the incident report, tell your manager, preserve the footage, and check the neighboring spaces. You call him back the next day with an update.

**Temecula.** A storm leaked water into a tenant's unit. You photograph the damage, report it, offer a dry unit, and explain the claim path for her own policy. You do not say Greens will pay. Your manager decides on a concession.

**Murrieta.** For three nights you see the same car parked late and a light in a unit at 6:00 AM. You do not go to the unit. You note the dates and times, pull the gate activity, and call your manager, who plans a contact with the police.

**A Texas site.** You are alone on site when a man who is not a tenant becomes aggressive about a relative's unit. You keep the counter between you, say your manager is in the back, and when he threatens you, you call 911.

## Key Points

- De-escalate: calm voice, let them finish, play it back, apologize for the experience, solve within your authority, set limits, escalate with a time. Any threat or safety concern: call 911.
- Break-ins: empathy, a facility lock, a police report number, an incident report, footage preserved and not shared with the tenant, neighbors checked, the claim path explained, no liability admitted, no blame, and follow-up.
- Damage: empathy without admitting fault, photos, report it, explain the claim path, never promise coverage.
- Lockouts: verify photo ID against the lease before any code or lock help.
- Access to someone else's unit: no. Do not confirm they rent here. A court order is the path.
- A death: condolence first, documents before access, next steps in writing, manager the same day.
- Domestic disputes: never take sides, never share one person's information with the other.
- Suspected living in a unit: document, tell your manager, never approach alone.
- Working alone: never enter a unit with anyone, never say you are alone, trust your instincts.
