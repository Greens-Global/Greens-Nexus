A woman at the Valley Center counter says her ex-husband rents a unit with "half my furniture" in it, and she wants the lock cut. She is upset, and she may even be right about the furniture. The answer is still no, and how you say it decides whether she leaves calmer or louder. This lesson gives you the words for the hard moments; the rules behind them are in Self-Storage Fundamentals, Lines You Never Cross and Safety and Emergencies.

## Angry Customers

Most anger at a storage counter is not about you: a code that failed after a long drive, a fee they did not expect, a bad week.

1. **Stay calm and lower your voice.** People match the volume around them. Let them finish.
2. **Play it back.** "So your code didn't work after a long drive. I'd be upset too."
3. **Apologize for the experience**, never for legal fault.
4. **Solve what you can** inside your authority, using the signed agreement.
5. **Set a limit if you need to.** "I'm happy to keep talking, but not if we move to yelling."
6. **Escalate with a time.** "My manager will call you within 24 hours."

> [!SAFETY]
> If you ever feel unsafe, you do not have to finish the conversation. Step back, keep the counter between you, and call 911.

## A Break-In Report

"My lock is cut and my tools are gone!"

1. **Empathy first.** "I'm so sorry. Let's take care of this together."
2. **Secure the unit** with a facility lock.
3. **Ask the tenant to file a police report** and get the number.
4. **Write the incident report** and tell your manager.
5. **Preserve camera footage and gate logs.** Footage goes to the police under Greens' rules, not to the tenant; never promise what it shows.
6. **Check neighboring units** and notify those tenants.
7. **Explain the claim path** (Existing Tenants: Payments and Move-Outs). No admitting liability, no guessing, no blame: "You should have bought a better lock" is never okay.
8. **Keep them updated.** What tenants hate most is silence.

## Damage: Leaks, Pests and Heat

"Your roof leaked. You're paying for this."

- **Empathy without admitting liability.** "I'm really sorry. That's awful to find." Not "This is our fault and we'll cover it."
- **Document it** with photos, and report it to maintenance and your manager.
- **Explain the claim path**; never promise coverage.
- **A leak:** offer a dry unit, a concession only if your manager authorizes it, and get the leak fixed.

## Lockouts and Gate Trouble

**Verify photo ID against the lease before any code or lock help.** A gate code is a password.

| Situation | What you do |
|---|---|
| Forgot the code | Verify ID, then give or reset it in Cubby. Never read a code to an unverified caller |
| Code stopped, account past due | Explain kindly that access is suspended and what restores it |
| Code fails, account current | Usually the steps or the wrong keypad. Walk them through it |

## "Let Me Into That Unit"

Only the tenant, or someone with the tenant's code and key, gets in.

> [!NOTE]
> "I can tell this is really hard. I'm not able to say whether any particular person rents here, and I can't give anyone access to a unit except the tenant. If some of the property is yours, the path is through the court; a court order is what would let us act."

Do not confirm or deny that the person rents here (Greens Privacy and Records). In a separation, never take sides and never tell one person about the other's unit or visits. Document it and tell your manager. If they threaten anyone, call the police.

## A Family After a Death

The documents Greens needs are in Self-Storage Fundamentals, Lines You Never Cross. Your part is the conversation.

> [!NOTE]
> "I'm so sorry for your loss. I want to help you get his things. To protect his belongings, we need a few documents first. Let me write down exactly what they are, and once we have them, I'll walk you through everything."

Lead with condolence. No account details and no access until your manager has reviewed the documents. Give the next steps in writing. Ask your manager whether late fees can pause, and tell your manager the same day.

## Someone Living in a Unit

Warning signs: the same car overnight, night after night; people on the property after hours; a door partly open from inside; extension cords into a unit; the same code very late and very early.

> [!SAFETY]
> Never approach alone. Do not knock, open the door or confront anyone. Call your manager; where there is any risk, the police.

Document dates, times, gate activity and footage, and tell your manager the same day. Any contact your manager plans is firm and respectful: the person is likely in a very hard place.

## At Greens

**Murrieta.** Three nights running, the same car and a light in a unit at 6:00 AM. You do not go to the unit. You note the times, pull the gate activity and call your manager.

## Key Points

- De-escalate: calm voice, play it back, sorry for the experience, a limit, a time. Unsafe means 911.
- Break-in: lock, police report, incident report, footage kept, neighbors checked, claim path, no blame, updates.
- Verify photo ID before any code or lock help.
- Access to someone else's unit: no; a court order is the path.
- A death: condolence, documents before access, steps in writing. Living in a unit: never approach alone.
