---

Reading about a sales call does not make you good at one. Saying the words out loud, with someone pushing back, does.

## How to Practice

- **Two or three people.** One plays the customer, one plays Greens staff, one scores. With two, the customer scores.
- **Five to ten minutes a week.** One scenario a week is plenty.
- **Score sales scenarios** with The Call Score Sheet. Score service scenarios with Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix and the lesson the scenario names.
- **The customer** plays it like a real person, pushes back once or twice, and holds back what was not asked. If staff never ask what you are storing, do not say.
- **Afterward:** the score and **two** things to work on, then read "great" together and swap roles.

## Scenario 1: The Price-Only Caller

**Escondido.** *Customer:* "How much is your 10x10? That's all I need." You are clearing out a three-bedroom house next month.

**Great:** gives a range from Cubby without dodging; asks what they are storing and learns it is a three-bedroom house; says honestly a 10x10 will not fit and recommends a 10x20 with a reason; asks about heat-sensitive items; quotes rent, any special and its end, and the admin fee; offers a free reservation; captures name, phone and email.

## Scenario 2: The Hours Question

**Valley Center.** *Customer:* "How late are you open today?" You are thinking about a unit for a kitchen remodel but only asked about hours.

**Great:** gives the office and gate hours first; asks "Are you looking to rent, or can I help with your account?"; learns about the remodel; recommends a drive-up unit of the right size and offers to hold it.

## Scenario 3: First-Time Renter After a Divorce

**Fairfield, enclosed unit.** *Customer:* overwhelmed, moving out this weekend with half of a two-bedroom apartment, never rented storage, not focused on price.

**Great:** acknowledges the stress briefly without prying; explains the gate and locks before being asked; recommends a size after asking what is going; offers carts and supplies; closes on a Saturday move-in time; does not make it about price.

## Scenario 4: An Estate, and a Father's Unit

**Escondido.** *Customer:* needs storage for her late father's furniture while the house sells. Then: "Also, Dad had a unit here. Can you let me into it?"

**Great:** condolences; rents her a unit and raises climate control for the wood furniture; on the father's unit, explains that documents are needed and the manager reviews them; gives no account detail or access; writes down the next steps.

## Scenario 5: The Competitor Is $20 Cheaper

**Temecula, covered parking.** *Customer:* "The lot across town has boat parking for $20 less a month." The boat is 24 feet on a trailer and the sun matters to you, but only say so if asked.

**Great:** stays positive about the competitor; asks the length on the trailer and how often it goes out; compares total cost (special and its end, admin fee, insurance); names covered parking as the difference that matters; offers no price match on their own; asks for the reservation.

## Scenario 6: "I'll Think About It"

**Valley Center, walk-in.** *Customer:* toured a 10x15, liked it, heads for the door: "I'll think about it." The real reason: the house sale may close a week late.

**Great:** asks "Is it the size, the price or the timing?"; offers a free reservation that moves with the date; writes a quote sheet; captures the email; sets "Thursday at 5:00 PM?" in Cubby, and calls.

## Scenario 7: Web Reservation, No Answer

**Valley Center.** *Customer:* reserved online four minutes ago for tomorrow. Do not answer the first call; answer the second.

**Great:** calls within 5 minutes; leaves a short voicemail with a callback time; sends a text and an email; calls again and confirms the size fits with a discovery question; confirms tomorrow's arrival time.

## Scenario 8: Contactless Renter Cannot Get Through the Gate

**Fairfield, 7:40 PM, office closed.** *Customer:* rented online this morning, code will not open the gate, tired and annoyed.

**Great:** stays calm; verifies identity before discussing the code; walks through the keypad and checks the code and gate hours; makes sure they can get back out; calls the next day with the orientation they missed.

## Scenario 9: Rate-Increase Anger

**Escondido.** *Customer:* "You raised me again! I've been here four years!" Hurt more than angry; open to a smaller unit if offered.

**Great:** listens without interrupting and thanks them for four years; gives real reasons; says the rate is below street only if Cubby shows it; offers a smaller unit and autopay; asks the manager about a concession; promises no reversal; escalates with a time if asked.

## Scenario 10: Collections Call, Hardship

**Murrieta, RV space.** *Customer:* behind on rent, lost your job last month, wants to keep the space.

**Great:** confirms identity before the balance; "That sounds really hard"; states the amount and next date factually; offers a partial payment or a split across cards and says honestly it does not stop the ladder; asks the manager about a plan; gets a commitment with a date and logs it; no threats, no "the system won't let me."

## Key Points

- Practice out loud, five to ten minutes a week, with a coworker playing the customer.
- Score sales calls with the 22-item sheet and give two things to work on, never ten.
- The customer holds back what staff did not ask for.
