## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Run a role-play with a coworker: caller, Greens staff and scorer.
- Score a practice call with the 22-item call score sheet (lesson 6).
- Handle the ten sales and tenant scenarios in this lesson the way a great Greens employee would.
- Pick your own weak spots and practice them before your Hands-On Check.

## How to Practice

Reading about a sales call does not make you good at one. Saying the words out loud, with someone pushing back, does. Lessons 16 and 17 give you twenty scenarios to practice. They are the same kinds of situations your trainer may use in the Hands-On Check's mock sales call.

### Set Up

- **Two or three people.** One plays the customer, one plays Greens staff, and (if you have three) one scores. With two, the customer scores after the call.
- **Five to ten minutes a week.** DeFazio (2026) recommends short weekly practice; one scenario a week is plenty.
- **Use the score sheet** from lesson 6 for every sales scenario. For the service scenarios, score with Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix (lesson 2) and the rules in the lesson the scenario points to.

### The Customer's Job

Read the prompt and play it like a real person. Push back once or twice. Do not make it impossible, and do not make it easy. Hold back information the staff member did not ask for: if they never ask what you are storing, do not tell them.

### The Staff Member's Job

Do not read the "what great looks like" section first. Handle the call, then compare.

### Afterward

1. The scorer gives the score and **two** things to work on. Only two.
2. Read "what great looks like" together.
3. Swap roles.

> [!TIP]
> The most useful thing to practice is the part that feels awkward. For most people that is asking for the rental (item 15) and capturing the email (item 17). Practice the ask until it feels natural.

## Scenario 1: The Price-Only Caller

**Site:** Escondido. **Lessons:** 4, 5, 11.

**Prompt (customer):** "How much is your 10x10? That's all I need." You are clearing out a three-bedroom house next month. Do not say so unless asked.

**What great looks like:**

- Gives a price range from Cubby right away, without dodging.
- Asks what they are storing and when they are moving. Learns it is a three-bedroom house.
- Says honestly that a 10x10 will not fit, and recommends a 10x20, with a reason.
- Asks about heat-sensitive items, since Escondido has climate-controlled units.
- Quotes the rent from Cubby, any special and when it ends, and the $25 admin fee (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Offers a free reservation and captures name, phone and email.

## Scenario 2: The Hours Question

**Site:** Valley Center. **Lessons:** 3, 4.

**Prompt (customer):** "How late are you open today?" You are actually thinking about renting a unit for a remodel, but you only asked about hours.

**What great looks like:**

- Gives the office and gate hours first.
- Asks: "Are you looking to rent, or can I help you with your account?"
- Moves into discovery: a kitchen remodel, about three months, furniture and boxes.
- Recommends a drive-up unit of the right size and offers to hold it.

## Scenario 3: First-Time Renter After a Divorce

**Site:** Fairfield, enclosed unit. **Lessons:** 1, 2, 4.

**Prompt (customer):** You sound overwhelmed. You are moving out this weekend with about half of a two-bedroom apartment. You have never rented storage. You are not focused on price.

**What great looks like:**

- Acknowledges the stress briefly ("That's a lot to handle in one week") without prying.
- Uses simple words and explains how the gate and locks work before being asked.
- Recommends a 10x10 with a reason, after asking what is going.
- Offers carts and a supply bundle.
- Closes on a Saturday move-in time.
- Does not make it about price.

## Scenario 4: An Estate, and a Father's Unit

**Site:** Escondido. **Lessons:** 4, 14.

**Prompt (customer):** You need storage for your late father's furniture while the house is sold. Then you add: "Also, Dad had a unit here. Can you let me into it?"

**What great looks like:**

- Offers condolences and rents her a new unit, with climate control discussed for his wood furniture.
- On the father's unit: condolence first, then explains that a death certificate and the document showing who handles the estate are needed, and that the manager reviews them.
- Does not confirm account details or give access without them.
- Offers to ask the manager about pausing late fees while she gathers papers, and gives the next steps in writing.

## Scenario 5: The Competitor Is $20 Cheaper

**Site:** Temecula, covered parking. **Lessons:** 11.

**Prompt (customer):** "The lot across town has boat parking for $20 less a month." Your boat is 24 feet, on a trailer, and you care about the sun on it, but only say so if asked.

**What great looks like:**

- Stays positive; never says anything about the competitor.
- Asks the boat's length on the trailer and how often it goes out. Learns the sun matters.
- Compares total cost honestly: is the other price a special, when does it end, an admin fee, insurance requirements.
- Names the real difference that matters to this caller: Temecula's covered spaces (27, From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Does not offer a price match on their own; offers one only if the manager has authorized it.
- Asks for the reservation.

## Scenario 6: "I'll Think About It"

**Site:** Valley Center, walk-in. **Lessons:** 8, 11.

**Prompt (customer):** You toured a 10x15, liked it, and start to leave: "I'll think about it." The real reason: your house sale might close a week late.

**What great looks like:**

- Asks: "Is it the size, the price, or the timing?" Learns it is the timing.
- Offers a free reservation that can move with the date.
- Writes a quote sheet with the unit, the rent, the admin fee and the site's number.
- Captures name, phone and email.
- Sets a specific follow-up ("Thursday at 5:00 PM?") and logs it in Cubby.
- Follows up as promised.

## Scenario 7: Web Reservation, No Answer

**Site:** A Texas site. **Lessons:** 10.

**Prompt (customer):** You reserved a unit online four minutes ago for a move-in tomorrow. Do not answer the first call. Answer the second.

**What great looks like:**

- Calls within 5 minutes.
- Leaves a short voicemail: name, site, a callback time.
- Sends a text and an email confirming the unit, size and price.
- Calls again later and reaches the customer. Confirms the size fits with a discovery question or two.
- Confirms tomorrow's arrival time.

## Scenario 8: Contactless Renter Cannot Get Through the Gate

**Site:** Fairfield. **Lessons:** 10, 14.

**Prompt (customer):** It is 7:40 PM and the office is closed. You rented online this morning and your code will not open the gate. You are tired and annoyed.

**What great looks like (on duty or on the on-call phone):**

- Stays calm and empathizes.
- Verifies identity before talking about the code.
- Walks them through the keypad step by step, and checks the code in Cubby and the gate hours.
- Makes sure they can get back out.
- The next day, calls with the orientation they missed: due on the 1st, late fee on the 11th (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026), autopay, protection, no living in units.

## Scenario 9: Rate-Increase Anger

**Site:** Escondido. **Lessons:** 12.

**Prompt (customer):** "You raised me 12% again! I've been here four years!" You are hurt more than angry. If offered a smaller unit, consider it.

**What great looks like:**

- Listens without interrupting, and thanks them for four years.
- Explains the reasons for the increase honestly.
- Says the new rate is still below the street rate only if Cubby shows that is true.
- Offers options within authority: a smaller unit, autopay, and asks the manager about a concession.
- Is not defensive and promises nothing they cannot deliver.
- Escalates with a time if asked.

## Scenario 10: Collections Call, Hardship

**Site:** Murrieta, RV space. **Lessons:** 13.

**Prompt (customer):** You are 20 days late on your RV space. You lost your job last month. You want to keep the space.

**What great looks like:**

- Confirms identity before naming the balance.
- Empathy: "That sounds really hard."
- States the amount and the next key date factually.
- Offers a partial payment or a split across cards, and asks the manager about a plan if that is above their authority. Explains honestly that a partial payment does not stop the ladder.
- Gets a commitment with a date, and logs it in Cubby.
- No threats, no fake urgency, no "the system won't let me."

## Key Points

- Practice out loud, five to ten minutes a week, with a coworker playing the customer.
- Score sales scenarios with the 22-item sheet; give two things to work on, never ten.
- The customer holds back what the staff member does not ask for.
- Scenarios 1 to 10 cover the price-only caller, the hours question, a first-timer after a divorce, an estate, a cheaper competitor, "I'll think about it," a web reservation, a gate problem after hours, a rate increase and a hardship collections call.
- Lesson 17 has scenarios 11 to 20 and working with Spanish-speaking customers.
