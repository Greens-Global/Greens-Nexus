## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Handle the ten service and safety scenarios in this lesson the way a great Greens employee would.
- Serve a customer who speaks limited English warmly and accurately.
- Explain why a child never interprets lease or lien terms, and why you never promise a translated contract.
- Know your weakest scenarios and practice them before your Hands-On Check.

## Using These Scenarios

Run them the way the Practice: Twenty Scenarios, Part 1 lesson describes: one person plays the customer, one plays Greens staff, and someone scores. Most of these are service and safety situations, not sales calls. Score them with Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix (the The Greens Service Standard lesson) and the rules in the lesson each scenario points to. Scenario 20 is a sales call: use the 22-item sheet.

## Scenario 11: The Wrong Person Answers a Collections Call

**Site:** Valley Center. **Lessons:** 13.

**Prompt (customer):** You are the tenant's roommate. You pick up and ask, "What's this about? I can pass on a message."

**What great looks like:**

- Gives only a first name, "Greens Storage," and a callback number.
- Does not give the reason, the balance, or the words "past due" or "lien," even when asked twice.
- If it goes to voicemail instead: name, company and number only.
- Logs the attempt in Cubby.

## Scenario 12: The Ex Wants In

**Site:** Temecula. **Lessons:** 14.

**Prompt (customer):** "My ex rented a unit here, but half the stuff in it is mine. Cut the lock." Get louder if told no.

**What great looks like:**

- Calm sympathy: "I can tell this is really hard."
- Does not confirm whether the ex is even a tenant.
- Explains that only the tenant controls access, and that a court order is the path. Refers them to civil court.
- Sets a limit if the shouting continues, and calls the police if threatened.
- Documents it in Cubby and tells the manager.

## Scenario 13: A Break-In Discovered by the Tenant

**Site:** Fairfield, trailer space. **Lessons:** 14, 12.

**Prompt (customer):** "The lock on my trailer is cut and my tools are gone!" You are upset and want to know what Greens is going to do about it.

**What great looks like:**

- Empathy first.
- Secures the trailer or space with a facility lock where possible.
- Asks the tenant to call the police and get a report number.
- Writes the incident report, tells the manager, preserves footage and gate logs, and checks nearby spaces.
- Explains the claim path for the tenant's own insurance (vehicle tenants carry their own; From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026, only one vehicle lease is on a Greens plan).
- No admission of liability, no speculation, no blame. Calls back with updates.

## Scenario 14: Damage From a Roof Leak

**Site:** Escondido. **Lessons:** 14.

**Prompt (customer):** Your boxes are soaked. "Your roof leaked. You're paying for this."

**What great looks like:**

- Apologizes for the experience without admitting liability.
- Photographs the damage and reports the leak.
- Offers a dry unit, and a concession only within authority or with the manager's approval.
- Explains the claim path for Greens' plan or their own policy. Never promises coverage.
- Escalates the claim per policy, and follows up.

## Scenario 15: A Suspected Live-In

**Site:** Murrieta. **Lessons:** 14.

**Prompt (scorer reads this to the staff member):** The same car has been parked overnight three nights in a row, and you saw a light in a unit at 6:00 AM. What do you do?

**What great looks like:**

- Does not approach the unit alone. Does not knock or open it.
- Documents dates, times, gate activity and footage.
- Informs the manager the same day and helps plan a police-assisted contact if needed.
- Any message about the rule is firm and respectful, with a local services card if Greens provides one.

## Scenario 16: Alone on Site, and the Prospect Wants You Inside

**Site:** Valley Center. **Lessons:** 8, 14.

**Prompt (customer):** You are touring a far unit. You insist the staff member come inside with you to help measure. If asked, say "Are you here by yourself?"

**What great looks like:**

- Stays in the doorway and hands over the tape measure.
- Keeps the phone in hand.
- Never says they are alone: "My coworker's up at the office."
- Ends the tour politely if anything feels wrong.

## Scenario 17: A Spanish-Speaking Walk-In

**Site:** A Texas site. **Lessons:** 4, 9, and the section below.

**Prompt (customer):** You speak limited English. You want a unit for your family's move. Your 10-year-old daughter offers to translate.

**What great looks like:**

- A warm greeting, slow and simple English, and patience.
- Brings in a Spanish-speaking teammate, or the approved translation help Greens provides.
- Uses Greens' Spanish materials, where Greens provides them.
- Covers the key lease points (due date, late fee, what happens if rent goes unpaid, no living in units, the gate code and key rule) and checks they were understood.
- Does not use the child to interpret lease or lien terms. Small talk is fine; the contract is not.
- Does not promise a translated contract; asks the manager.

## Scenario 18: "They Sold My Stuff!"

**Site:** Escondido. **Lessons:** 15.

**Prompt (scorer reads this):** A one-star review: "They sold everything I owned. Heartless. Stay away." The account went through the full lien process. Write the reply.

**What great looks like:**

- Tells the manager the same day.
- Replies within 48 hours, calm and short, with no account or lien details: "I'm sorry for how difficult this situation has been. I'm not able to discuss account details here, but I'd welcome a call at [number] to talk it through."
- Takes it offline.

## Scenario 19: A Move-Out Notice

**Site:** Temecula. **Lessons:** 12.

**Prompt (customer):** You emailed that you are leaving at the end of the month. If asked why: the rent went up and you only use half the unit.

**What great looks like:**

- A live call within 24 hours of the email.
- Asks why, and listens.
- Offers a save that fits: a smaller unit at the right rate, or autopay with a fee waiver within authority.
- If the tenant still leaves: thanks them, confirms the steps (date, broom-clean, lock removed), and asks for a review and a referral.

## Scenario 20: An RV Owner Shopping Covered Parking

**Site:** Temecula. **Lessons:** 4, and RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals, the Measuring for the Right Fit and Selling Vehicle Storage lessons.

**Prompt (customer):** "Do you have a covered spot for a 34-foot fifth wheel?" You take it out about once a month, mostly in summer.

**What great looks like:**

- Asks the overall length, the height, and how often it goes out.
- Checks that a covered space truly fits: length with a margin and the posted clear height. Never guesses a clearance.
- Highlights what matters to this caller: cover from the sun, security, access hours, and a wash or dump station only if the site has one.
- Explains that vehicle tenants carry their own insurance and will need registration and proof of insurance at move-in.
- Quotes the rent from Cubby and the $25 admin fee (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026), and reserves.
- Asks about seasonal use, and offers to keep in touch about a better space if one opens.

## Serving Customers Who Speak Spanish

Many Greens customers speak Spanish at home.

| Fact | Source and year |
|---|---|
| 28.8% of California residents aged 5 and older speak Spanish at home, the highest share of any state | American Community Survey, 2024, via USAFacts |
| 58.9% of U.S. Spanish speakers say they speak English "very well," so roughly 4 in 10 do not | Same source |
| Texas has more Spanish-speaking households with limited English (over 600,000) than any other state; California has over 587,000 | Boostlingo limited-English map, undated |

Storage call centers have noticed: PhoneSmart added a Spanish call group years ago. Many of your callers would be more comfortable in Spanish, and some need it to understand a contract.

### What You Do

- **Know who on your team speaks Spanish**, and how to warm-transfer a call or bring them to the counter: "One moment, my coworker Ana speaks Spanish. Let me bring her in."
- **If nobody is available**, speak slowly and simply, write prices and dates down, and offer to call back when a Spanish-speaking teammate is in. Use Greens' approved translation help if Greens provides one.
- **Use Greens' Spanish materials** (an agreement summary, the rules, notices) where Greens provides them. Never make your own translation of a legal document.
- **Never use a customer's child as the interpreter** for lease, lien or collections matters. A child should not carry that responsibility, and a wrong word can cost the family their belongings. Small talk is fine. Contract terms are not.
- **Check understanding.** Ask the customer to tell you, in their words, when rent is due and what happens if it is late.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

California Civil Code 1632 requires a translated contract for certain kinds of contracts negotiated mainly in Spanish and some other languages. Whether it covers a storage rental agreement is a question for Greens' counsel. **Never promise a customer a translated contract, and never negotiate a lease mainly in Spanish, without asking your manager first.**

3. Run those again, a week apart.
4. Score yourself on five real calls a week with the the The Call Score Sheet lesson sheet.
