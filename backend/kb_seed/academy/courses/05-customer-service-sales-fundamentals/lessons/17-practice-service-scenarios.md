Most of these are not sales calls. Run them the way Part 1 describes. Score them with Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix and the lesson each one names; scenario 20 is a sales call, so use the 22-item sheet.

## Scenario 11: The Wrong Person Answers a Collections Call

**Valley Center.** *Customer:* you are the tenant's roommate. "What's this about? I can pass on a message."

**Great:** first name, "Greens Storage" and a number only; no reason, balance, "past due" or "lien," even when asked twice; a voicemail would carry the same three things; the attempt is logged in Cubby.

## Scenario 12: The Ex Wants In

**Temecula.** *Customer:* "My ex rented a unit here, but half the stuff is mine. Cut the lock." Get louder if told no.

**Great:** calm sympathy; does not confirm the ex is a tenant; explains only the tenant controls access and a court order is the path; sets a limit if the shouting continues and calls the police if threatened; documents it and tells the manager.

## Scenario 13: A Break-In Discovered by the Tenant

**Fairfield, trailer space.** *Customer:* "The lock on my trailer is cut and my tools are gone! What is Greens going to do?"

**Great:** empathy first; a facility lock where possible; asks for a police report number; incident report, manager told, footage and gate logs kept, nearby spaces checked; explains the claim path for the tenant's own vehicle insurance; no liability admitted, no blame; calls back with updates.

## Scenario 14: Damage From a Roof Leak

**Escondido.** *Customer:* "Your roof leaked. You're paying for this."

**Great:** sorry for the experience without admitting liability; photographs and reports the leak; offers a dry unit, a concession only with the manager's approval; explains the claim path and never promises coverage; follows up.

## Scenario 15: A Suspected Live-In

**Murrieta.** *Scorer reads:* the same car has been parked overnight three nights running, and you saw a light in a unit at 6:00 AM. What do you do?

**Great:** does not approach, knock or open the unit; documents dates, times, gate activity and footage; tells the manager the same day.

## Scenario 16: Alone on Site, and the Prospect Wants You Inside

**Valley Center.** *Customer:* touring a far unit, you insist staff come inside to help measure, and ask "Are you here by yourself?"

**Great:** stays in the doorway and hands over the tape; keeps the phone in hand; "My coworker's up at the office"; ends the tour politely if anything feels wrong.

## Scenario 17: A Spanish-Speaking Walk-In

**Escondido.** *Customer:* limited English, a family move; your 10-year-old daughter offers to translate.

**Great:** warm, slow, simple English; brings in a Spanish-speaking teammate or Greens' approved help; covers the due date, late fee, unpaid rent, no living in units and the gate code rule, and checks understanding; does not use the child for lease or lien terms; does not promise a translated contract.

## Scenario 18: "They Sold My Stuff!"

**Escondido.** *Scorer reads:* a one-star review, "They sold everything I owned. Heartless. Stay away." The account went through the full lien process. Write the reply.

**Great:** tells the manager the same day; replies within 48 hours, calm and short, with no account or lien details; invites a call and takes it offline.

## Scenario 19: A Move-Out Notice

**Temecula.** *Customer:* you emailed that you are leaving at month's end. If asked why: the rent went up and you use half the unit.

**Great:** a live call within 24 hours; asks why and listens; offers a smaller unit; if the tenant still leaves, thanks them, confirms the date, broom-clean and lock removed, and asks for a review and a referral.

## Scenario 20: An RV Owner Shopping Covered Parking

**Temecula.** *Customer:* "Do you have a covered spot for a 34-foot fifth wheel?" You take it out about once a month, mostly in summer.

**Great:** asks overall length, height and use; checks the posted clear height and never guesses; highlights cover from the sun, security and access; explains vehicle tenants carry their own insurance and bring registration and proof; quotes rent and the admin fee from Cubby; reserves.

## Serving Customers Who Speak Spanish

Many Greens customers speak Spanish at home, and some need it to understand a contract.

- **Know who on your team speaks Spanish**, and how to bring them in: "One moment, my coworker Ana speaks Spanish."
- **Use Greens' Spanish materials** where they exist. Never make your own translation of a legal document.
- **Never use a customer's child as the interpreter** for lease, lien or collections matters. A wrong word can cost a family their belongings. Small talk is fine; contract terms are not.
- **Check understanding:** ask them to tell you, in their own words, when rent is due and what happens if it is late.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

California Civil Code 1632 requires a translated contract for certain contracts negotiated mainly in Spanish. **Never promise a translated contract, and never negotiate a lease mainly in Spanish, without asking your manager first.**

## Key Points

- Service scenarios are scored with Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix; sales scenarios with the 22-item sheet.
- Bring in a Spanish-speaking teammate or approved help, and check understanding.
- Never a child as interpreter for lease or lien terms; never promise a translated contract.
