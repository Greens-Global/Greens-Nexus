"How late are you open?" You could say "until six" and hang up. Most storage staff do. The caller then dials the next site and rents there, because the next person asked why she was calling: her apartment lease ends in two weeks. This lesson shows why the phone still decides most rentals, what to do with the plain questions, and how to handle voicemail and callbacks.

## The Phone Is Still the Sale

Customers look online first, then most of them call. Online rentals are growing (Online Renters and Digital Leads), but the phone is still where most rentals at a storage site are decided. And many calls come from current tenants: "what's my balance," "my code doesn't work." Each of those is a chance to keep a tenant happy, or to find a new rental hiding inside a simple question.

## What Most Staff Miss

When storage sites are mystery shopped, the pattern is the same year after year: **staff are friendly, but they do not sell.** Nearly everyone answers politely and invites the caller to visit. Far fewer:

- ask a real question about what the caller needs;
- offer a set time to visit instead of "come by anytime";
- capture a name, phone number and email;
- **ask for the rental.**

Being friendly is common. Asking good questions, asking for the rental, capturing contact details and following up are rare. Do those four things and you beat most of the sites your customer calls.

## Speed to Answer

- **Pick up by the third ring.** Callers who hear five rings hang up and call the next site.
- **With a customer at the counter**, say "Excuse me one moment," then pick up: "Thank you for calling Greens Storage Escondido, this is Ana. I'm with a customer. May I get your name and number and call you back within 15 minutes?" Then do it.
- **Missed calls get a callback within the hour** while the office is open.

## Every Call Gets a "Why"

Answer the question first (never dodge it), then ask one question that opens the conversation.

> [!NOTE]
> Caller: "How late are you open?"
>
> You: "The office is open until [closing time] and the gate until [gate closing time]. Are you looking at renting, or can I help with your account?"

> [!NOTE]
> Caller: "How much is a 10x10?"
>
> You: "Our 10x10s run [low] to [high] depending on the unit. What are you planning to store? I want to make sure a 10x10 is even the right size."

> [!NOTE]
> Caller: "Where exactly are you?"
>
> You: "We're at [address], near [landmark]. Are you coming to see a unit? I'd love to have one ready. What time works?"

## Voicemail and Callbacks

**Your outgoing message** says the property name, office and gate hours, the website for renting online, and a promise to call back within the hour.

**A message for a prospect** says your first name, the property, the unit and price they asked about, your number, and when you will try again.

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi Joe, this is Ana with Greens Storage Murrieta. You asked about a pull-through space for your fifth wheel. I have one that fits and I'd love to hold it for you. Call me at [number]. I'll also try you tomorrow around 10:00 AM."

Log the attempt on the lead in Cubby, then call at 10:00 AM.

**Never leave a balance, a late fee or anything about a lien on a voicemail** or with whoever answers. Collections With Respect has the rule.

## Overflow and After-Hours Calls

Some calls are taken by someone else: a call center, or Cubby's voice agent (the lead's source then reads AI Voice Agent). **A lead taken by someone else is yours the moment you see it.** Call, confirm the size fits, and confirm the move-in date.

## Recorded Calls

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

California requires the consent of everyone on a call to record it, so a recorded line plays a notice at the start. Greens decides whether calls are recorded. Never record a call on your own phone.

## At Greens

**Fairfield.** Three calls ring while you walk the lot. One goes to voicemail. You call it back within the hour: a man with a 32-foot trailer.

**Temecula.** A caller asks for "your cheapest parking space." You give the low end, then ask what he is parking: a 26-foot boat on a trailer. Now you can help.

**Escondido.** A tenant calls about her balance. Before you hang up, you ask if there is anything else. Her brother needs a unit next month. You take his name and number.

## Key Points

- Most rentals are still decided on the phone.
- Friendly is common; asking questions, asking for the rental, capturing details and following up are rare.
- Pick up by the third ring; return missed calls within the hour.
- Answer every question, then ask why they called.
- Voicemails to prospects carry the unit, the price and when you will call; never a balance or lien detail.
