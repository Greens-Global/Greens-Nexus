A tenant of four years calls Escondido: "You raised me again! I've been here four years!" She is more hurt than angry. You cannot make a rate change pleasant. You can make the call honest, calm and fair, and most tenants who are heard out that way stay. This short lesson is that call.

## What a Rate Change Is

Storage rental agreements run month to month, so the rent can change with the notice the agreement requires. A tenant who moved in on a special, or years ago, is often paying well under what a new customer pays today (the street rate). Self-Storage Fundamentals, How a Storage Site Makes Money explains why operators review rates; Self-Storage Fundamentals, The Rental Agreement explains what a California agreement must disclose about rent on its first page.

**Greens decides rates, not the person at the counter.** Your job is the conversation.

## How Much Notice

How much notice a tenant gets before a change comes from their rental agreement. Your manager has Greens' notice rules. Never tell a tenant a notice period you have not read in their agreement or confirmed with your manager.

It is Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix from The Greens Service Standard, shaped for this call.

1. **Hear.** Let them finish. They may have a lot to say.
2. **Own.** "I understand this increase is hard to hear, and thank you for being with us four years."
3. **Explain, honestly.** Ask your manager before increases go out what is driving them (taxes, insurance, repairs and improvements, the market) so you can answer with real reasons, not "corporate decided."
4. **Compare to the street rate, only if it is true.** Check Cubby first. "Your new rate is still below what a new customer pays for the same unit today" is a strong, fair thing to say when it is true, and a lie when it is not.
5. **Offer the real options** inside your authority:
   - a smaller unit that holds what they are keeping;
   - autopay, so at least late fees never happen;
   - a concession for a long-time, on-time tenant, **only if your manager authorizes it**.
6. **Escalate with a time** if they ask: "I'll share this with my manager today, and you'll hear back within 24 hours."

> [!NOTE]
> "I understand, and I'm glad you called me instead of just packing up. Here's what's driving it. I checked, and your new rate is still below what we'd charge a new customer for your unit. If you're not using all the space, I have a smaller unit that might hold what you're keeping. Want me to look at that with you?"

## What You Never Promise

- **"I'll get it reversed."** That is not yours to say.
- **"Your rate will never go up again."** It is not true, at Greens or anywhere.
- **A concession or a lower rate** before your manager has said yes.
- **A notice period** you have not read in the agreement.

## Before an Increase Goes Out

- The call started at move-in: a tenant who heard "rates are reviewed over time, and you'll get written notice" is far less surprised (Move-In Done Right).
- If a tenant recently had a leak, a break-in or a hard time, tell your manager before the notice goes out.

## At Greens

**Escondido.** You hear her out, thank her for four years, give the reasons, and check Cubby: her new rate is still below the street rate for her unit, so you say so. She asks about a smaller unit and stays.

**Temecula.** A tenant asks you to "just remove" the increase because he has been there five years. A concession is above your authority. "I'll ask my manager today and call you by 4:00 PM." You do.

**Valley Center.** A tenant posts a two-star review about a rate increase. Your manager replies kindly and invites a call (Reviews and Reputation). On the call, you go over a smaller unit together.

## Common Mistakes

- Getting defensive, or blaming "corporate."
- Saying the rate is below street without checking Cubby.
- Guessing the notice period.

## Key Points

- Agreements are month to month; Greens sets rates; you own the conversation.
- Hear, own, explain honestly, compare to street only if true, offer options inside your authority.
- Never promise a reversal, a frozen rate, a concession you do not have, or a notice period you have not read.
- Your manager has the notice rules and approves any concession.
