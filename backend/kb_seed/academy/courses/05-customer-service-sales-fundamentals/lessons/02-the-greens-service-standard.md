## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Use the Greens recovery method (Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix) when something goes wrong.
- Listen actively: let people finish, play it back, name the feeling, and use their words.
- Get yourself, your voice and your office ready before the customer arrives or calls.
- Name one "second mile" thing you can do for a customer this week.
- State the Greens response-time targets for calls, web leads, follow-ups, reviews and complaints.

## What Great Service Means Here

At Greens, great service is three things:

1. **We listen before we fix.** A fix for the wrong problem is not a fix.
2. **We are honest.** About prices, fees, rate changes, what we can do and what we cannot.
3. **We follow up.** When we say we will call at 3:00 PM, we call at 3:00 PM.

Almost every complaint you will ever hear in storage breaks one of those three. The trade research behind this course found the same thing over and over: staff are usually friendly, but they do not ask good questions, they do not explain things up front, and they do not call back. Friendly is the minimum. Listening, honesty and follow-up are what make Greens different.

## The Greens Recovery Method: Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix

When something goes wrong (a code that will not work, a surprise fee, a wet box) use these five steps, in this order. They are Greens' version of two methods taught widely in service training: LAST (Listen, Apologize, Solve, Thank) and HEARD (Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Resolve, Diagnose), which is usually credited to Disney.

| Step | What you do | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Hear** | Let them finish. Do not interrupt, defend or explain yet. Take notes. | Silence, then: "So you drove out after work and your code didn't open the gate. Did I get that right?" |
| **Own** | Acknowledge the feeling and apologize for the experience. You are not admitting fault for damage; you are saying you are sorry it happened. | "I'm sorry. That's a frustrating way to end a long day." |
| **Solve** | Fix what you can, inside your authority. If you cannot, say what happens next and when. | "Let me check your code in Cubby right now." |
| **Thank** | Thank them for telling you, and for their patience. | "Thanks for calling me about it instead of just leaving." |
| **Fix** | After the customer is taken care of, fix the cause so it does not happen to the next person. Log it, tell your manager, change the sign. | You add a note in Cubby and tell your manager the gate instructions at the exit are confusing. |

**Order matters.** The most common mistake is jumping to Solve before Hear. You fix the gate code when the real problem was that nobody told them the gate hours. Hear first.

**Fix is about the process, not blame.** Disney's version calls it "Diagnose." The question is "what made this happen?" not "whose fault is it?"

> [!TIP]
> Saying sorry for someone's experience is not the same as saying Greens caused the damage. "I'm sorry this happened" is always fine. "This is our fault and we'll pay for it" is never yours to say. Lesson 14 covers damage claims.

## Listening Like You Mean It

Active listening is a skill you can practice. Five habits:

1. **Let them finish.** Even if you know where they are going.
2. **Play it back.** "So the movers come on the 15th and you need somewhere for a two-bedroom until the house closes. Did I get that right?"
3. **Name the feeling.** "That sounds like a stressful week."
4. **Write it down.** In Cubby notes, so the next person knows.
5. **Use their words back.** If they said "my mom's dresser," say "your mom's dresser," not "the furniture."

The fifth habit matters most in sales. When you recommend a unit, tie every feature to something they said (lesson 4). "You mentioned your mom's dresser is solid wood, so a climate-controlled unit keeps it out of the summer heat" is a reason. "We have climate control" is a brochure.

## Before They Arrive: You, Your Voice and Your Office

### On the Phone

- **Before you pick up:** finish chewing, put the drink down, stop typing, and smile. Callers can hear attitude.
- **Pick up by the third ring.** Lesson 3 explains why.
- **Slow down.** A calm, unhurried voice is the single best thing you can give a stressed caller.

### At the Counter

- **Stand up and greet.** Mystery shoppers grade whether you stood up to greet them. Look up from the screen within seconds, smile and say hello.
- **Natural eye contact.** Not a stare, just attention.
- **Put your phone away.** A customer at the counter comes before a text.

### The Office and the Site

Customers judge security and care by what they see. A 2026 operator column listed what they notice: the office, the drive aisles, the keypads, the paint and the restrooms.

- [ ] Office clean, counter clear, lights on, temperature comfortable
- [ ] Rent due date and late-fee day posted where tenants can read it
- [ ] Gate instructions posted at the entrance AND the exit gate (most "my code doesn't work" problems are people not knowing the steps)
- [ ] Keypads wiped and working
- [ ] Drive aisles swept, no trash near the dumpster
- [ ] Restroom clean, if your site has one
- [ ] A clean vacant unit ready to show (lesson 8)

## The Second Mile

Chick-fil-A trains a "Second Mile Service" idea: every customer, every visit, gets at least one thing beyond what they expected. Storage versions that cost little or nothing:

- Carry the lock and boxes out to the customer's car.
- Write a short thank-you card at move-in.
- Call a few days after move-in: "How did the move go? Everything working with your code?"
- Leave a sympathy card in the unit for a family storing a parent's belongings.
- Hand an RV customer the site's dump station hours on a card, if the site has one.
- Remember the name of a tenant's dog.

Tron Jordheim, who ran StorageMart's call center, listed ten habits of great storage salespeople in a 2012 trade column. They are old and still true: keep learning, smile, make natural eye contact, listen, say you are there to help ("Sure, I can help you with that"), ask good questions, do something unexpected, share a laugh, ask for the sale, and ask for a referral. His tip for practice: pick two or three, track yourself on them for a week, then pick the next two.

## Your Authority

The Ritz-Carlton famously lets every front-line employee spend up to $2,000 per guest to fix a problem without asking. Greens' version is smaller and written down: your waiver and concession limits are on **Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds**. Know them before you need them.

Why it matters: a customer who hears "Let me fix that" from you is happier than one who hears "I have to ask someone" and waits two days. And a customer who hears "Yes, I'll waive it" and then gets a bill anyway is angrier than if you had said nothing. **Never promise a waive you are not allowed to give.** If it is above your limit: "That's my manager's call. I'll ask today and call you by 4:00 PM."

## The Greens Response Times

Every target below comes from a published source. Greens adopts them as its standard.

| Channel | Greens target | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Phone | Answer by the third ring | Sue Weinman, trade column, 2010 (dated, still the industry norm) |
| Web lead or online reservation | First live contact within 5 minutes; 30 minutes at the very most | Garrett Byrd, 2021 (5 minutes "impactful," 15 "professional," 30 "mandatory"); DeFazio, 2026 ("by minute six" they may have moved on) |
| Voicemail or missed call | Within one business hour while the office is open; first thing the next business morning for after-hours calls | Compiled practice from the same sources |
| Second follow-up on a lead | 24 to 48 hours after first contact, by the channel the customer prefers | Byrd, 2021 |
| Before a move-in | The day before, confirm the arrival time | Byrd, 2021 |
| Move-out notice | A live phone call within 24 hours, to learn why and try to help | Inside Self-Storage tenant retention article |
| Escalated complaint | Tell the customer they will hear back within 24 hours, and make sure they do | Tocarro Williams, 2022 |
| Online review | Reply within 48 hours | DeFazio, 2026 |
| A callback you promised | At the time you said. If you cannot, call before that time and say when | Greens standard |

> [!NOTE]
> These are minimums. Lesson 10 covers why minutes matter so much for web leads.

## Greens Scenarios

**Valley Center, 4:45 PM.** A tenant walks in angry: his late fee posted and he says he paid. You let him finish (Hear). "So you paid on the 10th and still got a fee. I'd be upset too" (Own). You open his ledger in Cubby and see no payment on the 10th; a declined card posts nothing (Cubby Fundamentals lesson 6). You show him gently and offer to take the payment now (Solve). You thank him for coming in (Thank). Then you note that his saved card expired, and update it with him (Fix).

**Fairfield.** A caller's voicemail came in at 7:15 PM, after the office closed. You call her back at the start of the next business day, not at lunch.

**Murrieta.** You promised a fifth-wheel owner a call by 2:00 PM about a pull-through space. At 1:45 PM you still do not have the answer. You call him at 1:45 PM: "I'm still checking with my manager; I'll have it by 4:00 PM." He trusts you more, not less.

**Temecula.** A tenant moving in a boat mentions it is her late husband's. You do not make a speech. You say you are sorry, help her with the paperwork, and later add a short handwritten card. That is the second mile.

**A Texas site.** A web reservation comes in at 10:02 AM. You call at 10:05 AM. The customer is still at her computer comparing sites. She stops comparing.

## Key Points

- Greens service is listening, honesty and follow-up. Friendly is the minimum.
- Recover with Hear, Own, Solve, Thank, Fix, in that order. Hear before you solve.
- Saying sorry for an experience is not admitting fault for damage.
- Get ready before you pick up or greet: stand, smile, slow down. Keep the office and the gate signs clean and clear.
- Do one second-mile thing for a customer every day.
- Know your waiver authority from Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds. Never promise what you cannot give.
- Response times: phone by the third ring, web leads within 5 minutes (30 at most), second follow-up in 24 to 48 hours, confirm the day before move-in, move-out notices called within 24 hours, escalations answered within 24 hours, reviews within 48 hours.
