A caller at Escondido wants a 5x10 for "a couch, a bed, a table, maybe 40 boxes." It will not fit. If you rent it to her, she finds out on moving day with a truck in the drive aisle, and she is angry at you. If you tell her why and recommend a 10x15, she thanks you, because the site down the road was about to rent her the 5x10. Recommending a size is a service, not a sales trick.

## Why Not "What Size Do You Need?"

A first-timer has never stood inside a 10x10. They do not know the ceiling height or whether a sofa stands on end. Ask them for a size and they guess, and they guess small because small is cheaper. **Ask what they are storing; never ask what size they need.** You are the expert.

## The Sizing Questions

Start with "What are you planning to store?" Then:

1. **"From what size home? About how many rooms?"** Rooms are the fastest way to a size.
2. **"Any big pieces, like a sectional, a piano or a king bed?"** Start from the largest item.
3. **"Will you need to get to things often?"** Then leave a walkway, and think ground floor or drive-up.
4. **"Anything that doesn't like heat?"** See below.
5. **"About how many boxes?"** People always underestimate. Add a third.

For vehicles: the overall length with the tongue or hitch, the height to the highest point, the width with mirrors, and how often it goes out (RV & Boat Storage Fundamentals, Measuring for the Right Fit).

The sizes themselves, what each compares to and what usually fits, are in Self-Storage Fundamentals, The Product: Units and Sizes.

## Put Their Things in the Unit, Out Loud

Customers trust a recommendation they can picture. Say a comparison they know ("about half a one-car garage" sells better than "100 square feet"), then place their things:

> [!NOTE]
> "In a 10x15, your sofa goes along the back wall on end, the bed frame and mattress beside it, the dresser on the left, and the boxes on the right up to about head height. That leaves a walkway down the middle to reach the kitchen boxes."

## When in Doubt, Go Up a Size

A customer who doubts their things will fit shops one more place. A customer told "this fits, with room to spare" stops shopping. Going up is not upselling when you say why:

> [!NOTE]
> "You could squeeze it into a 10x10, but you said you need your work files every week. A 10x15 gives you a walkway, so you're not unstacking everything for one box."

**If the right size is sold out**, offer the next size up at its own price. A lower price on the bigger unit is your manager's call, not yours. Never squeeze a customer into a unit that is too small to make the rental.

## Climate-Sensitive Items

Ask about heat when the customer mentions wood furniture or antiques, pianos and instruments, electronics, photos, documents and books, artwork, mattresses and upholstered furniture, or records and collectibles. Heat and humidity warp, crack, curl and ruin them, and inland California summers are hot. For these, climate control is a real need. Escondido has the climate-controlled units; the other sites mostly offer drive-up units.

For lawn furniture, tools, holiday decorations and boxes of clothes, it usually is not, and **pushing climate control for things that do not need it** is an add-on customers resent later. Say what the building does (the temperature is regulated); never promise humidity control or a number you have not confirmed.

## Screening for What Cannot Be Stored

"What are you planning to store?" also screens, kindly:

> [!NOTE]
> "Just so you know, we can't take anything flammable, like gas cans or propane, or any food, because of pests. Is any of that in the mix? ... Great, then a 10x15 is perfect."

The full list is in Self-Storage Fundamentals, The Rental Agreement. Discovery also spots business renters (storing inventory is fine; running a business from the unit is not) and anyone who mentions staying in the unit, which is never allowed. Explain the rule now, not at move-in.

## Add-Ons That Help

The test: **would the customer be upset later to learn you had not offered it?** If yes, offer it.

| Add-on | How to offer it |
|---|---|
| Protection | Lead with a real situation (a roof leak, a neighbor's unit), then the choice: Greens' plan or their own policy (Greens Tenant Protection). Never call the plan "insurance" |
| Boxes and supplies | Offer at every rental; offer to carry them to the car |
| Autopay | "You never have to think about it, and you'll never get a late fee." |

Recommend the unit and close first; add what helps after.

## At Greens

**Escondido.** A man storing his late father's antique desk and old photographs had not thought about heat. You explain what heat does to wood and photos and recommend climate control. He thanks you.

## Key Points

- Ask what they are storing, then recommend a size and say why.
- Rooms, big pieces, access, heat and boxes get you to a size.
- When in doubt, go up a size; never squeeze someone in.
- Climate control for things heat ruins, not for everything.
- Screen kindly for flammables, food, living things and living in a unit.
