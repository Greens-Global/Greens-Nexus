## Why This Lesson Is Different

Collections is where good people get into trouble. Not because they mean harm, but because they are tired of chasing money, and a little pressure seems harmless. "If you don't pay today, we're cutting your lock." "The system won't let me take less." "I'll have to talk to your wife about this." Every one of those lines can break the law.

Self-Storage Fundamentals lesson 10 taught the lien process and Greens Delinquency Timeline has the days. This lesson is about the **conversation**: how to talk to a tenant who is behind so that they pay, they keep their dignity, and Greens stays on the right side of the law.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## The Law in Plain Words

- **The federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA)** mostly covers third-party collectors (agencies collecting someone else's debts), not a business collecting its own rent. So you might think it does not apply to Greens. But:
- **California's Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act** (Civil Code 1788 and following) covers a creditor collecting its own consumer debts. Through Civil Code 1788.17 it pulls in most of the FDCPA's conduct rules. It bans false, deceptive or misleading statements and harassment. SB 1286 extended it to some small-business debts starting in July 2025.
- **Texas Finance Code chapter 392** defines "debt collector" broadly enough to include a creditor collecting its own consumer debts. Sections 392.301 to 392.306 forbid threats, harassment, unfair practices and misrepresentation (for example, 392.302 on harassment and 392.304 on misleading statements).

Whether a storage rent balance counts as a "consumer debt" under each law, and how each law fits with the lien statutes, is a question for Greens' lawyers. **Both laws likely apply to how Greens collects rent; confirm with counsel.** Greens does not wait for the answer. **We follow FDCPA-style conduct on every collections contact, at every site, in both states.** It is the right way to treat people, and it keeps everyone safe.

## The Greens Conduct Rules

| Rule | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Talk only to the tenant** | Or a verified authorized representative or their attorney. Confirm identity before you name the balance (Greens Privacy and Records). |
| **Call between 8:00 AM and 9:00 PM**, the tenant's local time | Never earlier, never later. Texts follow the same window. |
| **Not at work, if they say no** | If a tenant says they cannot take calls at work, stop calling them there. |
| **Do not call to annoy** | Repeated calls to wear someone down are harassment. Use more than 7 calls in 7 days as an absolute ceiling (the federal Regulation F presumption for third-party collectors), and stay well under it. The Greens ladder is a handful of calls a month. |
| **No threats, no profanity, no shaming** | Never threaten something Greens does not intend or is not allowed to do: arrest, "selling your things tomorrow" when the legal sale date is weeks away, telling their employer or family. |
| **No false statements** | About the amount, the fees, the dates, or the law. No fake urgency. |
| **Never "blame the system"** | Never say "the system won't let me" when it is a policy, a choice or a rule. Say what is true: "Greens' policy is..." or "That's my manager's decision." |
| **Third parties get nothing** | Anyone else who answers gets your first name, "Greens Storage," and a callback number. Never the reason, never the balance. |
| **Voicemail: name, company, number** | Never mention the debt, the balance or the lien in a voicemail. |
| **Stop when asked in writing** | If a tenant asks in writing that you stop calling, or stop calling at a certain place, stop and tell your manager the same day. |
| **Log every contact** | In Cubby notes: date, time, who you spoke to, what was said, what was promised. Facts only. |
| **Follow the process exactly** | Lien notices, overlocks and sales follow the statute and Cubby's workflow. Nobody "pulls" a unit, cuts a lock or sells anything outside the process. |

> [!WARNING]
> An old, widely shared collections tip says to "blame the system" so the tenant is not angry at you. Greens does not use it. Telling someone a computer forbids something it does not is a misrepresentation, and under California's and Texas' collection laws a misleading statement is a legal risk, not just bad manners.

## The Greens Collections Ladder

Cubby runs the ladder and raises the tasks; you make the calls (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026; full detail on Greens Delinquency Timeline).

| Day | Cubby task or event | The conversation |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | "2nd - Call Failed Autopay Tenants" | A service call: their card did not go through. Help them update it. |
| 6th | "6th - Call Past Due Tenants" | A helpful reminder: "I'm calling to help you avoid the late fee on the 11th." |
| 11th | The late fee posts and Cubby changes the gate code | No call task. If the tenant calls about the gate, see below. |
| 15th | The preliminary lien notice goes out | The tenant may call worried. Explain calmly and factually. |
| 30th | "30th - Final Collection Call" | Clear, kind and specific: the amount, the key date, the ways to pay. |
| 35th and later | The lien notice, and for vehicles the vehicle lien steps | Your manager guides these. |

> [!NOTE]
> The gate code change on the 11th and the overlock on the 20th are what Cubby does today and are under review with counsel. Follow Cubby, never explain or defend the timing to a tenant as "the law," and send a tenant who disputes it to your manager.

A payment in full stops the ladder at any step. A partial payment does not: Cubby applies money to the oldest charge first, so the lease stays past due until everything is paid. Tell the tenant that honestly before they decide how much to pay.

## The Calls, Word for Word

### Before You Dial

Go in with a plan (StoragePug, 03/2023): the balance, the date of the last payment, the next step on the ladder and its date, and your waiver authority. Sit down, close the other tabs, speak slowly. Never multitask on a collections call.

### The Past Due Call on the 6th

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi, may I speak with Sam Ortiz? ... Hi Sam, this is Lee with Greens Storage Fairfield. Before I go on, can you confirm [the details Greens requires]? ... Thanks. I'm calling because your rent for this month hasn't come through yet, and I'd like to help you avoid the late fee that posts on the 11th. Would you like to take care of it now?"

### The Final Call on the 30th

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi Sam, it's Lee at Greens Storage Fairfield. I'm calling about your account. The balance is [amount from Cubby]. I want to make sure you know where things stand: [the next step and its date from Greens Delinquency Timeline]. I'd really like to help you keep your unit. Can we take care of it today, or set a date you can pay?"

That is the whole call: the amount, the date, the ways to pay, said factually, with no threat and no drama.

### Common Answers

These responses are from StoragePug's collections guide (03/2023), adapted.

| The tenant says | You say |
|---|---|
| "I sent a check." | "Thank you. Can I get the check number and the date you sent it, so I can look for it?" |
| "I've been busy." | "I understand. I can take a payment right now over the phone, or send you a payment link." |
| "I never got the bill." | "I'm sorry about that. Let me send you a copy of the bill and your agreement right now. What's the best email?" |
| "I lost my job." | "I'm sorry, that sounds really hard." Then see "When a Tenant Cannot Pay" below. |
| "You're a crook." | Stay calm. "I understand you're upset. I want to help you keep your things. Can we look at the balance together?" |
| "Just sell it, I don't care." | "I understand. If you'd like to move out instead, I can help you do that and take your things. Would that help?" Tell your manager. |

## The Wrong Person Answers

A roommate, a spouse, a parent or a coworker picks up.

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi, this is Lee from Greens Storage. Is Sam available? ... No problem. Could you ask Sam to call me back at [number]? Thank you."

If they ask what it is about: "It's a personal matter for Sam. Please just ask Sam to call Lee at Greens Storage. Thanks so much." **Never** the reason, the balance, the word "past due" or the word "lien." Not even to a spouse, unless the spouse is on the account and verified.

## Voicemail

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi, this is Lee from Greens Storage. Please call me back at [number]. Thank you."

Nothing about the account. Someone else may hear it.

## Email and Text

Never put a balance or lien detail in a text or an email unless Greens' policy says so, and never in any public place: a review reply, social media, a note on the unit door. Payment links sent by Cubby are fine; your own words about the debt are not.

## When a Tenant Cannot Pay

Empathy does not mean waiving everything. It means treating a person with dignity while you work the problem. Aycha Williams' collections steps (Inside Self-Storage, 2013) still hold, minus her "blame the system" tip:

1. **Explain fees at move-in**, so nothing on the ladder is a surprise (lesson 9).
2. **Be consistent** with every tenant. Same rules, same tone.
3. **Collect emails** at move-in for reminders.
4. **Call early**, before the late fee, not just after.
5. **Be professional:** slow down, lower your voice, smile, and offer solutions, such as splitting the payment across two cards.
6. **Be organized:** schedule the follow-up in Cubby before you hang up.
7. **Expect emotion and do not take it personally.**
8. **Get a firm commitment with a date:** "So you'll pay [amount] on Friday the 14th. I'll note that."

What you can offer is on Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds. A payment plan, a fee waiver for a tenant in the lien process, or anything else above your limit is your manager's decision: "I'd like to help. Let me talk to my manager today and call you back by 4:00 PM."

## Stop and Escalate

Stop collecting and call your manager the same day if:

- the tenant or their spouse is on active military duty (Self-Storage Fundamentals lesson 11);
- you receive a bankruptcy notice;
- the tenant has died (lesson 14);
- the tenant disputes the balance in writing, or asks in writing that you stop contacting them;
- a California tenant sends a Declaration in Opposition;
- anyone mentions a lawyer.

## Greens Scenarios

**Temecula.** A boat tenant is 20 days late. He says he lost his job. You confirm it is him before naming the balance. "That sounds really hard." You tell him the amount and the next date on the ladder, factually. You offer to split it across two cards, and he commits to half today and half on the 1st. You note it, and tell your manager because a split plan is above your authority.

**Escondido.** You call a tenant on the 6th and her teenage son answers. You give your first name, Greens Storage and your number. He asks what it is about. "It's a personal matter. Just ask your mom to call Lee, thanks." Nothing more.

**Valley Center.** A tenant whose gate code stopped working on the 11th calls, angry: "Is that even legal?" You do not argue or explain the timing as law. You say you understand, give the amount needed to restore access, and offer to connect her with your manager about the timing.

**Murrieta.** On the final call, a frustrated coworker suggests telling the tenant "we're selling Monday" to scare him. The sale is weeks away. You do not say it. You give the real date.

**Fairfield.** A tenant emails: "Stop calling me at work." You stop calling the work number that day, note it in Cubby, and tell your manager.

**A Texas site.** A tenant is behind on a trailer space. You use the same conduct rules as in California: the tenant only, between 8:00 AM and 9:00 PM, no threats, a voicemail with just your name, Greens and the number. The Texas ladder days come from your Area Manager until the Texas sites are in Cubby.

## Key Points

- California's Rosenthal Act (Civil Code 1788 and following) and Texas Finance Code chapter 392 likely apply to how Greens collects its own rent; confirm with counsel. Greens follows FDCPA-style conduct everywhere.
- Talk only to the tenant, after confirming identity. Third parties and voicemail get your name, Greens and a number, never the reason.
- Call between 8:00 AM and 9:00 PM local time, not at work if they object, never to annoy; more than 7 calls in 7 days is an absolute ceiling.
- No threats, no shaming, no false statements, no fake urgency, and never "blame the system."
- The ladder (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026): failed autopay call on the 2nd, past due call on the 6th, final call on the 30th. Partial payment does not stop it; payment in full does.
- Get a firm commitment with a date, log every contact, and escalate military status, bankruptcy, a death, a written dispute or a lawyer the same day.
