## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Explain why online reviews decide rentals before the phone ever rings.
- Ask for reviews the right way: every customer, the same neutral words, no incentives.
- Explain what the FTC's 2024 rule on reviews forbids.
- Reply to every review within 48 hours, using the four Greens templates.
- Reply to a review about a lien sale without disclosing anything about the account.
- Turn a bad review into a fix, not an argument.

## Reviews Are the First Conversation

Lesson 1 showed that customers read reviews and look at Google photos before they call. For many people, your reviews **are** the first conversation with Greens, and they happen before you get a chance to help.

Two things shape what a shopper sees:

1. **The reviews themselves.** How many, how recent, how good.
2. **Your replies.** A calm, kind reply to a one-star review often does more for the next shopper than another five-star review. It shows how Greens treats people when something goes wrong.

## Asking for Reviews

### When

Ask once the customer is happy and settled:

- after a smooth move-in, at the call a few days later (lesson 9);
- after you solved a problem for them;
- at a happy move-out (lesson 12).

### How

Ask **every** customer, the **same neutral way**. Send the direct Google review link by text or email so it takes them ten seconds.

> [!NOTE]
> "Thanks again for choosing Greens Storage Valley Center. If you have a minute, we'd really appreciate a review about your experience. Here's the link: [link]. Good or bad, it helps us get better."

### What Is Not Allowed

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Offer anything in return for a review**: a discount, a free month, a gift card, an entry in a drawing | The FTC's rule on fake reviews (finalized in 08/2024) bans buying reviews that are conditioned on being positive. Neutral incentives (for any review) are allowed by the rule but risky. **Greens offers no incentives at all.** |
| **Ask only the happy customers** ("review gating") | Asking only people you expect to be positive skews the picture. Google's own policies are reported to bar selectively asking for positive reviews. Ask everyone. |
| **Suppress or bury negative reviews** | The FTC rule also covers suppressing negative reviews. |
| **Write a review of Greens yourself**, or ask family or friends to | Fake reviews are exactly what the FTC rule targets. |
| **Write a review of a competitor** | Never, good or bad. |

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## Replying to Reviews

### The Rules

- **Reply to every review within 48 hours** (DeFazio, 2026). Some operators aim for the same day.
- **Say who you are and your role.** "I'm Maria, the manager at Greens Storage Valley Center."
- **Keep it short.** About 70 words is plenty.
- **Thank positive reviewers by name** and mention one specific detail.
- **On a negative review, stay calm, own real mistakes, and take it offline:** give a direct phone number.
- **Never disclose account details** in a public reply: no balance, no lien status, no unit number, no dates of visits, not even whether the person still rents. That is a privacy problem and may break collections law (lesson 13).
- **Fix what the review is about.** The reply is the start, not the end.

Check with your manager who replies at your site. Many companies have one person reply for each site so the voice is consistent.

### Template 1: Positive Review

> [!NOTE]
> "Thank you, [Name]! We're so glad the move went smoothly and that [detail, such as the cart and the drive-up access] helped. We're here whenever you need us. - [Your name], Greens Storage [Site]"

### Template 2: Negative, About Service

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi [Name], I'm [Your name], the manager at Greens Storage [Site]. I'm sorry your visit on [day] didn't go the way it should have. That's not the experience we want for anyone. I'd like to make it right. Please call me directly at [number] or reply here, and I'll take care of it personally."

### Template 3: Negative, About a Rate Increase or a Fee

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi [Name], thank you for the feedback, and I understand how frustrating a rate change is. I'd like to go over your options with you, including other unit sizes. Please call me at [number] at your convenience. - [Your name], Greens Storage [Site]"

### Template 4: Negative, About a Lien or Auction

No account details at all. Not the balance, not the dates, not "we sent you three notices."

> [!NOTE]
> "Hi [Name], I'm sorry for how difficult this situation has been. I'm not able to discuss account details here, but I'd welcome a call at [number] to talk it through. - [Your name], Greens Storage [Site]"

## The "They Sold My Stuff" Review

This is the hardest review you will see. A former tenant posts: "They sold everything I owned. Heartless. Stay away."

What you feel: defensive. Greens followed the law, sent every notice, called on the 2nd, the 6th and the 30th. You want the world to know.

What you do:

1. **Breathe. Do not reply in the first hour.**
2. **Tell your manager** the same day.
3. **Reply within 48 hours** with Template 4. Calm, short, empathetic, no details.
4. **Take it offline.** If they call, listen. Your manager handles the conversation.
5. **Fix the process**, if there is something to fix: Was the move-in explanation of the lien process clear (lesson 9)? Were the calls made and logged (lesson 13)?

> [!WARNING]
> Never write "You were sent multiple notices," "Your account was 90 days past due," or anything else about the account in a public reply, however unfair the review feels. Every future shopper reads that reply, and so might a lawyer.

## When a Review Is About an Incident

A review about a break-in, an injury or a police matter gets no details of the incident or the investigation. Use Template 2 or 4 and take it offline. Tell your manager before you reply.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A tenant moved her late mother's furniture into a climate-controlled unit and posts five stars, naming you. You reply the same day: "Thank you, Linda! We're so glad we could help with your mom's things, and that the climate-controlled unit gave you peace of mind. - Daniel, Greens Storage Escondido."

**Valley Center.** A tenant posts two stars about a rate increase. You reply within 48 hours with Template 3. He calls; you go over a smaller unit with him. He stays and edits the review.

**Fairfield.** A trailer owner had a great move-in. At your follow-up call, you ask for a review with the link. A coworker suggests offering $10 off next month to everyone who posts five stars. You say no: Greens offers no incentives, and the FTC rule forbids paying for positive reviews.

**Temecula.** A one-star review says, "Gate was broken and nobody answered the phone." You reply with Template 2, then check: the gate keypad was faulty that weekend. You report it, and your manager fixes it. That is the Fix step.

**Murrieta.** A one-star review says, "They auctioned my RV." You do not reply with the dates of the notices. You tell your manager, then reply with Template 4.

**A Texas site.** A new Texas site has a handful of old reviews nobody answered. Your manager asks you to reply to each recent one, kindly and briefly, and to ask every new tenant for a review with the same neutral words.

## Key Points

- Reviews are often the first conversation a shopper has with Greens. Your replies matter as much as the stars.
- Ask every customer the same neutral way, with the direct link, once they are settled.
- No incentives of any kind, no asking only happy customers, no fake reviews, no reviews of competitors. The FTC's 2024 rule bans buying positive reviews and suppressing negative ones.
- Reply to every review within 48 hours (DeFazio, 2026). Say who you are, keep it to about 70 words, thank by name, and take negatives offline.
- Never disclose any account, lien or incident detail in a public reply.
- After a bad review, fix the cause.
