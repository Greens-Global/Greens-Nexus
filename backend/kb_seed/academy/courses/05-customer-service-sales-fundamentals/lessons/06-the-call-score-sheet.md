## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Name the 22 items on the Greens call score sheet and group them by the nine parts of a call.
- Score a call: yes, no or not applicable on each item, and work out the percentage.
- State the pass mark (70%) and what counts as excellent (90%).
- Review your own calls each week and pick one thing to improve.
- Explain how the sheet is used in the Hands-On Check.

## Why a Score Sheet

The Why the Phone Wins or Loses Rentals lesson showed that most storage staff are friendly but do not ask good questions, ask for the rental, capture details or follow up. A score sheet turns "be better on the phone" into 22 specific things you can check. You will use this sheet three ways:

1. **On yourself**, reviewing your own calls every week.
2. **With a coworker**, scoring each other on the practice scenarios in the Practice: Twenty Scenarios, Part 1 and Practice: Twenty Scenarios, Part 2 lessons.
3. **In the Hands-On Check**, the in-person sign-off at the end of the Greens Storage Academy. Your trainer will play a caller in a mock sales call and score you with this exact sheet.

No storage company publishes its mystery-shop score sheet; vendors keep them private. This sheet is rebuilt from the published criteria: the 2004 IntelliShop study, Carol Mixon's mystery-shop standards (SkilCheck Services, Inside Self-Storage, 10/08/2020) and DeFazio (2026).

## The 22 Items

| # | Item | Part of the call | What a "yes" looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Answered within 3 rings | Greeting | Picked up by the third ring, or called back within the hour if it was missed |
| 2 | Greeting with property name and own name | Greeting | "Thank you for calling Greens Storage Fairfield, this is Lee." |
| 3 | Asked why they were calling or offered help | Why they called | "How can I help?" plus a "why" after a simple question |
| 4 | Asked whether they had rented before | Discovery | Asked, not assumed |
| 5 | Asked what they are storing | Discovery | Asked before giving a size |
| 6 | Asked move-in date | Discovery | A real date or week |
| 7 | Asked length of stay | Discovery | "How long do you think you'll need it?" |
| 8 | Asked about climate-sensitive items | Discovery | For vehicles: asked length, height and how often they use it |
| 9 | Recommended a specific size and explained why | Recommend | "A 10x15, because..." Never "What size do you need?" |
| 10 | Named 2 or more features tied to stated needs | Build value | Each feature linked to something the caller said |
| 11 | Stated what makes this property different | Build value | One clear thing |
| 12 | Quoted price, special and fees accurately | Price | Rent from Cubby, any special with its end date, the $25 admin fee |
| 13 | Mentioned the protection plan | Price | Offered Greens' plan or own policy, with the approved wording |
| 14 | Mentioned supplies or locks | Price | Offered, not pushed |
| 15 | Asked for the rental or reservation | Close | A direct ask |
| 16 | Offered an appointment with a set time | Close | "11:00 AM or 3:00 PM?" not "come by anytime" |
| 17 | Captured name, phone and email | Capture | All three |
| 18 | Created urgency honestly | Close | A real reason: their move date, a unit held for them. Never a fake "only one left" |
| 19 | Closed with the property name and thanks | Capture | "Thank you for calling Greens Storage Fairfield." |
| 20 | Tone: warm, unhurried, not scripted | Throughout | Sounded like a person who cared |
| 21 | Took control of the call | Throughout | Guided the conversation; did not just answer questions |
| 22 | Rapport | Throughout | Used the caller's name and their words; a moment of real connection |

Items 1 to 19 are things you did or did not do. Items 20 to 22 are judgments. The scorer rates each of those three from 1 to 5, and a 4 or 5 counts as a yes.

## How to Score a Call

1. Mark each item **Yes**, **No** or **N/A**.
2. Use N/A only when an item truly could not apply. Examples: the caller said "I rented with you before" in the first sentence (item 4), or the caller hung up before a close was possible. Not asking is a No, not an N/A.
3. Count the Yes marks and the items that applied (22 minus the N/A items).
4. Score = Yes divided by items that applied.

| Score | What it means |
|---|---|
| Below 70% | Not yet. Pick the two items that would have changed the call most, practice them, and try again. |
| 70% to 89% | **Pass.** This is the mystery-shop minimum for a phone shop (Mixon, 2020). |
| 90% and above | **Excellent.** Mixon's standard for a certificate-level call. |

> [!WARNING]
> Any call that quotes a wrong price or fee, calls the protection plan "insurance," or promises something outside your authority is not a pass, whatever the percentage. Accuracy and honesty come first.

### Worked Example: Scoring a Call

A Murrieta call: a fifth-wheel owner.

- Items 1 to 3: Yes. Answered on the second ring, "Thank you for calling Greens Storage Murrieta, this is Sam," asked how she could help.
- Item 4: N/A. The caller opened with "I've had my RV at another place for years."
- Items 5 to 7: Yes. What he is storing, when, how long.
- Item 8: Yes. Asked the length with the hitch, the height, and how often he goes out.
- Item 9: Yes. Recommended a pull-through space because he said backing in was hard.
- Item 10: Yes. Pull-through and security, both tied to what he said.
- Item 11: No. Never said what makes Murrieta different.
- Item 12: Yes. Quoted the rent from Cubby and the $25 admin fee.
- Item 13: Yes. Explained that vehicles carry the owner's own insurance and asked for proof at move-in.
- Item 14: N/A. A vehicle space needs no supplies; the scorer agreed it did not apply.
- Items 15 and 16: Yes. Asked to reserve, and offered "Thursday at 10:00 AM or 2:00 PM" to see it.
- Item 17: No. Got a name and phone, but no email.
- Items 18 and 19: Yes.
- Items 20 to 22: rated 4, 3 and 4. So 20 Yes, 21 No, 22 Yes.

Items that applied: 22 minus 2 N/A = 20. Yes count: 17. Score: 17 / 20 = **85%. Pass.** The two things to work on: say what makes the site different (item 11), and always get the email (item 17).

## Reviewing Your Own Calls

DeFazio (2026) recommends three habits for staying sharp:

1. **Review five of your own calls each week.** If your line records calls, listen back. If it does not, have a coworker listen to a live call, or score yourself right after you hang up while it is fresh.
2. **Track outcomes per 10 calls or walk-ins.** Out of your last 10 new-rental calls, how many reserved or rented? Write the number down every week.
3. **Practice 5 to 10 minutes a week.** One scenario from the Practice: Twenty Scenarios, Part 1 and Practice: Twenty Scenarios, Part 2 lessons with a coworker, scored with this sheet.

Jordheim's tip from the The Greens Service Standard lesson applies here too: pick the two items you miss most, work only on them for a week, then pick the next two.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

If you review recorded calls, they must have been recorded the way Greens allows. California requires every person on the call to consent to recording (Penal Code 632), so a recorded line plays a notice at the start. Never record a call on your own phone to review later.

### Questions to Ask Yourself After a Call

- Did I find out why they called?
- Did I recommend a size, or ask them for one?
- Did I tie features to their words?
- Did I ask for the rental?
- Do I have their name, phone and email in Cubby, with a follow-up task?
- What one thing would I do differently?

## How the Hands-On Check Uses This Sheet

The Hands-On Check is the last course in the Greens Storage Academy. It is in person: a manager or senior team member signs off three things, and the first is **a mock sales call**. The trainer plays a caller, often one of the scenarios from the Practice: Twenty Scenarios, Part 1 and Practice: Twenty Scenarios, Part 2 lessons, and scores you on these 22 items. The pass mark is the same: 70% of the items that applied, with no wrong price or fee.

The best way to prepare: score yourself on real calls for a few weeks before your Hands-On Check. By the time you take it, the 22 items should feel like habits, not a checklist.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** You score your own call from this morning. You did everything until the close, then said "Let me know if you have any questions." Items 15 and 16: No. Your score is 74%, a pass, but you lost the rental. Next week you practice the assumptive close.

**Temecula.** A coworker scores your practice call as a boat owner. You forgot to ask how often he takes the boat out (item 8). On the next practice, you ask it in the first minute.

**Valley Center.** You count your last 10 new-rental calls: 3 rented, 2 reserved. That is 5 of 10. You write it down and check again next week.

**Fairfield.** You realize you never say what makes Fairfield different. You talk to your manager and settle on one honest line about the site's cameras: every Fairfield parking space is in a pricing group with the Security Camera feature (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

**A Texas site.** Your site records its lines with a notice at the start of each call. You listen to five of your calls on Friday afternoon and pick item 17, capturing the email, to work on next week.

## Key Points

- The 22 items cover the greeting, why they called, discovery, recommend, value, price, close, capture, and three judgment items: tone, control and rapport.
- Mark Yes, No or N/A. Not asking is a No. Score = Yes divided by the items that applied.
- 70% passes; 90% is excellent (Mixon, 2020). A wrong price or fee, calling the plan "insurance," or an unauthorized promise is never a pass.
- Review five of your calls a week, track results per 10 calls, and practice 5 to 10 minutes a week (DeFazio, 2026).
- The Hands-On Check scores your mock sales call with this same sheet.
