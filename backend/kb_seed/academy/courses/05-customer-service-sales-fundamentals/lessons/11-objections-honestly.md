A boat owner calls Temecula: "The lot across town is $20 a month cheaper." He has not said no. He has said "help me see why yours is worth it." An objection means the customer is still talking to you; the one who has really decided says "thanks, I'll let you know" and hangs up. Honesty is the whole method. A trick that works today becomes a one-star review next month.

## The Four Steps

1. **Listen.** Let them finish.
2. **Find what is underneath.** One question: "Is it the monthly price or the move-in cost?" "Is it the size, the price or the timing?"
3. **Answer honestly**, with facts from Cubby and what they told you. No invented urgency, no criticism of a competitor, no promises you cannot keep.
4. **Ask again.** Back to the close: a rental, a free reservation or a set time.

## The Objections Greens Hears Most

| Objection | Often underneath | A good response |
|---|---|---|
| "How much is a 10x10?" | Price shopping | Give the range, then: "What are you storing? I want to make sure a 10x10 is even right." |
| "Just getting prices" | Early, or wary of being sold to | "Totally fine, most people check a few places. When are you moving? I can hold a unit with a free reservation." Get their contact details |
| "I need to check with my spouse" | A shared decision | Email the quote, hold the unit, and set a call for when they are both free |
| "I don't need protection; my homeowner's covers it" | Sometimes true | "Many policies do. Check the off-premises limit. If it's covered, I'll note the policy number and expiration date." |
| "Online it said a different price" | A web rate, or an old page | Never argue. Check Cubby. If Cubby shows that price for the unit, honor it; if unsure, ask your manager |
| "Why is my rent going up?" | Feels betrayed after a low first month | Why Did My Rent Go Up? has the conversation |

## "The Place Down the Road Is $20 Cheaper"

**Acknowledge it**, without arguing: "That's a fair question, and I'm glad you're comparing. Can I ask a couple of things so we're comparing the same thing?"

**Compare total cost:**

- Is their price a move-in special? **When does it end**, and what is the rate after?
- Is there an admin fee? Is protection or insurance required, and at what cost?
- Is there a deposit? Greens charges none on new move-ins, and no reservation fee.
- Is the unit the same: drive-up, climate controlled, covered?

**Then the real differences** that matter to this caller: their own gate code, cameras, a clean site, staff on site, the space type they need.

> [!NOTE]
> "If their lower rate is a first-month special, after that you may be paying the same or more. And you said the sun on the boat matters; our covered spaces keep it off. Want me to hold one while you check?"

**A price match is your manager's decision, not yours.** Never promise one you do not have. **Never** say anything bad about the competitor ("I've heard things about them" makes you look worse), never invent urgency ("my last 10x10" only if it is true), and never guess their price.

## "I'll Think About It"

> [!NOTE]
> You: "Of course, it's a big decision. Is it the size, the price or the timing?"
>
> Customer: "Honestly, the buyer might push our closing."
>
> You: "Then let me reserve it with no charge, and if the date moves, we move the reservation. Can I get your email for the details? And can I call you Thursday at 5:00 PM to see where things stand?"

Write the quote sheet, put a task in Cubby for Thursday at 5:00 PM, and call at 5:00 PM.

## "Why Is There an Admin Fee?"

> [!NOTE]
> "Fair question. It's a one-time charge to set up your account when you move in. You won't see it again."

Short and confident. The best defense against "junk fee" anger is quoting every fee up front, every time (Greens Fees and Charges has the amount).

## "That Late Fee Isn't Fair"

> [!NOTE]
> "I understand, nobody likes seeing a fee. Let me look. Rent was due on the 1st, and the fee posted on the 11th because the payment hadn't come in by then. I can see you've paid on time every other month. Let me check what I can do."

If a waive is within your limit on Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds, waive it with a real reason in Cubby. If not: "That's my manager's call. I'll ask today and call you by 4:00 PM." Either way, offer autopay.

## What You May Offer

| You may | Only with your manager |
|---|---|
| A free reservation | A price match |
| A written quote | Any rate below the street rate in Cubby |
| A different size or type that fits better | A waive above your limit |
| Autopay, and a waive within your limit | Anything you are not sure about |

## Key Points

- An objection is a question: listen, find what is underneath, answer honestly, ask again.
- A cheaper competitor: compare total cost and the real differences; never criticize.
- "I'll think about it": size, price or timing; then a free hold, a quote and a set time.
- Quote every fee up front.
- Price matches and rates below Cubby are your manager's call.
