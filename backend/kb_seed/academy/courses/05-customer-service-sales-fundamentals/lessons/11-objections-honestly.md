## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Treat an objection as a question, not a no.
- Use the four steps: listen, find what is underneath, answer honestly, ask again.
- Answer the objections Greens hears most: price, "just looking," a cheaper competitor, "I'll think about it," a spouse, fees, protection, a late fee, a different price online, and rate increases.
- Compare total cost honestly without criticizing a competitor.
- Know what you may offer and what needs your manager.

## An Objection Is a Question

When a customer says "That's more than I wanted to spend," they have not said no. They have said "help me understand why this is worth it," or "is there something that fits my budget?" An objection means they are still talking to you. The customer who has truly decided no usually says "Thanks, I'll let you know" and hangs up.

Honesty is the whole method. You never need a trick to answer an objection, and a trick that works today turns into a one-star review next month.

## The Four Steps

1. **Listen.** Let them finish. Do not jump in with an answer.
2. **Find what is underneath.** Ask one question. "Is it the monthly price, or the move-in cost?" "Is it the size, the price, or the timing?"
3. **Answer honestly.** With facts from Cubby and what the customer told you. Never invent urgency, never criticize a competitor, never promise what you cannot give.
4. **Ask again.** Go back to the close (the The Nine Parts of a Sales Call lesson): a rental, a free reservation, or a set time.

## Worked Conversation: "The Place Down the Road Is $20 Cheaper"

This is the objection you will hear most. A caller says the place down the road has a 10x10 for $20 less a month.

**What you say first:** acknowledge it, without arguing or criticizing.

> [!NOTE]
> "That's a fair question, and I'm glad you're comparing. Can I ask you a couple of things so we're comparing the same thing?"

**Then compare total cost**, honestly:

- Is their price a move-in special? **When does it end**, and what is the rate after?
- Is there an **admin fee**? Greens' is $25, one time (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Is **protection or insurance required**, and what does it cost?
- Is there a **deposit**? Greens charges none on new move-ins (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Do they need to **buy a lock**?
- Is the unit the **same**: drive-up, climate controlled, ground floor?

**Then the real differences** that matter to this caller: their own gate code, cameras, a clean site, staff on site, the reviews, drive-up access. Pick the ones tied to what they told you.

> [!NOTE]
> "If their $20 lower rate is a first-month special, after that you may be paying the same or more. And you mentioned your mom's dresser, so the climate control here is worth thinking about. Would it help if I held the unit for you while you check?"

**If you are asked to match:** price matching is Greens' decision, not yours, and is above your authority unless your manager says otherwise. If your manager does allow a match, one trade trainer's advice (Ballard, 2011) is to limit it to the stay they expect: "If I can match that rate for the two months you think you'll need..." Never promise a match before you have it.

**Then ask again.**

> [!NOTE]
> "Let's do this: I'll reserve the 10x10 for Saturday at no charge. If you find the other place really is a better fit, just call me and I'll cancel it. Sound good?"

**Never:**

- Say anything bad about the competitor. "I've heard things about them" makes you look worse, not them.
- Invent urgency. "This is my last 10x10" is only okay if it is true.
- Guess their price. If you do not know it, say so.

## Worked Conversation: "I'll Think About It"

A Valley Center walk-in toured a unit, liked it, and is heading for the door.

> [!NOTE]
> You: "Of course. It's a big decision. Can I ask, is it the size, the price, or the timing?"
>
> Customer: "Honestly, I'm not sure the move is happening on the 1st. The buyer might push it."
>
> You: "That makes sense. Here's what I can do: I'll reserve the unit with no charge, and if the date moves, we'll just move the reservation. Can I get your email to send you the details? ... And can I call you Thursday at 5:00 PM to see where things stand?"

Then write the quote sheet, put a follow-up task in Cubby for Thursday at 5:00 PM, and call at 5:00 PM.

## Worked Conversation: "Why Is There an Admin Fee?"

> [!NOTE]
> "Fair question. It's a one-time $25 charge to set up your account when you move in. You won't see it again."

That is the wording on Greens Fees and Charges.

Keep it short and confident. The best defense against "junk fee" anger is to quote every fee up front, every time, so nobody hears about it for the first time at the counter.

## Worked Conversation: A Late Fee

A tenant at Fairfield is upset about a late fee.

> [!NOTE]
> You: "I understand. Nobody likes seeing a fee. Let me look. Your rent was due on the 1st, and the late fee posted on the 11th because the payment hadn't come in by then."
>
> Tenant: "I always pay. I was traveling."
>
> You: "I can see you've paid on time every month. Let me check what I can do."

If waiving it is within your authority on Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds, waive it with a real reason in Cubby ("first late payment, goodwill"). If not: "That's my manager's call. I'll ask today and call you by 4:00 PM." Either way, offer autopay so it does not happen again.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A caller opens with "How much is a 10x10?" You give the range and ask what she is storing. It is a two-bedroom apartment. You recommend a 10x15, and the price conversation is now about the right unit.

**Temecula.** A boat owner says a lot across town is $20 cheaper. You ask whether that is covered or open parking, whether there is an admin fee, and when the price changes. His boat is going under cover at Temecula; the other lot is open. He reserves the covered space.

**Murrieta.** A fifth-wheel owner says he needs to talk to his wife. You offer to email the quote, hold the pull-through space, and call when they are both home. They are free Saturday at 11:00 AM. You call at 11:00 AM.

**Valley Center.** A customer says the website showed a lower rate yesterday. You check Cubby; it shows the web price for that unit. You honor it and thank her for telling you.

**Fairfield.** A trailer owner says "just looking." You agree that most people compare, ask when he needs the space, and offer a free hold with his email. He gives it.

**A Texas site.** A caller says a competitor "includes the lock and has no admin fee." You do not argue or guess. You give your total move-in cost clearly, explain what is different about your site, and offer to reserve.

## Key Points

- An objection is a question. Listen, find what is underneath, answer honestly, then ask again.
- Price first: give a range, then ask what they are storing.
- A cheaper competitor: acknowledge, compare total cost (special end date, admin fee, protection, deposit, lock) and the real differences. Never criticize the competitor or invent urgency.
- "I'll think about it": ask whether it is the size, the price or the timing; offer a free hold, a written quote and a set follow-up time.
- Quote the $25 admin fee up front every time (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Price matches and anything below the Cubby rate are your manager's call. Never promise what you do not have.
