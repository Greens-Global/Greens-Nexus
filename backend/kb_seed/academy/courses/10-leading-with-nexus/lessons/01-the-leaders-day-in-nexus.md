7:55 AM on a Monday at Temecula. Dana has five minutes before the gate opens. Three Time Sheets wait for her review, one teammate has not clocked in for an 8:00 AM shift, and a time off request has sat since Friday. A leader who knows where to look clears all of it before the first tenant arrives.

## What Changes When You Lead

Nexus Fundamentals taught Nexus as an employee uses it. A leader keeps all of that and gains three things:

| Gain | Where | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Decisions** | Dashboard > Approvals, and the bell | Time off, punch fixes, Time Sheets, shift requests, reimbursements, purchase requests, hiring votes, reviews |
| **Your team's view** | The Team widgets; Team > Shifts and Shift Planner; Workday > Performance > My Team | Who is on the clock, who is late, who is out, what is due |
| **The manager's screens inside modules** | People > Time, the Tickets desk, Knowledge Base & Training > Manage | The same modules, with the team's side open |

Your tier and your grants decide how far each one reaches. The lesson Access, Settings and the Leader's Record comes back to this.

Click **Customize**, then **Add Widget**: the Team category holds the leader's widgets. Supervisors get **Team Clocked-In**, **Who Has What**, **My Ticket Queue** and **My Team**; Managers also get **Team Approvals**, **Time Off to Review**, **Pending Approvals**, **Team Time**, **Time Exceptions**, **Out Today** and **Pending Purchases**.

Build the view once: who is in and the exceptions at the top, decisions and the ticket queue under them, Out Today where you can see it. Save it and star it as your default. A leader's Dashboard is a control room, not a greeting.

## Two Things Every Morning

1. **Who is here.** Team Clocked-In and Time Exceptions: who is in, who is late against their shift, who has a break still open, who is out. A No-Show you learn about at 8:15 AM is a schedule problem; at noon it is a customer problem.
2. **What is waiting on you.** The Approvals tab and the bell. A time off request answered in a day keeps the schedule honest; a punch fix answered in a day keeps a Time Sheet signable; a Time Sheet reviewed on time keeps payroll on time.

## The One Rule: Decide in Nexus

Everything a leader decides about a person's time, pay, schedule or request is decided in Nexus. An approval given in the hallway is not an approval until it is in Nexus. A shift promised by text does not exist until it is in the planner. A ticket closed out loud is still open.

This is not paperwork for its own sake. A decision in Nexus tells the person (a bell, an email, the yellow bar), protects you both with a record of who decided what and when, and moves the next step (payroll, the schedule, the vendor) without a phone call. A decision made anywhere else leaves you defending it later from memory.

## At Greens

Dana opens the yellow bar: three Time Sheets to review, since the period ended Sunday. Team Clocked-In shows seven of nine in, one on approved time off, one not yet in for 8:00 AM. Pending Approvals holds a time off request marked Recommended: Approve and a punch fix with a clear reason. By 8:20 AM she has decided both, reviewed the three Time Sheets (sending one back with a note about a missing Thursday shift), and messaged the late teammate on Teams from the My Team widget. Nothing left her desk by text.

## Common Mistakes

- **Keeping the employee's Home layout** and never adding the Team widgets.
- **Deciding in the hallway** and forgetting to record it. The person never hears, and payroll waits.
- **Reading the bell as noise.** Every notice a leader gets is a decision or a fact about the team.

## Key Points

- A leader gains decisions, the team's view and the manager's screens inside modules, as far as tier and grants reach.
- Build your Dashboard from the Team widgets and make it your default.
- Every morning: who is here, and what is waiting on you.
- Decide in Nexus, every time: the person is told, the record stands, the next step moves.
