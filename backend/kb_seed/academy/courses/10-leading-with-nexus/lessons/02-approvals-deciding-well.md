## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Name each approval kind and say what you are really deciding.
- Use the recommendation on a time off request without being ruled by it.
- Approve or send back a punch fix on its reason and its evidence.
- Decide on a phone, one card at a time, and delegate your approvals when you are away.
- Write a decision that will stand: the reason, in the record, on time.

## What Comes to You

The Approvals tab gathers every decision waiting on you, counted in one place so the badge and the inbox never disagree. The kinds a site leader meets:

| Kind | What you are deciding | What to read first |
|---|---|---|
| **Time off** | Can the site run without them those days | The recommendation and its reasons; the team calendar; the balance |
| **Punch fix** | Did the time they ask for really happen | The reason, the Beginning and End of Day messages, the shift, the location words |
| **Time Sheet** | Are the hours right and complete for the period | The whole period; the attention strip; missed-break reports (the next lesson) |
| **Shift request** (swap, offer, release) | Does the trade keep the schedule whole | Both people's shifts, the rules warnings; the teammate must accept first |
| **Reimbursement** | Was it a company expense, with a receipt | The business purpose, the receipt, the amount against the policy |
| **Purchase request** | Does the site need it, and does the company already own one | The justification; Item Management for an existing one |
| **Salary advance** | Per the company's policy | HR's guidance |
| **Hiring vote or screen** | Your read on a candidate | The packet, the interview notes |
| **Probation** | Confirm, extend or end: a recommendation to HR | The person's record, the checklist |
| **HR letter, Nexus Sign** | A document that needs your signature or your word | The document itself |

Each one opens to its record. Decide from the record, never from the badge.

Every time off request carries a **recommendation**: **Recommended: Approve** when coverage, notice, the balance and the calendar all hold, else **Check First** with the reasons in plain words. It is advice, never a decision; nothing in Nexus approves or rejects a request by itself. Read the reasons, look at the team calendar, and decide. If your company requires HR's second approval, your yes reads Awaiting HR Approval until HR decides.

Two things to decide well: **answer within a day** (a request that waits a week is a plan the person cannot make), and when you say no, **say why** in the note, so the person can ask for different days.

## Punch Fixes

A punch fix asks you to change a pay record, so the standard is evidence. The request carries the person's reason; the record around it carries the rest: their Beginning of Day message at 8:00 AM is evidence they were there; a shift that ended at 5:00 PM supports a 5:00 PM clock-out; a punch marked Out of Location is a question, not an accusation. Approve what the record supports; send back what it does not, with what you need ("the time you left on Wednesday, and anything that shows it").

Never edit a punch yourself to "save time": the request, the reason and your decision are the record that protects you both, and an edit after approval reopens the Time Sheet (the next lesson).

## Approvals on a Phone

On a phone, Approvals is one card at a time with two decisions, and a Filter sheet to pick the kind. There is no bulk approve on a phone, on purpose: each decision is read before it is made. The desktop list is for working through a stack; the phone is for the one that cannot wait until you are back at a desk.

## When You Are Away

**Delegate My Approvals** (Workday > Time Off, managers only) hands your approvals to someone for the days you name. Set it before you go; a team whose leader is away for a week with no delegate has a week of requests nobody can answer and Time Sheets nobody can review.

## A Decision That Will Stand

- **In Nexus**, on the record, not in a hallway.
- **On time**: within a day for a request, by the review date for a Time Sheet.
- **From the evidence**: the recommendation's reasons, the messages, the shift, the receipt.
- **With the reason written**, when it is a no or a send-back.
- **The same for everyone**: the rule you apply to one person is the rule you apply to all.

## A Greens Scenario

Marco's punch fix asks for a 5:00 PM clock-out on Wednesday with the reason "Forgot to punch out, locked the gate at 5." Dana opens the record: his End of Day message posted to Teams at 5:02 PM and his Wednesday shift ended at 5:00 PM. She approves. Sam's request asks for a 6:30 PM clock-out with "stayed late" and nothing else; no message, no shift past 5:30, no ticket about anything after hours. She sends it back: "What kept you until 6:30? A ticket number or your End of Day message would do." He replies with the move-in that ran late and the tenant's name, and she approves the corrected request.

## Common Mistakes

- **Approving from the badge** without opening the record.
- **Treating Check First as a no**, or Recommended: Approve as a decision already made.
- **Editing a punch yourself** instead of deciding the request.
- **A no with no reason.**
- **Leaving with no delegate.**

## Key Points

- Approvals gathers every decision; each one opens to its record; decide from the record.
- A time off request carries Recommended: Approve or Check First with reasons; the decision is always yours; answer within a day and say why when it is no.
- A punch fix is decided on evidence: the messages, the shift, the location words; never edit a punch to save time.
- A phone decides one card at a time; Delegate My Approvals before you are away.
