## The Desk Is Queues

Nexus Fundamentals taught Help Center, the requester's side. The desk, **Tickets** in the left menu for people with the grant, is where tickets are worked. It is built as **queues**: filter presets counted over the whole workspace, so the number on a tab is the truth, not the page.

| Queue | What it asks |
|---|---|
| **Needs Reply** | The requester spoke last, or nobody has replied: answer |
| **Unassigned** | Nobody owns it: assign it |
| **Mine** | Yours: work it |
| **All Open** | The whole picture |
| **Due Soon**, **Waiting**, **Done** | Close to its clock; waiting on someone; finished |
| **To Route**, **To Approve** | Send it to the right team; decide it |
| **Preventive** | Tickets preventive schedules filed; its count is the overdue ones, red when any |
| **Raised by Me**, saved views | Yours as a requester; views you saved |

Needs Reply, Unassigned, Mine and All Open stand as tabs; the rest sit in **Other Queues**, and To Route, To Approve and Preventive show only when they hold something. On a computer a row opens in a side pane beside the list, and the arrow keys and Enter step the queue.

## Needs Reply and the Next Clock

**Needs Reply** is decided by the server for every ticket: the requester spoke last, or no one has replied yet. Work it first, every hour you are at the desk.

The **Next Clock** on each row is the one deadline wording on the list: the first reply due, the next update due, or the resolution due. It turns red only when overdue. A desk inside its clocks is a desk people trust.

## Assign, Route, Approve

- **Assign** every Unassigned ticket the day it arrives, to a person, not a team. A ticket with a name has a clock someone feels.
- **Route** what landed on the wrong team. A ticket in the wrong queue is a ticket nobody answers.
- **Approve** what needs a decision; a purchase inside a ticket follows the same limits as any purchase.
- **Waiting for Vendor** is a status, not a parking space. The vendor's name and promised date go in the ticket, and the desk still owes the requester an update on the clock.

## The Preventive Queue

Preventive maintenance schedules file their own tickets when they come due. The **Preventive** queue is those tickets, and its count is the overdue ones. Resolving a preventive ticket needs a photo; closing it writes the maintenance record and moves the schedule on. Preventive at zero overdue means the building is being kept.

## What You Cannot See, and Why

- **Confidential HR tickets** (Ask HR) are invisible to everyone but HR's audience: the company's HR contact, administrators and people with Full HR access. A leader with the Tickets grant does not see them, cannot count them, and should never ask to. They let a team member raise a pay or policy question without their manager in the thread.
- **Company walls**: an Administrator sees and works every company's tickets, but people pickers, departments, approvers and desk settings stay inside each company.

## At Greens

Dana assigns the light to Sam and replies to the requester: "Sam will check the photocell this morning." The gate ticket's requester asked for an update; she replies with the vendor's Thursday visit, and the status stays Waiting for Vendor with the date. Preventive shows two overdue: the quarterly gate service, now part of Thursday's visit (she writes that in it), and an extinguisher check nobody picked up, which she assigns. Twenty minutes later the desk is inside its clocks.

## Common Mistakes

- **Working Mine before Needs Reply.** The requester is waiting; your list can wait an hour.
- **Assigning to a team** instead of a person.
- **Waiting for Vendor with no vendor and no date.**
- **A red Preventive count** nobody owns.

## Key Points

- The desk is queues counted over the whole workspace; four stand as tabs, the rest sit in Other Queues.
- Needs Reply first, every hour. The Next Clock is the one deadline wording, red only when overdue.
- Assign to a person the day it arrives; route what is misfiled; Waiting for Vendor carries the vendor and the date.
- Keep Preventive at zero overdue. Ask HR tickets stay invisible to leaders, by design.
