## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Work the ticket desk by its queues and say what each one asks of you.
- Read Needs Reply and the Next Clock and keep the desk inside its promises.
- Assign, route and approve tickets, and use Waiting for Vendor honestly.
- Watch the Preventive queue and its overdue count.
- Know which tickets stay invisible to you and why that is right.

## The Desk Is Queues

Nexus Fundamentals taught Help Center, the requester's side. The desk, **Tickets** in the left menu for people with the grant, is where tickets are worked, and it is built as **queues**: filter presets over every ticket in the workspace, counted over the whole workspace so the number on a tab is the truth, not the page.

| Queue | What it asks |
|---|---|
| **Needs Reply** | The requester spoke last, or nobody has replied yet: answer |
| **Unassigned** | Nobody owns it: assign it |
| **Mine** | Yours: work it |
| **Due Soon** | The clock is close: finish or update |
| **Waiting** | Waiting for the requester, a vendor or on hold: check it is still true |
| **All Open**, **Done** | The whole picture; what was finished |
| **To Route** | A ticket the desk must send to the right team |
| **To Approve** | A ticket that needs a decision (a purchase inside it, a change) |
| **Preventive** | The live tickets preventive schedules filed; its count is the overdue ones, red when any |
| **Raised by Me**, saved views | Yours as a requester; the views you saved |

Only Needs Reply, Unassigned, Mine and All Open stand as tabs; the rest wait in **Other Queues**. Create is the last control in the toolbar. On a desktop a row opens in a side pane beside the list, and the arrow keys and Enter step the queue.

## Needs Reply and the Next Clock

Two things keep a desk honest. **Needs Reply** is decided by the server for every ticket: the requester spoke last, or no one has replied yet. Work it first, every hour you are at the desk. The **Next Clock** on each row is the one SLA wording on the list: the first reply due, the next update due, or the resolution due, red only when overdue. A desk inside its clocks is a desk people trust; one that is not learns about its problems from the manager above.

## Assign, Route, Approve

- **Assign** every Unassigned ticket the day it arrives, to a person, not a team; a ticket with a name has a clock that someone feels.
- **Route** what landed on the wrong team from To Route; a ticket in the wrong queue is a ticket nobody answers.
- **Approve** from To Approve what needs a decision; a purchase inside a ticket follows the same limits as any purchase.
- **Waiting for Vendor** is a status, not a parking space: the vendor's name and the promised date go in the ticket (the Property & Vendor Management course), and the desk still owes the requester an update on the clock.

## The Preventive Queue

Preventive maintenance schedules file their own tickets when they come due. The **Preventive** queue is those tickets, and its count is the **overdue** ones, in red when there are any. A preventive ticket needs a photo to resolve; closing it writes the maintenance record and moves the schedule on. A red Preventive count is the first thing a regional manager looks at on a site, because it is the one queue where a zero means the building is being kept.

## What You Cannot See, and Why

Two boundaries are built into the desk and are right:

- **Confidential HR tickets** (Ask HR) are invisible to everyone but HR's audience: the company's HR contact, administrators and Full HR inside their reach. A leader with the Tickets grant does not see them, cannot count them, and should never ask to. They are the private channel that lets a team member raise a pay or a policy question without their manager in the thread.
- **Company walls**: an Administrator sees and works every company's tickets (a hotfix with Neil's approval), but people pickers, departments, approvers and desk settings stay walled to their company.

## The Standards

- Needs Reply worked every hour at the desk; nothing overdue at the end of the day without a note saying why.
- Every ticket assigned to a person the day it arrives.
- Every Waiting ticket carries who it waits for and until when.
- Preventive at zero overdue.
- One problem per ticket; a photo on every property ticket; the ticket number on every vendor invoice.

## A Greens Scenario

Dana opens the desk at 8:30 AM: Needs Reply shows three, one with a first reply overdue by 40 minutes (a light out, unassigned, raised at night). She assigns it to Sam with a reply to the requester: "Sam will check the photocell this morning." The gate ticket's requester asked for an update; she replies with the vendor's Thursday visit, and the status stays Waiting for Vendor with the date. Preventive shows two overdue: the quarterly gate service (now rolled into Thursday's visit; she writes that in it) and an extinguisher check nobody picked up; she assigns it. By 9:00 the desk is inside its clocks.

## Common Mistakes

- **Working Mine before Needs Reply.** The requester is waiting; your list can wait an hour.
- **A ticket assigned to a team**, not a person.
- **Waiting for Vendor with no vendor and no date.**
- **A red Preventive count** nobody owns.
- **Asking to see Ask HR tickets.**

## Key Points

- The desk is queues counted over the whole workspace: Needs Reply, Unassigned, Mine and All Open as tabs; the rest in Other Queues; Preventive counts its overdue in red.
- Needs Reply first, every hour; the Next Clock is the one SLA wording, red only when overdue.
- Assign to a person the day it arrives; route what is misfiled; approve from To Approve; Waiting for Vendor carries the vendor and the date.
- Confidential HR tickets stay invisible to leaders; Administrators see every company's tickets but the pickers stay walled.
