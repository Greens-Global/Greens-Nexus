## Accounting for Your Entity

With the Accounting grant, **Accounting** shows the entities you have access to: your site's Income Statement, Balance Sheet, budget beside actual, and the reports under them. **Every figure drills down** to its ledger lines and on to the transaction behind it. The consolidated dashboard tabs, other sites, and pay are not part of a site leader's view.

What leadership asks a site leader about money is almost always one of four numbers and a comment on why it moved:

| Number | What it says |
|---|---|
| **NOI margin** | What the site keeps of each rent dollar |
| **Expense ratio** | Costs against revenue |
| **Economic occupancy** | Rent earned against what the site could earn |
| **Delinquency rate** | How well the site collects |

Read them monthly with the drill-down, and write the comment: what moved, why, timing or real, what next.

## Asset Management: Your Property

**Asset Management** is the property's record. On your property you keep:

- **Records**: warranties, inspections, permits, local authority contacts, utilities, vendors, plans, and their dates.
- **Preventive schedules** under Maintenance, and the tickets they file.
- The **Overview**: **Needs Attention** (what is expiring, overdue or warning), **Data Gaps** (what the record is missing) and **Recent Activity**.
- The **Timeline**, the property's history for the next manager and the insurer.

Nothing in Asset Management is deleted by code: a live asset goes to the Trash and its log stays.

## Construction Jobs and Money by Permission

A **construction job** is one record per piece of work: its schedule, crews, permits and inspections, subcontractors, drawings, and its money read from the books. Who sees the money is a permission of its own, per person, not per tier. Contract and change-order dollars follow it everywhere, including the weekly report and the PDFs. A leader without that permission sees the job without its dollars, and that is correct.

## Purchase Requests and Reimbursements

Both reach you as approvals, with the standard from Approvals: Deciding Well.

| Request | Ask | Check |
|---|---|---|
| **Purchase request** | Does the site need it now, and does the company already own one? | Item Management; the justification; your limit |
| **Reimbursement** | Was it a company expense with a business purpose? | The receipt; the amount against the policy |

The site never pays a vendor itself and never refunds from a drawer.

## What Stays Out of Reach

| Not yours | Why |
|---|---|
| **Your team's pay**, labor budgets, payroll | HR's; the planner shows hours |
| **Other entities** | Entity access decides what you read |
| **A job's dollars without the cost permission** | Permission is per person |
| **Personal financial statements** | Owners and a separate grant only |
| **Posting to the books** | Nexus reads the ledger; it does not write it |

## The Courses Behind This Lesson

**Property & Vendor Management for Storage Sites** covers the building, the schedules, vendors and their papers. **Fundamentals of Accounting** teaches how to read the statements and the four numbers. **Intacct Fundamentals at Greens** covers where the numbers come from. A site leader takes all three in the first year; they are in the Catalog.

## At Greens

Dana drills from repairs to three gate emergency invoices, opens the property in Asset Management, finds the operator's warranty record and confirms the quarterly service was kept. She writes the variance comment with the ticket numbers and the case for a replacement. The purchase request is over her limit, so it goes up with her case attached. She never saw a payroll figure or another site's books, and did not need to.

## Common Mistakes

- **Looking for consolidated tabs or payroll** in a site view.
- **Leaving the property's records to someone else.** The property is yours to keep.
- **Approving a purchase** the company already owns.
- **A variance comment with no reason.**

## Key Points

- Accounting shows your entity's statements; every figure drills to its lines. Own the margin, the expense ratio, economic occupancy and delinquency.
- Asset Management is your property's record: Records, schedules, Needs Attention, Data Gaps, the Timeline.
- A job's money is seen by permission; purchases and reimbursements are approvals by the usual standard.
- Pay, other entities and posting stay out of reach.
