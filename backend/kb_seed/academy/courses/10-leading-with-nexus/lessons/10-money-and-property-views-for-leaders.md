## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Read your site's statements in Accounting with your entity and know what you will not see.
- Run your property in Asset Management as the manager: records, preventive schedules, critical items.
- Say who sees a construction job's money and who does not.
- Decide purchase requests and reimbursements by the same standards as every approval.
- Know which course to open for the depth: Property & Vendor Management, Fundamentals of Accounting, Intacct Fundamentals.

## Accounting, One Entity

With the Accounting grant and your site as your entity, **Accounting** shows your site's Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Budget beside actual, and the reports under them, every figure drilling to its lines. It does not show the Dashboard tabs (consolidated), other sites, the family entities, or pay. The Fundamentals of Accounting course teaches how to read what you see; the Intacct course teaches where it comes from.

What leadership asks a site leader about money is almost always one of those four and a variance comment behind it. Read them monthly from the report, with the drill-down, and write the comment the Fundamentals course asked for: what moved, why, timing or real, what next.

## Asset Management, Your Property

**Asset Management** is the property's record and the Property & Vendor Management course is its manual. As the site's leader you own, on your property: the **Records** (warranties, inspections, permits, AHJ contacts, utilities, vendors, plans) and their dates; the **preventive schedules** and their tickets; the **critical items** on the Overview; the Egnyte folder the property pins; and the Timeline, which is the property's history for the next manager and the insurer.

Company is a field on an asset, not a wall: administrators see every company's assets, and only assigning a company is limited. Nothing in the module is ever deleted by code; a live asset goes to the Trash and its log stays.

## Construction Jobs and Money by Permission

A **Construction job** is one record per piece of work in its company: its schedule in its own task project, its crews from the planner, its permits and inspections, its subcontractors and their papers, its drawings, and its money read from the ledger by the job's Project-Job code. Who sees the money is a permission of its own (the cost view, the Accounting grant and entity access together); contract and change-order dollars follow it everywhere, including the weekly report and the PDFs. A superintendent sees the schedule and the punch list; a leader without the cost view sees the job without its dollars, and that is correct.

## Purchase Requests and Reimbursements

Both reach you as **approvals**, and the standard is the one from the Approvals lesson:

| Request | Ask | Check |
|---|---|---|
| **Purchase request** | Does the site need it, now, and does the company already own one | Item Management for an existing one; the justification; the limits |
| **Reimbursement** | Was it a company expense with a business purpose | The receipt; the amount against the policy; a pattern |

Approved purchase requests move to Ordered and Fulfilled by the people who buy; approved reimbursements go to HR for the receipt check and payment. Neither is money the site spends itself: the site never pays a vendor and never issues a refund from a drawer.

## What Stays Out of Reach, and Why

| Not yours | Why |
|---|---|
| **Pay** of your team, labor budgets, payroll reports | Managers never see pay; HR and above do; the planner shows hours |
| **Other sites' entities, the family entities** | Entity access; the ledger holds them all |
| **A job's dollars without the cost view** | Permission is per person, not per tier |
| **Personal financial statements** | Owners and the PFS grant only, whatever the tier |
| **Posting to the ledger** | One door; Nexus reads |

| Course | What it adds |

## A Greens Scenario

Dana's Temecula Income Statement shows repairs up and the margin down two points. She drills to the lines (three gate emergencies), opens the property in Asset Management (the operator's warranty record: eleven years old), checks the preventive schedule (quarterly service, kept), and writes the variance comment with the ticket numbers and the capital case. The purchase request for a replacement operator comes to her as an approval; it is above her limit, so it goes up with her case attached. She never saw a payroll figure or another site's book, and did not need to.

## Common Mistakes

- **Looking for the Dashboard tabs or payroll** with one entity. They are not yours.
- **Treating Asset Management as Neil's module** and leaving the property's records to someone else. The property is yours to keep.
- **Approving a purchase** the company already owns.
- **Asking for a job's dollars** when the cost view is not yours.

## Key Points

- Accounting with your entity shows your site's statements and reports with the drill-down; the four numbers you own are the margin, the expense ratio, economic occupancy and delinquency.
- Asset Management is your property's record: its records and dates, its schedules, its critical items, its timeline.
- A construction job's money is seen by permission; purchase requests and reimbursements are approvals by the usual standard; the site never pays anyone.
- Pay, other entities, PFS and posting stay out of reach; the three courses go deeper.
