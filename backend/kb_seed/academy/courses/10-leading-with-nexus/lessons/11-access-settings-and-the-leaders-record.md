A new assistant manager at Murrieta cannot see the ticket desk. Her manager could hand her his sign-in and she would be working in a minute. Every reply she sent would then carry his name. The right fix takes an afternoon and leaves a record that is true.

## Tiers and Walls

Greens runs several companies in one Nexus, and a **company wall** keeps each one's people, departments, approvers and settings to itself. With the walls on, an **Administrator is walled**: they run their company and see no other. Only a **Super Administrator**, or a member of a group flagged as global, crosses the walls. Tickets are the one exception: an Administrator works every company's tickets, while the people pickers stay walled.

Under a person's name Nexus shows their job title, never the tier. The tier is a small mark: a filled shield for a Super Administrator, an outline shield for an Administrator, a people glyph for a Manager.

## Tiers and Grants Are Different Things

| | What it is | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier** | How much you may approve and how far your view reaches | An administrator; Administrator and above only by a Super Administrator |
| **Grant** | A module opened to you at a level: Viewer, Editor or Full | An Access Group, under Settings > People & Access |

Two managers at the same site can have different menus because one holds the Tickets grant and the other does not. When a team member says "I cannot see it," usually nothing is broken: ask your administrator to add them to the Access Group that carries it, or raise a ticket from Help Center. **Never share a sign-in.** It makes every punch, approval and signature under it someone else's.

## Settings

**Settings** is one screen: a rail of subjects (Companies, Locations, People & Access, Security, Branding & Email, Emails & Reminders, Tickets, Time & Pay, Time Off & Leave, Workforce Analytics, Shifts & Scheduling, Hiring & Onboarding, Items & Equipment) and a **Scope** picker for every company or one. Every setting has one scope: the organization, one company, or a default a company may override.

It holds what shapes a company or a module and changes rarely: departments and holidays, roles and Access Groups, checklists and probation length, the shift switches, pay rules, the ticket desks, the Workforce Analytics policy. An **administrator** changes them; a **leader asks**, with the reason. What managers maintain daily stays in the module: the planner, the desk's saved views, a property's schedules.

Who may open a group is written on the group itself. If you cannot see a group, you are not meant to change it.

## The Record You Leave

Everything a leader does in Nexus is recorded with their name and the time: every approval and its note, every schedule change and the reason past a warning, every ticket reply, every unlock, every record changed. Sensitive changes, such as access and tiers, are also kept in the audit log. The record is not surveillance. It is what lets a decision stand a year later when the person, an auditor or a lawyer asks why.

## Leading on a System That Remembers

- **Decide in Nexus, with the reason.**
- **The same rule for everyone.** The record makes inconsistency visible.
- **Never a shared sign-in, a punch for someone else, or a signature that is not yours.**
- **Ask for access; never go around a wall.**
- **Keep pay, the record and Ask HR out of your hands.** They are private by design.

## At Greens

The Murrieta manager raises a ticket from Help Center asking the administrator to add his new assistant manager to the desk's Access Group. It is done that afternoon, and her first ticket reply carries her own name. A week later she asks to see Temecula's team to help cover a shift. He explains the wall, and asks the Temecula manager to share a cover shift in the planner instead.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing a tier with a grant.** Most "I can't see it" questions are a missing grant.
- **A shared sign-in** to get someone working faster.
- **Asking an Administrator to look across a wall.**
- **Changing a setting without reading its scope.** A company override is not the default.

## Key Points

- Five tiers. With the walls on, an Administrator is walled; only a Super Administrator or a global group crosses, and Tickets are the one exception.
- A tier is reach; a grant opens a module at a level. Request access; never share a sign-in.
- Settings has one scope per setting; administrators change it, leaders ask.
- Everything a leader does is recorded with a name and a time, and that record protects the leader who decides in Nexus.
