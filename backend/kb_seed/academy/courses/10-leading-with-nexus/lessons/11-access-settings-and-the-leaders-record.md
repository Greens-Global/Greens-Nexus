Greens runs several companies in one Nexus, and a **company wall** keeps each one's people, departments, approvers and settings to itself. An **Administrator is always walled**: they run their company and see no other. Only a **Super Administrator** (the five set in 11/2026: Neil, Visesh, Charmi, Pranshu, Sagar), or a member of a group flagged as global, crosses the walls. There is no fourth-tier exception. The one carve-out is Tickets, where an Administrator sees every company's tickets; the pickers stay walled.

What this means for a leader: your reach is your company, your team, and the grants you hold; a question about another company's people goes to a Super Administrator, not around the wall.

## Tiers and Grants Are Different Things

| | What it is | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier** | How much you may approve and how far your view reaches | Pinned by an administrator; administrator and owner only by a Super Administrator |
| **Grant** | A module opened to you, at a level: Viewer, Editor, Full | An Access Group, set under Settings > People & Access |

Two Managers at the same site can have different menus because one holds the Tickets grant and the other does not. When a team member needs a module ("I cannot see the desk"), nothing is broken: ask your administrator to add them to the Access Group that carries it, or raise a ticket from Help Center for access. Never share a sign-in to get around it; a shared sign-in makes every punch, approval and signature under it someone else's.

**Settings** is one tab: a rail of subjects, a scope picker (every company or one), and under each subject its groups, all open. It holds what defines the shape of a company or a module and changes rarely: departments and the holiday calendar, roles and Access Groups, hiring checklists and probation length, the shift switches and windows, pay rules and time-off reasons, the ticket desks, the Workforce Analytics policy. An **administrator** changes them; a **leader asks**, with the reason, and the module keeps what its managers maintain daily (the planner, the desk's saved views, a property's schedules).

Who may open a group is one rule, written on the group itself: the tier, an Access Group grant, or the people grant for an HR route. If you cannot see a group, you are not meant to change it.

## The Record You Leave

Everything a leader does in Nexus is recorded with their name and the time: every approval and its reason, every schedule change (and the reason past a warning), every ticket reply, every unlock, every announcement, every record changed in People or Asset Management. Where a change is sensitive (an access change, a tier, a payroll step-up), the audit log keeps it too. The record is not surveillance; it is what lets a decision stand a year later when the person, the auditor or the lawyer asks why.

A leader who decides in Nexus is protected by the record. A leader who decides outside it is not, and neither is the person.

## Leading on a System That Remembers

- **Decide in Nexus, with the reason.** The hallway and the text leave nothing.
- **The same rule for everyone.** The system makes inconsistency visible.
- **Never a shared sign-in, never a punch for someone else, never a signature that is not yours.**
- **Ask for access; never go around a wall.** Someone else's company is someone else's.
- **Keep pay, the record and Ask HR out of your hands.** They are private by design.

## A Greens Scenario

A new assistant manager at Murrieta cannot see the ticket desk. Her manager does not share his sign-in; he raises a ticket from Help Center asking the administrator to add her to the Facilities Desk access group. It is done that afternoon, and her first ticket reply carries her own name. A week later she asks whether she can see Temecula's team to help cover a shift; he explains the wall, and asks the Temecula manager to share a cover shift in their own planner instead.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing a tier with a grant.** Most "I can't see it" questions are a missing grant.
- **A shared sign-in** to get someone working faster.
- **Asking an Administrator to look across a wall.** They cannot; a Super Administrator can.
- **Changing a setting's face without reading its scope.** A company override is not the default.

## Key Points

- Five tiers; an Administrator is always walled; only a Super Administrator or a global group crosses; Tickets is the one carve-out.
- A tier is reach and approval; a grant is a module at a level, set by an Access Group; request access, never share a sign-in.
- Settings is one tab with one scope per setting; administrators change it, leaders ask; the module keeps the daily work.
- Everything a leader does is recorded with a name and a reason; the record protects the leader who decides in Nexus.
