Monday's email shows Sam at 32 of 40 hours last week, with Thursday empty. It would be easy to read that as a missed day. It turns out he worked it, at another site, without punching. The scorecard gave Dana a fact; what she did with it is the skill.

## What the Scorecard Measures

Every week, hourly staff are scored on **punched hours against expected hours**. It is a comparison, not a judgment, and the same rule applies to everyone so the comparison is fair.

- **Excused:** a company holiday or approved time off; a Sick or PTO punch is leave that covers the day.
- **Scored:** only days before the person's own local today. A week is not judged before it happens.
- **One rule:** the same expected-start rule the planner and the No-Show bell use.

Each person gets a score and a band (On Track, Below Expected, Well Below or Absent), with counts of absent, short and late days. Click a row for the day-by-day breakdown: holidays, approved time off and missing punches are written on the day.

## Who Sees Whom

The Scorecard is a tab inside **Team** > **Workforce Analytics**, and it opens only with the **Workforce Scorecard** grant. A manager without the grant does not see it.

| Grant level | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Viewer** (the usual one for a manager) | Your direct reports |
| **Editor** | Your whole reporting line |
| **Full** | The company |

When the weekly email is turned on for your company, every grant holder with people in scope gets it each Monday. **Email Me This Report** sends it to you any time. The screen and the email come from the same calculation, so they always agree.

## Reading a Red Week

A gap is a fact. Read it with the schedule beside it:

| What you see | What it usually is | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A whole day missing against a shared shift | A no-show, a forgotten punch, or a shift that should have changed | Check that day's No-Show bell. Worked? A punch fix request. Wrong shift? Fix the planner. |
| Short days | Late arrivals or early departures | Team > Shifts shows On Time, Late and Left Early by day. A pattern is a conversation. |
| No Schedule | A part-timer with no shared shifts | Share their shifts, or accept they cannot be scored. |
| A gap on a day that should be excused | The excuse was never recorded | Approve the time off, or ask for the missing holiday. |

What a red week is not: pay. The scorecard measures attendance against expectation; the Time Sheet is the pay record. A conversation about a red week is about the schedule and habits, with the person's own days in front of you both.

## The Policy Behind It

What Workforce Analytics measures, how it is used and who sees it is each company's policy, set by an administrator in Settings > Workforce Analytics. Your team was shown it before their first punch. Read it before you act on a number, and send questions about it up rather than inventing your own rule.

## At Greens

Dana finds no No-Show bell for Sam's Thursday, and his shift that day was shared. His Beginning of Day message that morning reads "At Murrieta covering a shift." He worked, at another site, with no punch. She asks him for a punch fix request with the hours and the site, approves it, and the week turns green. Then she asks the Murrieta manager to share cover shifts in the planner next time.

## Common Mistakes

- **Reading the scorecard as pay.**
- **Judging a part-timer with No Schedule.** Share their shifts first.
- **Acting on a red week without the schedule beside it.**
- **Making your own rule** when the policy answers the question.

## Key Points

- Expected hours: the published shift, else the preset, else the standard day for full-time staff; No Schedule is never guessed.
- Holidays and approved time off excuse a day; only past days are scored.
- The Workforce Scorecard grant sets your scope; the Monday email and the screen agree.
- A red week is a fact to read with the schedule, never a pay figure.
