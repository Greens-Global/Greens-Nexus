## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Read the team's week in Team > Shifts and build and share a schedule in the Shift Planner.
- Explain what the rules warn about, why a reason is required to share past a warning, and who sees the reason.
- Use Fill Automatically, Copy Week and the holiday skip, and ask the team to confirm.
- Say how Late, No-Show, early punch and forgotten-break notices reach you and what to do with each.
- Know which trading switches are off by default and what you never see about pay.

## Team > Shifts

**Team** in the left menu opens **Shifts**: your group's week with you pinned at the top, a green dot on who is on shift right now, and under each person their days: shared shifts, usual hours, time off, and on past days how their punches matched the shift (On Time, Late, Left Early, No-Show, Excused). Employees see it too and may change nothing; the planner is yours.

The **Shift Planner** is where a week is built. Place a shift on a person and a day, pick its **shift type** (the type carries its hours and its grace minutes) and its zone, or place an **open shift** for anyone to take where the company allows it. Three tools do the heavy lifting:

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Fill Automatically** | Lays each person's usual hours onto the week, skipping their company holidays and approved time off, and says how many days it skipped |
| **Copy Week** | Lays a past week down again onto a date range, with the same skips |
| **Clear Week** | Empties the week to start over |

## Share, the Rules and the Reason

**Share** publishes the week. Before it does, Nexus reviews it against the **rules**: rest between shifts, days in a row, daily overtime for people on the California rule, weekly overtime on the payroll week, the week caps, and conflicts with approved time off or a holiday. The rules **warn, never block**. Sharing past a warning requires a **reason**, which is kept only on the warned shifts' change log, for the record, and is never shown to staff. Every schedule write is logged.

When you share, every person gets a bell and an email and a banner asking them to **Confirm**. Confirm means "I have seen it." The planner shows who has not confirmed, and a nudge follows those who do not; a person who confirms and then has a problem talks to you or uses a shift request.

## How the Day Reaches You

The schedule is what makes the rest of the team's time visible. From it Nexus knows when each person is expected, so:

| Notice | When | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Late** | A person's first punch is past their shift start plus grace | Nothing automatic; it shows on Team > Shifts and the attendance widget; a pattern is a conversation |
| **No-Show** | No punch by the no-show window after shift start | One rollup bell per team and day to you, the backup manager and the group's schedulers, resolved in place as people arrive; call the person |
| **Early punch** | A person clocks in well before their shift | Recorded, never blocked; one grouped bell per team and day; a habit is a conversation about who asked them to start early |
| **Still on Break?** | A break open past the window | The person is told first; you are told if it stays open twice as long; usually a forgotten End Break |
| **Long shift** | Clocked in past the long-shift mark | The person gets the banner; a forgotten punch out becomes a punch fix for you to decide |

None of these change pay by themselves. They are the facts the Time Sheet review (the next lesson) and the Workforce Scorecard read.

## Trading, Open Shifts and Calendars

Swaps, offers, releasing a shift to the open pool and cross-team open shifts are **off by default** and switched on per company under Settings > Shifts & Scheduling. When they are on, a request goes to the teammate first (Accept or Decline) and then to you; until you approve, the original shift stands. Shared shifts can appear in each person's own calendar app through their personal feed, and in Outlook when a company has turned that on.

## What You Never See

The planner shows hours. **Managers never see pay.** The scheduled labor line and the labor budget are visible to HR and above only; a manager gets neither the figure nor a flag. Build the week for coverage and hours; the cost is somebody else's screen.

Weather calls (cancelling a day for weather) apply only to people in departments with Weather Calls on, Construction by default; a storage team's week is not cancelled by weather.

## A Greens Scenario

Sunday evening Dana builds Temecula's week: Fill Automatically lays everyone's usual hours and skips Marco's approved Saturday off; she drags Sam onto Saturday to cover the open move-ins and the rules warn: six days in a row. She asks Priya instead, the warning clears, and she shares. By Monday morning seven of nine have confirmed; the planner names the two who have not, and the nudge goes out. On Tuesday the no-show rollup tells her Sam has not punched in at 7:45; she calls, he is at the gate with a dead phone, and she resolves the rollup when he punches at 7:52.

## Common Mistakes

- **Sharing past a rules warning without thinking.** The reason is for the record; the warning is for the person.
- **Not asking for confirmation**, then being surprised nobody saw the change.
- **Promising a shift by text.** It does not exist until it is in the planner.
- **Reading an early punch as a problem.** It is a fact; a pattern is a conversation.
- **Looking for labor cost in the planner.** It is not yours to see.

## Key Points

- Team > Shifts is the team's week; the Shift Planner builds it with shift types, zones, Fill Automatically and Copy Week, skipping holidays and time off.
- The rules warn, never block; sharing past a warning needs a reason kept on the change log; Share bells everyone to Confirm.
- Late, No-Show, early punch, Still on Break and long shift reach you as facts and rollups; none change pay by themselves.
- Trading is off by default per company; managers never see pay; weather calls are for Construction.
