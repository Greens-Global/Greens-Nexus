Priya's probation window ends next week and the reminder lands in Dana's bell. Priya has also asked Dana, quietly, why her last paycheck looked short. Dana can answer one of these. The other belongs to HR, and knowing which is which is most of this lesson.

## What a Manager Sees in People

**People** opens to a manager for **their reporting line** only. A person's profile shows Overview, Role, Assets, Work Mode and Work Logs. Hiring, the Organizational Chart, Checklists, Analytics, Exits and Performance as People tabs are HR's screens and stay hidden. Pay and bank details need a separate Pay & Benefits grant that managers do not hold, and People > Time shows hours, never wages.

| Who | Changes what |
|---|---|
| **The person**, in Workday | Personal email and phone, emergency contact, their own settings |
| **You**, the manager | The team's day: shifts, checklist steps, goals and 1:1s, a probation recommendation |
| **HR** | The record: name, title, department, pay, bank details, start date, documents, the final word on probation, the exit |

A team member asks for a change to the record through **Ask HR**, the confidential desk you cannot see into. That is deliberate: a pay question should not have to pass through the person's manager. Your answer to Priya is "Open Ask HR and HR will have it today."

## Hiring and the Signing Order

Hiring is HR's screen; what reaches you are the votes and screens on a candidate, as approvals. One rule governs every document a new hire signs, and it never changes:

> [!NOTE]
> **The company never signs before the employee.** An offer letter is signed by the candidate only. Hiring packets, separation packages and every other HR packet are signed by the person first and countersigned by the company after.

US employees' federal forms (W-4, I-9, direct deposit) are filled and signed in Nexus Sign from the packet. Their sensitive fields are masked to everyone but the person, and never appear in a bell, an email or on the record.

## Onboarding Checklists

A new hire's onboarding is a **checklist** by role: the steps, who owns each, the due days. HR runs the checklist; each step reaches its owner as a **subtask on My Tasks**. Yours might be "Walk the lot with Priya" or "Set up her Team Sign-In." Tick it in Tasks when it is done and the checklist moves. The Academy onboarding courses are one of those steps, assigned by rule from the start date.

## Probation

New employees start a **probation window** from their start date, and a reminder comes to you before it ends. Probation shows on the person's Overview. Your **Confirm**, **Extend** or **End** is a **recommendation**; HR finalizes. Confirm or Extend sends a letter through Nexus Sign that the employee alone signs. End opens the Offboard flow. Contractors, externals and guests are never tracked.

## Performance for Your Team

Your team's performance pages are under **Workday** > **Performance** > **My Team**. Each person's page holds **My Numbers** (worked out from what Nexus already records, such as on-time arrival and Time Sheets signed on time; never time off, sick days, pay or HR requests), **My Goals**, **My Reviews** and **My 1:1s**.

Your part: set goals with the person, keep 1:1 notes there and not in a notebook the next manager will never see, and read the numbers as a conversation, not a verdict. Only the person, the people they report to and HR see the page.

## Exits

An exit runs **one way**: HR's Offboard flow, whether HR starts it or a probation End does. Nexus then handles the rest in order: the separation package (the person signs first), access ending on the date, future shifts cleared from the planner, assigned equipment coming due in Item Management, an unsigned Time Sheet finalized by HR with a reason, and the record kept. Your part is to tell HR, collect the equipment with photos, and never improvise a version of it.

## At Greens

Dana opens Priya's Overview: probation ends 11/20, her checklist steps are done, the Academy courses passed, two 1:1s with notes on her Performance page. Dana recommends **Confirm**. HR finalizes, the confirmation letter reaches Priya in Nexus Sign, and she signs it. The paycheck question went to Ask HR the same morning; Dana never saw it, which is how it should be.

## Common Mistakes

- **Answering a pay question yourself** instead of pointing to Ask HR.
- **Any document the company signs first.**
- **Keeping 1:1 notes in a notebook.**
- **Ending someone's employment any way but the Offboard flow.**

## Key Points

- A manager sees their reporting line: the profile, hours never wages, no pay or bank details. The record itself is HR's; Ask HR is the private channel.
- The company never signs before the employee.
- Checklist steps reach you as tasks; your probation call is a recommendation HR finalizes.
- Performance for your team is Workday > Performance > My Team. An exit runs one way, through HR's Offboard flow.
