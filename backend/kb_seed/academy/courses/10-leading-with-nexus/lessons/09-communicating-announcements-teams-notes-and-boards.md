## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Publish an announcement that reaches the right people, with acknowledgment when it matters, and read who has not acknowledged.
- Reach a person or a team on Teams from anywhere in Nexus and say why Nexus has no chat of its own.
- Run a meeting on a Note or a Board that the team can see and the next manager can find.
- Use the Directory and the organizational chart as a leader.
- Route anything about pay or a person's record to Ask HR.

## Announcements

An **announcement** is the company's word to staff: a policy change, new gate hours, a holiday schedule, a safety notice. Only administrators post them; a leader asks for one through their administrator or drafts it for them. What an announcement can do:

- **Audience**: everyone, a company, a department, or a **work site**; a person with no allowed sites is not a site member, so a site announcement reaches the crew that works there.
- **Notify**: Bell, Bell and Teams, or Bell and Email. The bell is also the push to the phone, in every mode.
- **Acknowledge**: when the post needs a confirmation that it was read, and the list of who has not is yours to follow up, by name.
- **Scheduled** posts stay hidden from staff until they publish; images and files live with the post.

The Dashboard shows announcements only through the **Announcements widget**, which is not on Home by default. Tell every new team member to add it (the Nexus Fundamentals course does), and expect to follow up the thirteen who have not acknowledged.

## Everything Else Is on Teams

Greens talks on **Microsoft Teams**, and Nexus deliberately has **no chat of its own**, so there is one place for conversations. Wherever Nexus shows a person (the Directory, a ticket, an approval, your team list) it shows **Message on Teams** and **Call on Teams**, and they open Teams straight to that person. The team's Beginning and End of Day messages post to its Teams chat, so the day's plan and its summary are already where the conversation is.

What this means for a leader: the conversation happens on Teams; the **decision** happens in Nexus (the first lesson). "Approved, see Nexus" is a fine Teams message; "approved" alone is not an approval.


A team meeting, a 1:1 agenda, a handoff between shifts: each is a **Note**, shared with the people who were there, so it is findable later by them and by the next manager. **Make a Task** turns a line into a task where the team uses Tasks. A **Board** is a drawing: the lot layout for the Saturday move-ins, a sketch of the canopy damage marked up on the photo and attached back to the ticket, a plan drawn live with someone at another site. Boards live in Notes; there is no separate whiteboard module and no second drawing tool.

A note is private to its owner and the people it is shared with; typing a name in one notifies nobody. A note is not a report: a problem written in a note is a problem nobody else will read; it goes in a ticket or a form.

## The Directory and the Chart

**Team > Directory** is everyone at Greens: search, filters (My Team, Department Leads, Off Today), the **Organizational Chart** and **Reporting Line** views, and each person's card with their job title (never their tier as words; the small mark beside a name shows a Manager or an Administrator) and the Teams buttons. For a leader the chart is the quickest answer to "who do I ask," and the Off Today filter is who is out before you plan the day.

## The One Channel for Pay and the Record

Anything about a person's pay, their name, their title, their department, their bank details or a document HR keeps goes through **Ask HR**, the confidential desk the person opens from Workday. Not through you, not on Teams, not in a note. If a team member brings you a pay question, the right answer is "open Ask HR and HR will have it today," and the reason is theirs to keep private.

## A Greens Scenario

The California gate hours change on 12/01. Dana drafts the announcement for her administrator: every California site, Bell and Email, Acknowledge required, the tenant sign attached. It publishes Monday. By Wednesday 31 of 44 have acknowledged; she opens the list of 13, messages her two on Teams ("Please read and acknowledge the gate hours post before Friday") and asks the other site managers to do the same. Her Saturday move-in plan is a Board drawn over the lot photo, shared with Sam and Priya, and the notes from the Friday team meeting are a Note with two lines turned into tasks.

## Common Mistakes

- **An announcement without Acknowledge** for something that must be read.
- **Not following up the not-yet list** by name.
- **Looking for a chat in Nexus.** It is on Teams by design.
- **A decision made on Teams** and never recorded in Nexus.
- **A pay question answered by the manager.** It goes to Ask HR.

## Key Points

- Announcements reach everyone, a company, a department or a site, with Bell, Teams or Email, acknowledgment when it matters, and a list of who has not.
- Conversations are on Teams, from the buttons beside every person; decisions are in Nexus.
- Meetings and plans live in Notes and Boards, shared with the people in the room; a note is never a report.
- The Directory's chart answers who to ask; Ask HR is the one channel for pay and the record.
