## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Name each system on the site and say what it is for.
- Recognize the early signs of failure in each one.
- Give the right first response: what staff do, what nobody but a trade touches.
- Name the trade that fixes each system, and what to tell them.

| System | What fails | Early signs | First response | Who fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gate and access** | The operator motor, the chain or belt, the controller board, the keypad, the loop detectors, the safety photo eyes | A slow or noisy cycle, a gate that stops short, a keypad that needs two tries, a gate that reverses for no reason | Clear the track; check power; put it on manual with your manager if it will not close; never work on the operator | The gate and access control vendor |
| **Roll-up doors** | Springs lose tension, curtains bend, guides fill with debris, latches bend, bottom bars rust | A door that is heavy, drifts up or down, binds on one side, or will not latch | Never force it; report it; mark the unit if it cannot be secured | The door vendor; springs are theirs alone |
| **Roofs and gutters** | Seams, screws and penetrations leak; gutters clog; downspouts disconnect; panels lift in wind | A stain inside a unit, a drip in a hallway, water running down a wall after rain, a gutter overflowing | Photograph it; tell the tenant below; bucket and tarp if safe; nobody on a roof without the training and the gear | The roofing vendor |
| **Electrical and lighting** | Photocells, timers, ballasts and LED drivers fail; breakers trip; a panel overheats | A dark row, flickering, a breaker that trips again, a warm panel, a burning smell | Reset a breaker once; if it trips again, leave it off and call; never open a panel | The electrician |
| **HVAC (climate buildings)** | Filters clog; condensate lines block; compressors and fans fail; thermostats drift | A building warmer or damper than usual, water under a unit, a unit that runs without stopping | Check the thermostat reading against the setting; change a filter if that is a staff task on your site; log the temperature | The HVAC vendor |
| **Fire protection** | Extinguishers lose charge or go past their tag; sprinkler valves get closed; backflow devices fail tests; alarm panels show trouble | A tag past its date, a sprinkler gauge at zero, a panel beeping | Never silence a panel without calling; never close a sprinkler valve; keep lanes clear | The fire protection vendor and the fire inspector |
| **Plumbing and drainage** | Office restroom fixtures; water heaters; site drains, swales and catch basins silt up | A slow drain, standing water in an aisle after rain, a wet spot that never dries | Shut a fixture's valve; mark a flooded aisle; photograph the water line | The plumber; a grading or paving vendor for drainage |
| **Cameras and recording** | Cameras fog or go blind, cables corrode, the recorder fills or fails | A black tile on the monitor, a camera pointing at the sky, footage that is not there | Note the dead camera the day you see it; preserve footage after any incident | The security and camera vendor |
| **Fence and perimeter** | Chain-link cut or peeled, slats missing, block cracked, a gate hinge sagging | A gap, a lean, graffiti, a pile outside that makes a ladder | Photograph it; temporary fix only if it is safe; a cut fence is a security event | The fencing vendor |
| **Elevators** | Doors, leveling, the phone, the inspection certificate | A car that stops out of level, a door that reopens, a phone that is dead | Take it out of service if anyone could be hurt; post a sign | The elevator contractor, under its inspection regime |

## The Three Systems That Hurt Most

### The Gate

A storage site with a gate that will not open is closed; a site with a gate that will not close is unlocked. Both are emergencies at 9:00 PM. The gate is also the system with the most parts: the operator (the motor and gearbox), the drive (a chain, a belt or a rack), the controller, the keypads, the intercom, the loop detectors in the pavement, the photo eyes, and the software that ties the codes to the management system.

What a manager knows about the gate:

- **Where the manual release is** and how to put the gate on manual (open, closed, or chained), with the sign that goes up when it is.
- **The vendor's number and the service contract**, if there is one: what it covers, the response time, and whether after-hours is included.
- **The hold-open time and the loop positions**, because a long rig needs both right (the RV & Boat Storage course).
- **The fire department access**: the Knox box or the code, and that it is never shared.

> [!WARNING]
> A gate operator can crush a hand and a sliding gate can crush a person. Staff never work inside an operator housing, never adjust a chain under tension and never stand in a gate's path to "help it close."

### The Doors

A site has hundreds of roll-up doors, and each one is a small machine with a spring under tension. The Facility lesson in Self-Storage Fundamentals names the parts. From the property side:

- **Lubrication on a schedule** is the cheapest door work there is. A floating schedule, row by row, by the door vendor or trained staff.
- **A spring is the vendor's.** A broken or slipping spring is a wrist, an eye or worse. Report it; do not touch it.
- **Doors tell you about water.** A rusted bottom bar or a stained slab at the door means water is getting in; the fix is the drainage, not the door.
- **A door that cannot be secured** is a unit that cannot be rented and, if it is rented, a tenant who must be called today.

### The Roof

Roofs fail quietly and the damage is the tenant's goods. Two walks a year on the ground (after the first storms and before the rainy season) and a trained roof walk when the vendor is on site find most problems before they reach a unit: standing water, lifted panels, loose screws, a gutter full of leaves, a downspout that drains onto a slab instead of into a drain.

> [!NOTE]
> Nobody goes on a roof without fall protection and the training to use it. A ladder against a gutter and a phone camera is how managers get hurt. If you need to see the roof, ask the vendor to photograph it, or use a drone operator the company has approved.

## The Quiet Systems

Three more systems rarely make a ticket until they fail an inspection or a claim:

- **Lighting** is security, safety and sales. Dusk-to-dawn photocells drift, timers lose power and reset, and one dark aisle is where a break-in and a trip both happen. Walk the site once a month after dark.
- **Fire protection** is the law. Extinguisher tags, sprinkler tests, backflow tests, alarm panel service: each has an inspection cycle set by the fire authority, and each produces a record that goes in Asset Management (the Inspections, Permits and Compliance lesson).
- **Cameras** are a record, not a guard. They matter on the day something happened, so the day to find a dead camera is the day it died, on the morning check of the monitor.

## Climate-Controlled Buildings

A climate-controlled building is a promise written into the rate: the building's temperature is regulated. The HVAC system that keeps the promise needs:

- **A log.** A thermometer reading at the same spot at the same time each day, written down. When a tenant claims heat damaged a piano, the log is the answer.
- **Filters and condensate lines** on a floating schedule.
- **A service contract** with a spring check before the heat, so a compressor does not fail in July with the building full.
- **A fast ticket** when the building drifts. A climate building running warm for a week is a claim waiting to be filed.

The Product lesson in Self-Storage Fundamentals teaches staff never to promise a temperature; the property side is to keep the building inside whatever the company specified, and to be able to prove it.

## A Greens Scenario

Escondido's climate building feels warm on a Monday in August. The manager checks the thermostat: set to 78, reading 86. She writes the reading in the log, raises a ticket to Facilities with the readings and the hour, marks it High, and calls the HVAC vendor from the Accounting vendor record. The vendor finds a failed capacitor and replaces it that afternoon. She records the temperatures morning and evening for the rest of the week, and the log shows the building back in range by Monday night. A month later a tenant asks whether "the building was hot in August"; the log answers in one line.

## Common Mistakes

- **Working on the gate operator, a door spring or an electrical panel** because the vendor is slow. The vendor is slow; the injury is forever.
- **Resetting a breaker a second time.** Once is a reset; twice is a fault.
- **Ignoring a slow gate** until it is a stuck gate.
- **Going on a roof** without fall protection.
- **A climate building with no temperature log.**

## Key Points

- Ten systems, each with early signs, a first response and a trade: learn the table.
- The gate, the doors and the roof hurt most; know the manual release, the door vendor and the roof's two walks a year.
- Staff never touch an operator, a spring, a panel, a sprinkler valve or a roof.
- Lighting, fire protection and cameras are the quiet systems; walk after dark, keep the inspection records, check the monitor every morning.
- A climate building keeps a temperature log; the log is the answer to the claim.
