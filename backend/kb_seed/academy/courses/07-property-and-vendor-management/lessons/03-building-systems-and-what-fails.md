Monday in August, Escondido's climate building feels warm. The thermostat is set to 78 and reads 86. Do you change a setting, call someone, or write something down first? This lesson gives you each system's early signs, your first move, and the trade that fixes it.

## The Systems in One Table

| System | Early signs | Your first response | Who fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gate and access** | A slow or noisy cycle, a gate that stops short or reverses, a keypad that needs two tries | Clear the track, check power; if it will not close, put it on manual with your manager | Gate and access vendor |
| **Roll-up doors** | A door that is heavy, drifts, binds on one side or will not latch | Never force it; mark the unit if it cannot be secured | Door vendor; springs are theirs alone |
| **Roofs and gutters** | A stain inside a unit, a drip, a gutter overflowing | Photograph, tell the tenant below, bucket and tarp if safe | Roofing vendor |
| **Electrical and lighting** | A dark row, flicker, a breaker that trips again, a warm panel | Reset a breaker once; if it trips again, leave it off and call | Electrician |
| **HVAC** | A building warmer or damper than usual, water under a unit | Compare reading to setting, log the temperature | HVAC vendor |
| **Fire protection** | A tag past its date, a sprinkler gauge at zero, a panel beeping | Never silence a panel or close a valve; call | Fire protection vendor |
| **Plumbing and drainage** | A slow drain, standing water after rain | Shut a fixture's valve, mark a flooded aisle | Plumber; grading vendor for drainage |
| **Cameras** | A black tile on the monitor, footage missing | Note it the day you see it; preserve footage after any incident | Camera vendor |
| **Fence** | A gap, a lean, a pile outside that makes a ladder | Photograph; a cut fence is a security event | Fencing vendor |
| **Elevators** | Stops out of level, a door that reopens, a dead phone | Take it out of service and post a sign | Elevator contractor |

## The Three That Hurt Most

### The Gate

A gate that will not open closes the site; one that will not close leaves it unlocked. Know four things:

- **Where the manual release is**, and the sign that goes up when the gate is on manual.
- **The vendor's number and the service contract**: what it covers, the response time, whether after-hours is included.
- **The hold-open time and loop positions**, because a long rig needs both right.
- **The fire department access** (the Knox box or the code), never shared.

> [!WARNING]
> A gate operator can crush a hand and a sliding gate can crush a person. Staff never work inside an operator housing, never adjust a chain under tension and never stand in a gate's path to help it close.

### The Doors

Each roll-up door is a small machine with a spring under tension. Lubrication on an After Each Completion schedule is the cheapest door work there is. A broken or slipping spring is the vendor's: report it, do not touch it. A rusted bottom bar or stained slab means water is getting in, and the fix is the drainage, not the door.

### The Roof

Roofs fail quietly and the damage is the tenant's goods. Two ground walks a year, after the first storms and before the rains, find most problems.

> [!SAFETY]
> Nobody goes on a roof without fall protection and the training to use it. If you need to see the roof, ask the vendor to photograph it.

## The Quiet Systems

Walk the site after dark once a month: one dark aisle is where a break-in and a trip both happen. Check the camera monitor every morning, and keep the fire protection cycle (see the lesson Inspections, Permits and Compliance).

## Climate-Controlled Buildings

A climate building is a promise written into the rate. Keep a **temperature log** (a reading at the same spot and hour each day), service the HVAC before the heat, and raise a fast ticket when the building drifts. Staff never promise a temperature; the property's side is to keep it in range and prove it.

## At Greens

The manager logs 86 against a setting of 78, raises a High ticket with the readings and calls the approved HVAC vendor, who replaces a failed capacitor that afternoon. She logs readings all week. When a tenant later asks about August, the log answers.

## Common Mistakes

- **Working on the operator, a spring or a panel** because the vendor is slow.
- **Resetting a breaker a second time.** Once is a reset; twice is a fault.
- **Ignoring a slow gate** until it is a stuck gate.
- **A climate building with no log.**

## Key Points

- Each system has early signs, a first response and a trade; learn the table.
- The gate, the doors and the roof hurt most.
- Staff never touch an operator, a spring, a panel, a sprinkler valve or a roof.
- A climate building keeps a daily temperature log; the log answers the claim.
