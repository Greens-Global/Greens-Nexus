- Tell operating expense from capital, and say why it matters which is which.

- Answer the questions leadership asks at budget time with the record, not a guess.

Money spent on a property is one of two kinds, and the line between them is not just accounting:

- **Operating expense** keeps today's property running: the repairs, the preventive service, the utilities, the contracts, the supplies. It is budgeted for the year, spent month by month, and the site manager runs it.
- **Capital** replaces or adds something that lasts for years: a roof, a repave, a gate operator, a canopy row, a new building. It is planned over years, approved by leadership, and recorded as an asset that depreciates. At Greens, capital work runs as a **Construction job**.

Why it matters to you: a manager who fixes a failing roof with patch after patch is spending operating money to delay a capital decision that is coming anyway, and the patches add up to more than the delay was worth. The right move is to write the roof into the capital plan with the evidence, and to keep patching only until the plan funds it.

## The Maintenance Budget

A site's maintenance budget is built once a year from three things:

| Source | What it gives |
|---|---|
| **Last year's actuals** | What the property really cost, by system, from the books |
| **The preventive schedules** | Every scheduled visit, priced from the vendor's rate on file |
| **What you know is coming** | The gate that had three tickets; the striping due with the sealcoat; the HVAC compressor the vendor flagged |

A budget line is a system and a number: "Gate and access: four quarterly services at $640, plus one corrective visit, plus the keypad we expect to replace." Lines built that way can be defended. Lines built as last year plus five percent cannot.

Reading the budget through the year:

- **Variance** is the gap between the plan and the actual, by line. A line running over by March is a conversation in March, not December.
- **Emergency spend** is the number to watch most. Every emergency dollar is a preventive dollar nobody spent, and a rising emergency line means the preventive schedules are wrong or skipped.
- **Cost per unit** and **cost per space** let leadership compare sites of different sizes. At a Greens site the figures come from the books by the property's Project-Job code; nobody keys them into Nexus.

## Service Life and the Reserve

Every system on the site has a life, and the money to replace it is set aside over that life. That is the **reserve**: the capital saved in advance so a roof is not a crisis. The manager's part is to know the life of each system and where each one is in it.

| System | Rough life | What shortens it | What you watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Metal roof** | Decades | Standing water, foot traffic, loose fasteners, no gutter cleaning | Leaks, rust lines, lifted panels |
| **Asphalt** | A couple of decades with the cycle kept; far less without | Skipped crack seal and sealcoat, water in the base, jacks | Alligator cracking, potholes, ruts |
| **Gate operator** | Years, not decades; cycles count more than years | Dirt, a dry chain, a long rig hitting the gate, no service | Slow cycles, noise, resets |
| **Roll-up door springs** | Cycles; a busy unit's door wears faster | No lubrication, a bent curtain, a tenant lifting by a corner | Heavy doors, doors that drift |
| **HVAC** | A decade or two | Dirty filters, blocked condensate, running without stopping | Drift in the log, water, the vendor's spring report |
| **Lighting** | LED fixtures last years; photocells and drivers fail first | Heat, water in the fixture | Dark rows, flicker |
| **Canopies** | Decades, structurally; panels and posts by the hit | Rigs hitting posts and beams | The post and beam walk |
| **Cameras and recorders** | Years; the recorder first | Heat, dust, a full drive | Black tiles, missing footage |
| **Fence** | Decades for block; years for slats and chain-link | Hits, cuts, sun on the slats | Gaps, leans, missing slats |

When you can say "the Building C roof is in its third decade and had four leak tickets this year," the reserve has a reason to fund it. When you can only say "the roof is old," it waits.

Capital work at Greens is a **Construction job**: one record per piece of work, in the property's company, with its own task project for the schedule, its permits and inspections from day one, its subcontractors (Intacct vendors, with the same Compliance papers), its commitments (the subcontracts and purchase orders), and its money read from Intacct by the job's Project-Job code, visible only to the people with the grant to see cost. A target finish that moves needs a reason, and the reason is kept.

What the site manager does on a capital job:

- **Builds the case**: the ticket history, the photos, the vendor reports, the system's age, and the quotes.
- **Keeps the site running around the work**: which rows are closed, which tenants are told and when, where the vendor's trucks park, the fire lanes kept clear.
- **Escorts and inspects** exactly as on a repair, and keeps the daily log honest.
- **Receives the close-out**: the new warranty, the finaled permit, the new clear heights, written into Asset Management as new records, never over old ones.

The money is never the site's to key in: change orders and contract dollars are read from the books and shown to the people who may see them.

## What Leadership Asks at Budget Time

Be ready with the record, not a guess:

| The question | Where the answer is |
|---|---|
| What did the property cost this year, by system? | Accounting, by vendor and account, for the property |
| How much was emergency? | The tickets marked emergency, and their invoices |
| Which systems are at the end of their life? | The Records tab (warranties, inspections) and the Timeline |
| What preventive work is scheduled next year and what will it cost? | The schedules, priced from vendor rates |
| What should be capital next year, and why? | The case: tickets, photos, life, quotes |
| What did we buy that we should have borrowed or already had? | Item Management and the purchase requests |

## A Greens Scenario

Murrieta's manager is asked for next year's budget. He pulls the property's cost from Accounting by vendor: the gate vendor is the biggest line, and a third of it is after-hours. He reads the gate tickets in the Timeline: three emergencies, all in the summer, all the same operator. He prices four quarterly services at the vendor's rate, adds one corrective visit, and writes the operator itself into the capital case: its age from the warranty record, the three emergencies, and a replacement quote. The operating line goes down; the capital line goes up with a reason; leadership funds the operator for spring.

## Common Mistakes

- **Patching a capital problem with operating money** for years.
- **Last year plus five percent.** A budget with no systems behind it.
- **Not knowing a system's age.** The warranty record is where it is.
- **Keying money into Nexus.** It is read from the books; the site builds the case.
- **A capital job run as a big ticket** instead of a Construction job.

## Key Points

- Operating keeps today's property running; capital replaces what lasts. The site manager runs the first and builds the case for the second.
- A budget is built from actuals, the schedules priced from vendor rates, and what you know is coming; watch variance and the emergency line.
- Every system has a life; the reserve funds its replacement; your record of age, tickets and photos is what gets it funded.
- Capital work is a Construction job: a Project-Job code, subs with papers, permits and inspections from day one, money read from the books, close-out written to Asset Management.
- At budget time, answer from the record.
