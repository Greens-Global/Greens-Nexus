County Fire's inspector is at Escondido's gate at 9:00 AM with last year's list. If every tag, test report and permit is in Records with its date, the next hour is a walk. If they are in an email thread, it is a scramble, and an inspector who sees a scramble starts looking harder.

## Who Inspects a Storage Site

An **AHJ** (authority having jurisdiction) is any agency with the right to inspect the property and require things of it. A storage site answers to several:

| Authority | Looks for |
|---|---|
| **Fire authority** | Extinguishers tagged, sprinklers and alarms tested, fire lanes and hydrants clear, address visible, Knox box current, no hazardous storage |
| **Building and planning** (city or county) | Permits for structural, electrical, plumbing and mechanical work; signs and fences to code |
| **State and county programs** | Elevator certificate, backflow test, stormwater |
| **The insurer** | Roofs, electrical, fire protection, housekeeping; recommendations with deadlines |

Each one is a record in Records > **AHJ**: who they are, their contact, the last visit and the next.

Fire can close a site. A prepared manager has this year's vendor reports in Records, fire lanes painted and empty (no tenant trailer parked "for an hour"), hydrants clear of vehicles and weeds, exits lit and unblocked, and no open panels or extension cords in the office.

> [!WARNING]
> Never silence an alarm panel, close a sprinkler valve or move an extinguisher to make an inspection look better. A false record is worse than a failed inspection; an inspector who finds one stops trusting every record you have.

## Permits

A permit is the building authority's permission to do work and its right to inspect it. The details vary by city, the pattern does not:

| Usually needs a permit | Usually does not |
|---|---|
| Structural work: a canopy, a wall, a roof replacement | Like-for-like repair of a roof panel or a door |
| New circuits, panels, light poles | A fixture or photocell on an existing circuit |
| New plumbing lines, water heaters, backflow devices | A faucet or flush valve |
| A new HVAC unit | A filter, a capacitor, a thermostat |
| A new pylon sign; new gate operators; grading that changes drainage | A faded sign panel; crack seal, sealcoat, striping |

Three rules:

1. **The licensed vendor pulls the permit**, and its cost is in the quote. A vendor who suggests skipping it is telling you what else they skip.
2. **The permit and its final inspection go in Records > Permits**, with the permit number and the date it was **finaled** (passed its last inspection). An open permit is a problem at sale, at refinance and at a claim.
3. **A Construction job carries its own permits and inspections** from day one; the superintendent is belled the day before an inspection.

A record with a next date warns the property's Needs Attention card 90 days ahead, so nothing lapses because nobody remembered. Warranties have a lesson of their own (Warranty Claims).

## Preparing for an Inspection

The week before:

- [ ] Open Records and check every dated item in the inspector's area.
- [ ] Walk the site with last year's list; every item should still be right.
- [ ] Fire lanes, hydrants, exits, address, Knox box: clear, visible, current.
- [ ] No open panels, cords or daisy chains in the office.
- [ ] The vendor reports and permits ready to show.

The day of: walk with the inspector, write every item, ask for the written notice, and raise a ticket for each item with its deadline that afternoon. Close each with a photo and file the closed notice in Records.

## At Greens

Escondido's notice lists an extinguisher past its tag, a tenant's trailer in the fire lane, and weeds at a hydrant. The manager raises three tickets that afternoon. The vendor's yearly count was one short, so the schedule note now says 31 units. The tenant moves the trailer; the weeds are cut. All three close with photos within a week, and the notice and closure go in Records.

## Common Mistakes

- **A fire lane used as parking** "for an hour."
- **Work done without a permit** because the vendor said it was fine.
- **An open permit never finaled.**
- **Inspection reports in an email thread** instead of Records.

## Key Points

- Fire, building and planning, state and county programs, and the insurer inspect a storage site; each is an AHJ record.
- The fire cycle is monthly, quarterly and yearly; never fake a record.
- Structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, sign and drainage work usually needs a permit; the licensed vendor pulls it; the final goes in Records.
- A dated record warns Needs Attention 90 days ahead; prepare the week before and ticket every item the day of.
