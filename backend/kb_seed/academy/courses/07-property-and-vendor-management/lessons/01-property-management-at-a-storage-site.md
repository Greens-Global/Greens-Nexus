## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Explain why a storage site's building is its product, and what that makes a manager responsible for.
- Name the four jobs of property management: protect the asset, keep it rentable, control the cost, keep the record.
- Tell the owner's decisions from the manager's.
- Find a property, its records and its maintenance in Nexus Asset Management, and know that tickets are the work orders.

## The Building Is the Product

A restaurant sells food and a store sells goods. A storage site sells **space that is dry, secure and reachable**. There is no other product. A roll-up door that will not open, a gate that sticks, a roof that drips, a lot that floods, a light that is out over the RV row: each one is the product failing in a tenant's hands.

That is why property management is not a back-office job at Greens. The person who runs the site runs the building, and the building is what the tenant pays for. The Self-Storage Fundamentals course walks the site from the street in (the Facility lesson); this course is about keeping every one of those parts working, and about the outside companies who do the work you cannot.

## The Four Jobs

| Job | What it means | What it looks like on a Tuesday |
|---|---|---|
| **Protect the asset** | The buildings, the lot, the gate and the systems last as long as they were built to, or longer | A gutter cleaned before the first rain; a bent bollard replaced before the wall takes the next hit |
| **Keep it rentable** | Every unit and space is clean, dry, lit, secure and reachable, so it can be sold today | A vacant unit swept, its door checked and its facility lock on the same day it is vacated |
| **Control the cost** | The right fix, by the right person, at the right price, at the right time | Three quotes on a canopy repair; a service contract for the gate instead of three emergency calls a year |
| **Keep the record** | Everything that happened to the property is written down with a date and a photo | A ticket for every problem, a maintenance record for every fix, a warranty and a permit filed where the next manager can find them |

New managers tend to do the second job (keep it rentable) because tenants ask for it, and forget the first and fourth, because nobody asks for them until a roof fails or an insurer wants the inspection history. Do all four.

## The Owner's Decisions and the Manager's

Greens owns its sites, so the owner is the company, and the owner's decisions are made by Greens' leadership through the budget and the approvals. The split, in plain words:

| The owner decides | The manager decides |
|---|---|
| The capital plan: a new roof, a repave, a new gate operator, a new canopy row | How to keep today's roof, pavement, gate and canopies working until then |
| The annual budget and the spending limits at each level | What to spend inside those limits, and when to ask |
| Which vendors are approved for big work, and the contracts | Which approved vendor to call for this job, and whether the work was done right |
| Insurance, claims and legal matters | The first response, the photos, the incident report, the call to the manager above |
| Rates and the product (which units are climate-controlled, which spaces are covered) | Keeping every one of them in the condition the rate promises |

When in doubt, ask up. A manager who asks about a $3,000 repair is doing the job; a manager who signs for it without asking is not.

Nexus keeps the property in one place, **Asset Management**, with four tabs:

| Tab | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | The property's details, its company, its Egnyte folder, its critical items, the map point |
| **Maintenance** | Preventive maintenance schedules and the maintenance records they produce; the open tickets on the property |
| **Records** | Seven collections: warranties, inspections, plans, utilities, AHJ (the authorities having jurisdiction), vendors and permits |
| **Timeline** | Everything that happened, in order, with who did it |

Two rules that make the rest of this course work:

- **Tickets are the work orders.** There is no separate maintenance queue. A problem becomes a ticket (raised from Help Center, from a site QR tag with Report a Problem, or by a preventive schedule when it comes due), the ticket is worked, and closing it writes the maintenance record. The Nexus Fundamentals course taught you to raise one; this course teaches you to run one.
- **Vendors are Accounting's.** A vendor is an Intacct vendor record reached through Accounting, Vendors & Customers, and its insurance, license and W-9 live on its Compliance tab. Nexus has no second vendor list, and nobody keys money into Nexus: what a vendor was paid is read from the books.

> [!NOTE]
> Nothing in Asset Management is ever deleted by code. A live property moved to the Trash keeps its log; a record is only ever marked, never erased. Write freely; the record is the point.

## A Greens Scenario

On her first week running Temecula, Priya opens Asset Management and reads the property's Overview. Four open tickets, two of them about the same gate. Eleven preventive schedules, one overdue: the gate operator's quarterly service. Two records expiring within 90 days: the fire extinguisher inspection and the gate vendor's insurance certificate.

She does not fix the gate. She calls the approved gate vendor, asks them to bring a current insurance certificate, books the overdue service and the two open gate tickets into one visit, and writes it in the ticket. Then she opens the Records tab and sets a reminder on the extinguisher inspection. Four jobs, one morning.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating the building as someone else's problem.** At a storage site, the building is the product and the manager runs it.
- **Fixing the same thing three times** and never making it a preventive schedule.
- **Keeping the record in your head.** The next manager, the insurer and the auditor cannot read it there.
- **Deciding the owner's decisions**: signing for capital work, picking an unapproved vendor, settling a claim.

## Key Points

- A storage site sells dry, secure, reachable space; the building is the product.
- Four jobs: protect the asset, keep it rentable, control the cost, keep the record.
- The owner sets the capital plan, the budget, the approved vendors and the claims; the manager runs the site inside them and asks up.
- Asset Management holds the property (Overview, Maintenance, Records, Timeline); tickets are the work orders; vendors are Accounting's.
