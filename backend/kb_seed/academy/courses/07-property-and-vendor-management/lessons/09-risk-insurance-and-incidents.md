## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Name the property's main risks and the insurance that answers each one.
- Prepare the site for the weather your region brings, and walk it afterward.
- Handle an incident on the property in the right order and record it in Nexus.
- Know what to do when a vendor, a tenant or a storm damages the property.
- Know what a manager never says to a tenant, a vendor or an insurer.

A storage property carries four kinds of risk, and each has its own insurance. Knowing which is which keeps a manager from promising the wrong thing on the worst day:

| Risk | Example | Whose insurance |
|---|---|---|
| **Damage to the property** | A storm takes roof panels; a rig hits a canopy; a pipe floods the office | Greens' property policy, with a deductible |
| **Injury to a person on site** | A visitor trips in a dark hallway; a gate closes on a car | Greens' liability policy |
| **Loss of a tenant's goods** | Water in a unit; a break-in; heat damage | The tenant's own protection plan or policy; the lease limits Greens' liability (the Tenant Protection and Insurance lesson) |
| **A vendor's work** | A roofer's torch starts a fire; a crew member is hurt | The vendor's own liability and workers comp, with Greens as additional insured (the Vendors lesson) |

The manager's job is not to decide claims. It is to make sure each one lands on the right policy with the evidence it needs, and that is a record job: the photos, the tickets, the inspection history, the vendor's papers, the temperature log.

> [!NOTE]
> Greens' policies, deductibles, the carrier and the claims contact are held by leadership, not by the site. When a claim is possible, the manager above you tells the carrier. You gather the evidence.

## Before the Weather

Each Greens region has its own weather, and the property prepares for it on a schedule (the Maintenance Mindset lesson's preventive year):

| Weather | Where | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Winter rain** | Inland and coastal California | Gutters, drains, culverts and low spots cleared in October; the gravel graded | The roof and lot walk; stains at drive-up doors; standing water |
| **Wind** | Fairfield, the inland valleys | Loose panels fastened; signs and canopy panels checked; tenants reminded about covers and awnings | The canopy and fence walk; anything blown into a neighbor's rig photographed |
| **Heat** | Temecula, Murrieta, Escondido, Texas | HVAC serviced in spring; the climate log kept; pads under jacks | The climate log; softened asphalt where jacks sank |
| **Hail** | Texas | Covered rows and roofs walked before the season | Every roof and canopy walked after each storm; dents photographed before any repair |
| **Fire season** | Inland California | Weeds and fence lines cut; fuel and propane rules enforced; the site's evacuation plan known | Ash and debris cleared; air filters on climate buildings changed |

Weather calls for crews (a day cancelled for weather) are a Construction and field decision, not a storage one; a storage site stays open, and the manager's weather work is the preparation and the walk after.

## After a Storm

The walk after a storm is a claim's first page. In order:

1. **Safety**: downed lines, standing water near electrical, a leaning canopy, a gate off its track. Keep people and vehicles away; call the utility or 911 where needed.
2. **The whole site, photographed**, before anyone touches anything: every roof you can see from the ground, every canopy row, the fence, the lot's low spots, the drive-up doors' bases, the gate.
3. **Tenants' property**: a cover gone, a rig hit by a panel, water around a trailer. Photograph it, do not touch it, and call the tenant.
4. **Tickets**: one per damaged thing, with the photos, marked by priority. A roof leak over rented units is an emergency; a dented canopy panel is corrective.
5. **The manager above you**, the same day, with the photos. If the damage looks like a claim, they tell the carrier; the carrier may send an adjuster before repairs start.
6. **Temporary protection** only: tarps, cones, a closed row. Permanent repair waits for the adjuster unless the damage is spreading.

> [!WARNING]
> Never start permanent repairs on storm damage before the manager above you has said so. A roof replaced before the adjuster saw it is a roof the carrier may not pay for.

The RV & Boat Storage course taught the damage incident between tenants. From the property side the order is the same, and two things are added:

- **The property's own damage is a ticket as well as an incident.** The bent post, the broken gate arm, the cracked block: each gets its ticket with the incident photos attached, so the repair and the claim share one record.
- **Footage is preserved the same hour.** Tell your manager which camera and which time, so the recording is saved before the system overwrites it (the Access, Locks and Security lesson).

The incident form is a Nexus form (the Safety and Emergencies lesson): filled on the phone, with photos and your location stamped, reviewed by someone else. It works with no signal.

## Damage by a Vendor

A vendor who damages the property or a tenant's vehicle is why the certificate of insurance exists. The order:

1. Photograph the damage and the vendor's equipment where it stands.
2. Get the crew lead's name and the vendor's own incident report, if they make one.
3. Write it in the ticket the vendor was working, and raise a ticket for the repair.
4. Tell the manager above you the same day; they take it to the vendor's insurer through the certificate.
5. **Do not accept the vendor's offer to "just fix it."** A quiet fix leaves no claim and no record, and the next damage has no history.

## Damage by a Tenant

A tenant who backs into a post, hits a door or damages a fence is responsible for it, and the lease says so. The order is the incident order above, then: the ticket for the repair, the repair's invoice, and the manager above you decides how the cost is billed to the tenant's account or claimed on their insurance. The site does not negotiate it at the counter.

## What a Manager Never Says

| Never | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "We'll cover it." | "I am documenting everything and passing it to my manager." |
| "That was our fault." | "Here is what I saw and photographed." |
| "Your insurance won't need to know." | Nothing. Every incident is recorded. |
| "Just fix it and we're square," to a vendor | "Please send your report; my manager will be in touch." |
| "The building is climate-controlled, your things are safe." | "The building's temperature is regulated. Here is the protection plan." |

Admitting fault, promising payment, settling at the counter and keeping an incident off the record are the four things that turn a small claim into a large one. Say what you saw, show what you photographed, and pass it up.

## A Greens Scenario

A winter storm at Fairfield lifts roof panels on Building A and drops one onto a tenant's travel trailer. At 7:00 AM the manager keeps everyone out of the row, photographs the roof, the panel, the trailer and the ground from every side, and calls the tenant. He raises two tickets: an emergency for the roof (rain is still coming) and a corrective for the row's lighting, which the panel took out. He fills the incident form with the photos and his location, and calls the manager above him, who calls the carrier. The roofer tarps the roof that morning and waits for the adjuster before replacing panels. The tenant's trailer goes to the tenant's own insurer, with the site's photos and the ticket number. Nothing is promised at the counter.

## Common Mistakes

- **Permanent repairs before the adjuster.**
- **Letting a vendor quietly fix what it broke.**
- **Promising a tenant the property will cover a loss.**
- **Footage overwritten** because nobody asked for it the same hour.
- **An incident in a text** instead of the form.

## Key Points

- Four risks, four policies: the property, people on site, tenants' goods, vendors' work. The manager's job is the evidence, never the decision.
- Prepare for the region's weather on a schedule; after a storm, safety, photos, tenants, tickets, the manager above you, temporary protection only.
- An incident: safety, photograph, names, say little, report in the form the same day, tell your manager; preserve the footage the same hour.
- A vendor's damage goes to the vendor's insurer through the certificate; a tenant's damage is billed through the lease by the manager above you.
- Never admit fault, promise payment, settle at the counter or keep an incident off the record.
