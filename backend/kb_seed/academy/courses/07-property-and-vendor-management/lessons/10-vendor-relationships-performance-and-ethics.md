## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Set a vendor up to succeed: a scope, a contact, a response time, a rate on file.
- Score a vendor on four things and act on the score.
- Choose between a service contract and on-call work.
- Share vendors across sites and ask the vendor for what a multi-site customer deserves.
- Keep the trail in the ticket and the Teams or email thread, never in your phone.
- Say no to the gift, the side job and the cash deal, and know why.

## A Vendor Is a Relationship With a Record

The best vendors on a storage site are the ones who have been there for years: they know the gate, they know which door in row D always binds, they bring the right part the first time. That knowledge is worth more than the lowest quote, and it is built the same way every relationship is: clear asks, fair treatment, prompt answers and an honest record.

What a vendor needs from you:

| They need | Why |
|---|---|
| **A scope in writing** before every job | So they quote the job you mean and bring the right crew |
| **One contact** at the site, with a phone that is answered | A vendor who cannot reach anyone sits in the lot |
| **The site ready**: the row cleared, the gate opened, the escort there | Their hour on site is what you pay for |
| **A prompt answer** to a question or a quote | A quote that waits two weeks is a vendor who moves on |
| **An invoice matched and paid on time** by Accounting | Slow payers get slow service and higher quotes |
| **Honest feedback**, in the ticket, when the work was not right | The alternative is quietly replacing them, which teaches nobody |

## Response Times and Contracts

Every system has a response time it needs, and the vendor should know it before the emergency:

| System | Response the site needs |
|---|---|
| Gate stuck open or closed | The same day, after hours included |
| Roof leak over rented units | The same day for temporary protection; days for the repair |
| Climate building off temperature | The same or next day |
| A door that cannot be secured | The same day |
| A dark row, a dead camera, a bent post | Days |
| The preventive schedules | Booked weeks ahead, on the schedule |

There are two ways to buy those response times:

- **A service contract** (gate, HVAC, fire protection, elevator, pest, landscaping): a fixed price for the scheduled visits and a defined response for emergencies, often with a better rate. Right for the systems that have schedules and emergencies both.
- **On-call work** at the vendor's rate on file, for everything else. Right for the trades you need a few times a year.

A contract is read once a year against what it delivered: the visits that happened, the response times that were met, the emergencies it prevented. A contract that did none of those is renegotiated or replaced.

The ticket history scores a vendor for you. Twice a year, for each vendor the site uses more than a few times, pull their tickets from the property's Timeline and answer four questions: did they come when they said; was the work right the first time (how many tickets reopened); was the invoice the quote; were the papers current on every visit. Write the answers in a note on the vendor's record. A vendor who scores badly on one thing gets the feedback and a chance; one who scores badly on papers or price twice is replaced.

## Sharing Vendors Across Sites

Greens runs five California sites and Texas sites coming. A vendor who serves Temecula and Murrieta, half an hour apart, is a vendor who can be on either lot the same day and who gives a multi-site customer a multi-site rate. Before adding a vendor, ask the other sites who they use; before renewing a contract, ask whether it can cover two sites. The vendor records in Accounting are company-wide, so the answer is one search away.

Ask a multi-site vendor for what a multi-site customer deserves: one rate sheet, one contact, a quarterly review of the open tickets across the sites, and the preventive schedules booked as a route rather than one site at a time.

## The Trail

Every decision about a vendor lives in two places:

- **The ticket**, for the job: the scope, the quote, the approval, the visit, the inspection, the invoice number.
- **The thread**, for the conversation: the email or the Teams message where the vendor was asked and answered. Greens talks to vendors by email and to each other on Teams; nothing is agreed by text message or by phone alone. A call is followed by a line in the ticket: "Spoke with Precision 11/03 2:10 PM: sprocket in stock, Thursday 8:00 AM confirmed."

A vendor relationship that lives in one manager's phone leaves with that manager. One that lives in the tickets and the vendor record is the site's.

## Replacing a Vendor

Vendors are replaced for four reasons: papers that lapsed and were not renewed, price that drifted from the quote more than once, quality that produced callbacks, and response that failed in an emergency. The order:

1. **Feedback first**, in writing, with the tickets that show the problem.
2. **A second vendor qualified** (the Vendors lesson) before the first is dropped; never a gap in gate or fire coverage.
3. **The manager above you decides** for any vendor under contract or used by more than one site.
4. **The old vendor's record stays** in Accounting, with the note on why. Nothing is deleted; the next manager should know.

## Ethics

The money a site spends on vendors is where a storage company is most often cheated, by vendors and, sometimes, by its own people. The rules are few and absolute:

| Never | Because |
|---|---|
| **Accept a gift** beyond a cup of coffee: no gift cards, no tickets, no holiday boxes to your home | A gift is a quote you cannot question |
| **Take side work** from a vendor, or give a vendor side work at your home | The next quote is not at arm's length |
| **Pay a vendor from the site**: cash, your card, a tenant's payment redirected | Every payment goes through Accounting against a matched invoice |
| **Use a relative's or a friend's company** without telling the manager above you in writing | Undisclosed interest is a conflict, whatever the price |
| **Split a job** to stay under your approval limit | A limit avoided is a control broken |
| **Approve your own invoice**, or sign off work you did not inspect | The inspection is the control |

If a vendor offers any of these, say no, write it in the vendor's record and tell the manager above you. If a coworker does, tell the manager above you or use Ask HR. The record protects the honest.

## A Greens Scenario

Temecula's manager reviews the gate vendor at year end: four preventive visits on schedule, two emergencies answered the same day, one invoice over the quote (a part, explained in the ticket before the work), papers renewed on time after two reminders. Good score; the note goes on the vendor record. She also notices Murrieta uses a different gate vendor at a higher rate. She asks the manager above her whether one contract could cover both sites, and brings both vendors' tickets to the conversation.

## Common Mistakes

- **Replacing a vendor quietly** instead of giving written feedback.
- **A relationship that lives in your phone.**
- **A contract renewed without reading what it delivered.**
- **The holiday gift box** accepted because "everyone does."
- **Two sites paying two rates** to two vendors for the same trade.

## Key Points

- A vendor needs a written scope, one contact, a ready site, prompt answers, on-time payment and honest feedback.
- Buy response times with a service contract for the systems that have schedules and emergencies, on-call for the rest; read every contract once a year against what it delivered.
- Score a vendor twice a year on response, quality, price and papers, from the tickets.
- Share vendors across sites and ask for a multi-site rate, contact and route.
- The trail is the ticket and the thread, never your phone; replace a vendor with feedback first, a second vendor qualified, and the record kept.
- No gifts, no side work, no paying from the site, no undisclosed interest, no split jobs, no approving your own work.
