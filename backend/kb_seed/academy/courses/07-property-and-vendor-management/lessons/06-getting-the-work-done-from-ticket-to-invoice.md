Temecula's gate grinds on every cycle. The approved gate vendor can come Thursday and quotes $640 plus parts. Before that vendor touches the gate, and before Accounting pays a cent, the job needs a file that answers four questions: what was wrong, what was agreed, who approved it, and was it done. That file is the ticket.

## The Ticket Is the File

Everything attaches to one ticket. A good vendor ticket says **where** (property, row, unit or space, equipment), **what is wrong** in the trade's words, **since when**, **what has been tried**, photos or video, and an honest **priority**. When the job leaves the building, set the status to **Waiting for Vendor** and write the vendor's name and promised date in the ticket, so the desk and your manager can see where it stands without a call.

## The Scope

A **scope of work** says what is to be done, where, to what standard, by when, and what is excluded. The vendor quotes against it, you inspect against it, and the invoice is matched to it. A small scope fits in the ticket ("Replace the failed photocell on light pole 4, RV row B, same type; test at dusk"); a large one is attached.

## Quotes and Approvals

The thresholds are set by Greens. The shape is the same everywhere:

| Size of job | Quotes | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| Small, routine, approved vendor at a rate on file | The rate on file | The site manager, inside the site's limit |
| Medium | A written quote, often two | The manager above the site, in writing |
| Large or capital | Several written quotes against one scope | Leadership, through the budget |
| Emergency | None at the time; reviewed after | The site manager acts and tells the manager above the same day |

Your manager has the approval limits for each level; ask before you approve anything beyond a routine visit at a rate on file. Two rules never change: **the approval is in writing, on the ticket, before the vendor starts**, and **a job split into two tickets to stay under a limit is still one job.**

## Inspect, Photograph, Close

The job is done when you have checked the work, not when the vendor drives off.

1. **Walk the work with the scope in hand.** Was every line done, to the standard?
2. **Test it.** Cycle the gate ten times; open and close the door; check the light at dusk.
3. **Photograph the finished work** with the place in the frame and attach it. A preventive ticket will not resolve without one.
4. **Write what the vendor found.** "Chain has about a year left" is next year's schedule.
5. **Mark Resolved.** Closing the ticket writes the maintenance record.

If the work does not meet the scope, the ticket stays open, the vendor comes back, and the invoice waits.

## The Invoice: Three Things Match

Accounting pays an invoice only when three things agree:

| Match | At Greens it is |
|---|---|
| **What was ordered** | The ticket's approved scope and quote, or a purchase order |
| **What was received** | The inspection and finished-work photo on the ticket; a receiving note for goods |
| **What was billed** | The invoice, carrying the ticket number |

The site never pays a vendor: no cash, no card, no "we'll settle next visit." Your job is to make the match possible in one look and to answer Accounting's questions the same day. Why the match exists is in the lesson Payables, Cards and the Bank in Fundamentals of Accounting.

> [!TIP]
> Ask every vendor to put the ticket number on the invoice. "Gate repair, Temecula" is a phone call; "#4830 gate operator service and sprocket" is a match.

## The Emergency Path

An emergency skips the quotes, never the record:

1. **Safety first**: people away from the hazard, 911 if needed.
2. **Call the approved vendor** for the trade; if none answers, call the manager above you.
3. **Raise the ticket** from your phone, with photos, before or during the call.
4. **Tell the manager above you the same day.**
5. **The invoice is reviewed after**, against the ticket and the vendor's rate on file, and the emergency becomes a preventive schedule if it could have been one.

## At Greens

The Temecula ticket gets a scope, the $640 quote and the written approval. On Thursday the vendor replaces the sprocket; staff cycle the gate ten times, photograph it and mark it Resolved. The invoice arrives with "#4830" on it and is matched in one look.

## Common Mistakes

- **A vendor started on a text message.** No scope, no approval, no record.
- **A job split to stay under a limit.**
- **Resolved before inspected.** The photo comes first.
- **An invoice with no ticket number**, or one paid by the site.

## Key Points

- One path: ticket, scope, quotes, approval, work, inspect, close, invoice.
- The ticket is the file; Waiting for Vendor plus the vendor's name and date shows where the job is.
- The approval is in writing on the ticket before work starts; a split job is one job.
- Accounting pays when ordered, received and billed agree; the site never pays a vendor.
- An emergency skips the quotes, never the record.
