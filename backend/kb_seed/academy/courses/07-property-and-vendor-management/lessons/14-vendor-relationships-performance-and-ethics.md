A holiday gift box from the gate vendor arrives at your home address, with a card thanking you for "a great year together." It is a nice gesture from a vendor who does good work. It is also the start of a quote you can no longer question, and this lesson ends with why it goes back.

## What a Vendor Needs From You

The best vendors know the site: which door in row D binds, which part the gate needs. That knowledge is worth more than the lowest quote, and it is built the usual way: a scope in writing, one contact who answers, the site ready when they arrive, prompt answers, invoices matched so Accounting pays on time (slow payers get slow service), and honest feedback in the ticket when the work was not right.

## Response Times and Contracts

A stuck gate needs the same day, after hours included; a dark row can wait days. Buy those response times one of two ways:

- **A service contract** (gate, HVAC, fire protection, elevator, pest, landscaping): a fixed price for scheduled visits and a defined emergency response. Right for systems with both schedules and emergencies.
- **On-call work** at the vendor's rate on file, for trades needed a few times a year.

Read every contract once a year against what it delivered: the visits that happened, the response times met. A contract that delivered neither is renegotiated or replaced.

## A Four-Part Scorecard

Twice a year, for each vendor the site uses more than a few times, read their tickets in the property's Timeline and answer four questions:

| Score | The question | Where the answer is |
|---|---|---|
| **Response** | Did they come when they said? | The promised date in the ticket against the visit |
| **Quality** | Was it right the first time? | Callbacks and reopened tickets |
| **Price** | Was the invoice the quote? | Changes explained before, not after |
| **Papers** | Were they current every visit? | The Compliance tab |

Write the answers in a note on the vendor's record. A weak score on one thing gets written feedback and a chance; a weak score on papers or price twice means replacement.

## Sharing Vendors Across Sites

A vendor serving Temecula and Murrieta, half an hour apart, can reach either lot the same day. Before adding or renewing a vendor, ask the other sites who they use, and ask a multi-site vendor for one rate sheet, one contact and preventive visits booked as a route.

## The Trail

Every vendor decision lives in two places: the **ticket** for the job (scope, quote, approval, visit, inspection, invoice number) and the **email or Teams thread** for the conversation. Nothing is agreed by text or by phone alone; a call is followed by a line in the ticket ("Spoke with Precision 11/03 2:10 PM: sprocket in stock, Thursday 8:00 AM confirmed"). A relationship in one manager's phone leaves with that manager.

## Replacing a Vendor

1. **Feedback first**, in writing, with the tickets that show the problem.
2. **A second vendor qualified** before the first is dropped; never a gap in gate or fire coverage.
3. **The manager above you decides** for a vendor under contract or used by more than one site.
4. **The old record stays**, with a note on why. Nothing is deleted.

## Ethics

The rules are few and absolute:

| Never | Because |
|---|---|
| **Accept a gift** beyond a cup of coffee | A gift is a quote you cannot question |
| **Take side work** from a vendor, or give one work at your home | The next quote is not at arm's length |
| **Pay a vendor from the site** | Every payment goes through Accounting against a matched invoice |
| **Use a relative's or friend's company** without telling the manager above you in writing | Undisclosed interest is a conflict, whatever the price |
| **Split a job** to stay under your limit | A limit avoided is a control broken |
| **Approve your own invoice** or sign off work you did not inspect | The inspection is the control |

If a vendor offers any of these, say no, note it on the vendor's record and tell the manager above you. If a coworker does, tell the manager above you or use Ask HR.

## At Greens

The gift box goes back with a polite note, recorded on the vendor's record. At year end the same vendor scores well: preventive visits on time, two same-day emergencies, one overage explained before the work. Noticing Murrieta pays another gate vendor more, the manager asks whether one contract could cover both sites.

## Common Mistakes

- **Replacing a vendor quietly** instead of giving written feedback.
- **A relationship that lives in your phone.**
- **A contract renewed without reading what it delivered.**
- **The holiday gift** accepted because "everyone does."

## Key Points

- Give vendors a scope, one contact, a ready site, prompt answers and on-time payment.
- Service contracts for systems with schedules and emergencies; on-call for the rest.
- Score response, quality, price and papers twice a year from the tickets.
- The trail is the ticket and the thread; replace with feedback first and the record kept.
- No gifts, no side work, no paying from the site, no undisclosed interest, no split jobs.
