## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Explain the asphalt cycle (crack seal, sealcoat, striping, overlay, repave) and when each step is due.
- Keep a gravel lot drivable and drained.
- Walk the canopies, bollards and signs of a vehicle lot with a property manager's eye.
- Run landscaping, weeds and pest control as schedules, not as surprises.
- Know which outdoor work staff do and which goes to a vendor.

## The Lot Is a Building Lying Down

Everything the Facility lesson says about a building applies to the lot: it has a structure (the base under the pavement), a skin (the asphalt or the gravel), drainage, lighting, signs and a service life. At a vehicle site the lot IS most of the product: Greens rents more than a thousand vehicle spaces across its sites, most of them open (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026), and every one of them is a rectangle of pavement or gravel with a number painted on it.

Asphalt dies from two things: the sun, which dries it until it cracks, and water, which gets through the cracks and washes out the base so the surface breaks up. The cycle above is the defense, and the order matters:

| Step | What it is | When | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crack seal** | Hot rubberized filler in cracks wider than a pencil | Every year or two, in dry weather, before the rains | A paving vendor; small jobs by trained staff with the right kit |
| **Sealcoat** | A thin coat over the whole surface that blocks sun and water | Every few years, depending on sun and traffic; the lot is closed in sections while it cures | A paving vendor |
| **Striping** | Space lines, numbers, arrows, fire lane curbs | With every sealcoat, and whenever lines fade enough that a tenant could park wrong | A striping vendor, from the site's space map |
| **Patching** | Cutting out a failed area and repaving it | As potholes and alligator cracking appear | A paving vendor |
| **Overlay** | A new asphalt surface over the old one, when the base is sound | Once in a lot's life | A capital project (the Budgets and Capital Planning lesson) |
| **Repave** | Removing the surface and rebuilding the base | When the base has failed | A capital project |

Two things to know about a lot full of RVs:

- **Jacks and tongue jacks sink into hot asphalt.** The rule that jacks sit on pads (Greens Vehicle Storage Rules) is a pavement rule as much as a courtesy.
- **Striping must match the management system.** A space that is 12x45 in Cubby and painted 12x40 on the ground is a dispute on move-in day. Stripe from the system's space map and check it afterward with a wheel.

## Gravel Lots

Fairfield's lot is gravel and parts of Escondido and Temecula are (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Gravel is cheaper to build and drains well, and it needs its own routine:

- **Grading**, so the lot stays crowned and water runs to the edges instead of pooling in the aisles. A gravel lot that has rutted into a wheel path collects water in the ruts.
- **Fresh gravel** where the surface has worn to dirt, and in the stacking area at the gate, which takes the most traffic.
- **Dust control** in dry months where the site needs it.
- **Space markers** that survive grading: posts, painted curbs or stakes at the corners, with the number on something that does not get scraped off.
- **Weeds**, which grow through gravel faster than through asphalt.

> [!TIP]
> After the first heavy rain of the season, walk the gravel lot before the vehicles come back out. Where water stood is where the grading goes next.

## Drainage

The Facility lesson said water is the quiet enemy of a building. On the lot it is the loud one. A lot drains by its slope, its swales (the shallow ditches along the edges), its catch basins and drains, and the culverts that carry water under the aisles. All of them fill with silt, leaves and trash.

- **Clean catch basins and drains** before the rains and after any storm.
- **Check culverts** for blockage at both ends.
- **Watch the low corners** of drive-up rows and the end of every aisle: standing water the day after rain means the slope or the drain has failed.
- **Never let a tenant store anything over a drain** or in a swale.

A lot that floods is a lot of damaged vehicles, and a damaged vehicle is a claim with the site's drainage in the middle of it.

Temecula has Greens' only covered spaces (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026), and every covered row has the same three wear points:

| Part | What goes wrong | The walk |
|---|---|---|
| **Posts** | Hit by rigs, especially at the ends of rows and at corners; a bent post carries the roof | Walk every post; photograph any bend, scrape or lean; a bent post is a ticket today |
| **Beams and roof panels** | Hit by a tall rig's air conditioner; panels lift in wind; fasteners back out | Look up at every bay; a bent beam or a lifted panel is a vendor visit |
| **Clear height signs** | Faded, missing, or wrong after a repair changed the height | Every row carries its measured clear height on a readable sign at both ends; re-measure after any canopy work |

**Bollards** (crash posts) stand at the ends of canopy rows, at building corners and beside keypads. A bent bollard did its job; it is replaced, not straightened, because a straightened post has lost its strength. **Wheel stops** keep tires off a fence but not a rear overhang (the Our Spaces lesson in RV & Boat Storage); a broken or loose wheel stop is a trip hazard and a ticket.

**Signs** are the lot's rules in the open: hours and rules at the gate, space numbers, row letters, lane arrows, fire lane curbs, clear heights, speed. A faded arrow on an angled row is the most expensive sign on the lot, because the next rig comes in the wrong way.

## Landscaping, Weeds and Pests

- **Landscaping** is curb appeal and sight lines: the street frontage, the office approach, the gate. A vendor on a schedule, with the sight lines to the gate and the cameras written into the scope.
- **Weeds** in fence lines, gravel and asphalt cracks are a fire fuel in dry months and a sign of a neglected site. A schedule, not a complaint.
- **Pest control** is a contract: bait stations along buildings and fence lines, checked and logged on a schedule, with the log in Asset Management. Rodents go for RVs (the Lot Rules and Risks lesson) and for interior units; a tenant's claim about rodent damage is answered by the pest log.
- **Trash and dumping** at the dumpster and along the fence are a daily walk item and, when they repeat, a camera and a sign.

## A Greens Scenario

At Murrieta, a tenant backs a fifth wheel into the end post of the angled row because the lane arrow has faded and he came in from the wrong end. Nobody is hurt. The manager handles the incident (photos, insurance details, the report), then does the property work: a ticket for the bent post, a ticket for the lane arrows on every angled row, and a look at the striping schedule, which shows the lot was last striped three years ago. She asks for a striping quote with the sealcoat the budget already planned for spring.

## Common Mistakes

- **Skipping crack seal** because the lot "looks fine." The water is already under it.
- **Striping that does not match Cubby's space sizes.**
- **Straightening a bent bollard** instead of replacing it.
- **A clear height sign nobody re-measured** after a canopy repair.
- **Standing water left for a week** because it will dry.

## Key Points

- The lot is a building lying down: a base, a surface, drainage, lights, signs and a service life.
- Asphalt: crack seal yearly, sealcoat every few years with striping, patch as needed; overlay and repave are capital projects. Water under the surface is the killer.
- Gravel: grade for drainage, replace worn areas, keep markers that survive grading.
- Clean drains before the rains and after storms; standing water a day after rain is a ticket.
- Canopy posts, beams and clear height signs are walked; bollards are replaced, never straightened; lane arrows are the lot's most expensive sign.
- Landscaping, weeds and pests are schedules with logs.
