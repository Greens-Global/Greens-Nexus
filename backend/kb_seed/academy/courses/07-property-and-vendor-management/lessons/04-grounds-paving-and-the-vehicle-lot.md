At Murrieta a tenant backs a fifth wheel into the end post of the angled row. Nobody is hurt, but he came in from the wrong end because the lane arrow had faded to nothing. The post is a ticket today; the arrow is the lesson. On a vehicle site the lot is most of the product, and it wears out like a building lying down.

Sun cracks asphalt; water through the cracks washes out the base. The cycle above is the defense. Crack seal, sealcoat, striping and patching are operating work by a paving vendor. Overlay and repave are capital projects (see the lesson Budgets and Capital Planning).

Two things a lot full of RVs adds:

- **Jacks sink into hot asphalt.** The rule that jacks sit on pads protects the pavement as much as the neighbor.
- **Striping must match Cubby.** A space that is 12x45 in Cubby and painted 12x40 is a dispute on move-in day. Stripe from the space map and check it with a measuring wheel afterward.

## Gravel Lots

Fairfield's lot is gravel, as are parts of others. Gravel drains well and needs its own routine:

- **Grading**, so the lot stays crowned and water runs to the edges instead of pooling in ruts.
- **Fresh gravel** where the surface has worn to dirt, especially in the stacking area at the gate.
- **Space markers that survive grading**: posts or stakes with the number on something the grader will not scrape off.
- **Weeds**, which grow through gravel fast.

> [!TIP]
> After the first heavy rain of the season, walk the gravel before the vehicles come out. Where water stood is where the grading goes next.

## Drainage

On the lot, water is the loud enemy. Swales, catch basins, drains and culverts all fill with silt, leaves and trash. Clean them before the rains and after any storm, check culverts at both ends, and watch the low corners of drive-up rows. Standing water the day after rain means a slope or a drain has failed, and that is a ticket. Never let a tenant store anything over a drain or in a swale. A flooded lot is a lot of damaged vehicles with the site's drainage in the middle of the claim.

## Canopies, Bollards and Signs

Where a site has covered rows (Temecula does), every row has three wear points:

| Part | What goes wrong | The walk |
|---|---|---|
| **Posts** | Hit by rigs, most at row ends and corners; a bent post carries the roof | Walk every post; a bend, scrape or lean is a ticket today |
| **Beams and panels** | Hit by a tall rig's air conditioner; panels lift in wind | Look up at every bay; a bent beam is a vendor visit |
| **Clear height signs** | Faded, missing, or wrong after a repair | Measured height on both ends; re-measure after any canopy work |

**Bollards** stand at row ends, building corners and keypads. A bent bollard did its job; it is **replaced, not straightened**, because a straightened post has lost its strength. A loose wheel stop is a trip hazard and a ticket.

**Signs** are the lot's rules in the open: hours, space numbers, row letters, lane arrows, fire lane curbs, clear heights, speed. A faded arrow on an angled row is the most expensive sign on the lot, because the next rig comes in the wrong way.

## Landscaping, Weeds and Pests

- **Landscaping** is curb appeal and sight lines: a vendor on a schedule, with the sight lines to the gate and cameras written into the scope.
- **Weeds** in fence lines and cracks are fire fuel in dry months. A schedule, not a complaint.
- **Pest control** is a contract with bait stations checked and logged. A tenant's rodent claim is answered by the pest log, filed in Records.
- **Dumping** at the dumpster and fence is a walk item, and when it repeats, a camera and a sign.

## At Greens

After the Murrieta incident is reported, the manager does the property work: a ticket for the bent post, a ticket for the lane arrows on every angled row, and a look at the striping history, which shows the lot was last striped three years ago. She asks for a striping quote to go with the sealcoat the budget already planned for spring.

## Common Mistakes

- **Skipping crack seal** because the lot looks fine. The water is already under it.
- **Striping that does not match Cubby's space sizes.**
- **Straightening a bent bollard.**
- **A clear height sign nobody re-measured** after a canopy repair.
- **Standing water left for a week** because it will dry.

## Key Points

- Asphalt: crack seal, sealcoat with striping, patch; overlay and repave are capital. Water under the surface is the killer.
- Gravel: grade for drainage, replace worn areas, keep markers that survive grading.
- Clean drains before the rains and after storms; standing water a day later is a ticket.
- Walk canopy posts, beams and clear height signs; replace bollards, never straighten them.
- Landscaping, weeds and pests run on schedules with logs.
