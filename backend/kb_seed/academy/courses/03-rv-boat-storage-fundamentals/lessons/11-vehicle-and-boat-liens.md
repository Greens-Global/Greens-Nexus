## Start Here

Self-Storage Fundamentals taught the unit lien process: the lien, the notices, the sale (see its Delinquency and the Lien Process lesson). **A registered vehicle or vessel does not follow that path.** This lesson covers what is different. You will never run a vehicle lien yourself; your manager and Greens' advisers do. Your job is to know the shape, collect the right information at move-in, and never take a step on your own.

## Why Vehicles Are Different

A vehicle or vessel has a **title** held by a state agency. That changes everything:

- A buyer at an auction **cannot register** a vehicle unless the sale followed the state's vehicle or vessel title process.
- There is often a **lender** (the legal owner) whose lien usually **ranks ahead of the storage lien**. Selling the vehicle may pay the lender first and the facility little or nothing.
- The state process takes longer and requires notice to owners and lienholders on the state's record.

Industry legal writers make a practical point: with a defaulted vehicle, the goal is often to **get it off the lot quickly**, to stop the unpaid rent and the liability growing (Inside Self Storage, 2014).

## California

### The Rule: Bus. & Prof. Code 21702.5

California's Self-Service Storage Facility Act sends a vehicle or vessel that must be registered under the Vehicle Code to a different path from the unit sale steps:

- a **vehicle** lien is enforced under **Civil Code 3071** (the DMV lien sale process);
- a **vessel** lien is enforced under **Harbors and Navigation Code 503**;
- recorded lienholders on the title come first;
- the storage lien **may not include rent or other charges accruing more than 60 days** after it attaches.

Sources: Bus. & Prof. Code 21702.5 (Justia and FindLaw text).

### Vehicles: The DMV Lien Sale (Civil Code 3071)

- A vehicle stored at a self-service storage facility is sold under **Civil Code 3071, whatever its value**. The DMV's shorter process for vehicles worth $4,000 or less (Civil Code 3072) **cannot be used** for a self-storage lien (California DMV).
- The facility applies to the DMV for authorization to conduct a lien sale (form **REG 656**).
- The DMV notifies the registered owner, the legal owner (the lender) and other interested parties. An owner who **opposes** the sale stops it; the facility must then go to court.
- If the sale goes ahead, a **Notice of Pending Lien Sale (REG 280)** is sent and published before the sale, and the sale is certified on **REG 168**.

### Vessels: The Boaters Lien Law (Harbors and Navigation Code 503)

- For a vessel worth more than **$1,500**, the DMV must authorize the sale.
- The owner gets notice by certified mail before the sale, and the sale is advertised.
- An owner's opposition stops the sale unless the facility wins in court.
- The vessel must be open for public inspection before the sale, and there are no sealed bids.
- The owner may **redeem** the vessel within **10 days after** the sale (H&N 506.5).

Sources: Harbors and Navigation Code 503 and 506.5; California DMV BOAT forms. A boat's trailer may need its own handling; that is your manager's and Greens' advisers' call.

### The Tow Option (21702.5)

Instead of a lien sale, California lets the owner of the facility have a **vehicle, watercraft or trailer towed** once:

- rent is **60 days unpaid**, and
- the preliminary lien notice has been sent, and
- the tenant got at least **10 days' notice** by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing, naming the tow company, its address and its phone number.

Source: Bus. & Prof. Code 21702.5 (FindLaw text), as taught in Self-Storage Fundamentals.

### California Training Message

"A vehicle or boat is never sold through our normal unit auction. It goes to the manager and the vehicle lien process through the DMV, or the tow option, which takes longer and needs the owner and the lender notified." Belongings stored inside a vehicle (tools in a toy hauler, for example) are a separate question for your manager.

## Texas

### The Rule: Property Code 59.0445

Texas Property Code chapter 59 is the self-storage lien law. Section **59.0445** adds steps for **titled motor vehicles** (Transportation Code chapter 501) and **titled motorboats, vessels and outboard motors** (Parks and Wildlife Code chapter 31), including ones titled in another state:

- Within **30 days after taking possession**, the facility sends written notice to the **last known owner and every recorded lienholder**, found through the Texas DMV or TPWD.
- The notice states the charges and that the property may be sold if they are not paid before the **31st day**.
- Notice goes by verified mail; publication is allowed only in narrow cases (for example, when the state agency does not answer within 21 days, or the owner cannot be identified or has no address).
- The owner or a lienholder may **redeem** by paying before the 31st day.
- Then the sale is a **public sale** at the facility or a reasonably near public place (59.045). A motor vehicle worth only parts or scrap may instead be disposed of under Transportation Code chapter 683.
- Knowingly putting false information in the notice is a **Class B misdemeanor**.

Sources: Texas Property Code 59.0445 and 59.045 (Texas Public Law, Justia).

For boats, TPWD's form **PWD 309B** (Affidavit of Statutory Lien Foreclosure Sale by Self-Service Storage Facility) is used, and it requires a **written, signed rental agreement containing the lien**.

### The Texas Towing Option (SB 1181)

Since **09/01/2021** (SB 1181, Property Code 59.051 to 59.055), when a tenant is in default and the property is a **motor vehicle, trailer, semitrailer, motorboat, vessel or outboard motor**, the facility may have it towed to a licensed **vehicle storage facility (VSF)** instead of holding a lien sale, if all three are true:

1. Towing is **expressly authorized in the written rental agreement**.
2. The required lien notice was delivered and the tenant **did not pay by the 14th day** after delivery.
3. The vehicle storage facility **agrees in writing** to accept it.

Once it is towed, the **storage lien ends** (59.053), so the unpaid rent is not recovered from the vehicle, and the facility is not liable for damage during or after the tow (59.055). Best practice is to **photograph the vehicle's condition before the tow** (Inside Self Storage, 2022).

Sources: Texas Senate bill analysis of SB 1181; Inside Self Storage (2021 and 2022).

## California and Texas Side by Side

| | California | Texas |
|---|---|---|
| The law | Bus. & Prof. Code 21702.5 sends vehicles to Civil Code 3071 and vessels to Harbors and Navigation Code 503 | Property Code 59.0445 adds steps for titled vehicles, boats and outboard motors |
| Who runs the title side | California DMV (vehicles and vessels) | Texas DMV (vehicles, trailers) and TPWD (boats, outboard motors) |
| Who must be told | Registered owner, legal owner (lender) and interested parties, through the DMV process | Last known owner and every recorded lienholder, within 30 days after taking possession |
| Owner's way out | Pay; or oppose, which sends it to court; a vessel owner may redeem within 10 days after the sale | Pay before the 31st day |
| Charges in the lien | No rent or charges accruing more than 60 days after the lien attaches | Per the notice and chapter 59 |
| Tow option | After 60 days unpaid and the preliminary notice, with at least 10 days' mailed notice naming the tow company | Since 09/01/2021: the lease must authorize towing, no payment by the 14th day after notice, and a VSF agrees in writing; the lien ends when towed |
| What the lease must have | The normal written agreement | Written, signed lease with the lien; towing expressly authorized |

Sources as in each section above.

Greens' collections ladder is the same for a vehicle as for a unit until day 61. Then Cubby raises the task **"61st - Vehicle Lien Filing/Tow Notice"**, and a **Vehicle Lien Fee of $350.00** applies (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Day by day, the ladder is on the Greens Delinquency Timeline page; the fees are on Greens Fees and Charges and Greens Vehicle Storage Rules.

When that task comes up at your site:

- It goes to your manager. You do not start a DMV filing, a tow or a notice on your own.
- Pull together what the manager will need: the lease, the vehicle details, the VIN or HIN, the plate and plate state, the title holder and lienholders, the insurance, and the move-in photos.
- Photograph the vehicle's current condition.

Texas sites will get their own steps when they join Cubby. Until then, ask your Area Manager.

## Never Do This

- Never put a vehicle or boat in a unit auction.
- Never tow, boot, lock or move a vehicle yourself, or call a tow company on your own.
- Never promise a tenant that their vehicle will or will not be sold or towed by a date.
- Never tell a lender or anyone else about the account unless your manager tells you to and they are entitled to know.
- Never accept a "just take it, I don't want it" from a tenant as permission. Write it down and give it to your manager.
- Never take a lien step against someone who is or may be in the military without your manager. Self-Storage Fundamentals covers the military protections.

**Escondido.** A coworker suggests adding a defaulted car to the next unit auction "to save time." You explain that vehicles never go to the unit auction and tell your manager.

**A Texas site.** A tenant with a defaulted boat has a lease that does not mention towing. You know that, without that language, the Texas towing option is not available, and you leave the decision to your manager.

## Key Points

- A titled vehicle or boat never goes through the unit auction.
- California: 21702.5 sends vehicles to Civil Code 3071 (DMV lien sale) and vessels to Harbors and Navigation Code 503; the lien cannot include charges accruing more than 60 days after it attaches; a tow option exists after 60 days unpaid with 10 days' notice.
- Texas: 59.0445 requires notice to the owner and every lienholder within 30 days of taking possession, payable before the 31st day; since 09/01/2021 a defaulted vehicle or boat may be towed to a licensed VSF if the lease authorizes it, and the lien then ends.
- At Greens, day 61 raises "61st - Vehicle Lien Filing/Tow Notice" with a $350.00 Vehicle Lien Fee (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). It goes to your manager.
- Never tow, move or lock a vehicle yourself. Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.
