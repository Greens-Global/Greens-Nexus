## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Use the RV, boat, lot and lien words of vehicle storage correctly.
- Explain to a customer, in plain words, what each one means for their storage.
- Avoid the mistakes new staff make most with vehicles.

## How to Use This Lesson

Read it once now, then come back to it when a customer uses a word you do not know. Every word includes why it matters to you, not just what it means. Words that Self-Storage Fundamentals already defines (lien, overlock, street rate, occupancy and so on) are not repeated here.

## RV Words

| Word | What it means | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|---|
| **Class A** | A bus-shaped motorhome, about 25 to 45 ft | Needs the longest spaces; often tows a car |
| **Class B** | A camper van | Often fits a 25 ft space; high-roof vans are tall |
| **Class C** | A motorhome with a truck cab and a bunk over the cab | Usually 21 to 35 ft |
| **Super C** | A Class C on a heavy truck chassis | Can be 35 to 40 ft or more |
| **Diesel pusher** | A Class A with a diesel engine in the back | Usually a large, valuable coach |
| **Travel trailer** | An RV towed by a ball hitch | Ask whether the stated length includes the tongue |
| **Fifth wheel** | A trailer with a raised front that hitches inside a pickup bed | The tallest towable; the pin box overhang may not be in the brochure length |
| **Toy hauler** | A trailer or fifth wheel with a rear ramp and a garage for ATVs or motorcycles | No loading or unloading in an aisle |
| **Pop-up (tent trailer)** | A folding canvas camper | Short and low; how it is left while stored is on Greens Vehicle Storage Rules |
| **Truck camper** | A camper that slides into a pickup bed | Ask whether it stays on the truck |
| **Teardrop** | A tiny sleeping trailer | Short space |
| **Toad (or dinghy)** | A car towed behind a motorhome | A separate vehicle, usually a separate space |
| **Tow vehicle** | The truck or SUV pulling a trailer | Leaves with the owner or needs its own space |
| **Coupler** | The front of a trailer tongue that fits over the hitch ball | Measure length from here |
| **Kingpin (pin box)** | Where a fifth wheel hitches to the truck | Measure length from the front of it |
| **Tongue jack** | The jack holding up the front of a trailer | Sinks into hot pavement or wet gravel without a pad |
| **Stabilizer jacks and leveling jacks** | Legs that steady or level a parked RV | Pads under them; watch for sinking |
| **Slide-out (slide)** | A wall section that moves out to widen the RV | Common rule: kept in while stored; see Greens Vehicle Storage Rules |
| **Awning** | A roll-out shade on the side | Common rule: kept closed while stored; wind tears it |
| **Holding tanks** | Fresh, gray (sink and shower) and black (toilet) water tanks | Emptied only at an approved dump station |
| **Dump station** | A sewer inlet where RVs empty gray and black tanks | The most requested RV amenity |
| **Shore power** | Plugging an RV into an outside outlet (30 or 50 amp RV plugs; 20 amp household) | Only on approved power |
| **Battery tender (trickle charger)** | A small charger that keeps a battery topped up | Needs approved power |
| **House and chassis batteries** | The living-area batteries and the engine's starting battery | Both drain in storage; theft target |
| **LP (propane) tanks** | Fuel for stove, fridge and furnace | Common rule: valves closed while stored |
| **Generator (genset)** | Built-in or portable power | Common rule: not run on a storage lot |
| **Boondocking (dry camping)** | Camping with no hookups | Common rule: no camping on a storage lot |
| **Full-timer** | Someone who lives in an RV year-round | Common rule: no living on a storage lot; see Greens Vehicle Storage Rules |
| **Snowbird** | Someone who travels south or to the desert for winter | Seasonal move-outs |
| **Winterize** | Draining water lines and adding RV antifreeze before a freeze | Freeze damage if skipped |
| **GVWR** | Gross vehicle weight rating, the most a vehicle may weigh loaded | Heavy rigs sink jacks |
| **Tail swing** | How far the back of a long vehicle swings out in a turn | Why long rigs need wide aisles |
| **Hitch-to-bumper length** | The true storage length of a trailer | The number you need |

## Boat Words

| Word | What it means | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|---|
| **LOA (length overall)** | Hull length, bow to stern | Not the storage length |
| **Beam** | The hull's widest point | Pontoons run about 8 to 8.5 ft |
| **Bow, stern, port, starboard** | Front, back, left, right | Describing damage and position |
| **Outboard** | A motor hung on the back | Often the rearmost point |
| **Inboard** | An engine inside the hull | |
| **I/O (sterndrive)** | An engine inside with a drive leg outside | The drive leg adds length |
| **Lower unit** | The bottom of an outboard or sterndrive | Measure length to here |
| **Trim or tilt** | Raising the motor | Changes length and height |
| **Swim platform** | A step across the stern | Adds length beyond LOA |
| **Bimini top** | A fabric sun top on a frame | Fold it under a roof; it can push a pontoon over 13 ft |
| **Wake tower** | A tall frame on ski and wake boats | The height problem, even folded |
| **T-top or hardtop** | A fixed roof over a fishing boat's console | Does not fold |
| **Mast** | A sailboat's spar | Lies on the boat; can overhang bow or stern |
| **Keel or swing keel** | A sailboat's fin under the hull | Lifts the boat higher on the trailer |
| **Bilge and bilge plug** | The lowest inside part of the hull; its drain plug | Plug out in storage so rain drains |
| **Pontoon** | A flat-deck boat on tubes | Wide and can be tall |
| **PWC (personal watercraft)** | A jet ski | Often two on one trailer |
| **HIN (hull identification number)** | The boat's VIN, on the transom | Must match the registration |
| **Boat registration number** | The state registration number shown on the bow | California registers most boats through the DMV; confirm with your manager which numbers to record |
| **Swing (folding) tongue** | A trailer tongue that folds | Saves a few feet of length |
| **Bunks and rollers** | The boards or rollers the boat sits on | Part of the trailer |

## Lot Words

| Word | What it means | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|---|
| **Open (uncovered) space** | A marked space with no roof | Cheapest; any height |
| **Covered (canopy) space** | A roof on posts, open sides | Height and post limits; only at Temecula |
| **Enclosed space** | A garage with a roll-up door | Door limits; only at Valley Center |
| **Clear height** | The real measured height under a beam or door header | Post it; never guess |
| **Drive-in (back-in) space** | A space open at one end only; in and out the same way. Cubby's name for the ordinary space | The norm; back a trailer in, tongue to the aisle |
| **Pull-through space** | An aisle at each end; drive in one end, out the other | Premium; Murrieta and Temecula; the lane runs one way |
| **Angled space** | Set at an angle to a one-way aisle | An easy turn from the right direction, a jackknife from the wrong one |
| **End space, corner space** | The space against a fence at the end of a row; the space where two rows meet | One neighbor and easy to ask for; the hardest turn on the lot |
| **Tandem space** | A double-length space, two vehicles nose to tail for one tenant | The front one moves for the back one |
| **Rear overhang** | The body behind the rear wheels | Hangs past a wheel stop; counts in the length |
| **Drive aisle** | The lane between rows | Must stay clear |
| **Stacking lane** | Room outside the gate for a rig to wait | Keeps the street clear |
| **Bollard (crash post)** | A post that protects a corner or canopy end | Note hits on walks |
| **Drip pan** | A pan under an engine or generator | Catches leaks |

## Paperwork and Lien Words

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

| Word | What it means | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|---|
| **VIN** | Vehicle identification number | Must match on every document |
| **Registered owner** | The person the vehicle is registered to (the user) | Must match the tenant |
| **Legal owner** | On a California title, the lender, if there is a loan | Gets the lien notice |
| **Lienholder** | Anyone with a recorded lien on the title, usually a lender | Captured at move-in; ranks ahead of the storage lien |
| **Title** | The state record of ownership | Why vehicle liens go through the state |
| **DMV lien sale** | California's process for selling a vehicle to satisfy a lien (Civil Code 3071) | Never the unit auction |
| **Vessel lien sale** | California's process for boats (Harbors and Navigation Code 503) | Never the unit auction |
| **VSF (vehicle storage facility)** | A licensed Texas facility a defaulted vehicle may be towed to | The Texas towing option |
| **Care, custody and control** | Taking responsibility for someone's property by handling it | Why staff never move vehicles |
| **Operable vehicle** | A vehicle that runs and moves, with current tags | A common house rule |

## Mistakes New Staff Make

1. **Taking the customer's length at face value.** "A 30 ft trailer" is often 34 ft hitch to bumper. Ask.
2. **Using a boat's LOA.** Measure tongue tip to motor.
3. **Guessing a height.** Check the posted clear height. Never guess.
4. **Forgetting the boat trailer's registration.** Two registrations.
5. **Leaving Cubby's vehicle type blank.** It is blank on most Greens vehicle leases today (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
6. **Skipping the lienholder question.** You will need it if the account defaults.
7. **Moving a tenant's vehicle "just a few feet."** Never.
8. **Telling a tenant "you're covered."** Their own insurance covers their vehicle.
9. **Treating a vehicle like a unit in collections.** Vehicles never go to the unit auction.
10. **Promising an amenity the site does not have.** Check the Site Profile.

## Key Points

- Know the RV classes, boat types and their parts well enough to ask the right follow-up question.
- Storage length is hitch to bumper or tongue tip to motor, not the brochure or the LOA.
- Clear height is measured and posted, never guessed.
- Lienholders, titles and registrations are why vehicle liens follow the state's process.
- Staff never move a tenant's vehicle: care, custody and control.
