### Open Spaces

An open space is a painted or marked rectangle on a paved or gravel lot. It suits anything, because there is no roof to hit. It is the budget choice and the right choice for many trailers, work vehicles and boats that live under their own cover.

What to tell customers honestly: the sun, the rain and the wind reach the vehicle. A good fitted cover, tied down properly, helps a lot.

### Covered Spaces

A covered space is a canopy: a roof on posts, open on the sides. In a common design the posts stand about every **24 ft**, so each bay holds two 12 ft spaces, and the clearance under the roof is about **14 ft** (Inside Self Storage, 2015).

That gives two limits to learn:

- **Height.** The clear height is to the **lowest** point under the roof: the beam, not the roof sheet. A rig that is too tall tears off its air conditioner or antenna on the beam. That is a big damage claim and a building repair.
- **Posts.** Tenants back into posts. The posts at the end of a row and at corners take the most hits.

Covered space protects against sun, UV and hail. It does not stop blowing rain or dust, and low-angle sun still reaches the west side of a vehicle in the afternoon.

### Enclosed Spaces

An enclosed vehicle space is a garage with a roll-up door. A common design is **14 ft wide and 30 to 45 ft deep**, with a door **12 ft wide and 14 ft high**. Options include a motorized door and power (Inside Self Storage, 2015).

The door is the limit, and the real door is smaller than its label:

> [!WARNING]
> A door sold as 14 ft high may measure about **13 ft 8 in** once it is hung in its frame, and a raised rain lip at the bottom can cost another inch (Inside Self Storage, 2007). A ski boat with a tower or a pontoon with its bimini can be over 13 ft on its trailer. Always measure the actual door, and post the clear height at the door.

## Space Sizes

Space sizes, like unit sizes, are **width x depth (length) in feet**. Typical self-storage parking sizes run from **10x20** for a car up to **12x50** for a large Class A.

| Size range | Typical vehicles |
|---|---|
| 10x15 to 10x20 | Cars, small SUVs, motorcycles, jet ski trailers, pop-ups, small utility trailers |
| 10x25 to 11x30 | Long pickups, Class B vans, short Class C motorhomes, most bass boats and runabouts on trailers, cargo trailers |
| 11x30 to 12x35 | Most travel trailers, Class C motorhomes, pontoons and wake boats on trailers |
| 12x38 to 12x45 | Long travel trailers, most fifth wheels, shorter Class A motorhomes, toy haulers |
| 12x45 to 12x50 | The longest Class A motorhomes and fifth wheels |

Width matters too. A 10 ft wide space is tight for a rig with wide mirrors, an awning or a slide that sticks out. A 12 ft or wider space gives room to open doors and walk around.

> [!TIP]
> These ranges are a starting point. The vehicle's measured length (next lesson) decides the space, not its type.

## Surface: Gravel and Paved

- **Paved** lots are easier to drive on and keep clean, but the pavement softens in summer heat. Jacks and tongue jacks can sink into it unless they sit on pads.
- **Gravel** costs less to build, drains well and is common for big lots. It is dustier and can rut. Heavy trailer tongue jacks can sink in when it is wet.

At Greens, Fairfield is all gravel, Murrieta is all paved, and Escondido and Temecula have both (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

## The Shape of a Space: Drive-In, Pull-Through and Angled

Length and cover are two of the three things that decide a space. The third is its **shape**: how many ends are open, and at what angle it meets the aisle. The shape decides whether the tenant has to back up, which way the rig faces, and how hard the hook-up is next time. Cubby names each of Greens' spaces by it, which is why a pricing group reads "12' x 32' Parking - Gravel, Drive-In" (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

### Drive-In Spaces

A **drive-in** space is open at **one end only**. The other end is closed: a fence, a wall, a curb, a wheel stop, or the tail of the space in the next row, nose to nose. A vehicle goes in through the open end and comes out through the same end. At Greens this is the ordinary space, and the Site Profile pages call it **back-in (drive-in)**: the two names are one shape, named from the two ways of using it.

There are only two ways into a drive-in space, and the right one depends on what is being stored:

| Vehicle | Go in | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **A trailer** (travel trailer, fifth wheel, boat on a trailer, cargo trailer) | **Back it in**, so the tongue or the pin box faces the aisle | Next time, the tow vehicle backs up to the coupler in the aisle, hooks up and pulls straight out. A trailer nosed in has its coupler at the dead end: the owner has to hook up in the dark corner and then back the whole rig out, blind |
| **A motorhome, a van or a truck camper** | **Back it in**, so the windshield faces the aisle | Driving out forward is safer than backing out into an aisle full of people and open doors. Nosing in is quicker today and costs a blind reverse every time after |
| **A car, a pickup or a small utility trailer** | Either way | Short enough that it hardly matters, as long as it is inside the lines |

Three nuances of the closed end that catch new staff:

- **What is at the dead end decides the length that is really there.** A wheel stop marks where the tires stop, not where the vehicle ends. A motorhome's **rear overhang** (the body behind the rear wheels, often 8 to 12 ft on a Class A) hangs past the wheel stop. Fine against a painted line; a damage claim against a fence or a wall.
- **The tongue is in the aisle.** A backed-in trailer's tongue, jack and coupler sit at the open end. The measured length (next lesson) already includes them, and they must stay inside the space line, never in the aisle.
- **The neighbors.** A drive-in space between two others gives a mirror's width on each side. An **end space**, against a fence, has a neighbor on one side only, and tenants ask for them; a **corner space**, where two rows meet, is the hardest turn on the lot and the one a 40 ft rig should not have.

### Pull-Through Spaces

A **pull-through** space is open at **both ends**, with an aisle at each. The rig drives in one end and, next time, drives out the other. Nobody backs up more than a truck length, ever:

1. Arriving, the tow vehicle pulls the trailer straight in from the entry aisle and unhooks. The trailer is left facing the exit.
2. Leaving, the tow vehicle enters from the entry aisle behind the trailer, backs the few feet to the coupler, hooks up and drives out through the exit aisle.
3. A motorhome simply drives in, and drives out the other end.

That is why pull-throughs are the premium product: for the owner of a big rig, or anyone who hates backing, it is the difference between a five-minute visit and a stressful one. It is also why there are few of them. A pull-through uses **two aisle frontages**, so a lot gives up spaces to make one, and the rent reflects it: Murrieta's pull-throughs run $249 to $279 a month, above its typical $184 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

The nuances:

- **The lane has a direction.** A pull-through row usually runs one way, so every rig faces the same way and nobody meets nose to nose in a space. Tell the tenant which end is in and which is out, and walk it with them the first time (the direction at Murrieta is on its Site Profile, pending).
- **Both ends must stay clear.** A pull-through that someone has used as a drive-in, with the tail in the far aisle, blocks a lane.
- **It is still a length.** A pull-through has the same measured-length rule as any space: hitch to bumper, plus the margin, inside the lines at both ends.
- **The trailer faces the exit.** The owner leaves the tongue toward the entry aisle, where they will hook up, and the back of the trailer toward the exit. If a tenant parks it the other way, their next hook-up is at the far end.

At Greens, Murrieta has 36 pull-through spaces and Temecula 28 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026); Murrieta's 12x50 spaces are all pull-through (Site Profile: Murrieta).

### Angled Spaces

An **angled** space is set at an angle to the aisle, usually 45 or 60 degrees, on a **one-way** aisle. Turning into an angled space from the right direction is a gentle curve instead of a 90-degree swing, so a long rig needs a narrower aisle and a less skilled driver gets in without a spotter. Some angled rows are back-in, some are pull-in; the row's arrows say which.

The one rule: **come from the right direction.** Enter an angled row against its arrow and every space is a jackknife turn. Faded arrows on an angled row are a maintenance item worth a same-day report.

### Other Shapes You Will Hear About

- **Tandem (double-length) spaces** hold two vehicles nose to tail for one tenant, such as a boat and its tow vehicle, or a trailer and a car. The one in front has to move for the one behind.
- **Covered row spaces** are drive-in spaces with **posts** at the ends of each bay (the covered section above): the post, not the line, is what gets hit.
- **Enclosed spaces** are drive-in by definition: a garage has one door. Backing a trailer into a garage, between the door jambs, is the hardest parking on the site, which is why the Measuring lesson adds a margin and why a motorized door and a spotter earn their keep.

### Who Gets Which Shape

| The customer | Offer first |
|---|---|
| A big motorhome, or anyone who says backing is hard | A pull-through, if the site has one and the length fits; else the longest drive-in on an easy aisle |
| A trailer the owner tows often | A drive-in they back into, tongue to the aisle, near the gate |
| A boat on a trailer that goes out at dawn | A drive-in with a straight run to the gate; a pull-through if they want it |
| A long rig and a nervous driver, no pull-through open | An end space on a wide aisle, and the pull-through waitlist (the Waitlists and Upgrades lesson) |
| A small car, a jet ski trailer, a pop-up | Any drive-in that fits; angled is fine |

> [!TIP]
> Ask every vehicle customer one question: "How do you usually park it?" The answer tells you whether to lead with a pull-through, a wide drive-in or a hand on the spotter's whistle, before you ever quote a price.

## Aisles, Gates and Turning Room

A 40 ft rig needs room to swing. That is why vehicle lots need wide drive aisles and why sites are laid out the way they are. Design articles recommend aisles of roughly 35 to 50 ft or more, depending on angle and building type; sources differ (Inside Self Storage, 2009 to 2015).

Things a good vehicle lot has (Inside Self Storage, 2009 and 2015):

- **Stacking room at the gate**, so a long rig waiting for the gate does not block the street.
- **Gate sensors or timers** that hold the gate open long enough for a 60 ft combination to clear.
- **Bollards (crash posts)** at building corners and canopy ends.
- **Clear painted lines and space numbers**, with the same numbers in the management software.

Staff do not design lots, but you notice when something stops working: a gate that closes too early, a faded line, a missing space number, a bent post. Report it.

## Greens Scenarios

**Temecula.** A customer wants covered space for a fifth wheel. Before you look at Cubby, you ask the height to the top of the air conditioner and check the clear height posted for the covered rows.

**Valley Center.** A collector wants an enclosed space for a classic car. Both 12x38 garages are leased (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). You add them to the waitlist and explain the open options at Escondido.

**Murrieta.** An older customer struggles to back his 40 ft trailer. You show him the 12x50 pull-through spaces and quote the rate from Cubby.

**Fairfield.** A customer asks why the lot is gravel. You explain that gravel drains well and keeps prices down, and suggest a pad under the tongue jack when the ground is wet.

**A Texas site.** In spring, a caller wants covered space because of hail. You explain that a canopy blocks hail and sun but not blowing rain, quote the covered rate, and check the clear height against their rig.

## Key Points

- Open: cheapest, any height, no protection. Covered: roof on posts, height and post limits. Enclosed: best protection, door limits.
- A covered space's clear height is to the lowest beam; an enclosed space's is the measured door opening.
- A "14 ft" door may really be about 13 ft 8 in. Measure and post the clear height.
- Sizes are width x depth; typical parking runs 10x20 to 12x50.
- A drive-in (back-in) space is open at one end; a pull-through has an aisle at each end and nobody backs more than a truck length; an angled space needs the right direction. Back a trailer in, tongue to the aisle.
- Greens is mostly open space; covered is only at Temecula (27) and enclosed vehicle space only at Valley Center (2) (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Learn your own Site Profile, and know where to send a caller when your lot is full.
