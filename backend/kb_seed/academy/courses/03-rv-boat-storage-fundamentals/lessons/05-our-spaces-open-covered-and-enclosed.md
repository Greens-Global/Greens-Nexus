## The Three Products

| Product | What it is | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open (uncovered)** | A marked space on a paved or gravel lot | The lowest price; any height fits | No protection from sun, rain or hail; the least protected from theft |
| **Covered (canopy)** | A roof only, with open sides | Protection from sun, UV and hail; a middle price | A height limit; posts to hit; blowing rain and dust still reach the vehicle |
| **Enclosed (garage)** | A building unit with a roll-up door | The best security and protection; the highest rent | Door width and height limits; the hardest to back into |

Source for the layout and size figures in this lesson: Inside Self Storage (Mako Steel, 10/20/2015), an industry design article. These are industry norms; Greens' real numbers are on the Site Profile pages.

### Open Spaces

An open space is a painted or marked rectangle on a paved or gravel lot. It suits anything, because there is no roof to hit. It is the budget choice and the right choice for many trailers, work vehicles and boats that live under their own cover.

What to tell customers honestly: the sun, the rain and the wind reach the vehicle. A good fitted cover, tied down properly, helps a lot.

### Covered Spaces

A covered space is a canopy: a roof on posts, open on the sides. In a common design the posts stand about every **24 ft**, so each bay holds two 12 ft spaces, and the clearance under the roof is about **14 ft** (Inside Self Storage, 2015).

That gives two limits to learn:

- **Height.** The clear height is to the **lowest** point under the roof: the beam, not the roof sheet. A rig that is too tall tears off its air conditioner or antenna on the beam. That is a big damage claim and a building repair.
- **Posts.** Tenants back into posts. The posts at the end of a row and at corners take the most hits.

Covered space protects against sun, UV and hail. It does not stop blowing rain or dust, and low-angle sun still reaches the west side of a vehicle in the afternoon.

### Enclosed Spaces

An enclosed vehicle space is a garage with a roll-up door. A common design is **14 ft wide and 30 to 45 ft deep**, with a door **12 ft wide and 14 ft high**. Options include a motorized door and power (Inside Self Storage, 2015).

The door is the limit, and the real door is smaller than its label:

> [!WARNING]
> A door sold as 14 ft high may measure about **13 ft 8 in** once it is hung in its frame, and a raised rain lip at the bottom can cost another inch (Inside Self Storage, 2007). A ski boat with a tower or a pontoon with its bimini can be over 13 ft on its trailer. Always measure the actual door, and post the clear height at the door.

## Space Sizes

Space sizes, like unit sizes, are **width x depth (length) in feet**. Typical self-storage parking sizes run from **10x20** for a car up to **12x50** for a large Class A.

| Size range | Typical vehicles |
|---|---|
| 10x15 to 10x20 | Cars, small SUVs, motorcycles, jet ski trailers, pop-ups, small utility trailers |
| 10x25 to 11x30 | Long pickups, Class B vans, short Class C motorhomes, most bass boats and runabouts on trailers, cargo trailers |
| 11x30 to 12x35 | Most travel trailers, Class C motorhomes, pontoons and wake boats on trailers |
| 12x38 to 12x45 | Long travel trailers, most fifth wheels, shorter Class A motorhomes, toy haulers |
| 12x45 to 12x50 | The longest Class A motorhomes and fifth wheels |

Width matters too. A 10 ft wide space is tight for a rig with wide mirrors, an awning or a slide that sticks out. A 12 ft or wider space gives room to open doors and walk around.

> [!TIP]
> These ranges are a starting point. The vehicle's measured length (next lesson) decides the space, not its type.

## Surface: Gravel and Paved

- **Paved** lots are easier to drive on and keep clean, but the pavement softens in summer heat. Jacks and tongue jacks can sink into it unless they sit on pads.
- **Gravel** costs less to build, drains well and is common for big lots. It is dustier and can rut. Heavy trailer tongue jacks can sink in when it is wet.

At Greens, Fairfield is all gravel, Murrieta is all paved, and Escondido and Temecula have both (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

## Back-In, Pull-Through and Angled Spaces

- **Back-in** spaces are the norm. The driver backs in so they can drive straight out.
- **Pull-through** spaces let a rig drive in one end and out the other with no backing at all. They are the easiest and are sold at a premium.
- **Angled** spaces make backing easier and allow narrower aisles.

At Greens, Murrieta has 36 pull-through spaces and Temecula has 28 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

## Aisles, Gates and Turning Room

A 40 ft rig needs room to swing. That is why vehicle lots need wide drive aisles and why sites are laid out the way they are. Design articles recommend aisles of roughly 35 to 50 ft or more, depending on angle and building type; sources differ (Inside Self Storage, 2009 to 2015).

Things a good vehicle lot has (Inside Self Storage, 2009 and 2015):

- **Stacking room at the gate**, so a long rig waiting for the gate does not block the street.
- **Gate sensors or timers** that hold the gate open long enough for a 60 ft combination to clear.
- **Bollards (crash posts)** at building corners and canopy ends.
- **Clear painted lines and space numbers**, with the same numbers in the management software.

Staff do not design lots, but you notice when something stops working: a gate that closes too early, a faded line, a missing space number, a bent post. Report it.

## Greens Scenarios

**Temecula.** A customer wants covered space for a fifth wheel. Before you look at Cubby, you ask the height to the top of the air conditioner and check the clear height posted for the covered rows.

**Valley Center.** A collector wants an enclosed space for a classic car. Both 12x38 garages are leased (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). You add them to the waitlist and explain the open options at Escondido.

**Murrieta.** An older customer struggles to back his 40 ft trailer. You show him the 12x50 pull-through spaces and quote the rate from Cubby.

**Fairfield.** A customer asks why the lot is gravel. You explain that gravel drains well and keeps prices down, and suggest a pad under the tongue jack when the ground is wet.

**A Texas site.** In spring, a caller wants covered space because of hail. You explain that a canopy blocks hail and sun but not blowing rain, quote the covered rate, and check the clear height against their rig.

## Key Points

- Open: cheapest, any height, no protection. Covered: roof on posts, height and post limits. Enclosed: best protection, door limits.
- A covered space's clear height is to the lowest beam; an enclosed space's is the measured door opening.
- A "14 ft" door may really be about 13 ft 8 in. Measure and post the clear height.
- Sizes are width x depth; typical parking runs 10x20 to 12x50.
- Greens is mostly open space; covered is only at Temecula (27) and enclosed vehicle space only at Valley Center (2) (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Learn your own Site Profile, and know where to send a caller when your lot is full.
