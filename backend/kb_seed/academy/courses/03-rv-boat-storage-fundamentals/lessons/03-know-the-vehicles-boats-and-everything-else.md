## The Moment

A caller at Temecula asks for "a 20-foot space for my 20-foot bass boat." On its trailer, from the tip of the tongue to the back of the motor, that boat is about 26 ft. Put it in a 10x20 and its motor hangs in the aisle on day one. This lesson is why, and how to ask.

## Boat Words

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Bow, stern, port, starboard** | Front, back, left, right |
| **LOA (length overall)** | Hull length, bow to stern. **Not** the motor, the swim platform or the trailer |
| **Beam** | The hull's widest point |
| **Outboard; inboard; I/O (sterndrive)** | A motor hung on the back; an engine inside; an engine inside with a drive leg outside |
| **Lower unit** | The bottom of an outboard or sterndrive, often the rearmost point on a trailer |
| **Trim or tilt** | Raising the motor. Less length, more height |
| **Swim platform** | A step across the stern |
| **Bimini; wake tower; T-top** | A fabric sun top; a tall frame on ski and wake boats; a fixed roof over a fishing console |
| **Mast** | A sailboat's spar, laid down on the boat for the road |
| **Bilge plug** | The drain plug, pulled for storage so rain drains out |
| **HIN** | Hull identification number: the boat's VIN, on the transom |

A performance boat (go-fast) can run 30 to 45 ft and is long, wide and heavy. Kayaks and canoes ride on a small, light trailer.

## Listed Length Is Not Storage Length

This is the most important thing in this lesson. A boat's LOA runs bow to stern **on the hull only**. For storage you need the footprint on the trailer: **from the tip of the trailer tongue to the rearmost point**, usually the motor's lower unit or the swim platform.

- An owner measured his "16 ft" boat on its trailer at **22 ft**: the tongue alone was 6 ft.
- One maker lists its 23 ft wake boat at **31 ft** on the trailer with the tongue out, 28 ft 2 in with the tongue folded, and about **10 ft** tall with the tower down.

So the "23 ft boat" needs about 31 ft plus a margin, and 10 ft of clearance even with its tower folded.

> [!TIP]
> Ask: "How long is it on the trailer, from the tip of the tongue to the back of the motor? Does the tongue fold?"

**Length changes with** a folding (swing) tongue (saves a few feet), the motor's tilt, a bow pulpit or trolling motor, and a mast overhanging either end.

**Height changes with** a wake tower (even folded), a bimini left up (it can push a pontoon past 13 ft), a fixed T-top, a tilted-up outboard, a sailboat's keel lifting it on the trailer, and antennas. Height matters only under a roof or a door, but there it decides everything.

## The Boat Trailer Is a Vehicle

A boat trailer has its **own registration**, separate from the boat, and goes on the lease. Its tongue jack carries real weight and sinks into soft ground or hot pavement without a pad. Paperwork and Move-In covers what to collect for both.

## Everything Else on the Lot

- **Cars, SUVs and pickups.** Most fit a 10x20. A crew-cab long-bed pickup runs 20 to 22 ft. Duallies and towing mirrors are wider than 8 ft. Collectors want enclosed space, which Greens has only at Valley Center.
- **Work and cargo trailers.** Enclosed cargo boxes run 6x12 to 8.5x24 ft, plus 3 to 4 ft for the V-nose and tongue. Equipment and dump trailers are heavy and sink their jacks. Watch for business use the rules do not allow: working on site, or materials and fuel stacked beside the trailer.
- **Motorcycles, jet skis, ATVs, car haulers.** Registration, insurance, a hitch-to-bumper length and a line on the lease, like any vehicle. Two jet skis on one trailer are one trailer plus two registered craft.

In Cubby, a boat on its trailer is recorded as a boat; the type list is trailer, RV, boat, truck, SUV, sedan and other. Pick the closest and add a note.

## At Greens

**Fairfield.** A 24 ft pontoon is 31 ft on the trailer. With a 3 ft margin that is 34 ft, so the 12x32 is too short and the 12x48 fits. The lot is open, so height does not matter, but you ask the owner to fold and tie down the bimini because Fairfield's wind tears them.

**Escondido.** A 22 ft sailboat is 30 ft on its trailer, and the mast sticks 3 ft past the stern: 33 ft. With the margin that is 36 ft, so a 12x38 fits.

**Murrieta.** A 7x16 cargo trailer is about 20 ft with the V-nose and tongue: 23 ft with the margin, so a 10x24 fits.

## Common Mistakes

- **Using the hull length.** Measure tongue tip to the back of the motor.
- **Forgetting the trailer.** It has its own registration and goes on the lease.
- **Ignoring the tower under a roof.** A folded tower still sets the height.

## Key Points

- A boat's LOA is the hull only; storage length is tongue tip to the rearmost point.
- A "16 ft" boat can need 22 ft; a "23 ft" wake boat can need 31 ft.
- Towers, biminis, T-tops, masts, keels and tilted motors set the height under a roof.
- A boat trailer is a separate vehicle with its own registration.
