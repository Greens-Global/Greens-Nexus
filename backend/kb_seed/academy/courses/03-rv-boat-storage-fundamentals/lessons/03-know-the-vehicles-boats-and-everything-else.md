## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Tell the common boat types apart and name the part of each that causes fit problems.
- Explain why a boat's advertised length is not its storage length.
- Work out a boat's storage length from the tip of the trailer tongue to its rearmost point.
- Recognize the parts that change a boat's height: towers, biminis, T-tops and masts.
- Size cars, pickups, work trailers, motorcycles and jet skis.

## Boat Words First

You will hear these on every boat call.

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Bow and stern** | Front and back |
| **Port and starboard** | Left and right, looking forward |
| **LOA (length overall)** | Hull length from bow to stern. It does **not** include the motor, the swim platform or the trailer. |
| **Beam** | The hull's widest point |
| **Outboard** | A motor hung on the back (the transom) |
| **Inboard** | An engine inside the hull |
| **I/O or sterndrive** | An engine inside with a drive leg outside the back |
| **Lower unit** | The bottom of an outboard or sterndrive; often the rearmost point on a trailer |
| **Trim or tilt** | Raising the motor. It changes both length and height. |
| **Swim platform** | A step across the stern |
| **Bimini top** | A fabric sun top on a frame |
| **Wake tower** | A tall frame on ski and wake boats; often folds down |
| **T-top or hardtop** | A fixed roof over the console of a fishing boat |
| **Mast** | A sailboat's spar; laid down on the boat when trailered |
| **Bilge and bilge plug** | The lowest inside part of the hull, and the drain plug that is pulled for storage so rain drains out |
| **HIN** | Hull identification number: the boat's VIN, on the transom |

## A Boat's Length Is Not Its Storage Length

This is the single most important thing in this lesson.

The advertised length of a boat (its LOA) runs from bow to stern **on the hull only**. It leaves out the swim platform, the outboard motor, the bow pulpit and the trailer (Ace Boater). On a trailer, a boat takes up much more room than its LOA.

For storage, the number you need is the **total footprint on the trailer**: from the **tip of the trailer tongue (the coupler)** to the **rearmost point**, which is usually the lower unit of the motor or the swim platform.

### Two Real Examples

**A 16 ft boat.** One owner measured his 16 ft boat on its trailer at **22 ft**, with a 72 inch (6 ft) tongue (iboats forum example). That is 6 ft more than the "16 ft" the customer will tell you.

**A 23 ft wake boat.** The manufacturer of the Arc Sport, a 23 ft boat, lists it at **31 ft** long on its trailer with the tongue out, **28 ft 2 in** with the tongue folded, and **121 inches (about 10 ft)** tall on the trailer with the tower down (Arc Boats).

So the "23 ft boat" needs about 31 ft of space unless the tongue folds, and about 10 ft of clearance even with its tower down.

> [!TIP]
> Always ask: "How long is it on the trailer, from the tip of the tongue to the back of the motor?" Then ask: "Does the tongue fold?"

### Things That Change the Length

- **Folding (swing) tongues** shorten the storage length by a few feet. Some manufacturers list both a towing length and a storage length.
- **The motor's trim.** Tilted up, the outboard sticks out less at the back but stands taller.
- **Bow pulpits and trolling motors** at the front.
- **A sailboat's mast** overhanging the bow or stern.

### Things That Change the Height

Height matters only for covered and enclosed spaces, but there it matters a lot:

- **Wake towers** (even folded)
- **Bimini tops** (must be folded down; up, they can push a pontoon over 13 ft)
- **T-tops and hardtops** (fixed; they do not fold)
- **Outboards tilted up**
- **The keel** on a sailboat, which lifts the whole boat higher on the trailer
- **Antennas and outriggers** on fishing boats

## Boat Trailers

A boat trailer is a vehicle in its own right:

- It has its **own registration** separate from the boat. In Texas, boat trailers are titled and registered through the Texas DMV (the county tax office), while the boat is titled through Texas Parks and Wildlife (TPWD).
- It has **bunks** (carpeted boards) or **rollers** the boat sits on, a winch at the front and a coupler on the tongue.
- Its **tongue jack** carries weight. On soft ground or hot pavement it can sink; a pad helps.

The Paperwork and Move-In lesson covers what to collect for the boat and the trailer.

## Everything Else on the Lot

### Cars, SUVs and Pickups

Most cars and SUVs fit a 10x20. A crew-cab long-bed pickup can be about 20 to 22 ft, so it may need a longer space. Dually pickups (dual rear wheels) and trucks with towing mirrors are wider than 8 ft at the mirrors.

Car collectors and owners of a second car usually want enclosed space. At Greens, the only enclosed vehicle spaces are the two 12x38 garages at Valley Center (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

### Work and Cargo Trailers

Contractors and small businesses store enclosed cargo trailers, utility trailers, equipment trailers and dump trailers.

- Enclosed cargo trailers commonly run from 6x12 to 8.5x24 ft for the box, plus 3 to 4 ft for the V-nose and tongue.
- Equipment and dump trailers are **heavy**. Their tongue jacks can sink into pavement and gravel. Note it on site walks.
- Watch for business use that is not allowed: working on site, or storing materials or fuel beside the trailer. The Lot Rules and Risks lesson covers this.

### Motorcycles

Motorcycles fit small spaces and sometimes a storage unit, where the site allows vehicles in units. Registration and insurance still apply, just as for any vehicle.

### Jet Skis, ATVs and Dirt Bikes

Jet skis usually come two to a trailer. ATVs and dirt bikes often ride inside a toy hauler. A jet ski trailer is a trailer: it needs its own registration and goes on the lease.

### Car Haulers and Utility Trailers

Treat them like any trailer: registration (a trailer plate), insurance, measured hitch to bumper, and listed on the lease.

## What Cubby Calls Them

When you record a vehicle in Cubby, the vehicle type choices are trailer, RV, boat, truck, SUV, sedan and other (Cubby research, 11/2026). A boat stored on its trailer is a boat; record the trailer's plate in the notes your manager asks for. When in doubt, pick the closest type and add a note.

## Greens Scenarios

**Temecula.** A caller asks for a 20 ft space for a "20 ft bass boat." You ask for tongue tip to the back of the motor. It is 26 ft. You quote an 11x30 rather than a 10x20, and explain why.

**Fairfield.** A customer with a 24 ft pontoon asks for the cheapest space. You ask for the length on the trailer: 31 ft. With a 3 ft margin that is 34 ft, so the 12x32 is too short and you suggest the 12x48 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Fairfield's lot is open, so the bimini height does not matter there, but you remind them to fold it and tie it down because wind can tear it.

**Escondido.** A sailor with a 22 ft sailboat says the mast sticks out "a little." You ask how far past the stern. Three feet. The total is 30 ft on the trailer plus 3 ft of mast, so 33 ft. With a 3 ft margin that is 36 ft, so you look at Escondido's 12x38 spaces (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

**Murrieta.** A contractor wants to store a 7x16 enclosed cargo trailer. With the V-nose and tongue it is about 20 ft. With a 3 ft margin that is 23 ft, so Murrieta's 10x24 fits (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). You ask whether anything stored in it is hazardous, such as fuel or propane.

**A Texas site.** A customer has a center console with twin outboards and a T-top and wants covered space. You ask for the height to the top of the T-top and the length to the back of the tilted-down motors, because both are bigger than the hull numbers.

## Key Points

- A boat's advertised length (LOA) is the hull only; storage length is tongue tip to the rearmost point.
- A 16 ft boat can need 22 ft; a 23 ft wake boat can need 31 ft with the tongue out.
- Ask whether the tongue folds; it can save a few feet.
- Towers, biminis, T-tops, masts and tilted motors decide the height under a roof.
- A boat trailer is a separate vehicle with its own registration.
- Cars fit 10x20s; long pickups, cargo trailers and duallies need more length or width.
