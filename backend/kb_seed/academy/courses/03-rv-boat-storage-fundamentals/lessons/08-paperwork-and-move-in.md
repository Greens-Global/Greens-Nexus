## What You'll Be Able to Do

- List every document a vehicle move-in needs and say why each one matters.
- Check that the VIN or hull number matches on every document.
- Explain why a boat and its trailer need two registrations.
- Capture the title holder and lienholder, and explain why a lien on a vehicle depends on them.
- Take a complete set of move-in photos.
- Record who may drive the vehicle out, and why staff never move it.
- Explain the Texas rules that depend on the lease and the title.

## Why Paperwork Matters More for Vehicles

For a storage unit, the rental agreement and an ID cover most of what you need. A vehicle is different because:

- **The state knows who owns it.** A vehicle or vessel has a title and a registration held by a state agency. If the tenant ever stops paying, the lien process must reach the owner and the lender the state has on record.
- **A lender may own part of it.** Many vehicle owners still owe a loan, and the lender's lien usually ranks ahead of the storage lien.
- **It can be driven away.** Who may take it out matters as much as who may get in.
- **It can be damaged, leak or be stolen,** and the tenant's own insurance is what pays.

Industry legal writers make the same points: collect the documents at signing, because you will not get them later (Inside Self Storage, 2009 to 2014). Every item below comes back in a later lesson.

## The Documents

### 1. Photo ID

Check a government photo ID and compare the name to the registration and the insurance card. The person signing the lease must be the person (or the business) the vehicle belongs to, or someone they authorize in writing as your manager directs.

### 2. Current Registration for Every Vehicle

Every vehicle, trailer and boat on the lease needs **current registration**:

- the RV or car;
- the boat **and** its trailer, which are registered **separately**;
- each jet ski;
- a cargo or utility trailer (trailer plate).

**Reject documents not in the tenant's name**, or the name of a business they sign for. Some operators rent only to the title holder because of theft and creditor risk (Inside Self Storage, 2011). Greens' rule is on the Vehicle Storage Rules page.

Where the numbers come from:

| | California | Texas |
|---|---|---|
| Cars and RVs | California DMV | Texas DMV (through the county tax office) |
| Boats | California registers most boats through the DMV; confirm the numbers to record with your manager | Texas Parks and Wildlife (TPWD); every motorized boat is titled whatever its length, outboard motors are titled too, and sailboats 14 ft and longer are registered |
| Boat and other trailers | Confirm with your manager | Texas DMV, not TPWD (boat trailers) |

Texas details: TPWD and Galveston County tax office pages, as of 2026.

### 3. Proof of Insurance

The tenant's own insurance covers the tenant's vehicle. Greens' property insurance does not, and protection plans usually exclude motor vehicles, boats and trailers. At Greens, only one vehicle lease is on a Greens protection plan; vehicle tenants carry their own policy (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). See Greens Tenant Protection.

- Record the policy number and expiration date in Cubby.
- Cubby raises a "Remind Tenant of POI Obligation" task when proof is missing or expired (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Work it.
- If coverage lapses, the tenant carries the risk. Industry writers suggest asking to be notified if the policy is cancelled, which is not the same as being named an additional insured (Inside Self Storage, 2009).

> [!WARNING]
> Never tell a vehicle customer "we're insured" or "you're covered here." The answer to "Do you cover damage?" is: "Your own vehicle insurance covers your RV. We need proof of it at move-in."

### 4. VIN or Hull Number, and the Vehicle Details

Record in the lease and in Cubby:

- **VIN** (vehicle identification number) or **HIN** (hull identification number, on the boat's transom);
- make, model, year and color;
- plate or registration number, and the plate's state;
- Cubby's vehicle type (trailer, RV, boat, truck, SUV, sedan or other).

**The VIN or HIN must match** on the registration, the title (if you see it) and the insurance card. A mismatch is a stop sign: ask your manager before you continue.

> [!TIP]
> Cubby's vehicle type is blank on 696 of 759 Greens vehicle leases today (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Filling it in takes five seconds and lets Greens see what is on its lots.

### 5. Title Holder and Lienholder

Ask: **"Is there a loan on it? Who is the lender?"** Record the title holder (the legal owner) and every lienholder.

Why: if the tenant ever stops paying, both California and Texas require the vehicle lien steps to reach the owners and lienholders on record, and a lender's lien usually ranks ahead of the storage lien. The Vehicle and Boat Liens lesson explains this. Getting the names at signing is far easier than finding them later.

### 6. Out-of-State Plates

Note an out-of-state plate clearly. It makes a later lien lookup harder, because the records sit with another state's agency (Inside Self Storage legal articles, 2009 to 2014). Cubby shows 60 active Greens vehicle leases with a plate state other than California (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

### 7. Which Vehicle, Exactly

Ask directly: **"Will you store a vehicle? Which one?"** Write the specific vehicle into the lease or addendum. "Vehicle" means different things to different people, and some tenants swap vehicles. If the tenant wants to swap, update the lease and Cubby first.

### 8. Value and Operability

- **Value limits.** Many leases cap the value of stored property. Boats and RVs are worth far more; that is why their own insurance matters.
- **Operable vehicles.** A common industry rule is that a stored vehicle must run, move under its own power (or be towable on inflated tires) and have current tags. No vehicles on blocks. Greens' rule is on the Vehicle Storage Rules page. A Non-Operational Vehicle Deposit of $350.00 has been charged once, at Murrieta (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

## Move-In Photos

Photos are the baseline for every later conversation about damage, leaks or abandonment.

- [ ] All four sides of the vehicle (and the trailer)
- [ ] Close-ups of any existing damage: dents, scratches, cracked fiberglass, torn covers
- [ ] The plate and the VIN or HIN
- [ ] The space itself, showing the lines, before and after the vehicle goes in
- [ ] Anything unusual: a leak stain, a flat tire, an open hatch

Keep them in the tenant's file, dated. If the tenant later says "your lot did this," the photos answer.

## Who May Drive It Out

- **List every authorized driver** on the lease, and give gate access only to them.
- **Staff never drive or move a tenant's vehicle.** An operator who parks or moves vehicles takes on "care, custody and control" of them and needs matching insurance (Inside Self Storage, 2011). If a vehicle must be moved, the tenant moves it, or your manager decides.
- When someone not on the lease arrives to take a vehicle, stop and check with your manager. Privacy rules from Self-Storage Fundamentals apply: never confirm that someone is a tenant to a person who is not on the account.

## Texas: The Lease Does Work Here

At a Texas site, two things depend on the signed lease:

- **Boat liens.** For TPWD's lien foreclosure process to work on a boat, there must be a **written, signed rental agreement containing the self-service storage lien** (TPWD form PWD 309B).
- **The towing option.** Texas lets a facility tow a defaulted vehicle or boat to a licensed vehicle storage facility only if the **written rental agreement expressly authorizes towing**. The Vehicle and Boat Liens lesson covers this.

So at a Texas site, never let a vehicle in without a fully signed lease.

> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## The Move-In, Step by Step

1. Run the fit-check (previous lessons) and pick the space.
2. Check photo ID against the registration and insurance card.
3. Check current registration for every vehicle, boat and trailer, in the tenant's name.
4. Record VIN or HIN, make, model, year, color, plate and plate state, and Cubby's vehicle type.
5. Record the insurance policy number and expiration date.
6. Record the title holder and every lienholder.
7. Have the lease signed, with the specific vehicle written in.
8. List authorized drivers; set up gate access.
9. Measure with a tape and take the move-in photos.
10. Walk the tenant through the house rules (Greens Vehicle Storage Rules).

## Greens Scenarios

**Temecula.** A customer brings a boat registration but no trailer registration. You explain that the trailer is registered separately and you need both. They email a photo of it within the hour; you record both.

**Fairfield.** The registration is in the customer's father's name. You stop and ask your manager, because documents not in the tenant's name are a problem.

**Murrieta.** A customer's insurance card shows a different VIN from the registration. They had swapped trailers and forgotten to update the policy. You do not complete the move-in until the numbers match.

**Escondido.** A customer has Arizona plates. You note the out-of-state plate clearly in Cubby and record the lender's name and address.

**A Texas site.** A customer wants to sign "later" and drop the boat off now. You explain that the lease must be signed first, and you wait.

## Key Points

- Every vehicle move-in: photo ID, current registration for every vehicle, boat and trailer, and the tenant's own insurance.
- The VIN or HIN must match on every document; a mismatch is a stop.
- Record make, model, year, color, plate, plate state and Cubby's vehicle type.
- Capture the title holder and every lienholder at signing; the lien process depends on them.
- Take dated photos of all four sides, damage, the plate and VIN or HIN, and the space.
- List authorized drivers. Staff never move a tenant's vehicle.
- In Texas, the signed lease makes the boat lien and the towing option possible.
