## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Run a vehicle discovery conversation that finds what the customer really needs.
- Present a space by what it does for the customer, using Greens' real rates from Cubby.
- Answer the common RV and boat objections without arguing or over-promising.
- Explain why a longer space costs more.
- Name the amenities that win and keep vehicle tenants, and only promise what your site has.

## Vehicle Customers Buy Differently

The Customer Service and Sales course later in the Academy teaches the full sales call. This lesson covers what is different about vehicle customers:

- They usually **know exactly what they own** and what it is worth.
- They often **shop several sites** and compare aisle widths, access hours and surfaces, not just price.
- **Fit is a dealbreaker.** A space that does not fit is no sale at any price.
- **Access matters**: pre-dawn launches, Friday-night departures, Sunday-night returns.
- Once they find a site that works, **they tend to stay** (Inside Self Storage, 2007).

## Discovery Questions

Ask before you quote. These questions find the right space and the real reason they are calling.

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| "What are you storing?" Then the length hitch to bumper, and the height for covered or enclosed | Fit first (the Measuring lesson) |
| "How often do you use it, and when? Weekday mornings? Weekends? Months at a time?" | Access hours and gate habits |
| "Where do you take it? The lake, the desert, the coast?" | Whether the site is on their way |
| "Where is it now? Driveway, another facility, a friend's lot?" | The real reason: an HOA letter, a city notice, a rate increase, a theft |
| "What matters most to you: price, protection from sun and hail, security, access, amenities?" | What to lead with |
| "Is it registered and insured in your name? Is there a loan on it?" | Sets up the paperwork step |
| "Anything else to store? A second trailer, a boat as well as the RV?" | A second space, or a household unit too |

> [!TIP]
> Listen for the trigger. "My HOA gave me 10 days" means speed matters more than price. "My battery was stolen" means security is the sale.

What drives the price:

- **Length.** Longer spaces cost more because they use more land and more aisle.
- **Covered.** At Temecula the typical covered rate is $229 against $164 for open gravel (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- **Pull-through.** Murrieta's pull-throughs run $249 to $279, above its typical $184 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- **The site.** Every market is different.

Prepay discounts, specials and rate increases are set by Greens; ask your manager what applies today.

## Present the Space, Not the Price

Turn features into what they mean for this customer:

| Feature | What it means for them |
|---|---|
| Gated access with a personal code | "Only people with a code get in, and every entry is logged." (Say "logged" only where the gate reports, such as Fairfield.) |
| Cameras and regular lot walks | "We walk the lot and check every space; if something's wrong with your rig, we call you." |
| Pull-through space | "You drive straight in and straight out. No backing up, no spotter." |
| Covered space | "Your roof, seals and tires stay out of the sun, and hail never hits it." |
| Paved space | "No mud, no dust, and your jacks sit on a solid surface (with pads)." |
| A space sized to their rig | "It fits with room to walk around. You won't have to move it later." |

> [!WARNING]
> Never promise "nothing will happen to it," "we're responsible for it" or "you don't need insurance." The tenant's own insurance covers their vehicle.

## Objections You Will Hear

### Price by Length

*The customer says: "Why do you charge by the foot? Why is the bigger space so much more?"*

"A longer rig needs a longer space and more room to turn in, and that land is what you're paying for. The good news is that the right size means it fits the first time and nobody clips your bumper in the aisle."

### Too Expensive

*The customer says: "It's too expensive for a parking spot."*

Compare it to what it prevents: HOA fines, a sun-damaged roof, a hail claim deductible, a stolen battery or catalytic converter, a replaced cover. Then offer a lower-priced option: an open space instead of covered, a shorter space if their rig really fits, or a site with lower rates. Fairfield's typical rate is $104 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

### Security

*The customer says: "Is it secure?"*

Describe what is real: gate access by code, cameras where the site has them, lighting, lot walks. Offer enclosed space if the site has it. Never promise that nothing will happen; insurance is still required.

### A Cheaper Competitor

*The customer says: "The place down the road is cheaper."*

Ask what they get there: aisle width, access hours, a dump station, paved or dirt, covered height, distance to the lake or freeway. Then show what Greens offers. **Never criticize a competitor.** "They're a good option for some people. Here's what you'd get with us."

### Parking at Home

*The customer says: "I'll just park at home."*

Agree it is an option. Ask about HOA or city rules, the neighbors and theft, without quoting any rules. Leave the door open: "If anything changes, here's my number. Ask for me."

### Will It Fit Under the Roof

*The customer says: "Will my 13 ft 6 in fifth wheel fit under your covered spaces?"*

**Never guess.** Check the posted clear height (on the Site Profile). If it is not clearly enough, say so and offer an open space now, with the covered waitlist if a taller spot might work.

### Pausing for the Off Season

*The customer says: "I only use it in summer. Can I pause?"*

Explain that the rental is month to month, so they can leave, but the space may not be there when they come back. Offer to keep their details for the waitlist. Some customers choose to keep the space year-round because of that.

### Who Covers Damage

*The customer says: "Do you cover damage?"*

"No. Your own vehicle insurance covers your RV or boat, and we need proof of it at move-in. Protection plans for storage units don't cover vehicles."

## Amenities That Win and Keep Tenants

Industry interviews with vehicle storage operators (Inside Self Storage, 2007 to 2012) name the amenities vehicle tenants want most:

| Amenity | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Dump station** | The most requested by RV owners; many operators keep it free |
| **Wash bay** | Builds loyalty, even when it does not earn much |
| **Air for tires, water fill, propane fill** | Trip prep on site |
| **Power** at covered or enclosed spaces | Battery tenders keep batteries charged |
| **Wide aisles, pull-throughs, long access hours, good lighting, cameras** | Easy, safe coming and going |

Which amenities your site has is on its Site Profile. **Only promise what your site has.** If a customer asks for something you do not have, say so and tell them what you do have.

## Partners and Referrals

RV dealers, repair shops, marinas and boat dealers meet your customers first. One operator reported getting about 10% of rentals from RV shops and dealers (Inside Self Storage, 2012). Whether Greens works with referral partners, and how, is your manager's call.

## Greens Scenarios

**Fairfield.** A caller says Fairfield is "just a gravel lot." You agree it is gravel, explain that gravel drains well and keeps the price at about $104 for most spaces (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026), and describe the gate and the walks.

**Murrieta.** A retired couple with a 42 ft fifth wheel say backing in is the hardest part of every trip. You lead with the 12x50 pull-through and quote it from Cubby.

**Temecula.** A wake boat owner wants covered but balks at the price. You offer the open space now, at a lower rate, and the covered waitlist.

**Escondido.** A caller says a competitor is $40 cheaper. You ask what the other site's aisles and hours are like, then describe what Escondido offers without saying a word against the competitor.

**Valley Center.** A caller wants the cheapest spot for a utility trailer. Valley Center's one open space is leased (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). You offer Escondido or Temecula.

**A Texas site.** In March, a bass angler asks about 4 AM access. You check Greens Site Hours and Access for that site before you answer, and you never promise hours the gate does not have.

## Key Points

- Ask before you quote: what, how long, how tall, how often, where to, why now, what matters most.
- Quote from Cubby. Length, cover, pull-through and site drive the price.
- Sell what the space does for the customer: fit, access, protection, security.
- Answer objections with reasons, never argue, never criticize a competitor.
- Never promise "nothing will happen" or that Greens covers damage.
- Only promise amenities your site has; check its Site Profile.
