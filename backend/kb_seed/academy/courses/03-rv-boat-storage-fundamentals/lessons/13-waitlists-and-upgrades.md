## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Explain why covered, enclosed, pull-through and long spaces need a waitlist.
- Take a waitlist entry that has everything needed to make an offer that fits.

- Send a caller to another Greens site when your lot is full.
- Handle a seasonal move-out honestly.
- Keep an unhappy vehicle tenant before they drive away.

## Scarce Spaces

Some spaces are always in short supply. At Greens (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026):

| Scarce product | Where | How many | Leased |
|---|---|---|---|
| Covered | Temecula | 27 | 22 |
| Enclosed vehicle garage | Valley Center | 2 | 2 |
| Pull-through | Murrieta and Temecula | 36 and 28 | 35 and 16 |
| 40 ft and longer | All sites | 215 | 135 |
| Any space at Murrieta | Murrieta | 161 | 150 |

Covered and enclosed spaces often run full across the industry, and operators keep waitlists (Inside Self Storage, 2012). When a scarce space opens, the person who gets it should be the right fit, found fast.

## A Good Waitlist Entry

A name and a phone number are not enough. Capture what you need to make an offer that fits, so you can call and say "I have your space" without a second fit-check.

- [ ] Name, phone and email
- [ ] What they store: type, year, make and model
- [ ] Length hitch to bumper (and whether a tongue folds)
- [ ] Height to the highest point, for covered or enclosed
- [ ] Which product they want: covered, enclosed, pull-through, a size
- [ ] Which sites they would accept
- [ ] Whether they rented an open space meanwhile (and which)
- [ ] The date they asked

How Greens keeps the waitlist in Cubby or elsewhere is your manager's call. Ask before your first entry, and use one place only, so nobody gets skipped.

> [!TIP]
> When you call someone from the waitlist, re-check the length and height. Customers trade up to bigger rigs.

## Open Now, Covered Later

The most useful path in vehicle storage:

1. A customer wants covered (or enclosed, or a pull-through) and none is open.
2. You offer an **open space that fits, today**, at the open rate.
3. You put them on the **waitlist** for the product they want.
4. When it opens, you **upgrade** them: a transfer to the new space at its rate.

Why it works:

- The customer is on the lot today, not at a competitor.
- They see the site, the gate and the staff for themselves.
- When the upgrade comes, the rent goes up for a product they asked for.

When you make the upgrade:

- Re-check the fit, especially the height under the roof.
- Do the move as a transfer in Cubby (the Cubby course shows how).
- Take new move-in photos in the new space.
- Update the space number on everything: the lease, the gate access if it is space-based, and your walk notes.

Each Greens site is different, and a caller your lot cannot fit may fit another (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026):

| If the caller needs | Try |
|---|---|
| A long space (40 ft and up) | Temecula, with the most open long spaces; Fairfield's 12x48 |
| Covered | Temecula only |
| Enclosed vehicle space | Valley Center (2 garages; usually a waitlist) |
| A pull-through | Murrieta or Temecula |

| Paved | Murrieta; asphalt rows at Temecula and Escondido |

Check the other site's open spaces in Cubby before you send anyone. A caller you send to a sister site who finds it full is a caller lost twice.

## Seasonal Move-Outs

Some owners take the RV or boat away for a season (to the desert for winter, to a lake for summer) and want to stop paying while it is gone.

- Be honest: the rental is month to month, so they may leave, but **the space may not be there when they come back**, especially a long, covered or pull-through space.
- Offer to put them on the waitlist for their return date.
- Some customers keep the space for the security of having it. Let them decide; do not pressure.
- Note the reason for the move-out in Cubby. Seasonal patterns help Greens plan.

## Keeping an Unhappy Vehicle Tenant

A unit tenant who wants to leave has to empty a unit. A vehicle tenant hitches up and is gone in 15 minutes. You may get one conversation.

### Why Vehicle Tenants Leave

- A rate increase they think is unfair
- Damage to their rig they blame on the site
- A neighbor's rig parked too close, leaking or abandoned
- Gate problems: a code that fails at 4 AM, a gate that closes on their trailer
- Dirty or dark lot, missing amenities, slow answers
- A better offer nearby

### What to Do

1. **Listen first.** Let them finish. Do not defend or explain until they have.
2. **Thank them for telling you.** Most unhappy tenants just leave.
3. **Fix what you can, now.** A gate code reset, a neighbor's leaking rig reported, a lot light fixed.
4. **Offer what you are allowed to.** A transfer to a better space, the waitlist for covered, a different site. Waivers and credits follow Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds; never offer one you are not allowed to give.
5. **Escalate the rest.** Rate questions and damage claims go to your manager, with notes.
6. **Follow up.** Call back when you said you would.

> [!WARNING]
> Never admit fault for damage or promise to pay for it while trying to keep a tenant. Document it and pass it to your manager.

## Greens Scenarios

**Temecula.** A caller wants a covered 12x50 for a 44 ft fifth wheel. All eight are leased (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Temecula's open spaces stop at 45 ft, short of the 47 ft the rig needs with a margin (44 + 3). You check Cubby for an open 12x48 at Fairfield or 12x50 at Murrieta or Escondido, offer it, add the caller to Temecula's covered waitlist with the length and height, and explain the upgrade path.

**Murrieta.** The lot has no 12x30 open. A caller has a 27 ft trailer. You check Temecula in Cubby, find an 11x35 open, and offer it.

**Valley Center.** A caller wants one of the two enclosed garages. Both are leased. You take a full waitlist entry and mention Escondido's open lot for now.

**Fairfield.** A tenant calls to cancel because the rate went up. You listen, then explain what the space includes, and pass the rate question to your manager with notes before the tenant leaves.

**Escondido.** A snowbird says she is taking her motorhome to the desert from November to March and will cancel. You explain the space may not be there in March, and offer to note her return date for the waitlist.

**A Texas site.** A tenant is angry that hail dented his roof in an open space. You listen, document it with photos, do not admit fault, pass it to your manager, and mention the covered waitlist.

## Key Points

- Covered, enclosed, pull-through and long spaces are scarce at Greens; keep a waitlist (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- A waitlist entry needs the fit details: type, length, height, product and sites.
- Open now, covered later keeps the customer and raises revenue; re-check the fit at upgrade.
- Know which Greens site can fit a caller your lot cannot; check Cubby first.
- Seasonal move-outs may not get their space back; be honest.
- A vehicle tenant can leave in 15 minutes: listen, fix, offer what you may, escalate, follow up.
