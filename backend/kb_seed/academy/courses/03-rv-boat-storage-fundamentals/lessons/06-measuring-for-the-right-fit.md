## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Measure a travel trailer, fifth wheel, motorhome and boat the way that matters for storage.
- Measure height to the highest point, and width including mirrors and slides.
- Run the fit-check conversation with a caller, in order.
- Add a safe margin and choose the right space size.
- Check a fit on move-in day with a tape and photos.
- Explain why the clear height must be posted at every covered row and enclosed door.

## Why the Fit Matters

Getting the fit wrong is one of the most expensive mistakes on a vehicle lot.

| If the space is | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Too short** | The tail sticks into the aisle. Other tenants hit it. It blocks a fire lane. The tenant has to move. |
| **Too low** (covered or enclosed) | The roof air conditioner or antenna is torn off on a beam or door header. That is a large damage claim and a building repair. |
| **Too narrow** | Mirrors get clipped; doors and slides cannot open; neighbors get dinged. |
| **Too big** | The customer pays for room they do not need, and you have used a long space that a long rig needed. |

A wrong fit on day one means a bad first experience, a transfer, and often a rate dispute. Measure first.

Ask for the **overall length as it will sit in the space**, never the model name or the brochure number.

| Vehicle | Measure from | To |
|---|---|---|
| **Travel trailer** | The coupler (where it hooks to the hitch ball) | The rear bumper, including the spare tire, bike rack or ladder on the back |
| **Fifth wheel** | The front of the nose or pin box | The rear bumper and anything on it |
| **Motorhome** | The front bumper | The rearmost item: ladder, spare, bike rack, tow bar |
| **Boat on a trailer** | The tip of the trailer tongue (the coupler) | The rearmost point: the motor's lower unit, the swim platform or a mast overhang |
| **Car or truck** | Front bumper | Rear bumper, including a hitch or rack |

A towed car behind a motorhome is a **separate vehicle** and usually a separate space.

### Add a Margin

Add about **3 to 4 ft** to the measured length for walking room and a safety margin. An operator's rule of thumb is that a 38 ft RV needs at least a 42 ft space (Inside Self Storage, 2007). Your manager may set a different margin for your site.

## Height: Measure to the Highest Point

```figure
  "note": "Not the roof line. A covered or enclosed space has to clear this with room to spare."

```

Height only matters for covered and enclosed spaces, but there it decides everything. Measure from the ground to the **highest point**:

- the roof air conditioner shroud, antenna, satellite dome, solar panel or roof rack on an RV;
- the wake tower, bimini, T-top, antenna or mast step on a boat;
- a kayak or bikes on a roof.

Three things change height by inches:

- **Towers and biminis down, antennas lowered.** That is the number that counts, but only if the tenant lowers them every single time.
- **Tire pressure and air-bag suspension.** A rig sitting on full air bags is taller.
- **A trailer's tongue jack.** Cranking the front up raises the rear, and the other way round.

## Width: Body, Mirrors and Slides

The body of an RV or boat is about 8 to 8.5 ft wide. **Mirrors, awnings and slide-outs** are wider. A common rule is that **slides stay in and awnings stay closed while stored**; Greens' own rule is on Greens Vehicle Storage Rules. In a 10 ft wide space, a rig with towing mirrors may have only inches on each side.

## The Fit-Check Conversation

Use these steps, in this order, on the phone or at the counter.

1. **"What are you storing?"** The type, then the year, make and model.
2. **"What is the overall length, hitch to bumper, including anything on the back like a ladder, spare tire or motor?"**
3. **For covered or enclosed: "What is the height to the top of the air conditioner, antenna or tower?"**
4. **"Does it have slides? Do they stay in?"** For a boat: **"Does the tongue fold?"**
5. **"How do you usually park it? Do you back in? Do you have someone to spot you?"** This tells you whether a pull-through is worth offering.
6. **"How often will you take it out, and what time of day?"** Early launches and Sunday-night returns are about access hours, which are on Greens Site Hours and Access.
7. **Offer the right size:** the smallest space at least as long as the measured length plus the margin.
8. **Write the measurements in Cubby**, so the next person does not have to ask again.
9. **Check it on move-in day with a tape and photos.**

> [!TIP]
> "I want to make sure it fits the first time, so you never have to move it" is a sentence customers like. Measuring is service, not suspicion.

## A Worked Fit-Check at Fairfield

A caller wants to store a travel trailer at Fairfield.

**Step 1.** "What are you storing?" A 2019 travel trailer, "29 feet."

**Step 2.** "Is that hitch to bumper, with anything on the back?" The caller checks: the 29 ft is the box. The tongue adds 4 ft, and there is a 1 ft bike rack on the back. Total: **29 + 4 + 1 = 34 ft**.

**Add the margin.** 34 + 3 = **37 ft** needed.

**Check Fairfield's sizes.** Fairfield's spaces are 12x20, 12x22, 12x26, 12x32 and 12x48 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

- The 12x32 is too short: 32 ft is less than the 34 ft trailer, before any margin.
- The **12x48** is the next size up. It fits with room to spare.

**Height and width.** Fairfield is all open, so height does not matter there. The 12 ft width gives room for the mirrors.

**Result.** You quote a 12x48 from Cubby's current rate, write "34 ft hitch to bumper, bike rack" in the notes, and ask the customer to bring the trailer, registration and insurance.

Had you taken "29 feet" at face value, you would have rented a 12x32, and the trailer would have stuck 2 ft into the aisle on day one.

## A Second Worked Fit-Check: Covered at Temecula

A customer wants **covered** space at Temecula for a wake boat. Length on the trailer, tongue out: 31 ft. Height on the trailer, tower folded: 10 ft 1 in.

**Length plus margin.** 31 + 3 = 34 ft. Temecula's covered sizes are 10x20, 11x30, 12x35, 12x40 and 12x50 (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). The 11x30 is too short, so the **12x35** is the smallest covered space that fits.

**Height.** You compare 10 ft 1 in to the clear height posted for the covered rows (on the Temecula Site Profile). You also tell the customer the tower must be folded **every time** they park.

**Width.** A wake boat on its trailer is about 8.5 ft wide; a 12 ft space gives room.

If no 12x35 or longer covered space is open, you offer an open space that fits now and the covered waitlist.

## Post the Clear Height

Every covered row and every enclosed door should carry a sign that says its **real measured clear height**. That one sign prevents most roof damage. It also gives the tenant a number to check against their rig every time they come in.

- Measure the lowest point: the beam under a canopy, the bottom of a door header, minus any rain lip.
- Post it where the driver sees it before they pull in.
- Put the same number on the Site Profile page.
- If a sign is missing, faded or wrong, report it to your manager.

## Move-In Day: Check the Fit

On move-in day, before the tenant leaves:

- [ ] Measure the vehicle with a tape: length hitch to bumper, and height for covered or enclosed.
- [ ] Watch it go into the space. Is anything over the line or into the aisle?
- [ ] Check the mirrors and anything on the sides against the neighbors.
- [ ] Check slides and awnings against Greens Vehicle Storage Rules.
- [ ] Take the move-in photos (the next lessons cover which ones).
- [ ] Update the measurements in Cubby if they differ from the phone call.

Whether staff measure every vehicle at Greens is set by your manager. Measuring when in doubt is always right.

## Greens Scenarios

**Murrieta.** A caller wants a 10x30 for a "26 ft Class C." Bumper to bumper with the ladder and bike rack it is 28 ft. With a 3 ft margin that is 31 ft, so the 10x30 is too tight. You offer the 12x33 or a 10x35 if one is open (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

**Escondido.** A 40 ft Class A towing a car. You look for a 12x45 or 12x50 for the coach (40 + 3 = 43 ft), checking Cubby for one that is open, and a separate shorter space for the car.

**Temecula.** A fifth wheel measures 13 ft 6 in to the top of its air conditioners. You do not guess. You check the posted clear height for the covered rows; if it is not clearly higher, you offer open space.

**Valley Center.** A customer asks whether a 36 ft boat fits a 12x38 garage. On the trailer it is 41 ft. It does not fit, whatever the door height.

**A Texas site.** A customer's toy hauler is 38 ft hitch to bumper, and they plan to load ATVs on site. You quote a space with room for the rig and remind them that nothing is loaded or unloaded in an aisle.

## Key Points

- Measure what will sit in the space: hitch to bumper, nose to bumper, tongue tip to motor.
- Add about 3 to 4 ft; a 38 ft RV needs about a 42 ft space (operator rule, 2007).
- Height is to the highest point (air conditioner, tower, bimini, T-top), with everything lowered.
- Width includes mirrors; slides and awnings stay in.
- Run the fit-check conversation in order, write it in Cubby, and check with a tape on move-in day.
- Post the measured clear height at every covered row and enclosed door.
