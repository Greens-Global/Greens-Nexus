## The Moment

A caller wants a space at Fairfield for "a 29-foot travel trailer." Rent a 12x32 on that number and the trailer sticks 2 ft into the aisle on day one, because hitch to bumper it is 34 ft. A wrong fit means a transfer, a rate dispute and a bad first week. A tape and five questions prevent all of it.

| If the space is | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Too short** | The tail sits in the aisle or a fire lane, and gets hit |
| **Too low** | An air conditioner or antenna is torn off on a beam or door header |
| **Too narrow** | Mirrors get clipped; slides and doors cannot open |
| **Too big** | The customer overpays, and a long rig loses the space it needed |

| Vehicle | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| **Travel trailer** | The coupler | The rear bumper and anything on it: spare, rack, ladder |
| **Fifth wheel** | The front of the nose or pin box | The rear bumper and anything on it |
| **Motorhome** | The front bumper | The rearmost item: ladder, spare, rack, tow bar |
| **Boat on a trailer** | The tip of the tongue | The motor's lower unit, swim platform or mast overhang |

Then **add a 3 to 4 ft margin** for walking room. A 38 ft RV needs at least a 42 ft space. Your manager may set a different margin for your site. Pick the smallest space at least that long.

## Height: The Highest Point

```figure

  "kind": "drawing",
  "title": "Height Is to the Highest Point",

  "label": "Ground to the top of the air conditioner or antenna",
  "note": "Not the roof line."

```

Height matters only for covered and enclosed spaces, but there it decides everything. Measure from level ground to the **highest point**: the air conditioner shroud, antenna, dome, solar panel or rack on an RV; the tower, bimini or T-top on a boat. Count it with everything lowered, but only if the tenant lowers it every single time. Full air-bag suspension and a cranked tongue jack each add inches.

## Checking Clearance

A fit under a roof is two numbers: the rig's height and the space's **clear height**, the lowest point the rig must pass under.

1. **Find the lowest point on the whole path,** not only over the space: the beam under a canopy, the bottom of a door header less any rain lip, and anything overhead between the gate and the space.
2. **Get the measured number.** Cubby records no heights. Your manager has the clear heights until the site profile is filled in; once measured, the number is posted where the driver sees it before pulling in.
3. **Compare with room to spare.** A rig only a few inches under the clear height, or one you are not sure of, does not go under the roof. Offer open space.
4. **Never guess, and never let a tenant "try it."** The test is the damage.

A missing, faded or wrong height sign is a same-day report to your manager.

## Width

The body is about 8 to 8.5 ft. Mirrors, awnings and slide-outs are wider. Slides stay in and awnings closed while stored (Greens Vehicle Storage Rules). In a 10 ft space, towing mirrors may leave inches each side; a 12 ft space is the safe choice for a wide rig.

**Covered at Temecula.** A wake boat is 31 ft on its trailer and 10 ft 1 in tall with the tower folded. 31 + 3 = 34 ft, so the 11x30 is too short and the 12x35 is the smallest covered space that fits. Height: you compare 10 ft 1 in with the covered rows' clear height from your manager, and tell the owner the tower is folded every time. No 12x35 or longer covered space open? Open space now and the covered waitlist.

## The Fit-Check Conversation

1. "What are you storing?" Type, year, make, model.
2. "What is the overall length, hitch to bumper, with anything on the back?"
3. For covered or enclosed: "What is the height to the top of the air conditioner, antenna or tower?"
4. "Does it have slides?" For a boat: "Does the tongue fold?"
5. "How do you usually park it?" (see The Shape of a Space)
6. Offer the smallest space that fits the length plus the margin.
7. Write the measurements in Cubby.
8. Check with a tape on move-in day.

> [!TIP]
> "I want it to fit the first time, so you never have to move it." Measuring is service, not suspicion.

## Move-In Day

- [ ] Tape the length, and the height for covered or enclosed.
- [ ] Watch it go in. Nothing over the line or in the aisle.
- [ ] Mirrors and sides clear of the neighbors; slides in, awnings closed.
- [ ] Update Cubby if the numbers differ from the phone call.

## At Greens

**Murrieta.** A "26 ft Class C" is 28 ft with the ladder and rack: 31 ft with the margin, too long for a 10x30. You offer a 12x33 or a 10x35 if open.

**Valley Center.** A 36 ft boat is 41 ft on its trailer. A 12x38 garage cannot take it, whatever the door height.

## Common Mistakes

- **Quoting from the brochure number.** Ask hitch to bumper.
- **Skipping the margin.** A rig that "just fits" blocks the aisle.
- **Checking only the space's clear height.** The gate or a beam on the way in may be lower.

## Key Points

- Measure what sits in the space, then add 3 to 4 ft.
- Height is to the highest point, compared with the lowest point on the path, with room to spare.
- Never guess a clear height; your manager has them until the site profile is filled in.
- Run the conversation in order, record it in Cubby, check with a tape.
