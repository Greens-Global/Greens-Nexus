Your first week in the accounting office. A coworker says, "Check the Tickets tab for the printer repair." You look, and there is no Tickets in your menu. Nothing is broken, and by the end of this lesson you will know why, and where to go instead.

## What Nexus Is

Nexus is the Greens company portal: the one place for your own work life, whatever team you are on. If you are hourly, it holds your time clock and your Time Sheet. For everyone, it holds your schedule and time off, your HR requests, the equipment assigned to you, the tickets you raise when something breaks, the forms your team uses, and this training. It works the same in an office, on a construction job or at a site.

Nexus does three things an email thread and a text message cannot:

1. **One place** holds everything, under your Microsoft work account.
2. **The work comes to you.** A document to sign, a schedule to confirm or a reply on your ticket shows up in your bell.
3. **Everything leaves a record.** Every punch is timestamped and every request shows who has it. That record protects you as much as the company.

## Tiers: How Far Your View Reaches

Every person in Nexus has a tier. It sets what you can approve and how far your view reaches.

| Level | Tier | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Employee | Your own work: punches, Time Sheet, requests, tickets, training. |
| 2 | Supervisor | Your own work, plus some team screens a grant opens. |
| 3 | Manager | Approves for their team and builds its schedule. |
| 4 | Administrator | Runs one company inside Nexus. |
| 5 | Super Administrator | Everything, across every company. |

Most people at Greens are Employees. That says nothing about how important your job is. It means Nexus shows you your own work and keeps other people's private information out of your way.

## Grants: Why Menus Differ

On top of your tier, your job role and any Access Groups you belong to open extra modules. Each one is a **grant**. The desk side of Tickets, where teams work the tickets they receive, is a grant. Tasks is a grant. Accounting is a grant. So two Employees in the same office can have different menus.

> [!TIP]
> A module a coworker talks about and you cannot see is a grant you do not have. If you need it for your job, ask your manager or raise a ticket asking for access.

Some modules are open to everyone:

- **Dashboard**: your home page.
- **Workday**: your clock (if you are hourly), Time Sheet, time off, schedule and HR requests.
- **Team**: your group's shifts and the Directory.
- **Item Management**: borrow equipment and see what is assigned to you.
- **Help Center**: raise tickets and reach Knowledge Base & Training.

## The Mark Beside a Name

Wherever Nexus shows a person, it shows their **job title** under the name, such as "Payroll Specialist" or "Site Manager." It never prints the tier as words. A small mark beside the name shows the higher tiers instead:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A filled shield | Super Administrator |
| An outline shield | Administrator |
| A small people icon | Manager |
| No mark | Employee or Supervisor |

## What Nexus Asks of You

From your first week, Nexus expects a few habits. Each has its own lesson in this course:

- If you are hourly, punch in and out yourself, every shift (Punching In, Breaks and Punching Out), and check your Time Sheet weekly and sign it when it is approved (Your Time Sheet).
- Confirm your schedule when a new one is shared, and ask for time off in Nexus (Schedules and Time Off).
- Ask for things in Nexus, not by text (Ask HR, Reimbursements and Purchases).
- Report problems with a ticket, with photos (Getting Help: Tickets and Forms).
- Finish your training by its due date (Training and Policies).

## At Greens

Back to the printer. The ticket your coworker mentioned lives on the desk side of Tickets, which you do not have. You do not need it: **Help Center**, which everyone has, lets you raise your own ticket and follow the ones raised for you. You also notice an outline shield beside the name of the person who approves your expenses, so you know they are an Administrator.

## Common Mistakes

- **Thinking a missing module means Nexus is broken.** It is a grant you do not have.
- **Asking by text or email for things Nexus handles.** A text leaves no record and can be missed.
- **Calling a tier by an old name.** The words are Employee, Supervisor, Manager, Administrator and Super Administrator.

## Key Points

- Nexus is the Greens portal for your own work, for every team.
- Five tiers set how far your view reaches. Grants open extra modules on top.
- A missing module is a missing grant: ask your manager or raise a ticket.
- Under a name Nexus shows the job title; a small mark shows a Manager, Administrator or Super Administrator.
