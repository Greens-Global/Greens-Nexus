## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Explain in one sentence what Nexus is and what it is for.
- Name the five tiers in Nexus and say roughly what each one can do.
- Explain why two people at the same site can see different menus, and what to do when you cannot find something.
- Read the small mark beside a person's name and know what it means.

## What Nexus Is

Nexus is the Greens company portal. It is the one place where your work life lives: your time clock, your Time Sheet, your schedule, your time off, your HR requests, the equipment you borrow, the tickets you raise when something breaks, the forms you fill in on a walk, and the training you are taking right now. You sign in with your normal Microsoft work account, the same one you use for Outlook and Teams.

Nexus is not Cubby. Cubby is the self-storage software where tenants, units, payments, gate codes and move-ins live. Nexus is where you, the employee, live. A simple way to remember it:

| Question | Where you go |
|---|---|
| Is unit 214 paid up? | Cubby |
| Did I punch in this morning? | Nexus |
| What is the gate code for a new tenant? | Cubby |
| Who do I call about a broken gate motor? | Nexus (a ticket) |
| How many hours did I work this week? | Nexus |
| When is my next shift? | Nexus |

You will learn Cubby in its own course. This course is about Nexus, because you need Nexus from your very first day: to punch in, to get paid correctly, to ask for help, and to take the rest of the Greens Storage Academy.

## Why Greens Uses One Portal

Before a portal like Nexus, a site team member had to remember a different place for everything: a paper sign-in sheet, an email to HR, a text to a manager about a broken light, a spreadsheet for borrowed tools. Things got lost. Nexus fixes that in three ways:

1. **One sign-in.** The same Microsoft account opens everything.
2. **The work comes to you.** When something needs you (a document to sign, a schedule to confirm, a reply on your ticket), it shows up in your bell. You do not have to check every module every day.
3. **Everything leaves a record.** Every punch is timestamped. Every request shows who has it and what happened. That record protects you as much as it protects the company.

> [!NOTE]
> Nexus works in a computer browser and on your phone. Many site team members do most of their Nexus work from their phone during the day and use the office computer for longer tasks. The Nexus on Your Phone lesson shows you how to install Nexus on your phone.

## Tiers: Why Menus Differ From Person to Person

Every person in Nexus has a tier. The tier sets what you can approve and how far your view reaches. There are five tiers, from 1 to 5:

| Level | Tier | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Employee | Your own work: your punches, your Time Sheet, your requests, your tickets, your training. |
| 2 | Supervisor | Your own work, plus some supervisor screens where a module grant opens them. |
| 3 | Manager | Approves for their team: time off, punch fixes, Time Sheets, schedules. Builds the schedule in the Shift Planner. |
| 4 | Administrator | Runs a whole company inside Nexus, within that company's walls. |
| 5 | Super Administrator | Everything, across every company. |

Most site team members are Employees. That is not a limit on how important your job is; it simply means Nexus shows you the screens for your own work and keeps other people's private information out of your way.

### The Tier Is Only Part of the Story

On top of the tier, your job role and any Access Groups you belong to open extra modules. Each extra module is called a grant. For example, Tasks and Tickets (the desk side, where tickets are worked) show up in the left menu only for people who have been granted them. Two Employees at the same site can have slightly different menus because one has a grant the other does not.

This is the most common surprise for new team members, so here it is plainly:

> [!TIP]
> If a coworker talks about a module you cannot see, nothing is broken. You have not been granted it. If you need it for your job, ask your manager, or raise a ticket from Help Center and ask for access. You will learn how in the Getting Help: Tickets, Report a Problem and Forms lesson.

### Modules Everyone Has

Some modules are open to everyone, whatever the tier:

- **Dashboard:** your home page.
- **Workday:** your time clock, Time Sheet, time off, schedule and HR requests.
- **Team:** the Directory (find anyone) and Shifts (your group's week).
- **Item Management:** borrow equipment and see what is assigned to you.
- **Help Center:** raise tickets, read the guide, and reach the Knowledge Base & Training.

The lessons from Your Dashboard and the Bell through Training and Policies walk through each of these.

## The Mark Beside a Name

When Nexus shows a person (in the Directory, on a ticket, in an approval), it shows their **job title** under their name, for example "Site Manager" or "Assistant Manager." It never prints the tier as words under a name.

Instead, a small mark beside the name shows the higher tiers:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A filled shield | Super Administrator |
| An outline shield | Administrator |
| A small people icon | Manager |
| No mark | Employee or Supervisor |

So if you see an outline shield beside a name, you know that person is an Administrator and can help with company-level settings. If you see the small people icon, that person is a Manager and can approve things for their team.

## What Nexus Will Ask of You

From your first day, Nexus expects a few habits. Each one has a full lesson later:

- **Punch in and out yourself, every shift**, and take your breaks on the clock (the Punching In, Breaks and Punching Out lesson).
- **Check your Time Sheet** and sign it when your manager approves it. Only you sign your Time Sheet (the Your Time Sheet lesson).
- **Confirm your schedule** when a manager shares a new one (the Schedules and Time Off lesson).
- **Ask for things in Nexus**, not by text: time off, HR letters, reimbursements, equipment (the Schedules and Time Off, Ask HR, Reimbursements and Purchases and People, Equipment and Notes lessons).
- **Report problems with a ticket or a form**, with photos, so nothing is lost (the Getting Help: Tickets, Report a Problem and Forms lesson).
- **Finish your training** by its due date (the Training and Policies lesson).

## A Greens Scenario

Dana starts as a relief team member covering Escondido and Murrieta. On her first morning, her coworker at Escondido says, "Just check the Tickets tab for the gate repair." Dana opens Nexus and does not see Tickets anywhere in her menu.

What should Dana think? Nothing is broken. Tickets (the desk side, where tickets are worked) is a granted module, and Dana does not have that grant. She does not need it to follow the gate repair either. The gate ticket was raised by her coworker. If Dana wants to follow it or raise her own, she uses **Help Center**, which everyone has, and the Getting Help: Tickets, Report a Problem and Forms lesson shows her exactly how.

Dana also notices a small outline shield beside the name of the person who approves her Time Sheet. Now she knows that person is an Administrator, not just a Manager.

## Common Mistakes

- **Thinking a missing module means Nexus is broken.** It almost always means you do not have the grant. Ask, do not worry.
- **Using texts and email for things Nexus handles.** A text to a manager asking for a day off leaves no record and may be missed. Request Time Off in Workday instead.
- **Mixing up Nexus and Cubby.** Tenant and unit questions are Cubby. Your own hours, requests and training are Nexus.
- **Calling a tier by an old name.** The words are Employee, Supervisor, Manager, Administrator and Super Administrator.

## Key Points

- Nexus is the Greens company portal for your own work: time, schedule, HR requests, equipment, help and training. Cubby is for tenants and units.
- There are five tiers: Employee, Supervisor, Manager, Administrator and Super Administrator.
- Grants from your job role and Access Groups open extra modules on top of your tier, so menus differ from person to person.
- A missing module means you do not have the grant. Ask your manager or raise a ticket.
- Under a name Nexus shows the job title. A filled shield marks a Super Administrator, an outline shield an Administrator, a small people icon a Manager.
