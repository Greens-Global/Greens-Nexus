## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Sign in to Nexus with your Microsoft work account and get past the policy notice.
- Find any module in the left menu and switch between its tabs.
- Use the header: Search Nexus, the + menu, the bell, the ? Help panel and your profile menu.
- Know what changes when you use Nexus on a phone instead of a computer.

## Signing In

Open Nexus in your browser. The sign-in screen says **Welcome to Nexus** and offers two buttons:

| Button | Who uses it |
|---|---|
| **Team Sign-In** | Every Greens employee. It uses your Microsoft work account, the same one as Outlook and Teams. |
| **Partner Sign-In** | Outside partners only (people who are not Greens employees). They get a code by email instead. |

As a Greens team member, you always click **Team Sign-In**. Microsoft may ask for your work email, your password and your usual sign-in check (for example, an approval on your phone). Once that is done, Nexus opens.

> [!WARNING]
> Never use Partner Sign-In with your work email, and never share your Microsoft password with anyone, including a coworker who offers to "punch you in." Your sign-in is how Nexus knows every punch, signature and request is really yours.

## The Policy Notice

The first time you sign in, and again each time the company publishes a new version of its policies, Nexus shows a policy notice before anything else. It explains the company policies, including the monitoring disclosure (what Nexus records, such as where a punch was made). At the bottom is a tick box that reads "I have read and understood the policies above, including the monitoring disclosure, and I agree to comply." Read the notice, tick the box, and click **Accept & Continue**.

You cannot skip it. If you see it again a few weeks later, that is normal: the policies changed and Nexus is asking you to read the new version.

## The Layout on a Computer

On a computer, Nexus has three parts:

1. **The left menu** (the sidebar), which moves you between modules.
2. **The header** across the top, with search, create, the bell, help and your profile.
3. **The page** in the middle, with the module's tabs at the top.

### The Left Menu

The left menu is grouped into four sections, from top to bottom:

| Group | What is in it |
|---|---|
| My Desk | Dashboard, Workday and Team |
| Work | Tasks, Tickets, Files, Documents, Credentials and Workflows, each only if you have the grant |
| Modules | Department tools such as Item Management (everyone), Operations, Construction, Asset Management and more, by grant |
| System | Help Center (everyone) and Settings (by tier or grant) |

You only see what your tier and grants allow, so your menu may be shorter than this table. Three tips:

- The left menu moves you between modules. Inside a module, use the **tabs across the top** of the page to switch between its parts. For example, Workday has the tabs Overview, Time Sheet, Time Off and Performance.
- Click the **N** at the top of the menu to go back to the Dashboard from anywhere.
- A small **dot** beside a module means something there is waiting on you. Open it, and the tab with a red number is where it is.

### The Clock in the Left Menu

At the foot of the left menu is a small time clock. It shows your shift and hours, even when the menu is folded to icons. Its dial fills toward 8 hours and changes color: green while you are on the clock, amber on a break, red when you are off. Its buttons work like a music player: Play punches in (or ends a break), Pause starts a break, Stop punches out. Clicking the dial opens the full Time Clock. Lesson 6 covers punching in detail. If your job is exempt from time tracking, there is no clock at all.

### The Header

Across the top of every screen, from left to right on the right side:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Search Nexus** | Finds tasks, projects, people, items, purchase requests and pages. Press Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on a Mac) or the / key to jump to it from anywhere. |
| **+ (Create)** | Starts something new from any screen: New Note, New Ticket, Request an Item, Fill a Form, New Email, New Event, and more. You only see the rows for modules open to you. |
| **The bell** | Your notifications. A red number means unread alerts. Lesson 4 covers it. |
| **? (Help)** | The Help panel: Search for Help, What's New in Nexus, Help with This Page and Take the Tour. Pressing the ? key opens it too. |
| **Your photo or initials** | Your profile menu. |

### The Profile Menu

Click your photo or initials in the top-right corner. The menu shows:

- **Profile:** your appearance (such as Dark Mode), Date & Time, Email Signature, Task Emails, Phone Notifications and Security. Lesson 3 covers the phone settings.
- **What's New:** every change to Nexus, newest first.
- **Legal:** the privacy policy and terms.
- **Tools:** only for people who can use it. Most site team members will not see it.
- **Sign Out:** signs you out. On a shared office computer, always sign out at the end of your shift.

> [!TIP]
> Signing out also clears the copy of your Nexus data saved on that device for offline use. That is exactly what you want on a shared computer, and it is one more reason to sign out before you leave.

## Search Nexus

Search is the fastest way around Nexus. Press **Ctrl+K** (or **Cmd+K** on a Mac), type a few letters, and pick a result with the mouse or the arrow keys and Enter. Searching a coworker's name opens their contact card. Searching "time off" or "ticket" can take you to the right screen faster than clicking through menus.

## Tours and Help on Every Screen

Every screen has a guided tour. Open the **?** Help panel and click **Take the Tour** to have Nexus point out each part of the screen you are on. **Help with This Page** opens the guide for that screen. Use these whenever a screen is new to you; they are always one click away.

## Nexus on a Phone

Nexus works in your phone's browser, and you can also install it (lesson 3). The phone layout is built for one hand:

| On a computer | On a phone |
|---|---|
| Left menu always on the side | A menu button opens the menu as a drawer; a dot on it means something is waiting |
| Search bar in the header (Ctrl+K) | A search button opens a search sheet |
| Clock at the foot of the left menu | A small clock pill on the screen that opens the same clock |
| Popups slide in from the right as sheets | Popups come up from the bottom as full-screen sheets |
| Module tabs in the header | Tabs in the page itself, which you can swipe |
| Paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V | Use the camera to take a photo |

Everything you learn in this course works on both. Where a step is different on a phone, the lesson says so.

## A Greens Scenario

Luis opens Nexus on the office computer at Valley Center for the first time. He clicks **Team Sign-In**, signs in with his work account, reads the policy notice, ticks the box and clicks **Accept & Continue**. He lands on the Dashboard.

His manager asks him to check the Directory for the Fairfield site manager's phone number. Luis presses Ctrl+K, types the manager's first name and clicks the result. The contact card opens. Done in five seconds, without hunting through the menu.

At the end of his shift, Luis clicks his initials in the top-right corner and clicks **Sign Out**, because the office computer is shared with the next shift.

## Common Mistakes

- **Choosing Partner Sign-In.** Greens employees always use Team Sign-In.
- **Clicking past the policy notice without reading it.** It tells you what Nexus records about your work. Read it.
- **Staying signed in on a shared computer.** The next person could punch or request things as you. Sign out.
- **Hunting through menus.** Use Ctrl+K. It is faster.

## Key Points

- Greens employees sign in with **Team Sign-In** (your Microsoft work account). Partner Sign-In is for outside partners.
- Read the policy notice, tick the box and click **Accept & Continue**. It comes back when the policies change.
- The left menu has four groups: My Desk, Work, Modules and System. You only see what your tier and grants allow.
- The header has Search Nexus (Ctrl+K or /), the + menu, the bell, the ? Help panel and your profile menu.
- On a phone the menu is a drawer, popups are bottom sheets and the clock is a small pill. Everything else works the same.
- Always sign out of a shared computer.
