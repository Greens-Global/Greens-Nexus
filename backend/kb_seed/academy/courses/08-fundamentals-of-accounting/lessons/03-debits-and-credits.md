## Reading an Entry

You open a ledger line and see "Debit 640.00, Repairs" with a payable on the other side. Is that money in or out? Has the vendor been paid? Once you know which side increases which account, an entry tells you what happened in one look.

## Two Sides to Everything

Every transaction touches at least two accounts, because something came from somewhere and went somewhere. A rent payment puts cash in the bank and earns revenue. A vendor invoice records an expense and a debt to the vendor. Recording both sides is **double-entry** bookkeeping, and it is why the books check themselves: the debits in every entry equal the credits, or something was left out.

**Debit** means left and **credit** means right, nothing more. Which side increases an account depends on its kind:

| Kind | Increase | Decrease |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | Debit | Credit |
| Expense | Debit | Credit |
| Liability | Credit | Debit |
| Equity | Credit | Debit |
| Revenue | Credit | Debit |

A memory aid: debits are what the business has and what it spent; credits are where the money came from. Debit does not mean bad. A debit to cash is cash coming in; a credit to cash is cash going out.

## Five Storage Transactions

**1. A vendor services the gate and sends a $640 invoice. Nothing is paid yet.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Repairs and Maintenance | 640.00 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 640.00 |

The expense is recorded when the work was done, not when it is paid (see Cash and Accrual, and the Period). The company now owes the vendor.

**2. Accounting pays that invoice two weeks later.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts Payable | 640.00 | |
| Operating Cash | | 640.00 |

The debt is gone and so is the cash. No expense here: it was recorded in step 1.

**3. A tenant pays December's $150 rent on 11/28.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Operating Cash | 150.00 | |
| Prepaid Rent | | 150.00 |

Nothing is earned until December. On 12/01 the liability becomes revenue: debit Prepaid Rent 150, credit Rental Income 150.

**4. A $20 late fee posts to a delinquent tenant's account.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant Receivables | 20.00 | |
| Late Fee Income | | 20.00 |

The tenant owes it and the fee was earned. Whether it is ever collected is the lesson Receivables, Delinquency and Bad Debt.

**5. The site buys a $3,800 replacement gate operator on account.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Site Equipment | 3,800.00 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 3,800.00 |

Not an expense: an asset that serves for years and is expensed a little each month as depreciation.

Payroll works the same way: wages earned this period are a debit to wages expense and a credit to accrued payroll, even though payday falls in the next period. Your signed Time Sheet is the evidence.

> [!TIP]
> Before you read or write an entry, say the sentence: "This account went up because ..., and that one went up (or down) because ...". If you cannot finish both halves, you have not understood the transaction yet.

## At Greens

A Fairfield tenant pays $185 for this month and $185 for next month on the same day, by card. The desk does not touch the books; Cubby records the payments and Accounting posts them. But the assistant manager can read what will happen: $370 of cash, $185 of rental income this month, $185 of prepaid rent that turns into income on the 1st. When the site's report shows only $185 from that tenant this month, she knows why.

## Common Mistakes

- **Thinking debit means minus.** It means left; it increases assets and expenses.
- **Expensing when paying.** The expense belongs to when the work was done; paying only clears the payable.
- **Calling next month's rent this month's revenue.**
- **Expensing a long-lived purchase.**

## Key Points

- Every entry has two sides and the debits equal the credits.
- Debit increases assets and expenses; credit increases liabilities, equity and revenue.
- An unpaid invoice is an expense now and a payable until paid; rent paid ahead is a liability until its month; a long-lived purchase is an asset.
