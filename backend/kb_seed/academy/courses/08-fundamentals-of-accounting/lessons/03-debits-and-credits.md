## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Explain why every entry has two sides and why the debits always equal the credits.
- Say which side increases each kind of account without looking it up.
- Read a journal entry and tell what happened.
- Write the entry for six everyday storage transactions.

## Two Sides to Everything

Every transaction touches at least two accounts, because something came from somewhere and went somewhere. A tenant's rent payment puts cash in the bank (one account) and earns revenue (another). A vendor's invoice records an expense (one) and a debt to the vendor (another). Recording both sides is **double-entry** bookkeeping, and it is why the books can check themselves: the sum of every debit must equal the sum of every credit, always, or something was left out.

The words **debit** and **credit** mean nothing more than left and right. A debit is an entry on the left side of an account; a credit is an entry on the right. Which side increases an account depends on its kind:

| Kind | Increase | Decrease |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | Debit | Credit |
| Expense | Debit | Credit |
| Liability | Credit | Debit |
| Equity | Credit | Debit |
| Revenue | Credit | Debit |

Nothing about debit means "bad" or credit means "good." A debit to cash is cash coming in. A credit to cash is cash going out.

## Reading a Journal Entry

A **journal entry** lists the accounts touched, the debits first, the credits indented, with the amounts and a memo. Here is a tenant paying $150 of rent for the current month:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 1010 Operating Cash | 150.00 | |
| 4010 Rental Income, Storage Units | | 150.00 |
| *Memo: Unit B14, November rent, Cubby batch 11/03* | | |

Read it as: cash went up by $150 (debit to an asset), and the company earned $150 of rent (credit to revenue). Debits $150, credits $150. Balanced.

## Six Storage Transactions, Worked

**1. A vendor services the gate and sends a $640 invoice. Nothing is paid yet.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 6210 Repairs and Maintenance, Gate and Access | 640.00 | |
| 2010 Accounts Payable | | 640.00 |

The expense is recorded when the work was done, not when it is paid (the Cash and Accrual lesson). The company now owes the vendor.

**2. Accounting pays that invoice two weeks later.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 Accounts Payable | 640.00 | |
| 1010 Operating Cash | | 640.00 |

The debt is gone (debit reduces a liability), and so is the cash (credit reduces an asset). No expense: that was recorded in step 1.

**3. A tenant pays December's rent of $150 on 11/28.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 1010 Operating Cash | 150.00 | |
| 2300 Prepaid Rent (Unearned Revenue) | | 150.00 |

Cash came in, but nothing has been earned yet: December has not happened. The company owes the tenant a month of storage. On 12/01 the liability becomes revenue:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 2300 Prepaid Rent (Unearned Revenue) | 150.00 | |
| 4010 Rental Income, Storage Units | | 150.00 |

**4. A $20 late fee posts to a delinquent tenant's account.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 1200 Tenant Receivables | 20.00 | |
| 4210 Late Fee Income | | 20.00 |

The tenant owes it (an asset to the company) and the fee was earned. Whether it is ever collected is the Receivables and Bad Debt lesson.

**5. The site buys a $3,800 replacement gate operator.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 1520 Site Equipment | 3,800.00 | |
| 2010 Accounts Payable | | 3,800.00 |

Not an expense: an asset that will serve for years. It becomes expense a little each month through depreciation (the Fixed Assets lesson).

**6. Payroll for the period: $18,000 of wages earned, paid next week.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 5010 Wages | 18,000.00 | |
| 2100 Accrued Payroll | | 18,000.00 |

The expense belongs to the period the hours were worked (your signed Time Sheet is the evidence), even though payday is in the next one.

> [!TIP]
> Before you write an entry, say the sentence: "This account went up because ..., and this account went up (or down) because ...". If you cannot finish both halves, you have not understood the transaction yet.

## A Greens Scenario

A Fairfield tenant pays $185 for a 12x48 space for the current month and $185 for next month on the same day, by card. The assistant manager does not touch the books; Cubby does, and Accounting posts the batch. But she can read what will happen: $370 of cash, $185 of rental income for this month, $185 of prepaid rent that turns into income on the 1st. When the site's income statement shows only $185 from that tenant this month, she knows why.

## Common Mistakes

- **Thinking debit means minus.** It means left; it increases assets and expenses.
- **Expensing when paying.** The expense belongs to when the work was done; paying it only clears the payable.
- **Calling next month's rent this month's revenue.**
- **Expensing a long-lived purchase.**

## Key Points

- Every entry has two sides and the debits equal the credits; that is how the books check themselves.
- Debit increases assets and expenses; credit increases liabilities, equity and revenue.
- Rent paid ahead is a liability until the month arrives; an unpaid invoice is an expense now and a payable until paid; a long-lived purchase is an asset.
