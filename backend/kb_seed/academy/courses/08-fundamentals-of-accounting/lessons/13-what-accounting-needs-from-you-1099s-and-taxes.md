## A Letter From the County

A county envelope arrives at the office: a new assessed value for the property, with a date to appeal. It goes in the mail tray and is found three weeks later, after the appeal window closed. The tax rules are Accounting's job. Getting the paper to them in time is yours.

## Why It Starts at the Site

Taxes are filed by Accounting and Greens' tax advisers, not by managers. But the facts they file from start where you work: the vendor you hired, the parts you bought, the letter that came to the site. Three duties cover almost all of it. Rates, thresholds and deadlines change and differ by state and county; your Accounting team has the ones that apply.

## 1099s: A W-9 Before the First Payment

Every year Greens reports to the IRS what it paid many vendors for services, on **Form 1099**, using each vendor's taxpayer identification number (TIN). Accounting cannot file without the TIN, and chasing a vendor for it in January, months after the work, rarely works.

So the rule is simple: **no first payment without a W-9.** A W-9 is the vendor's own signed form giving their legal name, tax classification and TIN.

- Ask for the W-9 when you first bring a vendor in, before any work is billed.
- A new vendor is requested in Nexus under Accounting > Records > Vendors; it shows **Pending Approval**, then **Approved - Awaiting Intacct** until Accounting keys it in. The vendor's papers, the W-9 among them, are tracked on its **Compliance** tab.
- The vendor record shows only the last four digits of the tax ID. Never email a full TIN or put it in a ticket; send the form the way Accounting asks.
- A name on the invoice that does not match the W-9 is a question for Accounting before payment, not after.

## Sales and Use Tax: Say What and Where

Sales tax is charged by the seller; **use tax** is owed by the buyer when a taxable item was bought without sales tax (often online or from out of state) and is used in a state that taxes it. Whether a purchase is taxable depends on what it is and where it is used.

What Accounting needs from you is the plain facts on every purchase: **what it is** ("12 disc locks for overlocks," not "supplies") and **which property it is for**. That is the memo and the entity coding you already owe on every card charge and invoice (see Payables, Cards and the Bank). With those two facts, Accounting can tell whether tax was charged correctly and whether use tax is owed.

Sales tax that Greens collects at the counter on merchandise is the tenant's money passing through: a liability until it is remitted, never revenue.

## Property Tax: Forward It the Day It Arrives

Each property is assessed and taxed by its county. The notices come by mail, often to the property itself:

- **Assessment notices** (a new value), which carry an appeal deadline that can be short.
- **Tax bills**, with due dates and late penalties.
- **Business property statements** or similar forms asking what equipment is on site.

Forward every one to Accounting **the day it arrives**: a photo or scan of every page, envelope included if it shows a date, with the property's name. Do not pay it from the site and do not set it aside for later. A missed appeal deadline can cost a year at the higher value.

## At Greens

The next county envelope at Valley Center is opened the morning it arrives. The manager scans all four pages, sends them to Accounting with "Valley Center - assessment notice received today," and puts the appeal date in the email. Accounting decides whether to appeal. The manager's part took five minutes.

## Common Mistakes

- **Paying a new vendor before the W-9 is in.**
- **A full tax ID number typed into a ticket or an email.**
- **A purchase memo of "supplies"** with no property named.
- **A tax notice left in the mail tray,** or paid from the site.

## Key Points

- Accounting files; the facts start at the site.
- No first payment without a W-9; the vendor's papers live on its Compliance tab.
- On every purchase, say what it is and which property it is for; that settles sales and use tax.
- Forward every property tax notice and bill, every page, the day it arrives.
