## Accounting Is Keeping Score, Honestly

Accounting is the system that records what a business owns, owes, earns and spends, in a way that other people can check. That last part is the point. A manager knows roughly how the site did last month; the books say exactly, in a form the owners, the bank, the insurer, the tax authorities and an auditor can all read and agree on.

At Greens the score is kept in **Sage Intacct**, the accounting system Charmi's team runs, and Nexus shows the parts of it the rest of the company needs: the Accounting module's reports and dashboards, a vendor's record and its papers, a construction job's money, a lease's rent received. Nexus never keeps a second set of books; it reads the one set.

## The Three Questions

Every set of books answers three questions, and each one has a statement that answers it:

| Question | The statement | At a storage site |
|---|---|---|
| **What do we own and what do we owe?** | The **balance sheet**, at a moment | The buildings, the lot, the cash in the bank, what tenants owe; the loans, what vendors are owed, rent paid ahead |
| **Did we make money?** | The **income statement** (profit and loss), over a period | Rent, fees and merchandise, less payroll, utilities, repairs, insurance, taxes and interest |
| **Where did the cash go?** | The **cash flow statement**, over a period | Rent collected, bills paid, loan payments, a new roof |

A business can make money on paper and run out of cash (a profitable site whose tenants pay late), or have cash and be losing money (a loan drawn down). That is why there are three statements, not one.

The same is true the other way around:

| What happened at the site | What it becomes |
|---|---|
| A tenant pays rent | Revenue for the period, cash in the bank |
| A tenant pays next month early | Cash now, but a **liability** (rent paid ahead) until the month arrives |
| A vendor fixes the gate and sends an invoice | An **expense** for the period and an **account payable** until it is paid |
| A new gate operator is installed | An **asset** on the balance sheet, expensed a little each month as **depreciation** |
| You punch in and out | **Payroll expense** for the period, through the Time Sheet you sign |
| A lien sale recovers part of an unpaid balance | The unpaid rent is written off; the sale proceeds reduce the loss |

## Why Traceability Is the Whole Game

A number in a report is only as good as the trail behind it. In Nexus Accounting every amount on every report drills down to the ledger lines that make it up, and every line opens the transaction it came from. That is not decoration; it is how a reviewer, an auditor or a manager who does not believe a number finds out whether it is right.

The habits that keep the trail whole start at the site: a ticket number on every vendor invoice, a receipt on every reimbursement, a photo on every work order, a reason on every waiver. The Property & Vendor Management course taught the vendor side; this course teaches what the books do with it.

> When a figure surprises you, do not argue with it. Click it. The lines under it either explain the surprise or show the mistake.

## A Greens Scenario

Priya, running Temecula, reads the site's income statement in Nexus Accounting and sees repairs and maintenance a third higher than last year. She clicks the figure. The ledger lines show three gate vendor invoices in the summer, two of them after-hours. She opens one: the invoice carries a ticket number, and the ticket says the operator failed on a Saturday night. The number was right; the gate was the story. She writes it into next year's budget case.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating the books as Accounting's problem.** Every number started at a site.
- **Confusing profit and cash.** A site can be profitable and short of cash in the same month.
- **A number nobody can trace.** Without the ticket, the receipt or the reason, the trail is broken.

## Key Points

- Accounting records what the business owns, owes, earns and spends so that others can check it. Greens keeps one set of books, in Intacct; Nexus reads it.
- Three questions, three statements: the balance sheet, the income statement, the cash flow statement.
- Every number started at a site. Every amount drills to its lines and each line to its transaction.
