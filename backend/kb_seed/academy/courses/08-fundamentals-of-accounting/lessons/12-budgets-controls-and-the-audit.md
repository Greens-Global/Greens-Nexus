## The Auditor Picks Your Invoice

An auditor pulls one repairs invoice from Temecula's ledger, $640 plus parts, and asks to see everything behind it. If the ticket has the scope, the approval, the photo and the number that matches the invoice, the auditor moves on and nobody calls you. If not, you spend the afternoon looking for paper.

## The Budget

A **budget** is next year's income statement written in advance, line by line, for each site. Revenue is built from the rent roll (units, rates, expected occupancy, planned rate changes and concessions); expenses from the schedules and contracts (preventive maintenance, service contracts, payroll by shift, insurance and property tax from the renewals), plus what the site knows is coming. The maintenance lines are built in Property & Vendor Management; Accounting assembles the whole.

A budget is the plan leadership approved and the yardstick each month is measured against. In Nexus it is Accounting > Trackers > Budgets, with Budget vs. Actual, Year to Date and Month to Date under Reports beside it. A line nobody can explain is not a plan; it is a wish.

## Variance Comments

The difference between budget and actual is the **variance**, favorable or unfavorable. More revenue than planned is favorable; more expense than planned is unfavorable. The report is useless without its comments:

| A comment that helps | One that does not |
|---|---|
| "Utilities $900 over: October's bill arrived in November, timing; November alone was on budget." | "Utilities over." |
| "Rental income $2,400 under: two free-month concessions on the new garages, full rent from 12/01." | "Promotions." |

Write it from the drill-down, with the ticket numbers, and the next question answers itself.

## Internal Controls

**Internal controls** are the rules and checks that keep the books honest, protect cash and property, and make sure what is reported is what happened. Each asks something of the site:

| Control | What it asks of the site |
|---|---|
| **Segregation of duties** | The person who takes payments does not reconcile them; the person who approves a vendor does not pay it |
| **Authorization limits** | Approval in writing on the ticket before the work, at the level the amount needs; never splitting a job to stay under a limit |
| **Three-way match** | Ticket numbers on invoices, scopes, finished-work photos |
| **No cash outside the system** | Deposits intact, refunds through Cubby and Accounting, no vendor paid from the site |
| **Reasons on waivers and credits** | The reason on every waiver, within the limits |
| **Reconciliations** | Same-day answers to Accounting's questions |
| **Access control** | No shared sign-ins; sign out of shared computers |
| **Record retention** | Photos on tickets, receipts on charges, incident forms the same day |

The limits themselves (who approves what amount) are set by Greens leadership and your Accounting team; ask your manager for the ones that apply to you.

## The Records

An auditor, insurer, lender or tax examiner will one day ask for the records behind a number. What must exist: every payment and refund in Cubby; every waiver and credit with its reason and approver; every vendor invoice with its ticket, scope, approval, inspection and payment; every card charge with its receipt; every pay period with its signed Time Sheets; every capital asset with its invoice and in-service date; every lien sale with its notices and proceeds; every incident with its form and photos. Retention is long and nothing on this list is ever "cleaned up."

## The Audit

An **audit** is an independent check that the statements fairly present what happened. The auditor does not re-add every number; they test. They pick transactions and follow the trail (a rent payment to the bank, an invoice to the ticket to the photo), confirm balances with outsiders (the bank, the lender), test the controls (did the approvals and reconciliations happen), and look hardest at the judgments: the allowance, capitalization calls, accruals.

> [!TIP]
> Test every record you make: could a stranger, a year from now, understand what happened and why from this alone? If not, add what is missing today.

## At Greens

The auditor's $640 sample: the invoice carries "#4830"; the ticket shows the scope, the quote, the approval, the finished-work photo and Resolved; the vendor's papers were current on the day of the visit. Next sample.

## Common Mistakes

- **A budget line that is last year plus a percent.**
- **A variance comment with no ticket number** and no "timing or real."
- **Skipping a control on a busy day.** That is what controls are for.

## Key Points

- A budget is next year's income statement; Budget vs. Actual is read through its variance comments.
- A good comment says what moved, why, timing or real, and what next.
- Each internal control asks something of the site; the limits are set by leadership and Accounting.
- An audit follows trails and tests controls; complete records mean nothing to prepare.
