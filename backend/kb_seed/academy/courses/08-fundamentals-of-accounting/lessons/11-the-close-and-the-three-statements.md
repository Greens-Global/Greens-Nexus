## NOI Fell, Occupancy Held

The owner's review asks why Murrieta's net operating income fell while occupancy held. You have five minutes. If you can read the three statements, you already know which line moved, whether you control it, and what you are doing about it.

## The Close

The **close** is the work between the last day of a period and the day its statements are final. Nothing about a month is true until it is done: late bills, depreciation, the allowance and the accruals all land during it.

## The Balance Sheet

A photograph at one moment, the last day of the period: the accounting equation laid out. **Current assets** (cash, receivables less the allowance, prepaids) and **property and equipment** (less accumulated depreciation) on one side; **current liabilities** (payables, accrued payroll, prepaid rent, deposits held, taxes payable), **long-term liabilities** (the mortgage) and **equity** on the other. "Current" means within a year. It tells you how much cash there is against what is owed this year, how much of the property is financed by debt, and how old the assets are.

## The Income Statement

A film of a period: what was earned and what it cost.

| Line | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Rental income** | Earned rent by product |
| **Other income** | Fees, protection plan, merchandise |
| **= Total revenue** | |
| **Operating expenses** | Payroll, utilities, repairs, property tax, insurance, marketing, management fees, bad debt |
| **= Net operating income (NOI)** | Revenue less operating expenses |
| **Depreciation and interest** | The spread of the assets' cost; the cost of the loan |
| **= Net income** | What is left for the owners |

**NOI** is the storage industry's number: what a site earns from operating, before financing and depreciation. Two sites with the same NOI are worth about the same whatever their loans. A manager moves NOI; a manager does not move interest or depreciation.

## The Cash Flow Statement

It explains the change in cash in three parts:

| Section | At a storage company |
|---|---|
| **Operating** | Rent collected less bills and payroll paid; starts from net income, adds back depreciation, adjusts for receivables and payables moving |
| **Investing** | A new roof, a repave, a gate operator; proceeds from selling equipment |
| **Financing** | Loan draws and payments, owner distributions |

That is how a profitable month can leave less cash (a repave under investing) and a loss month more (a loan draw under financing). Depreciation is added back because it was an expense that moved no cash.

## How the Three Tie Together

Net income flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet. The change in cash between two balance sheets is what the cash flow statement explains. Statements that do not tie this way have an error somewhere, and finding it is what the review step does.

## Four Ratios for a Manager

| Ratio | How | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **NOI margin** | NOI divided by revenue | How much of each rent dollar the site keeps |
| **Expense ratio** | Operating expenses divided by revenue | Rising means costs outrun rent |
| **Economic occupancy** | Earned rent divided by gross potential rent | Potential actually earned after vacancy, discounts and concessions |
| **Delinquency rate** | Past-due balances divided by rent billed | How well the site collects |

A site with $80,000 of revenue and $36,000 of operating expenses has NOI of $44,000, an NOI margin of 55% and an expense ratio of 45%.

## At Greens

Murrieta's manager has read the statements: revenue flat, repairs up after three gate emergencies, NOI margin down two points. Depreciation and interest did not move; the fall is in the operating line he controls. He brings the gate's ticket history and the capital case. The review takes five minutes.

## Common Mistakes

- **Reading a month before the close.**
- **Judging how a site is run on net income** instead of NOI.
- **Treating depreciation as cash.**
- **Not knowing the four ratios** for your own site.

## Key Points

- The close: cut off, reconcile, revenue, payables and accruals, adjustments, review, lock.
- Balance sheet is a moment; income statement is a period down to NOI and net income; cash flow explains the change in cash in operating, investing and financing.
- Net income flows into equity; cash flow explains the change between two balance sheets.
- NOI margin, expense ratio, economic occupancy and delinquency rate: one sentence each, every month.
