## Double the Utilities

Murrieta's November report shows utilities at double the usual. A broken meter? A leak? Or a bill that simply landed in the wrong month? Knowing when something counts is how you answer in two clicks instead of a site visit.

## When Does Something Count

The hardest question in accounting is not what happened but **when it counts**. There are two answers:

## Matching

Accrual rests on **matching**: revenue in the period it was earned, and the expenses that helped earn it in the same period. A tenant who stores in October and pays on 11/08 earned October revenue. The October electricity bill that arrives 11/12 is an October expense. A year of insurance paid in January is twelve months of expense, not one.

Matching is what makes October comparable to September. Without it a month is just whatever cleared the bank.

## The Adjusting Entries

At the end of every period, Accounting makes **adjusting entries** that move things into the right period. Five recur at a storage company:

| Adjustment | What it does | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| **Earn down prepaid rent** | Rent paid ahead for this month becomes revenue | Debit Prepaid Rent, credit Rental Income |
| **Record rent earned, unpaid** | This month's storage counts even if unpaid | Debit Tenant Receivables, credit Rental Income |
| **Allowance for doubtful accounts** | The expected loss is recognized now | Debit Bad Debt Expense, credit Allowance |
| **Expense a prepaid** | One month of the insurance year | Debit Insurance Expense, credit Prepaid Insurance |
| **Accrue an expense** | Wages earned, a utility bill not yet in | Debit the expense, credit Accrued Liabilities |

Depreciation (a month of each asset's cost) is the sixth, taught in Fixed Assets and Depreciation. Each adjustment reverses or earns down over time, so nothing is counted twice: the accrued utility bill is cleared when the real bill arrives.

## A Storage Month, Both Ways

A site in October: tenants were billed $60,000 of October rent; $52,000 arrived in October, $6,000 in early November, and about $2,000 will never be collected. Tenants also paid $4,000 of November rent early, in October. The October electricity bill of $1,800 arrived 11/12. The gate was serviced in October for $640, paid in November. The $12,000 annual insurance premium was paid in January.

| | Cash basis, October | Accrual basis, October |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | $56,000 collected ($52,000 + $4,000 early) | $60,000 earned |
| Bad debt | $0 | $2,000 allowance |
| Electricity | $0 | $1,800 |
| Gate service | $0 | $640 |
| Insurance | $0 | $1,000 (one twelfth) |

The cash view says October took in $56,000 and spent almost nothing. The accrual view says October earned $60,000, expected to lose $2,000 of it, and cost $3,440 in these lines. Only the second tells you how the site did.

## Timing or Real

When a month surprises you, ask whether it is **timing** (a bill in the wrong month, an accrual reversing) or **real** (more repairs, fewer tenants, a rate change). Timing reverses next month; real does not. Click the figure: one accrued invoice dated in the wrong month is timing; three gate calls are real.

## At Greens

Back at Murrieta, the manager clicks the utilities figure: two bills, October's that arrived late and November's. Timing, not a leak. He says so in his variance note. Repairs are up too: three invoices for three different systems. That one is real, and it goes in the budget case.

## Common Mistakes

- **Reading the bank balance as the month's result.**
- **Judging a month before the close.** The adjusting entries land during it.
- **Chasing a timing variance** as if it were real.
- **Counting next month's early rent as this month's income.**

## Key Points

- Cash counts when money moves; accrual counts when earned or incurred. Greens' statements are accrual.
- Matching puts revenue and the expenses that earned it in the same period.
- Month end earns down prepaid rent, records unpaid rent, builds the allowance, expenses prepaids, accrues bills and records depreciation.
- A surprise is timing or real; the drill-down tells you which.
