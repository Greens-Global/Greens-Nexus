## Monthly in Advance, and the 1st

Storage rent is billed **monthly in advance**: on the 1st, every tenant's account is charged the coming month. That makes the 1st the biggest day in a storage company's books. In one batch, every lease gets a charge (a receivable, and prepaid rent where the tenant already paid), and over the month those charges are earned and collected.

A tenant who moves in mid-month pays a **prorated** first month (the days left in the month) or, at some operators, a full month that then runs to the same day next month. Cubby prorates by its own rule; the books follow it. Prorated rent is earned over the days it covers.

> [!NOTE]
> The books see a charge on the 1st and a payment whenever it arrives. The gap between them is why receivables and prepaid rent are the two accounts that move most at a storage company, and why a jump in receivables on the 10th is the first sign of a collections month going wrong.

## Four Rents That Are Not the Same Number

The storage business uses four rent figures, and an argument about "the rent" is usually two people using two of them:

| Figure | What it is | What the gap to the one before it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross potential rent (GPR)** | What the site would bill if every unit were rented at today's street rate | |
| **Billed rent** | What was actually charged on the 1st: rented units at their own rates | Vacancy and the gap between street and in-place rates |
| **Earned rent** | Billed, less concessions and credits, for the period | Promotions, waivers, credits |
| **Collected rent** | What was paid | Delinquency (the Receivables and Bad Debt lesson) |

The income statement reports earned rent. GPR and the gaps live on the operating reports and are the language of revenue management (the How a Storage Site Makes Money lesson).

## Concessions

A **concession** (a free month, 50% off the first month, a waived admin fee) is a reduction of revenue, recorded so leadership can see how much was given away to rent units. It is never hidden in the rent line and never recorded as an expense. A waiver or credit after move-in (a late fee waived for a good tenant) is the same: a reduction of the revenue it waives, with the reason on the record (Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds).

## Protection Plans Are Revenue; Insurance Is Not

Greens' **protection plan** is the company's own program: the tenant pays a monthly amount and the company takes on a limited promise about their goods. That payment is the company's revenue, earned over the month, and claims paid under it are the company's expense.

**Tenant insurance** bought from a third-party insurer is the insurer's revenue, not Greens'. If the site collects it with the rent and passes it on, the collected amount is a liability until it is remitted, never revenue. The Tenant Protection and Insurance lesson explains the difference to a tenant; the books keep it.

## Lien Sale Proceeds

When a defaulted unit is sold at auction, the proceeds are applied first against the tenant's unpaid balance (the receivable, which was probably already in the allowance), then against the lien costs. Anything left over is owed to the tenant under the lien law and is a liability until paid or escheated. A lien sale is not a profit center; it is the end of a receivable.

## A Greens Scenario

Valley Center's income statement for the month shows rental income down slightly while the manager knows the site is full. She clicks the line: two concessions, both free first months on the new 12x38 garages, plus a late fee waived with the reason "first offense, long tenant." Earned rent is down because of promotions, not vacancy; the next month's figure will show the garages at full rent. She writes that in the variance note.

## Common Mistakes

- **Counting a tenant's early payment as revenue.**
- **Recording a concession as an expense** or hiding it in the rent line.
- **Treating third-party insurance collected as revenue.**
- **Calling GPR "the rent."** The income statement reports earned rent.

## Key Points

- Revenue lines: unit rent, parking rent, climate and specialty, protection plan, admin and late fees, other fees, merchandise, lien proceeds; each earned when its service happens.
- Rent is billed monthly in advance; the 1st is the biggest day in the books; prorations follow Cubby's rule.
- Gross potential, billed, earned and collected rent are four numbers; the gaps are vacancy, rate, concessions and delinquency.
- Concessions reduce revenue and are recorded with the reason; the protection plan is revenue, third-party insurance is a pass-through.
