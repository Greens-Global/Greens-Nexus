## Full Site, Lower Rent

Valley Center is full, yet its rental income dipped this month. The manager clicks the line and finds two free first months on the new garages. Nothing is wrong; earned rent went down because of promotions, not vacancy. Knowing what each revenue figure means is how you say that in one sentence.

## The Revenue Lines

| Line | What it is | When it is earned |
|---|---|---|
| **Rental income** | Monthly rent on units, vehicle spaces, mailboxes | Over the month of storage, whatever day it was paid |
| **Protection plan** | What tenants pay for Greens' own protection plan | Over the month it covers |
| **Administrative fee** | The one-time move-in fee | On the day of move-in |
| **Late fees** | Charged when rent is past due | The day the fee posts |
| **Other fees** | Lock cutting, returned payment and similar | The day the service happens |
| **Merchandise** | Locks, boxes, tape sold at the counter | The day of sale, with the cost of the goods matched against it |

What each fee is and what to tell a tenant is in Self-Storage Fundamentals (the Greens Fees and Charges page). This lesson is what the books do with them.

## Monthly in Advance, and the 1st

Storage rent is billed **monthly in advance**: on the 1st, every tenant is charged the coming month. That makes the 1st the biggest day in the books. Every lease gets a charge: a receivable where the tenant has not paid, a draw on prepaid rent where they paid ahead. Over the month those charges are earned and collected.

A tenant who moves in mid-month pays a **prorated** first month. Cubby prorates by its own rule and the books follow it. Prorated rent is earned over the days it covers: a $150 unit rented on the 21st of a 30-day month brings $50 for the ten days, earned across those ten days.

## Four Rents That Are Not the Same Number

An argument about "the rent" is usually two people using two of these:

| Figure | What it is | The gap to the one before it |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross potential rent** | Every unit rented at today's street rate | |
| **Billed rent** | What was charged on the 1st, at each lease's own rate | Vacancy, and street versus in-place rates |
| **Earned rent** | Billed, less concessions and credits | Promotions, waivers, credits |
| **Collected rent** | What actually arrived | Delinquency |

The income statement reports **earned** rent. Gross potential rent and the gaps live on the operating reports (How a Storage Site Makes Money, in Self-Storage Fundamentals).

## Concessions and Waivers

A **concession** (a free month, half off the first month, a waived admin fee) reduces revenue, recorded so leadership can see what was given away to rent units. It is never hidden in the rent line and never booked as an expense. A waiver or credit after move-in is the same: a reduction of the revenue it waives, with the reason on the record (the Greens Waivers, Credits and Refunds page).

## Protection Plans and Insurance

Greens' **protection plan** is the company's own program: the tenant pays monthly and the company makes a limited promise about their goods. The payment is Greens' revenue, earned over the month; claims paid are Greens' expense.

**Tenant insurance** from a third-party insurer is the insurer's revenue. If it is collected with the rent and passed on, it is a liability until remitted, never revenue.

## Lien Sale Proceeds Are Not Revenue

When a defaulted unit is sold at a lien sale, the proceeds are applied to the tenant's balance owed: rent and fees that were already billed and already counted as revenue in their months. Counting the proceeds as revenue again would count that rent twice. Whatever is left after the balance owed belongs to the tenant: the company holds it as a liability and handles it as the state's lien law says. A lien sale is not a profit center; it is the end of a receivable.

## At Greens

Valley Center's manager writes her variance note: "Rental income $2,400 under: two free-month concessions on the new garages, both at full rent from 12/01." Earned rent is down by the concessions; billed rent shows the site full. Next month the line recovers on its own.

## Common Mistakes

- **Counting a tenant's early payment as this month's revenue.**
- **Booking a concession as an expense** or burying it in the rent line.
- **Treating third-party insurance collected as revenue.**
- **Calling lien sale proceeds income.** They settle a balance already counted.

## Key Points

- Each revenue line is earned when its service happens; rent is billed monthly in advance on the 1st, and prorations follow Cubby's rule.
- Gross potential, billed, earned and collected rent are four numbers; the income statement reports earned rent.
- Concessions and waivers reduce revenue, with the reason recorded.
- The protection plan is revenue; third-party insurance is a pass-through; lien sale proceeds settle the balance owed and any surplus is the tenant's.
