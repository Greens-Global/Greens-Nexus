## The Invoice Nobody Can Match

Accounting emails you: "Invoice from the gate vendor, $1,240, 'gate repair, Temecula.' Which job is this?" You have three open gate tickets. Until someone answers, the invoice waits and the vendor waits. A ticket number on the invoice would have made the question unnecessary.

## Accounts Payable

When a vendor invoice arrives, Accounting records the expense (or the asset) and a liability to the vendor, **accounts payable**. The invoice waits in payables until it is matched, approved and paid; then the liability and the cash both go down. How a bill moves through Intacct to a payment is the next lesson, Bills, Payments and Aging.

## The Three-Way Match

Before an invoice is paid, Accounting checks that three things agree:

| What | At Greens it is |
|---|---|
| **What was ordered** | The ticket's approved scope and quote, or a purchase order |
| **What was received** | The inspection and finished-work photo on the ticket; for goods, a receiving note |
| **What was billed** | The invoice, carrying the ticket number |

In most companies this is called matching the purchase order, the receiving report and the invoice. At a storage site the ticket usually plays the first two parts. When the three agree, the invoice is paid; when they do not, it waits and someone at the site is asked why. An invoice that cannot be matched is not paid, and a vendor who is not paid on time stops answering the phone at 9:00 PM. The site's side of this, from ticket to invoice, is in the Property & Vendor Management course.

## Coding

Every payable is **coded**: the account (repairs and maintenance, say) and the Intacct dimensions that say where: the **entity**, which is the property; the department; and, for a Construction job, its Project-Job. Coding is what makes a manager's cost report true. When you click a figure and find another property's vendor in it, that is a coding error to report to Accounting.

## Company Cards: Your Duties

Greens uses **Ramp** cards for purchases that do not go through payables: supplies, small parts, travel, software. A card charge is the same expense it would be on an invoice; only the timing and the paperwork change. In Nexus the card lines appear under Accounting > Reports > Ledger & Banking > Credit Card Activity.

Whoever holds a card owes four things on every charge:

- **A receipt**, attached promptly. The receipt is the proof; without it the expense cannot be defended to an auditor.
- **A memo that says what and why**: "Padlocks for vacant unit checks," not "supplies."
- **The right coding**, to the account and to the entity that used it. A Murrieta purchase coded to Fairfield puts the cost on the wrong site.
- **On time.** A charge with no receipt or coding sits in a holding account until someone explains it, and holds up the close.

The limits, categories and deadlines on your card are set in Ramp and by your Accounting team. A card is never how a vendor is paid for work on the property; that goes through payables and the three-way match.

## Cash Controls

Cash is where businesses are stolen from, so its controls are strict and the same everywhere:

| Control | What it means at Greens |
|---|---|
| **Segregation of duties** | The person who approves a payment does not make it; the person who reconciles the bank is neither |
| **No site pays a vendor** | Every vendor payment runs through payables, against a matched invoice |
| **Deposits intact** | What Cubby took in is what reaches the bank, nothing netted against it |
| **Card receipts on time** | A missing receipt is followed up; a pattern is a conversation |
| **Refunds through the system** | A tenant refund goes through Cubby and Accounting, never from a drawer |
| **The monthly reconciliation** | See Bank Reconciliation |

## At Greens

The $1,240 invoice turns out to be ticket #4790. The manager replies within the hour with the number, Accounting matches the approved quote, the finished-work photo and the invoice, and the bill goes on the next payment run. She asks the vendor to put the ticket number on every invoice from now on.

## Common Mistakes

- **An invoice with no ticket number.** It cannot be matched, so it is not paid.
- **Paying a vendor on a card** because it is faster.
- **A card charge with no receipt, a memo of "misc," or the wrong entity.**
- **Netting cash**: paying a small expense out of the day's money.

## Key Points

- An invoice is an expense and a payable when received; it is paid after the three-way match: ordered, received, billed.
- Coding (account, entity, department, Project-Job) puts the cost on the right site.
- On a company card: a receipt, a memo that says what and why, the right coding, on time.
- Cash controls: segregated duties, no payments from the site, deposits intact, refunds through the system, a monthly reconciliation.
