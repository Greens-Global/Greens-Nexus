## Bad Debt Is Up

Escondido's bad debt expense rose for the quarter, and the owner's review wants to know why. Accounting did not cause it and cannot fix it. The answer is in the site's aging, and so is the fix.

## A Receivable Is Rent Owed

When the 1st bills every tenant, each unpaid charge is a **receivable**: money the tenant owes the company. Most clear within days as autopay runs and tenants pay. The ones that do not are **delinquent**, and delinquency is the one part of accounting the site controls directly.

## Reading the Aging

An **aging report** sorts every unpaid balance by how long it has been unpaid. In Nexus it is **Aged Receivables** under Operations > Reports, read from Cubby for one facility or all of them:

| Column | Where the tenant usually is |
|---|---|
| **0-30 Days** | The normal lag after the 1st, and the first late fee |
| **31-60 Days** | Calls, the gate code changed, the overlock |
| **61-90 Days** | Lien notices under way |
| **91-120 Days** | Near or at the sale |
| **120+ Days** | Should be rare; ask why it is still here |

The columns follow the delinquency process taught in Self-Storage Fundamentals (the Greens Delinquency Timeline page has Greens' own days). Accounting reads the same process as money: how much sits in each column, and how much of it will ever be collected. The further right a balance sits, the less of it arrives.

## The Allowance: Expecting Some Loss

Accrual books record rent as earned on the 1st, paid or not. Some of it will never arrive, and matching says that loss belongs in the same period as the revenue, not months later. So each month Accounting records an **allowance for doubtful accounts**: an estimate of what will not be collected, built from the aging (a small share of the younger columns, most of the oldest).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Bad Debt Expense | the estimate | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | the estimate |

The allowance is a **contra asset**: it sits against receivables, so the balance sheet shows what the company really expects to collect. Receivables of $90,000 with a $9,000 allowance is a net $81,000.

## Write-Off and the Lien Sale

When a balance is judged uncollectible, it is **written off** against the allowance: debit the allowance, credit tenant receivables. No new expense is recorded, because the expense was recorded when the allowance was built.

A lien sale's proceeds are applied to the tenant's balance owed, so less is written off. They are not revenue: that rent and those fees were counted as revenue when they were billed. If the sale brings more than the tenant owed, the surplus is the tenant's, held as a liability and handled as the state's lien law says (see Revenue at a Storage Company). A payment from a former tenant after write-off comes back as a **recovery**, never as new rent.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. The lien process is the law's, step by step. Accounting records what the process produced; it never speeds the process up or decides when a unit is sold.

## The Numbers a Site Is Measured On

| Number | What it is | What moves it |
|---|---|---|
| **Delinquency rate** | Past-due balances as a share of rent billed | Autopay offered at move-in, calls on schedule, complete move-in details |
| **Aging mix** | How much sits in the older columns | How promptly the lien process runs |
| **Bad debt as a share of revenue** | Allowance expense against revenue | All of the above, over the year |
| **Autopay share** | Tenants paying by autopay | Offered at every move-in |

## At Greens

Escondido's manager opens Aged Receivables. The 61-90 and 91-120 columns have grown, and the units in them are ones where the lien process stalled waiting on a notice. The manager works the list with the desk, the units move through the process on schedule, the balances are settled or written off against the allowance that already expected them, and next quarter's aging is shorter.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating receivables as money in the bank.** A receivable is a promise; the allowance says how good.
- **Expecting a write-off to show up as a new expense.**
- **Counting lien sale proceeds or a recovery as rent.**
- **Blaming Accounting for bad debt.** The site's habits set it.

## Key Points

- A receivable is rent owed; Aged Receivables sorts it into 0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 91-120 and 120+ day columns.
- The allowance records the expected loss in the period of the revenue; a write-off clears the balance against it with no new expense.
- Lien sale proceeds settle the balance owed and are not revenue; any surplus is the tenant's, a liability.
- Delinquency rate, aging mix, bad debt share and autopay share are the site's numbers, and the site's habits move them.
