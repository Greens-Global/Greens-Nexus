## Three Payments, Three Homes

On 11/28 a new assistant manager at Escondido takes December's rent from a tenant, a $100 security deposit on a new unit, and buys $40 of printer paper on the office card. Cash moved three times. Each one lands in a different kind of account, and by the end of this lesson you can say which.

## The Equation

Everything a business has was paid for by someone. Either the business owes it to somebody (a loan, an unpaid invoice, rent a tenant paid ahead) or it belongs to the owners:

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity.**

A $500,000 building bought with a $400,000 loan and $100,000 of the owners' money is $500,000 of assets on the left and $400,000 of liability plus $100,000 of equity on the right. When the building earns rent, cash goes up and so does equity (the profit is the owners'). When a vendor is paid, cash goes down and so does the liability. It never stops balancing.

## The Five Kinds of Account

Revenue and expenses are how equity changes during the year, so they get accounts of their own and roll into equity at year end. That makes five kinds:

| Kind | What it holds | Grows on the | Storage examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asset** | Things of value the business controls | Debit side | Cash, tenant receivables, prepaid insurance, land, buildings, equipment |
| **Liability** | What the business owes | Credit side | Accounts payable, accrued payroll, prepaid rent, security deposits held, sales tax payable, the mortgage |
| **Equity** | The owners' share | Credit side | Capital put in, profits kept, less distributions |
| **Revenue** | What was earned in the period | Credit side | Rental income, late and admin fees, protection plan, merchandise |
| **Expense** | What it cost to earn it | Debit side | Payroll, utilities, repairs, property tax, insurance, depreciation, interest |

Debit and credit are the next lesson. For now: assets and expenses grow on one side; liabilities, equity and revenue grow on the other.

Not on the revenue list: lien sale proceeds. The money an auction brings in is applied to the tenant's balance owed, rent and fees that were already billed and counted as revenue. Any surplus belongs to the tenant under the lien law and is a liability until it is paid out. See the lesson Revenue at a Storage Company.

## The Chart of Accounts

The **chart of accounts** is the numbered list of every account the company uses. It is organized by kind, so the number tells you what something is before you read its name. The common convention runs 1000s assets, 2000s liabilities, 3000s equity, 4000s revenue, 5000s and up expenses. A storage chart usually splits rental income by product (units, parking, mailboxes), separates fees from rent, and gives repairs their own accounts by system so cost by system can be read without a spreadsheet.

On top of the account, Intacct tags every line with **dimensions**: which entity (the property), which department, which vendor or customer. That is how one repairs account is read per site. Greens' own account numbers and names are your Accounting team's; the General Ledger Accounts list under Accounting > Records shows them.

## The Same Word, Different Accounts

| The word | Could be | Kind of account |
|---|---|---|
| **Deposit** | Cash taken to the bank | An increase in the cash asset |
| | A security deposit a tenant paid | A **liability**: the company owes it back |
| | A deposit the company paid a vendor | An **asset** (a prepaid) until the work is done |
| **Rent** | Rent earned this month | **Revenue** |
| | Rent a tenant paid for next month | A **liability** (prepaid rent) until next month |
| **Insurance** | The premium Greens pays on the buildings | An **expense**, spread over the policy year |
| | What tenants pay for the protection plan | **Revenue** |

When you are not sure, ask: does the company own it or owe it (balance sheet), or did it earn or spend it this period (income statement)?

## At Greens

Back to Escondido. The December rent is prepaid rent, a liability, until 12/01. The $100 is a deposit held, a liability, until the tenant moves out. The paper is an office supplies expense. Cash went up by the first two and down by the third, and the equation still balances.

## Common Mistakes

- **Calling every payment revenue.** Rent paid ahead and deposits are liabilities.
- **Reading an account by its name alone.** Read its number and its kind.
- **Treating a big purchase as an expense.** A gate operator is an asset; see Fixed Assets and Depreciation.

## Key Points

- Assets = Liabilities + Equity, always.
- Five kinds of account: asset, liability, equity, revenue, expense.
- The chart of accounts is numbered by kind; dimensions such as the entity let one account be read per site.
- The same word can belong in different accounts: ask whether it is owned, owed, earned or spent.
