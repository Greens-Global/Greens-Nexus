## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Describe the month-end close as a sequence and say what each step produces.
- Read each of the three statements and say what question it answers.
- Connect the statements: how profit, cash and the balance sheet move together.
- Use four ratios to judge a storage site in a sentence each.

## The Close

The **close** is the work between the last day of a period and the day its statements are final. Nothing about a month is true until the close is done: the bills that arrived late, the depreciation, the allowance and the accruals all land during it.

Where the site comes in: the cut-off depends on tickets being resolved and invoices carrying their numbers; the revenue step depends on Cubby's move-ins, move-outs and waivers being complete and reasoned; the review step asks the manager to explain what moved. A manager who answers variance questions the same day shortens the close for everyone.

The balance sheet is a photograph of the company at a moment: the last day of the period. It is the accounting equation laid out.

What a storage balance sheet tells you: how much cash there is against what is owed this year (liquidity), how much of the property is financed by debt (leverage), how much rent has been paid ahead (a liability that is also a sign of a healthy tenant base), and how old the assets are (accumulated depreciation against cost).

## The Income Statement

The income statement is a film of a period: what was earned and what it cost. A storage income statement reads from the top:

| Line | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Rental income** | Earned rent by product |
| **Other income** | Fees, protection plan, merchandise, lien proceeds |
| **= Total revenue** | |
| **Operating expenses** | Payroll and taxes, utilities, repairs and maintenance, property tax, insurance, marketing, management fees, office, bad debt |
| **= Net operating income (NOI)** | Revenue less operating expenses: the number the industry values a site on |
| **Depreciation and amortization** | The non-cash spread of the assets' cost |
| **Interest** | On the mortgage |
| **= Net income** | What is left for the owners |

**Net operating income** is the storage industry's number: a site's revenue less the cost of operating it, before financing and depreciation. Two sites with the same NOI are worth about the same whatever their loans. A manager moves NOI; a manager does not move interest or depreciation.

## The Cash Flow Statement

The cash flow statement answers the third question, where the cash went, in three parts:

| Section | What it holds | At a storage company |
|---|---|---|
| **Operating** | Cash from running the business | Rent collected less bills and payroll paid; starts from net income and adds back depreciation, adjusts for receivables and payables moving |
| **Investing** | Cash spent on or received from assets | A new roof, a repave, a gate operator; proceeds from selling equipment |
| **Financing** | Cash from or to lenders and owners | Loan draws and payments, owner distributions |

The statement explains how a profitable month can leave the bank balance lower (a repave in investing) or how a loss month can leave it higher (a loan draw in financing). Depreciation is added back because it was an expense that moved no cash.

Net income from the income statement flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet. The change in cash between two balance sheets is what the cash flow statement explains. A set of statements that does not tie together this way has an error in it somewhere, and finding it is what the close's review step does.

## Four Ratios for a Storage Manager

| Ratio | How | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **NOI margin** | NOI divided by revenue | How much of each rent dollar the site keeps after operating it; the number to compare sites on |
| **Expense ratio** | Operating expenses divided by revenue | The mirror of the margin; rising means costs are outrunning rent |
| **Economic occupancy** | Earned rent divided by gross potential rent | How much of the site's potential it is collecting, after vacancy, discounting and concessions |
| **Delinquency rate** | Past-due balances divided by rent billed | How well the site collects |

Each one is a sentence a manager should be able to say about their site every month, from the report, not from memory.

## A Greens Scenario

The owner's review of Murrieta asks why NOI fell while occupancy held. The manager has already read the statements: revenue was flat, repairs and maintenance rose (three gate emergencies), and the margin fell two points. Depreciation and interest did not move; the fall is in the operating line he controls. He brings the gate's ticket history and the capital case for the operator. The review takes five minutes.

## Common Mistakes

- **Reading a month before the close.** It is not a month yet.
- **Judging a site on net income** when the question is how it is run; NOI is the operating number.
- **Treating depreciation as cash.** It is added back on the cash flow statement for a reason.
- **Not knowing the four ratios** for your own site.

## Key Points

- The close turns a period into statements: cut off, reconcile, revenue, payables and accruals, adjustments, review, lock; the site's habits shorten it.
- The balance sheet is a moment (assets = liabilities + equity); the income statement is a period (revenue to NOI to net income); the cash flow statement explains the change in cash in operating, investing and financing.
- Net income flows into equity; cash flow explains the change between two balance sheets; the three tie together or something is wrong.
- NOI margin, expense ratio, economic occupancy and delinquency rate: one sentence each, every month.
