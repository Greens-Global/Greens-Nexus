## A New Operator, Not a Repair

Temecula's gate operator fails for the third time and a $3,800 replacement is approved. The manager expects repairs to blow past budget this month. It will not: the new operator is an asset, and only about $32 of it reaches the income statement each month.

## A Building Is Not an Expense

If Greens expensed a $4 million building the year it was bought, that year would show a huge loss and every year after a free building. Neither is true. The building serves for decades, so its cost is spread over the years it serves. That spreading is **depreciation**, and the building, the lot, the canopies and the gate operators are **fixed assets**.

Which spends are capitalized is the company's **capitalization policy**: a dollar threshold (small purchases are expensed whatever they are) and a test. Does the spend **add value or extend life** (capital), or **keep the asset working as it was** (repair)? Your Accounting team has Greens' threshold and the useful lives it uses for each kind of asset. The site's job is to describe the work accurately on the ticket and the invoice so the right call can be made.

| The work | Usually |
|---|---|
| Replace every roof panel on a building | Capital |
| Replace two damaged panels | Repair |
| Repave the lot | Capital |
| Crack seal, sealcoat, stripe | Repair |
| New gate operator | Capital |
| New sprocket, chain, photo eye | Repair |
| New canopy row | Capital |
| Replace a bent canopy post | Repair |

One visit can be both. A roofer who replaces a whole roof section and clears the gutters sends one invoice; Accounting splits it by the scope on the ticket. That is why a scope is written line by line.

## Depreciation

**Straight-line depreciation** spreads cost evenly over the useful life: cost divided by life, recorded every month.

| Asset | Cost | Life used in this example | Per year | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A gate operator | $3,800 | 10 years | $380 | $31.67 |
| A canopy row | $120,000 | 25 years | $4,800 | $400 |
| A repave | $90,000 | 15 years | $6,000 | $500 |
| Land | $1,000,000 | Never | $0 | $0 |

Each month: debit Depreciation Expense, credit **Accumulated Depreciation**. Accumulated depreciation is a contra asset that sits against cost, so the balance sheet shows **net book value** (cost less depreciation so far). A ten-year-old operator that cost $3,800 has a net book value near zero, one more reason its replacement is a planned capital case, not a surprise.

Land never depreciates; it does not wear out. Tax depreciation follows its own rules, so the books and the tax return differ; Accounting keeps both.

While a project is under way its costs sit in **construction in progress**. When the work is done and in use, it is **placed in service** and depreciation begins. Every capitalized asset then has a row in the **fixed asset register**: what it is, which entity, in-service date, cost, life, accumulated depreciation, net book value. The register is the accounting twin of the property's records in Asset Management: the warranty there says who installed it; the register says what is left of its cost.

## When an Asset Leaves

- **Replaced:** the old asset's cost and accumulated depreciation come off; any net book value left is a loss (or a gain, if sold for more).
- **Sold:** proceeds against net book value give a gain or a loss.
- **Destroyed:** a storm takes a canopy row; the net book value is written off and the insurance recovery is recorded against the loss. The photos and the incident record carry both the claim and the write-off.

## At Greens

The new Temecula operator is placed in service on 03/14 and goes on the register at $3,800 with a ten-year life: $31.67 a month from March. The old one, almost fully depreciated, comes off with a small loss. In Asset Management the manager adds the new warranty with its term and installer. Two records, one asset, each in its home.

## Common Mistakes

- **Expensing a capital purchase** so the budget looks better. It misstates this year and every year after.
- **Capitalizing a repair** to spread it out.
- **An invoice with no scope**, so capital and repair cannot be split.
- **A replaced asset left on the register.**

## Key Points

- A fixed asset serves for years and is depreciated; a repair is expensed in its period. The capitalization policy decides.
- Straight line is cost divided by life, monthly; net book value is cost less accumulated depreciation; land never depreciates.
- Project costs wait in construction in progress until placed in service; the register is the twin of Asset Management's records.
- A replaced, sold or destroyed asset comes off at net book value, with a gain, a loss or an insurance recovery.
