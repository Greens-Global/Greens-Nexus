## Where the Money Comes From

A storage site earns money from four sources:

| Source | Examples | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **Rent** | Monthly rent on units, parking spaces and mailboxes | By far the largest |
| **Fees** | Admin fee, late fee, lien and auction fees | Smaller, and many are tied to delinquency |
| **Protection** | Monthly protection plan charges (lesson 7) | A real and steady line |
| **Merchandise** | Locks, boxes, tape, covers | Small, but adds up |

Fees, protection and merchandise together are called **ancillary income**: income beyond rent.

## Rates

### Street Rate

The **street rate** is today's price for a new customer for a given unit type: what you would quote a caller right now. It changes often. Operators adjust it by size, by how full that size is, by season and by what competitors charge.

### In-Place Rate

The **in-place rate** is what an existing tenant actually pays now. Because rates change over time, two tenants in identical 10x10s next to each other can pay very different amounts. That is normal and not something to discuss between customers.

In mature facilities, in-place rates are often well above street rates. One large example: Public Storage's average in-place rate was 74% above its average move-in rate in Q4 2024 (as reported by Inside Self-Storage). The reason is the next topic.

### Web Rates and Counter Rates

Many operators price lower online than at the counter to win web shoppers. Always quote what the management system shows for that channel. Never invent a rate or a discount.

### Rate Increases on Existing Tenants

An **ECRI** (Existing Customer Rate Increase) is a rent increase for a current tenant. Storage agreements are month-to-month, so the rent can change with the notice the agreement requires.

Why operators do it: moving out costs a tenant a truck, a day and a lot of work, so many will accept a modest increase rather than move. And a tenant who moved in on a promotional rate is often paying well under the street rate.

Why it is risky: increases that are too large or too frequent drive move-outs, bad reviews and attention from lawmakers. California's SB 709 (lesson 8) came out of that concern.

What you do:

- **Never promise "your rate will never go up."** It is not true and, in California, the agreement now discloses the maximum the rent can reach in the first 12 months for agreements signed on or after 01/01/2026.
- **Explain calmly** when a tenant calls about an increase: it was sent with notice under the agreement, and you can help them look at options such as a smaller unit or an upper-floor unit.
- **Send a tenant who wants an exception to your manager.** Greens decides rate policy, not individual employees.

## Occupancy: Three Kinds

"How full is the site?" has three different answers. Knowing all three is what separates a professional from a beginner.

| Kind | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit occupancy** | Units rented / total units | How many doors are rented |
| **Physical occupancy** | Rented square feet / total rentable square feet | How much space is rented. The most quoted figure. |
| **Economic occupancy** | Rent actually achieved / gross potential rent at street rates | How much of the possible money the site actually earns |

**Gross potential rent** is what the site would earn if every unit were rented at today's street rate.

### Worked Example 1: Unit Occupancy at Greens

These are real counts of active and leased units (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026):

| Site and kind | Units | Leased | Unit occupancy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Escondido enclosed storage | 717 | 697 | 697 / 717 = 97.2% |
| Valley Center enclosed storage | 568 | 465 | 465 / 568 = 81.9% |
| Murrieta parking | 161 | 150 | 150 / 161 = 93.2% |
| Temecula parking | 481 | 333 | 333 / 481 = 69.2% |
| Fairfield parking | 303 | 211 | 211 / 303 = 69.6% |

What this tells you: Escondido's enclosed units are nearly full, so there is little to sell and rates can be firm. Temecula and Fairfield have many open spaces, so every vehicle caller matters.

### Worked Example 2: Unit Versus Physical Occupancy

A site has 200 units. 100 are rented, so unit occupancy is 50%. But suppose the rented units are mostly the big ones, and they add up to 80% of the site's square feet. Physical occupancy is 80%.

Same site, two very different numbers. Big units rented can make the site look fuller than its door count; small units rented can make it look emptier.

### Worked Example 3: Economic Occupancy

A small row has ten 5x10 units at a street rate of $50.

- Gross potential rent: 10 x $50 = **$500**
- All ten are rented, but one tenant pays $45 (an older rate). Rent achieved: 9 x $50 + $45 = $495. Economic occupancy: $495 / $500 = **99%**.
- Now one unit is given free to a charity. Rent achieved: 8 x $50 + $45 + $0 = $445. Economic occupancy: $445 / $500 = **89%**.

Unit occupancy is still 100%. Economic occupancy is 89%. Discounts, free months, units given away and tenants who do not pay all pull economic occupancy down. A common guide is to keep the gap between unit occupancy and economic occupancy small, about 3 points or less (Inside Self-Storage).

> [!TIP]
> "Full" is not the goal. Revenue is. A site at 95% physical occupancy with heavy discounts and lots of unpaid rent can earn less than a site at 88% where everyone pays a fair rate.

### Worked Example 4: What a Late Payer Does to the Numbers

A Temecula parking space rents for $164 a month (Temecula's median parking street rate, From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). The tenant is unpaid all month. The space counts as rented in unit and physical occupancy, but the $164 is not achieved, so economic occupancy drops. Good collections calls on the 2nd and the 6th (lesson 10) protect economic occupancy.

## Concessions

A **concession** is a discount or promotion that brings in a move-in: a first month free, $1 for the first month, half off for two or three months, or a free truck rental.

| Concessions do | Concessions also |
|---|---|
| Bring in move-ins and win price shoppers | Lower economic occupancy while they last |
| Fill units that have sat empty | Anchor the customer to a low rate, which is one reason a first rate increase can come early |

In California, a promotional rate and its length must be disclosed on the first page of agreements signed on or after 01/01/2026 (SB 709, lesson 8). At Greens, Cubby showed no active discount programs (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Never invent a promotion. Offer only what the system shows.

## Fees

Storage sites charge fees for setup, for late payment and for the costs of the lien process. Typical fees in the industry:

| Fee | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Admin fee** | One time, at move-in, for setting up the account |
| **Late fee** | When rent is late; capped by law in California |
| **Lien and notice fees** | The cost of sending statutory notices |
| **Advertising and auction fees** | The cost of advertising and holding a lien sale |
| **Lock removal or cut-lock fee** | Cutting a lock and inventorying a unit |
| **Returned payment (NSF) fee** | A bounced check or failed payment, where charged |
| **Deposit** | Some operators charge a security or cleaning deposit |

Greens' amounts are on the Greens Fees and Charges page, the next lesson in this course. A few you will use every day:

- Administration Fee: **$25.00**, once, at move-in (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Rent is due on the **1st** of every month at every Greens site (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Late fee: posted by Cubby at 12:00 AM on the **11th**, after 10 full days unpaid, at the California legal maximum for the rent (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).

### Worked Example 5: California Late Fee Math

California's late fee cap (Bus. & Prof. Code 21713.5) depends on the monthly rent:

| Monthly rent | Maximum late fee |
|---|---|
| $60 or less | $10 |
| Over $60 and under $100 | $15 |
| $100 or more | The greater of $20 or 15% of monthly rent |

- **$180 rent:** 15% of $180 = $27. $27 is more than $20, so the maximum is **$27**.
- **$120 rent:** 15% of $120 = $18. $20 is greater, so the maximum is **$20**.
- **$75 rent:** over $60 and under $100, so the maximum is **$15**.
- **$50 rent:** $60 or less, so the maximum is **$10**.

Greens' CA Late Fee equals this maximum for every rent band (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). You never type a late fee by hand; Cubby posts it.

> [!WARNING]
> Never add a fee that is not in the rental agreement and on Greens Fees and Charges. Charging a fee that is not in the agreement is something an operator cannot do.

## Merchandise

Locks, boxes (small, medium, large, wardrobe, dish-pack), tape, bubble wrap, mattress and furniture covers, moving blankets and dollies. Nationally, 41% of renters buy supplies (SSA Demand Study, 2023). A disc lock at move-in is the most important: it is revenue and security at the same time.

How to offer without pressure: "Do you have a lock, or would you like one of ours? It's a disc lock, which is much harder to cut." And: "Do you have enough boxes and tape for the move?"

## Revenue Management, in Plain Words

Revenue management is how an operator sets prices. The basic idea:

- **Raise rates** on sizes that are nearly sold out.
- **Hold or lower rates** on sizes with many empty units.
- Watch **seasons**: spring and summer are peak moving months.
- Watch **competitors'** rates.
- Balance **street rates** (to fill units) and **rate increases** (to grow revenue from sitting tenants).

Greens sets these decisions centrally. Your job is not to set prices. Your job is to keep the inputs honest and the sales clean.

## How Your Habits Make or Lose Money

| Your habit | What it does to revenue |
|---|---|
| Answer every call and follow up on every web lead | More move-ins |
| Quote the system's current rate | No lost money from made-up discounts |
| Size customers correctly | Fewer early move-outs and transfers |
| Offer protection, a lock and supplies at every move-in | Ancillary income |
| Offer autopay | Fewer late payments |
| Call on the 2nd and the 6th | Fewer late fees, fewer liens, higher economic occupancy |
| Keep unit status accurate on your walk | No unit lost as "occupied" when it is empty |
| Turn vacant units fast | Units back on the market sooner |

A unit wrongly marked occupied or not rentable is lost revenue every day it sits that way.

## Common Mistakes

- **Making up a discount** to close a sale.
- **Promising a rate will never change.**
- **Comparing two tenants' rates** with either of them.
- **Thinking full means successful.** Watch economic occupancy.
- **Forgetting to offer** a lock, supplies, protection and autopay.

## Key Points

- Street rate is today's price for a new customer; in-place rate is what an existing tenant pays.
- Rate increases on existing tenants are normal on month-to-month agreements, but never promise a rate will not change.
- Unit occupancy counts doors, physical occupancy counts square feet, and economic occupancy counts money actually earned. Revenue, not fullness, is the goal.
- Concessions bring move-ins but lower economic occupancy; offer only what the system shows.
- Greens' fees are on Greens Fees and Charges: $25.00 admin fee, rent due on the 1st, late fee posted on the 11th at the California maximum (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Locks, supplies, protection and autopay at every move-in are part of the job.
