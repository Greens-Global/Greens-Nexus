## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Protect tenant information and confirm identity before discussing any account.
- Answer a caller who is not on the account without confirming anything.
- Respond correctly to police with a search warrant, police without one, and a subpoena.
- Handle deceased-tenant and bankruptcy situations by escalating correctly.
- Apply the ADA basics: accessible units, accessible routes and reasonable help.
- Treat every customer the same way, every time.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## Tenant Information Is Private

Everything about a tenant is confidential:

- their name, address, phone and email;
- their unit number and gate code;
- their balance and payment history;
- **even the fact that they rent from Greens.**

You discuss an account only with the tenant, after you confirm who they are, and with people the tenant has listed as authorized on the account.

### Who Gets What

| Person | Account information | Access to the unit |
|---|---|---|
| The tenant, identity confirmed | Yes | Yes, with their own gate code |
| An authorized person listed on the account | As Greens' policy allows | As Greens' policy allows |
| The California alternate contact | Receives lien notices only; no account details unless also listed as authorized | No, unless also listed as authorized |
| A spouse, parent, roommate, ex, landlord, neighbor | No | No |
| A process server or private investigator | No | No |
| Police without a warrant | Not without legal process; call your manager | No |

The words to use with anyone not on the account:

> "I'm not able to share whether that person is a customer."

Say it kindly, the same way every time. Do not hint, do not say "I can't say much, but...", and do not take a message "for them" in a way that confirms they rent.

### Why It Matters So Much

Some customers store their things because they are leaving a dangerous relationship. Others are in a divorce, a custody dispute or a fight with a landlord. Telling the wrong person that someone rents at Greens, or which unit, can put a person in danger. Privacy is a safety rule, not just a policy.

> [!TIP]
> Gate codes are passwords. Never read one to an unverified caller, never say one out loud where others can hear, and never write one on a receipt in front of others.

### Confirming Identity

- **On the phone:** confirm identity the way Greens requires before you discuss a balance (see Greens Privacy and Records).
- **In person:** photo ID that matches the lease.
- **When in doubt:** do not discuss the account. Offer to call the tenant back at the number on file.

## Police and Legal Requests

Police officers, lawyers and courts sometimes ask for tenant information or access to a unit. You do not need to know the law in detail. You need to follow the steps and call your manager.

### A Search Warrant

A search warrant is signed by a judge and lets police search a named place.

1. **Ask to see the warrant.** Check that it names Greens' facility and the unit or tenant.
2. **Note** the lead officer's name, agency, badge number and the warrant number.
3. **Get a copy** of the warrant.
4. **Provide access as needed:** open the gate. Officers may cut the lock.
5. **Do not argue** about whether the warrant is valid, **do not help search,** and **do not get in the way.**
6. **Do not tip off the tenant** and do not turn off cameras.
7. **Call your manager** and write an incident report.
8. **Ask for the inventory** of anything the officers take.

### Police Without a Warrant

Politely explain: "Greens requires a warrant to open a unit, and legal process for records. Let me call my manager." Then call your manager. Special situations (an emergency, a probation or parole search, a unit already in the lien process) are decisions for your manager and counsel, never the front desk.

### A Subpoena for Records

A subpoena is a legal order to produce records.

1. **Do not ignore it.** Note the deadline.
2. **Send it to your manager the same day.**
3. **Keep the records it asks for,** camera footage included. Tell your manager so the system's automatic deletion is stopped.
4. Produce only what is asked, through your manager.

### Requests for Everything

"Give us a list of all your tenants": **never** from the front desk. Your manager handles any request for a rent roll or many records.

### Telling the Tenant

Generally, do not tell the tenant about a police request or a subpoena. Some warrants come with orders not to tell. Leave it to your manager.

## Collections Messages and Privacy

Never put a balance or a lien detail in a text, an email or a voicemail unless Greens' policy says so, and never in a public reply such as an online review. California's Rosenthal Act (Civil Code 1788 and following) and Texas Finance Code chapter 392 forbid harassing or misleading a person about a debt (see Greens Privacy and Records).

## Deceased Tenants

The Lines You Never Cross lesson covered the steps. In privacy terms: a family member is not on the account until documents say they have authority. Ask for a death certificate and the court or affidavit document showing who handles the estate, and give no account details or access until your manager has reviewed them.

## Bankruptcy

The Lines You Never Cross lesson covered the automatic stay. In privacy terms: you may confirm details to the tenant's bankruptcy attorney only as your manager directs, and you stop all collection contact right away.

## Fair Treatment for Everyone

A storage site is a business open to the public. Federal and state civil rights laws forbid refusing service or treating customers differently because of who they are.

- In **California**, the **Unruh Civil Rights Act** (Civil Code 51) covers every business establishment and protects characteristics such as race, religion and disability, among others.
- In **Texas**, federal law applies, including the Americans with Disabilities Act.

The practical rule is simple: **same rates, same rules, same deposits, same lien treatment for everyone.**

- Ask for the same ID and information from every customer.
- **Never ask about immigration status or nationality.**
- Never steer a customer toward or away from a unit, a building or a site because of who they are.
- Apply late fees, waivers and rules the same way for everyone.

People sometimes assume fair housing law applies to storage. It does not, because storage is not housing, but the civil rights laws above do.

## The ADA Basics

The **Americans with Disabilities Act** applies to storage sites.

### Accessible Units

The 2010 ADA Standards require a share of storage units to be accessible, spread across the different unit types and sizes:

| Total units at the facility | Accessible units required |
|---|---|
| 1 to 200 | 5% of the units, at least 1 |
| More than 200 | 10, plus 2% of the units over 200 |

**Worked examples:**

- A facility with **180 units:** 5% of 180 = **9** accessible units.
- A facility with **320 units:** 10 + 2% of (320 - 200) = 10 + 2% of 120 = 10 + 2.4, so **13** accessible units (rounded up).
- A made-up facility with **700 units** (a practice number, not a Greens site): 10 + 2% of 500 = 10 + 10 = **20** accessible units.

### Accessible Routes

The route to accessible units, the office, the restroom, the keypad and accessible parking must be accessible. Storage sites are frequent targets of ADA lawsuits over barriers. **Report any barrier right away:** a car in the accessible parking space, a broken ramp, a keypad blocked by a planter.

### How You Help

- **Offer an accessible unit** when a customer asks for one.
- **Never ask about the nature of a disability.**
- **Service animals** (dogs trained to do a task) are allowed in the office.
- **Offer help** reading or signing documents.
- **Ask your manager** about reasonable changes to a rule when a customer asks (for example, letting an authorized helper sign or open the unit).

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A woman calls: "My husband has a unit there. What's the balance?" She is not on the account. You say, "I'm not able to share whether that person is a customer," kindly, and offer nothing more.

**Temecula.** Two officers arrive with a search warrant for a space. You read it, note the lead officer's name, badge and the warrant number, get a copy, open the gate and call your manager. You do not call the tenant.

**Valley Center.** A process server asks which unit a man rents so she can serve papers. You do not confirm he is a customer, and you tell your manager.

**Fairfield.** A customer in a wheelchair asks for a ground-floor unit near the office. You show what is available and accessible, and offer to help with the paperwork. You do not ask about her disability.

**Murrieta.** A subpoena arrives asking for a tenant's gate log for a two-week period. You send it to your manager that morning and ask that the footage and logs be preserved.

**A Texas site.** A man asks whether Greens "rents to people without papers." You explain that Greens asks every customer for the same ID and information, and you do not ask about anyone's immigration status.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confirming** that someone rents at Greens to a person not on the account.
- **Reading a gate code** to an unverified caller.
- **Handing records or footage** to police without the process.
- **Tipping off a tenant** about a warrant.
- **Asking about a disability** or about immigration status.
- **Treating customers differently** on rates, rules or fees.

## Key Points

- Everything about a tenant is private, even the fact that they rent. "I'm not able to share whether that person is a customer."
- Confirm identity before you discuss any account. Gate codes are passwords.
- Search warrant: read it, note the details, get a copy, open the gate, do not help or obstruct, do not tip off the tenant, call your manager.
- No warrant: Greens requires one; call your manager. Subpoenas go to your manager the same day, and records are preserved.
- Deceased tenants and bankruptcies are escalated; no access or details without documents.
- Same rates, same rules, same treatment for everyone; never ask about immigration status or a disability.
- ADA: 5% of units (at least 1) up to 200 units; 10 plus 2% over 200; report any barrier.
