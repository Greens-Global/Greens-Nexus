## "What's My Husband's Balance?"

A woman calls Escondido: "My husband has a unit there. What does he owe?" She sounds friendly and sure. She is not on the account. This lesson teaches the one sentence you say to her, how to confirm who you are really talking to, what to do when police or a subpoena arrive, and the fair-treatment and accessibility rules every site follows.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## Tenant Information Is Private

Everything about a tenant is confidential: name, address, phone, email, unit number, gate code, balance, payment history, and **even the fact that they rent from Greens.** You discuss an account only with the tenant, once you have confirmed who they are, and with people the tenant listed as authorized.

| Person | Account information | Access |
|---|---|---|
| The tenant, identity confirmed | Yes | Yes, with their own gate code |
| An authorized person on the account | As Greens' policy allows | As Greens' policy allows |
| The California alternate contact | Lien notices only | No, unless also authorized |
| Spouse, parent, roommate, ex, landlord | No | No |
| Process server, private investigator | No | No |

To anyone not on the account, kindly and the same way every time:

> "I'm not able to share whether that person is a customer."

Do not hint, do not say "I can't say much, but...", and do not take a message "for them" in a way that confirms they rent. Some customers are leaving a dangerous relationship. Privacy is a safety rule.

## Confirming Who You Are Talking To

- **The caller gives the details; you never read them out first.** Ask for the full name on the lease and details only the tenant would know, such as the unit number or the phone or email on file. Never say "Is this about unit 214?" or "Your number ends in 55, right?"
- **In person:** photo ID that matches the lease.
- **When in doubt:** do not discuss the account. Offer to call back at the number on file.
- **Gate codes are passwords.** Never read one to a caller you have not verified, never say one where others can hear, never write one on a receipt in front of others.

Exactly which details Greens accepts on the phone: your manager has this until the Greens Privacy and Records page lists it.

## Police and Legal Requests

You need the steps, not the law.

**A search warrant** is signed by a judge.
1. Ask to see it; check it names Greens' facility and the unit or tenant.
2. Note the officer's name, agency, badge number and the warrant number.
3. Get a copy.
4. Open the gate and step back. Officers may cut the lock.
5. Do not argue, help search, get in the way, tip off the tenant or turn off cameras.
6. Call your manager and write an incident report; ask for the inventory of anything taken.

**Police without a warrant:** "Greens requires a warrant to open a unit, and legal process for records. Let me call my manager."

**A subpoena** is an order to produce records. Never ignore it: note the deadline, send it to your manager the same day, and ask that the records it names, camera footage included, be preserved before anything is overwritten.

**"Give us a list of all your tenants":** never from the front desk. Your manager handles it.

Generally, do not tell the tenant about a police request or subpoena; leave that to your manager.

A family member after a death, or a bankruptcy attorney, follows the steps in the lesson Lines You Never Cross.

## Fair Treatment

Federal and state civil rights laws forbid refusing service or treating customers differently because of who they are; in California the Unruh Civil Rights Act covers every business. The rule is simple: **same rates, same rules, same fees, same lien treatment for everyone.** Ask every customer for the same ID and information. Never ask about immigration status or nationality. Never steer anyone toward or away from a unit, building or site because of who they are.

## The ADA Basics

The Americans with Disabilities Act applies to storage sites. A share of units must be accessible, spread across sizes and types:

| Units at the facility | Accessible units required |
|---|---|
| 1 to 200 | 5%, at least 1 |
| More than 200 | 10, plus 2% of the units over 200 |

**180 units:** 5% of 180 = 9. **320 units:** 10 + 2% of 120 = 12.4, rounded up to 13. **700 units:** 10 + 2% of 500 = 20.

The route to accessible units, the office, the restroom, the keypad and accessible parking must stay clear; report any barrier right away (a car in the accessible space, a planter blocking the keypad). Offer an accessible unit when asked, never ask about the nature of a disability, allow service animals in the office, offer help reading or signing documents, and take requests for a change to a rule to your manager.

## At Greens

**Escondido.** The wife gets the sentence, kindly, and nothing more.

**Temecula.** Officers bring a warrant. You read it, note the details, get a copy, open the gate and call your manager. You do not call the tenant.

**Fairfield.** A customer in a wheelchair asks for a unit near the office. You show what is accessible and offer help with the paperwork. You do not ask about her disability.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confirming that someone rents** to a person not on the account.
- **Reading account details to a caller** so they can say "yes, that's me."
- **Handing records or footage** to police without legal process.
- **Asking about a disability** or immigration status.

## Key Points

- Everything about a tenant is private, even the fact that they rent: "I'm not able to share whether that person is a customer."
- The caller gives the identifying details; you never read them out first.
- Warrant: read it, note it, copy it, open the gate, do not help or obstruct, call your manager. Subpoenas go to your manager the same day.
- Same rates, rules and treatment for everyone.
- ADA: 5% of units up to 200 (at least 1); 10 plus 2% over 200.
