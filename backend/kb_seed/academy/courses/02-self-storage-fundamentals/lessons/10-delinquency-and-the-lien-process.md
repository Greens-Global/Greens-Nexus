## Why This Lesson Matters

A lien sale is the most serious thing a storage operator does: it sells a person's belongings. The law sets strict steps, notices and minimum days, and a mistake can make a sale unlawful and expose Greens to real liability. Your part is to do each step the day it is due, record it, never skip or shortcut anything, and escalate the moment something unusual happens.

Most delinquencies never reach a sale. A tenant who is reached early, with a kind and clear call, usually pays. Good collections are the best lien prevention there is.

Greens' lien workflow is run exactly as Greens' counsel, its rental agreement and Cubby's configured steps say. This lesson teaches the law so you understand why each step exists.

## The General Shape

Both states follow the same broad path:

1. Rent is due.
2. A short period passes.
3. A late fee posts (in California, not before 10 days unpaid).
4. Reminder calls, texts and emails.
5. The first formal, statutory notice.
6. The lien attaches or the goods are seized, and access is denied.
7. A notice of sale.
8. The sale is advertised.
9. A public sale, often online.
10. The proceeds pay the lien and costs, and any excess is held for the tenant.

At almost any point before the sale, the tenant can stop it by paying in full.

## California: The Self-Service Storage Facility Act

California's law is the **California Self-Service Storage Facility Act**, Bus. & Prof. Code 21700-21716. Here it is step by step.

### Step 1: The Lien Exists (21702)

The owner has a lien on all property stored for rent, labor, late fees and other charges, and for the expenses of preserving, selling or disposing of the property. The lien includes late fees.

### Step 2: The Preliminary Lien Notice (21703)

Once any part of the rent or other charges has been unpaid for **14 consecutive days**, the owner may send a **Preliminary Lien Notice**. It must go to:

- the tenant's **last known address**, AND
- the **alternate address**, if the tenant gave one,

by **certified mail**, by **first-class mail with a certificate of mailing**, or by **email** if the agreement and the tenant's signed consent allow it (21712).

The notice must contain:

- an itemized statement of what is owed and when each amount became due;
- a statement that the right to use the space ends on a date **not less than 14 days after mailing** unless the tenant pays;
- notice that access may then be denied and a lien imposed;
- the owner's or agent's name, street address and phone number.

### Step 3: The Lien Attaches and Access May Be Denied (21705(a))

If the full amount is still unpaid on the termination date in the preliminary notice, the lien attaches as of that date. The owner **may deny access, enter the space and remove the property to a place of safekeeping.**

### Step 4: The Notice of Lien Sale (21705(b))

Next, the owner sends a **Notice of Lien Sale** to both addresses by the same methods. It must say:

- the right to use the space has ended and the tenant no longer has access;
- the property is subject to a lien, the current amount, and that it grows with unpaid rent;
- the property may be sold on a date **not less than 14 days from mailing** unless the tenant returns a **Declaration in Opposition** by certified mail;
- the tenant can regain full use by paying the full lien before that date;
- any excess from the sale will be held for the tenant or another claimant for **one year**, then paid to the county.

A **blank Declaration in Opposition** form must be enclosed.

### Step 5: The Declaration in Opposition (21706, 21710)

A **Declaration in Opposition** is a form the tenant signs under penalty of perjury, with a current physical address and phone number, and returns by certified mail before the sale date. If a valid declaration arrives, **the owner may not sell.** The owner must instead go to court (civil or small claims) to enforce the lien.

> [!WARNING]
> Recognize a Declaration in Opposition the moment it arrives. Stop and give it to your manager the same day. Selling after a declaration arrives is a serious violation.

### Step 6: Advertising the Sale (21707)

Before the sale, the owner must advertise it in one of two ways:

| Option | What it takes |
|---|---|
| **(a) Newspaper only** | Once a week for **two consecutive weeks** in a newspaper of general circulation |
| **(b) Newspaper plus online** | **Once in a newspaper AND** on a publicly accessible website that customarily conducts or advertises online auctions, with the online ad up **at least 7 days** before the sale. This option came from **AB 542**, effective 01/01/2024. |

Where there is no qualifying newspaper, the notice is posted at least **10 days** before the sale in **six or more conspicuous places** near the sale. The ad must give the account holder's name and the facility's name and location.

### Step 7: The Sale (21707, 21711)

The sale must be **commercially reasonable**: an in-person auction or a sale on a publicly accessible auction website. A good-faith buyer at a lawful lien sale takes the goods free of the tenant's rights (21711).

### Step 8: Excess Proceeds (21705(b), 21707)

After the lien and the costs of sale are paid, any money left over is **held for one year** for the tenant or someone with a court order, then paid to the **county treasury**.

### The California Steps at a Glance

| # | Step | Minimum timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rent unpaid | Day 0 is the due date |
| 2 | Late fee may post | Not before 10 days unpaid (21713.5) |
| 3 | Preliminary Lien Notice to both addresses | After 14 consecutive days unpaid (21703) |
| 4 | Termination date; lien attaches; access may be denied | At least 14 days after the notice is mailed (21705(a)) |
| 5 | Notice of Lien Sale with blank Declaration in Opposition | After the lien attaches (21705(b)) |
| 6 | Sale date | At least 14 days after that notice is mailed |
| 7 | Advertising | Two weeks of newspaper, or newspaper plus 7 days online (21707) |
| 8 | Commercially reasonable sale | In person or on a public auction website |
| 9 | Excess proceeds | Held one year, then the county |

From due date to sale, the California process takes at least about six weeks, and in practice often 45 to 60 days or more.

### Email Notices in California

Email lien notices and sales on an auction website were added by **AB 1108**, effective **01/01/2018**, and made permanent by **AB 2960** in 2022. Email notice is allowed only when the agreement says lien notices may be emailed and the tenant signed consenting, and the owner must be able to show the notice was actually delivered and received (for example, the tenant acknowledged it, viewed it in a secure portal or replied). If delivery cannot be shown, the notice is re-sent by mail the way the statute requires.

## Texas: Property Code Chapter 59

Texas's law is **Texas Property Code chapter 59**, Self-Service Storage Facility Liens. Greens' Texas sites will follow it.

### Step 1: The Lien Exists (59.006, 59.021)

The lessor has a lien on **all property in the facility** for charges due and unpaid. Unlike California, the Texas lien **attaches when the property is placed** in the facility.

### Step 2: Enforcement Requires Bold or Underlined Lien Language (59.041)

The lien may be enforced without a court only if it is a contractual landlord's lien **underlined or in conspicuous bold print** in a written rental agreement, and chapter 59 is followed. Otherwise, only a court judgment will do.

### Step 3: The Notice of Claim (59.042, 59.043)

Before seizing and selling, the lessor must deliver a written **notice of claim**:

- **in person, by verified mail, or by email** to the tenant's last known postal or email address. Verified mail is mailed with proof of mailing; it is delivered when deposited with the U.S. Postal Service or a common carrier. Email is delivered when sent, and is allowed only if the agreement has underlined or bold email-notice language.

It must contain:

- an itemized account of the claim;
- the lessor's or agent's name, address and phone number;
- a statement that the contents have been seized under the contractual landlord's lien;
- a statement that if the claim is not paid by the **14th day after delivery**, the property may be sold at public auction (or a vehicle towed);
- an **underlined or bold request that a tenant in military service notify the lessor of their status immediately.**

When the notice of claim goes out is set by Greens' agreement and policy.

### Step 4: Advertising (59.044)

If the claim is unpaid after 14 days, the sale is advertised **once in each of two consecutive weeks** in a newspaper of general circulation in the county. If there is none, notices are posted at the facility and at least **five other conspicuous places** nearby. The sale may be held no earlier than the **15th day after first publication**, or after the **10th day after posting**.

### Step 5: The Sale (59.045)

The sale is public: at the facility, at a reasonably near public place, or through a **publicly accessible internet website** (allowed since 09/01/2017). It follows the advertised terms and goes to the highest bidder.

### Step 6: Redemption and Excess (59.008, 59.046)

Before the sale, the tenant may redeem by paying the lien plus the lessor's reasonable expenses. After the sale, the lessor sends written notice of any excess to the tenant's last known address and pays it if the tenant asks **within two years**; after that, the lessor keeps it.

## California and Texas Side by Side

| Step | California | Texas |
|---|---|---|
| Law | Bus. & Prof. Code 21700-21716 | Property Code ch. 59 |
| When the lien exists | Attaches on the termination date after the preliminary notice (21705) | Attaches when goods are placed (59.006) |
| Agreement must have | Written; 14-consecutive-day lien statement; alternate contact requested; signed email consent | Lien underlined or bold; email language underlined or bold; towing authorized |
| First notice | Preliminary Lien Notice after 14 days unpaid; at least 14 days to pay | Notice of claim; 14 days after delivery to pay |
| Delivery | Certified mail, first-class with certificate of mailing, or email with proof of receipt; to the tenant AND the alternate | In person, verified mail, or email |
| Access denied | After the termination date, when the lien attaches | On seizure under the notice of claim, as policy and counsel set |
| Tenant's shield | Declaration in Opposition stops the sale; owner must sue | Pay or redeem before sale; court |
| Advertising | Two weeks of newspaper, or newspaper plus 7 days online | Two consecutive weeks of newspaper, or posting |
| Sale | Commercially reasonable: in person or online | Public: at the facility, nearby, or a website; highest bidder |
| Excess proceeds | Held one year, then the county treasury | Held two years, then the lessor's |
| Late-fee cap | Yes (21713.5) | None in ch. 59 (as of 2026) |
| Military | Federal SCRA | Federal SCRA plus 59.010 and the bold military request in the notice |

## Vehicles and Vessels Follow a Different Path

A registered vehicle or vessel is not sold through the normal unit lien sale.

- **California:** a storage lien on a vehicle is enforced under **Civil Code 3071** (the DMV lien sale process), and on a vessel under **Harbors and Navigation Code 503**, not the unit sale steps above. Recorded lienholders on the title come first. As another path, the owner may tow a vehicle, watercraft or trailer once rent is 60 days unpaid and the preliminary notice has been sent, with at least 10 days' notice by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing (21702.5).
- **Texas:** vehicles, boats and outboard motors need their own notice to the last known owner and every recorded lienholder (59.0445). Since 2021 (SB 1181), a vehicle, boat or trailer may instead be towed to a licensed vehicle storage facility if the agreement authorizes it (59.052).

## Collections: Where You Make the Difference

The calls on the 2nd and the 6th are the most valuable thing you do in this whole process.

A good collections call:

1. **Confirm you are speaking with the tenant** before you mention a balance (the Privacy and Compliance lesson).
2. **Be kind and direct.** "Hi, this is Sam at Greens Storage. Your payment for this month didn't go through, and I wanted to help you get it sorted before any late fee."
3. **Offer the easy path:** take the payment now, or update the card for autopay.
4. **Record what happened:** reached or not, what was promised, by when.
5. **Never threaten, never mislead,** and never leave a balance or lien detail in a voicemail, text or email unless Greens' policy says so.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A tenant's autopay fails on the 1st. On the 2nd you call and help her update her card. She pays the same day. Nothing else posts. This is how most delinquencies should end.

**Valley Center.** A tenant pays half his balance on the 18th. The preliminary notice already went out on the 15th. Because a partial payment does not stop the ladder, you tell him the account is still past due and what the full amount is, and you note the call.

**Temecula.** A Notice of Lien Sale went out for a unit. A week later a certified envelope arrives with a signed form titled Declaration in Opposition. You give it to your manager that morning. The sale does not happen unless Greens goes to court.

**Murrieta.** A boat tenant is 61 days behind. Cubby raises "61st - Vehicle Lien Filing/Tow Notice." This is the vehicle path, not the unit sale. Your manager handles it with the RV course process.

**A Texas site.** A tenant's notice of claim was delivered by verified mail. She calls on day 10 and pays in full, including the lessor's reasonable expenses. The process stops.

## Key Points

- Most delinquencies end with an early, kind phone call. Calls on the 2nd and 6th prevent liens.
- California (B&P 21700-21716): Preliminary Lien Notice after 14 consecutive days unpaid, to the last known AND alternate addresses; termination at least 14 days later; lien attaches and access may be denied; Notice of Lien Sale with a blank Declaration in Opposition; sale at least 14 days later; advertised two weeks in a newspaper or newspaper plus 7 days online (AB 542); commercially reasonable sale; excess held one year, then the county.
- A Declaration in Opposition stops a California sale. Escalate it the same day.
- Texas (ch. 59): lien attaches when goods are placed; lien must be underlined or bold; notice of claim in person, by verified mail or by email; 14 days to pay; two consecutive weeks of ads; public sale, online allowed; excess held two years.
- Vehicles and vessels follow their own path in both states.
- Greens' ladder is on Greens Delinquency Timeline; the day-11 gate code change and day-20 overlock are under review with counsel (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- Payment in full stops everything; partial payment does not.
