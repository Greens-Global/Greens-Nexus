## The Numbers That Matter

Managers and owners watch a short list of numbers. You do not need to run the reports (Cubby Fundamentals covers that), but you should know what each number means and what you do that moves it.

| Metric | What it is | How to work it out | The habit that moves it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unit occupancy** | Share of units rented | Units rented / total units | Answer every call, follow up every lead |
| **Physical occupancy** | Share of square feet rented | Rented sq ft / total rentable sq ft | Size customers well; rent the big units too |
| **Economic occupancy** | Share of possible rent actually earned | Rent achieved / gross potential rent | Quote real rates, collect early, no made-up discounts |
| **Move-ins** | New rentals in a period | Count | Fast callbacks, good tours, clean site |
| **Move-outs** | Vacates in a period | Count | Good service, correct sizing, kind handling of rate changes |
| **Net rentals** | Growth or shrinkage | Move-ins minus move-outs | Both of the above |
| **Rentable units** | Units ready to sell | Count | Turn vacant units fast and mark them rent-ready |
| **Delinquency %** | Share of rent or units past due | Past-due amount / rent due (or units) | Calls on the 2nd and 6th, autopay at move-in |
| **Lien and auction count** | Units that reached a lien sale | Count | Early collections and complete move-in details |
| **Conversion rate** | Inquiries that become rentals | Rentals / inquiries | A great first call and a fast follow-up |
| **Average length of stay** | How long tenants stay | Average months | Service, sizing, honest rates |
| **Rent per square foot** | Rent level | Rent / square feet | Revenue management (set by Greens) |
| **Protection penetration** | Share of tenants with Greens' plan | Plan leases / all leases | Offer protection at every move-in |
| **Merchandise per move-in** | Supplies sold with rentals | Merchandise sales / move-ins | Offer a lock and supplies every time |
| **Autopay %** | Share of tenants on autopay | Autopay leases / all leases | Offer autopay at every move-in |
| **Online reviews** | Count and star rating | From review sites | Clean site, kind service, problems solved |

### Worked Example 1: Net Rentals

In one month a site has 18 move-ins and 13 move-outs. Net rentals = 18 - 13 = **+5**. The site grew by five rentals. If move-outs had been 21, net rentals would be **-3**, and the manager would ask why people left.

### Worked Example 2: Delinquency

A site bills $50,000 in rent on the 1st. On the 10th, $4,000 is still unpaid. Delinquency = $4,000 / $50,000 = **8%** of rent. If calls on the 2nd and 6th collect half of that before the 11th, delinquency drops to 4% and fewer late fees, gate changes and liens follow.

### Worked Example 3: Protection Penetration at Greens

Greens has 961 active plan leases and 1,078 tenants with their own policy (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Most own-policy leases are vehicles, which carry their own insurance by design. For storage units, the habit that matters is simple: offer the plan at every move-in, and record the own policy when the tenant has one.

### Worked Example 4: Conversion

A site gets 60 phone and web inquiries in a week and rents 15 units. Conversion = 15 / 60 = **25%**. If faster callbacks bring that to 20 rentals, conversion is 33%, with no more marketing spending.

### Worked Example 5: Average Stay and Value

The average stay in the industry is about a year and a half (the Why People Rent lesson). At Escondido's median enclosed street rate of $164 a month (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026), 18 months is $164 x 18 = **$2,952** of rent from one move-in. That is what a missed call can cost.

## Words to Know: The Glossary

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

| # | Term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Access control system | The gate keypad, codes and software that log who enters the property |
| 2 | Access hours | The hours tenants can enter through the gate; different from office hours |
| 3 | Admin fee | A one-time setup fee charged at move-in ($25.00 at Greens, From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026) |
| 4 | Alternate contact (California) | A second person and mail or email address the tenant names to receive lien notices (B&P 21712) |
| 5 | Ancillary income | Revenue beyond rent: protection, merchandise, fees |
| 6 | Auction (lien sale) | The public sale of a defaulted tenant's goods to satisfy the lien |
| 7 | Autopay | Automatic recurring card or bank payment |
| 8 | Bailment | A relationship where one party takes custody of another's goods; storage agreements create no bailment |
| 9 | Climate-controlled unit | A unit in a temperature-regulated (sometimes humidity-regulated) space |
| 10 | Commercially reasonable (California) | The standard a lien sale must meet: in person or on a public auction website (B&P 21707) |
| 11 | Concession | A discount or promotion, such as a free month |
| 12 | Conversion rate | The share of inquiries or reservations that become rentals |
| 13 | Cylinder lock | A lock built into the door latch, with a unique core per tenant |
| 14 | Declaration in Opposition (California) | A sworn form a tenant returns to stop a lien sale; the owner must then go to court |
| 15 | Delinquency | Rent past due |
| 16 | Disc lock | A round lock with a shrouded shackle; hard to cut |
| 17 | Drive-up unit | An exterior unit you can park in front of |
| 18 | ECRI | Existing Customer Rate Increase: a rent increase for a current tenant |
| 19 | Economic occupancy | Rent actually achieved / gross potential rent at street rates |
| 20 | Exclusive control | The tenant alone controls access to the unit and its contents |
| 21 | Gate code | A tenant's personal entry code; a password |
| 22 | Gross potential rent | The rent if every unit were rented at street rate |
| 23 | In-place rate | What an existing tenant pays now |
| 24 | Interior unit | A unit reached from inside a building |
| 25 | Late fee | A fee for late rent; capped in California (B&P 21713.5) |
| 26 | Lessor, owner, operator | The facility side. California law says "owner"; Texas says "lessor." |
| 27 | Occupant, tenant, lessee | The renter. California law says "occupant"; Texas says "tenant." |
| 28 | Letters testamentary, letters of administration | Court documents showing who controls a deceased person's estate |
| 29 | Lien | The facility's legal claim on stored property for unpaid charges |
| 30 | Preliminary Lien Notice (California) | The first statutory notice, after 14 consecutive days unpaid (B&P 21703) |
| 31 | Lockout | Suspending a tenant's gate access and/or overlocking the unit |
| 32 | Management system | The facility software for rentals, payments, gates and reports; at Greens, Cubby |
| 33 | Month-to-month | A rental that renews each month until ended |
| 34 | Move-in, move-out | A new rental; a vacate |
| 35 | Net rentable square feet | The total square feet available to rent |
| 36 | Net rentals | Move-ins minus move-outs |
| 37 | Notice of claim (Texas) | The statutory notice that starts the Texas lien sale clock (59.043) |
| 38 | Notice of Lien Sale (California) | The second notice, giving a sale date at least 14 days out (B&P 21705(b)) |
| 39 | Overlock | A facility lock placed on a unit to block access |
| 40 | Physical occupancy | Rented square feet / total rentable square feet |
| 41 | Protection plan | An operator program, not insurance, that waives the liability release and covers losses up to a limit |
| 42 | Rate lock | A promise not to raise rent for a period |
| 43 | REIT | Real Estate Investment Trust: a publicly traded real estate owner |
| 44 | Rent roll | A list of all rented units, tenants and rents |
| 45 | Rentable, unrentable unit | Ready to sell, or out of service |
| 46 | Reservation | A unit held for a future move-in |
| 47 | SCRA | Servicemembers Civil Relief Act: no storage lien enforcement on an active-duty servicemember, or within 90 days after, without a court order (50 U.S.C. 3958) |
| 48 | Smart entry | Bluetooth or app-based gate and unit access |
| 49 | Stabilized | A facility that has reached its target occupancy after its first lease-up |
| 50 | Street rate | Today's advertised move-in price |
| 51 | Tenant insurance | A third-party insurance policy covering the tenant's goods |
| 52 | Third-party management | An outside company running a facility for its owner |
| 53 | Unit mix | The combination of unit sizes and types at a facility |
| 54 | Verified mail (Texas) | Mail with proof of mailing through USPS or a common carrier; used for Texas notices since 2012 |

Also useful: **unit occupancy** (units rented / total units), **value limit** (a contract cap on the value a tenant may store), **walk-through** (the routine check of every unit and lock) and **vacant unit prep** (making a unit rent-ready).

## Ten Myths to Bust

| # | The myth | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "The facility's insurance covers my stuff." | It covers the facility. The tenant needs Greens' plan or their own policy (the Tenant Protection and Insurance lesson). |
| 2 | "Staff have a key and can check on my unit." | The tenant has the only key and exclusive control (the Access, Locks and Security lesson). |
| 3 | "Storage auctions are full of treasure." | Most liened units hold everyday household goods; TV shows are entertainment. |
| 4 | "You can sell a unit as soon as someone is late." | The law sets strict steps and minimum days (the Delinquency and the Lien Process lesson). |
| 5 | "A partial payment stops the auction," or "We can just toss the junk." | At Greens, a partial payment does not stop the ladder; nothing is thrown out outside the process (the Delinquency and the Lien Process and Lines You Never Cross lessons). |
| 6 | "Rent is fixed as long as I rent." | Agreements are month-to-month; rates can change with notice. California agreements from 01/01/2026 show the 12-month maximum on page one (the The Rental Agreement lesson). |
| 7 | "Police can always get in." | Only with a warrant or other legal basis, handled by the manager (the Privacy and Compliance lesson). |
| 8 | "Family can get into a relative's unit after a death." | Only with a death certificate and proof of authority (the Lines You Never Cross lesson). |
| 9 | "Storage is just real estate; service doesn't matter." | Most renters are in a life change; service drives reviews and how long they stay (the Why People Rent lesson). |
| 10 | "Bigger is always better to sell." | Over-selling leads to quick move-outs; under-selling ruins move-in day (the The Product: Units and Sizes lesson). |

## The Mistakes New Hires Make Most

1. Giving account or gate information to someone not on the account.
2. Not collecting complete contact information, the alternate contact (California) and military status at move-in.
3. Skipping or mis-dating a lien step, or emailing a notice without consent and proof.
4. Forgetting to remove an overlock after payment, or overlocking the wrong unit.
5. Marking units vacant or rented incorrectly, or skipping the honest walk-through.
6. Quoting rates or discounts the system does not show, or calling the protection plan "insurance."
7. Accepting cash without a system receipt.
8. Allowing unit power for appliances, letting someone "just sleep in the unit tonight," or ignoring hazardous storage.
9. Entering a unit or cutting a lock without the authorized process and a witness.
10. Repairing a roll-up door spring without training.
11. Confronting a suspected thief instead of calling 911.
12. Not escalating a Declaration in Opposition, a bankruptcy notice, a military flag or a death report.

## Key Points

- Know the core metrics: unit, physical and economic occupancy; move-ins, move-outs and net rentals; delinquency; conversion; length of stay; protection penetration; autopay.
- Every metric has a habit behind it: answer and follow up, size well, quote real rates, collect early, offer protection, a lock and autopay, keep the walk honest.
- Learn the 54 words; they are the language of the business and of this academy.
- Bust the ten myths kindly and accurately.
- Escalate the unusual: a Declaration in Opposition, bankruptcy, military service or a death.
