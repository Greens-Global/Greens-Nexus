- Recite the never-do list for delinquency and liens.

- Handle a report that a tenant has died.
- Apply the stop-and-escalate rule the moment something unusual happens.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## Why a Never-Do List

Most of storage work has room for judgment. The lien process does not. Every step is set by statute, by the rental agreement and by Greens' Cubby workflow, and one shortcut can make a sale unlawful, expose Greens to lawsuits and federal enforcement, and hurt a person who was already in a hard place.

The rule for this whole lesson: **when in doubt, stop and escalate.** Stopping is always safe. Pressing on is where the damage happens.

## The Never-Do List

### Notices and Timing

- **Never skip, shorten, re-order or back-date any notice or deadline.** The minimum days in the Delinquency and the Lien Process lesson are the law.
- **Never send a California notice to only one address** when an alternate contact is on file. Both get it.
- **Never "just email it"** without the agreement's email clause, the tenant's signed consent and a way to prove delivery. Otherwise, mail it the way the statute requires.
- **Never change a date in the system** to make a step look on time.

### Sales and Property

- **Never proceed after a Declaration in Opposition** arrives in California. The owner must go to court instead.
- **Never proceed after a bankruptcy notice** or any sign the tenant is on active military duty.
- **Never enter, remove, keep, give away, throw out or buy anything from a liened unit** outside the authorized process. Not "junk," not "something they'd never miss," not a box of old magazines.
- **Never let friends or family bid** at a Greens lien sale, and never bid yourself.
- **Never promise a customer they can "get their stuff back after the auction."** Once lawfully sold, the goods belong to the buyer.
- **Never treat personal papers and photographs carelessly.** Many operators set them aside for the tenant; follow your manager's direction.

### People and Information

- **Never discuss a tenant's delinquency with anyone not on the account:** a spouse, a parent, a roommate, an ex, a neighbor, a caller who "just wants to help."
- **Never threaten or mislead** a tenant about a debt, and never put a balance or lien detail in a public reply.

### Locks and Access

- **Never lock out or overlock by hand** outside what Cubby flags.
- **Never cut a lock** without the authorized, documented process (the Access, Locks and Security lesson).

> [!TIP]
> A simple test before any lien action: "If a judge read the file tomorrow, would every notice, date and decision be there, on time, and approved?" If you are not sure, stop and ask.

## Active-Duty Military: The SCRA

### What the Law Says

The federal **Servicemembers Civil Relief Act**, 50 U.S.C. 3958, says that a person holding a lien on a servicemember's property or effects (storage liens are named expressly) **may not foreclose or enforce the lien during the person's military service, or for 90 days after it ends, without a court order.** The court may pause the case or adjust what is owed.

How seriously it is taken:

- The U.S. Department of Justice treats it as **strict liability**: good intentions and honest mistakes are no defense.
- The Department of Justice has sued and settled with storage and towing companies that auctioned servicemembers' belongings without court orders. The settlements required operators to **check military status before every lien sale** (for example, through the Defense Manpower Data Center's SCRA website) and to get a court order first.

### Texas Adds More

- **Texas Property Code 59.010:** members of the Texas State Guard and Texas National Guard on active duty (more than 30 consecutive days) get the same protection as federal servicemembers under the SCRA.
- **Texas Property Code 59.043:** every Texas notice of claim must contain an **underlined or bold request** that a tenant in military service tell the lessor right away. The lessor may ask for written proof, such as Department of Defense documentation.

### California and Texas Side by Side

| | California | Texas |
|---|---|---|
| Federal SCRA (50 U.S.C. 3958) | Applies: no lien enforcement during service or 90 days after without a court order | Applies the same way |
| State addition | Ask your manager; state rules may add to the SCRA | 59.010 covers Texas State Guard and National Guard on active duty |
| In the notice | No state-specific military request | Bold or underlined military-status request required (59.043) |

### What You Do

1. **At move-in, ask:** "Are you or your spouse on active-duty military service?" Record the answer in the management system.
2. **When a tenant mentions military service at any time,** record it and tell your manager.
3. **Before any lien sale,** the military status check is done and kept with the file (your manager or the lien process owner runs it).
4. **If the tenant is or may be on active duty: stop.** No sale without a court order. Escalate the same day.

Fairfield sits next to Travis Air Force Base, and Austin-area Texas sites are within reach of Fort Cavazos (formerly Fort Hood). This is not rare.

## Bankruptcy

When a tenant files for bankruptcy, the filing creates an **automatic stay** (11 U.S.C. 362) the moment it is filed. The automatic stay means:

- **Stop the lien sale,** even on the day of the auction.
- **Stop collection calls, texts and emails** about the old debt.
- **Stop adding late fees** to the old debt.
- **Stop negotiating** the old debt.

Selling or collecting during the stay can make Greens liable.

What you do:

1. If a tenant, a lawyer or a court notice says the tenant filed for bankruptcy, **write down the case number, the court and the attorney's name and phone number.**
2. **Stop all collection activity** on the account right away and tell your manager the same day.
3. Your manager verifies the filing and decides next steps with counsel. Greens can ask the bankruptcy court for relief, and rent that comes due after the filing is handled through the court process.

## A Tenant Has Died

Death is one of the 4 Ds, and you will meet it. A family member calls or walks in: "My father rented a unit here. He passed away last week."

What the law and good practice say:

- **Only the tenant, and people listed as authorized on the account under Greens' policy, have access.** A family member does not automatically get in.
- To give access or account information, the facility generally needs a **death certificate** plus **proof of authority over the estate**, such as **letters testamentary** (an executor named in a will), **letters of administration** (a court-appointed administrator), or a **small-estate affidavit** where state law allows one (California and Texas both have small-estate procedures).
- **Rent keeps accruing,** and the estate is responsible for it.
- If the facility knows the tenant died, **a lien sale becomes legally sensitive.** Notices may need to go to the estate. Escalate.

What you do:

1. **Be kind.** "I'm so sorry for your loss."
2. **Do not confirm account details or give access** until your manager has reviewed the documents.
3. **Explain what is needed:** a death certificate and the court or affidavit document showing who handles the estate.
4. **Note the report** and tell your manager the same day, especially if the account is past due.

## The Stop-and-Escalate Rule

Stop the ladder and call your manager **the same day** when any of these happen:

| Event | Why you stop |
|---|---|
| A Declaration in Opposition arrives (California) | The owner may not sell; it must go to court |
| The tenant or spouse says they are on active military duty, or a check shows it | SCRA: no enforcement without a court order |
| A bankruptcy notice arrives, or a tenant says they filed | Automatic stay |
| A death report | Access and notices change; the sale is sensitive |
| The tenant disputes the balance in writing | The amount may be wrong |
| Anything you do not understand about a notice or a date | Stopping is safe; guessing is not |

When you escalate, give your manager the facts: the tenant, the unit, what arrived or what was said, when, and copies or photos of any document.

## Greens Scenarios

**Fairfield.** A tenant 25 days behind tells you on the phone, "I just got back from deployment." You record his military status, stop, and call your manager the same day. No lien step goes forward without your manager's decision.

**Escondido.** A cousin of a liened tenant asks if he can bid on the unit at the auction "to save the family stuff." You explain that you cannot discuss any tenant's account. Separately, you know that friends and family of staff never bid, and you mention the request to your manager.

**Valley Center.** A coworker suggests throwing out "obvious trash" from a liened unit before the sale to make it look better. You say no and tell your manager. Nothing leaves a liened unit outside the process.

**Temecula.** A woman calls to say her late husband rented an RV space. She is upset and wants the gate code to get his trailer. You offer condolences, explain the documents needed, and pass it to your manager. You do not give the code.

**A Texas site.** A tenant's attorney emails a bankruptcy case number the morning of an online auction. You stop, call your manager, and the unit is pulled from the sale.

## Common Mistakes

- **Pressing on to "keep things moving"** after one of the stop events.
- **Telling a family member** about an account before the documents are reviewed.
- **Continuing collection calls** after a bankruptcy notice.
- **Forgetting the military question** at move-in.
- **Taking or buying** anything from a liened unit, however small.

## Key Points

- Never skip, shorten, re-order or back-date a notice; in California, both addresses get every notice.
- Never sell after a Declaration in Opposition, a bankruptcy notice or a sign of active military duty.
- Never enter, take, keep, give away, throw out or buy anything from a liened unit; never let friends or family bid.
- Never discuss an account with anyone not on it.
- SCRA (50 U.S.C. 3958): no lien enforcement on a servicemember during service or 90 days after without a court order; strict liability. Texas adds 59.010 and a bold military request in every notice.
- Bankruptcy creates an automatic stay: stop the sale, the calls and the fees.
- A death report needs a death certificate and proof of authority before any access.
- When in doubt, stop and escalate the same day.
