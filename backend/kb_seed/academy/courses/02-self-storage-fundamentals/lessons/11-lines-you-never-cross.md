## "I Just Got Back From Deployment"

A Fairfield tenant 25 days behind tells you on the phone that he just got back from deployment. The ladder says the lien steps continue. The law says something else. Most of storage work has room for judgment; the lien process does not. This lesson is the never-do list and the four situations (military service, bankruptcy, death and a disputed balance) that stop everything.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## The Rule

**When in doubt, stop and escalate.** Stopping is always safe. Pressing on is where the damage happens.

> [!TIP]
> Before any lien action: "If a judge read the file tomorrow, would every notice, date and decision be there, on time and approved?" If you are not sure, stop.

## The Never-Do List

**Notices and timing**
- Never skip, shorten, re-order or back-date a notice or deadline, and never change a date in Cubby to make a step look on time.
- Never send a California notice to only one address when an alternate contact is on file.
- Never "just email it" without the agreement's clause, the tenant's signed consent and proof of delivery.

**Sales and property**
- Never go ahead after a Declaration in Opposition, a bankruptcy notice or any sign of active military duty.
- Never enter, take, keep, give away, throw out or buy anything from a liened unit outside the process.
- Never bid at a Greens lien sale, and never let friends or family bid (see the lesson Auction Day).

**People and information**
- Never discuss a delinquency with anyone not on the account.
- Never threaten or mislead a tenant about a debt, and never post a balance in a public reply.

**Locks**
- Never overlock by hand outside what Cubby flags; never cut a lock without the authorized process.

## Active-Duty Military: The SCRA

The federal **Servicemembers Civil Relief Act** (50 U.S.C. 3958) names storage liens: a lien on a servicemember's property **may not be enforced during military service, or for 90 days after it ends, without a court order.** The Department of Justice treats it as strict liability: an honest mistake is no defense, and it has sued operators that sold servicemembers' belongings. Settlements required a military status check before every sale.

Texas adds two things: Texas State Guard and Texas National Guard members on active duty get the same protection (59.010), and every Texas notice of claim carries a bold or underlined request that a tenant in military service say so (59.043).

What you do:
1. **At move-in, ask:** "Are you or your spouse on active-duty military service?" Record the answer in Cubby.
2. **Whenever a tenant mentions service,** record it and tell your manager.
3. **Before any sale,** the status check is run and kept with the file.
4. **If the tenant is or may be on active duty, stop.** No sale without a court order.

Fairfield sits next to Travis Air Force Base. This is not rare.

## Bankruptcy

A bankruptcy filing creates an **automatic stay** (11 U.S.C. 362) the moment it is filed: stop the lien sale (even on auction day), stop collection calls, texts and emails about the old debt, stop adding late fees to it, and stop negotiating it. Write down the case number, the court and the attorney's name and phone number, stop all collection on the account and tell your manager the same day. Your manager verifies the filing and decides next steps with counsel.

## A Tenant Has Died

A daughter calls: "My father rented here. He passed away last week."

- **Be kind:** "I'm so sorry for your loss."
- **A family member does not automatically get access or information.** The facility generally needs a **death certificate** plus **proof of authority over the estate**: letters testamentary (an executor under a will), letters of administration (a court-appointed administrator), or a small-estate affidavit where state law allows one.
- **Give no account details or access** until your manager has reviewed the documents. Explain what is needed.
- **Rent keeps accruing,** and the estate is responsible. A lien sale on a deceased tenant's unit is legally sensitive, so tell your manager the same day, especially if the account is past due.

## Stop and Escalate

| Event | Why you stop |
|---|---|
| A Declaration in Opposition (California) | The owner may not sell; it must go to court |
| Active military duty, said or found | SCRA: no enforcement without a court order |
| A bankruptcy notice, or the tenant says they filed | The automatic stay |
| A death report | Access and notices change; the sale is sensitive |
| A written dispute of the balance | The amount may be wrong |
| Anything you do not understand about a notice or date | Stopping is safe; guessing is not |

Call your manager the same day with the facts: the tenant, the unit, what arrived or was said, when, and copies or photos of any document.

## At Greens

**Fairfield.** You record the deployment, stop, and call your manager. No lien step moves without your manager's decision.

**Valley Center.** A coworker suggests tossing "obvious trash" from a liened unit. You say no and tell your manager.

**Temecula.** A widow wants the gate code to get her late husband's trailer. You offer condolences, explain the documents needed and pass it to your manager. You do not give the code.

## Common Mistakes

- **Pressing on** after a stop event to "keep things moving."
- **Continuing collection calls** after a bankruptcy notice.
- **Telling a family member** about an account before the documents are reviewed.

## Key Points

- Never skip, shorten or back-date a notice; in California both addresses get every notice.
- SCRA: no lien enforcement during service or 90 days after without a court order; honest mistakes are no defense.
- Bankruptcy: the automatic stay stops the sale, the calls and the fees.
- A death report needs a death certificate and proof of authority before any access.
- When in doubt, stop and escalate the same day.
