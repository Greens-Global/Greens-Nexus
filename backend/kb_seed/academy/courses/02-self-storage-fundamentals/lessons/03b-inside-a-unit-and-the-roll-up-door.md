## "Anyone Could Climb In"

A tenant on the second floor of the Escondido climate building says her unit "has no ceiling." Down the row, a Murrieta tenant has lifted his door by one corner and it is stuck a foot up. Both are normal storage-site moments. This lesson teaches what a unit is made of and how its door works, so you can reassure the first tenant and not make the second problem worse.

## A Unit Is Walls, Not a Room

```figure
{"kind": "drawing", "title": "Partitions, Mesh, Roof", "draw": "unit-mesh-ceiling", "label": "Steel partitions with mesh above them", "note": "The partitions stop short of the roof. Mesh closes the gap so nobody climbs over, and sprinklers, air and light reach every unit."}
```

- **The floor** is the building's concrete slab. A drive-up unit sits a little above the aisle, with a small rain lip at the door.
- **The walls** are steel partitions bolted to posts: not load-bearing, not soundproof.
- **There is no ceiling of its own.** Partitions stop a foot or more below the roof and the gap is closed with **wire mesh**. Nobody can climb through, and the building's sprinklers, conditioned air and light reach every unit. It is also why smoke, a strong smell or pests in one unit reach the neighbors.
- **There is usually no light, no outlet and no water** in a unit. An extension cord run to a unit breaks the lease and is a fire risk.
- **"Climate-controlled" describes the building,** not a thermostat in the unit.

## The Roll-Up Door

```figure
{"kind": "drawing", "title": "The Parts of a Roll-Up Door", "draw": "door-roll-up", "label": "Hood, guides, curtain, bottom bar, latch", "note": "The curtain rolls onto a drum in the hood. Springs in the drum carry its weight. The latch at the bottom corner is where the lock goes."}
```

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Hood and drum** | The box above the opening; the curtain rolls onto a drum whose **springs** carry the door's weight |
| **Guides** | The vertical tracks the curtain rides in |
| **Curtain** | The corrugated steel sheet that is the door |
| **Bottom bar** | The stiff bottom edge with the handle and a weather seal |
| **Latch** | A plate and slide bolt at the bottom corner; the bolt goes into the guide, and the lock goes through its hole (see the lesson Locks Up Close: Hasp, Latch and Cylinder) |

How doors go wrong, and what you do:

- **Lift from the handle, in the middle.** Lifting by a corner twists the curtain in the guides and starts the binding.
- **Heavy, or will not stay up:** the springs have lost tension. Report it. A spring under tension can break a wrist; adjusting one is a trained technician's job.
- **Will not close all the way:** something in a guide or a bent bottom bar. Look; never force it.
- **Flies open when unlatched:** over-tensioned. Report it.
- **The latch needs a kick:** report it, because a tenant will leave it undone.

> [!WARNING]
> A rented unit standing open with nobody there, unlatched with a lock hanging open, or with a damaged door is a security event. Secure it, photograph it and tell your manager (see the lesson Running the Site: Open to Close).

## Walk Your Site

Before the Hands-On Check, walk your own site with this list. Where your site has none, write "none."

- [ ] The perimeter: fence or wall, and where a building is the fence.
- [ ] The gate: type, entry keypad, exit keypad or loop, pedestrian gate, manual release, fire access.
- [ ] The office's view of the gate, the retail wall, where the facility keys and overlocks are kept.
- [ ] The fire lanes, one-way arrows and bollards.
- [ ] Each building's type and how its unit numbers run.
- [ ] One vacant unit of each kind: slab, partitions, mesh, door and latch.
- [ ] The lights and their timers, the cameras and the monitor.
- [ ] The drains, downspouts and low spots.
- [ ] The extinguishers and their tags, and the hydrants.
- [ ] The vehicle lot and, at Escondido and Valley Center, the mailbox room.

Your manager has your site's own gate type, exit method and building list until each Site Profile page carries them.

## At Greens

**Escondido.** You explain the mesh to the tenant: nobody can get through it, and it lets the sprinklers and the conditioned air reach her unit.

**Murrieta.** You ask the tenant to stop, photograph the twisted door, raise a ticket for the door company, and offer him access once it is freed.

**Temecula.** After the first rain you find stains at the base of three doors in the lowest row. The downspout above them is disconnected. You photograph each, report it, and your manager calls the three tenants.

## Common Mistakes

- **Forcing a binding door** or touching a spring.
- **Promising a unit has its own ceiling, light or thermostat.**
- **Leaving an open, unlocked rented unit** without securing and reporting it.

## Key Points

- A unit is a slab, steel partitions, mesh up to the roof and a roll-up door. No ceiling, light or outlet of its own.
- A roll-up door is a hood and drum with springs, guides, a curtain, a bottom bar and a latch.
- Lift from the middle; never force a door; springs are a technician's job.
- Walk your own site and find every part before the Hands-On Check.
