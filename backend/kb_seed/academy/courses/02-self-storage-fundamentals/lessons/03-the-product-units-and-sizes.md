## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Read a unit size (width by depth) and work out its square feet.
- Match each standard size, from 5x5 to 10x30, to what fits in it.
- Explain the difference between drive-up, interior and climate-controlled units.
- Explain why ground-floor and drive-up units usually cost more.
- Describe the other things Greens rents: parking spaces and mailboxes.
- Size a customer by asking about their largest items, not guessing.

## How Unit Sizes Work

A unit size is written as **width x depth in feet**. A 10x15 is 10 feet wide (the door side) and 15 feet deep. Multiply the two numbers to get the **square feet**:

- 5x10 = 50 square feet
- 10x10 = 100 square feet
- 10x15 = 150 square feet

Height matters too. Most units have ceilings of roughly 8 to 10 feet, which is typical across the industry; check your site's real heights. To think in volume, multiply square feet by height: a 10x10 with an 8-foot ceiling holds about 800 cubic feet. Customers who stack boxes high get much more into a unit than customers who do not.

> [!TIP]
> When a customer cannot picture a size, compare it to something they know: "A 10x10 is about half a one-car garage." Comparisons sell better than numbers.

This is the guide to learn by heart. Capacities are approximate and assume reasonable packing (U-Haul, RentCafe and industry size guides).

| Size | Square feet | About the size of | What typically fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5x5** | 25 | A hall closet | Boxes, seasonal clothes and decorations, files, a small dresser, a twin mattress set, sports gear |
| **5x10** | 50 | A walk-in closet | A studio or small one-bedroom apartment: a full mattress set, a dresser, a TV, boxes and small items |
| **10x10** | 100 | Half a one-car garage, or a large bedroom | A one-bedroom apartment or one to two rooms with furniture and small appliances; 20 to 30 medium boxes plus furniture. **The most popular size.** |
| **10x15** | 150 | About two-thirds of a one-car garage | A two-bedroom home, or about four rooms of furniture |
| **10x20** | 200 | A one-car garage | A three-bedroom home including a washer, dryer and refrigerator; or a car, where the site allows it |
| **10x30** | 300 | A two-car garage | A four- to five-bedroom home, major appliances and heavy outdoor gear |

Some sites also have odd sizes: 4x4 and 5x8 lockers, 5x15, 10x25, and larger units. At Greens, the range of enclosed units is wide: the median enclosed street rate is $164 a month at Escondido and $129 at Valley Center, and Escondido's largest enclosed space has a street rate of $8,375.00 a month (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Learn your own site's sizes and current prices from the management system, not from memory.

### How to Size a Customer

Bad sizing costs everyone. A unit that is too small means a frustrated customer on move-in day and a transfer. A unit that is too big means they are paying for air and may move out early to save money.

Ask these questions in this order:

1. **"What is the largest thing you are storing?"** A sectional sofa, a king bed, a piano or a refrigerator decides the minimum size more than boxes do.
2. **"How many rooms are you moving out of?"** Use the guide above.
3. **"Any appliances?"** A washer, dryer and refrigerator take a lot of floor space.
4. **"Do you need to get to things while they are stored?"** If yes, they need an aisle, which means a bigger unit.
5. **"How long do you expect to store?"** Longer stays often mean climate control is worth it.

### Worked Examples

**A one-bedroom apartment with a queen bed, a couch, a dresser, a small table and 25 boxes.** That is the classic 10x10. If they want an aisle to reach boxes, a 10x15.

**A college student with a twin mattress, a desk chair and 12 boxes.** A 5x5 is tight; a 5x10 is comfortable.

**A three-bedroom house with a washer, dryer and refrigerator.** A 10x20, or a 10x15 plus a 5x10 if a 10x20 is not available. Remember that about a quarter of tenants rent more than one unit (SSA Demand Study, 2023).

**A contractor's tools, a table saw on a stand and stacked materials.** Start from the longest material (lumber, pipe). A 10x15 or 10x20 with a drive-up door.

> [!WARNING]
> A unit packed to the door leaves no aisle. If the customer needs to reach things often, go up one size or plan the packing so the most-used items are at the front.

## Unit Types

Size is half the decision. Type is the other half.

| Type | What it is | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Drive-up (exterior)** | A roll-up door facing a drive aisle; you park in front of it. Usually ground level and not climate-controlled | Heavy and bulky items, contractors, frequent access, anyone who wants to load straight from a truck | Dust and outside temperature reach the unit |
| **Interior** | Inside a building, reached through a hallway, sometimes by elevator for upper floors | Household goods that benefit from more protection from dust and pests | Carts and elevators; longer walk from the vehicle |
| **Climate-controlled** | A unit in a space where the temperature (and sometimes humidity) is regulated | Wood furniture, electronics, photos, documents, musical instruments, mattresses, leather, art, wine | Do not promise humidity control unless that unit has it |

Nationally, **55.7%** of rentals are exterior drive-up units (SSA Demand Study, 2023).

### Climate Control in Hot Places

Inland California and Texas summers are hot. Temecula, Murrieta, Valley Center and Fairfield all see long, hot summers, and so do the Texas sites. A closed metal unit in the sun gets much hotter than the air outside. That is a real reason to recommend climate control for items that warp, crack, melt or mold.

How to explain it without over-promising:

- Say "climate-controlled," not "air-conditioned," unless that is literally true at your site.
- Say what the unit does: "The temperature in this building is regulated." If it also controls humidity, say so; if it does not, do not imply it.
- Never quote a specific temperature range unless your site manager has given you the site's real specification.

> [!NOTE]
> Greens' enclosed units are mostly at Escondido and Valley Center (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Ask your site manager which buildings and units are climate-controlled before you quote one.

## Ground Floor Versus Upper Floor

In multi-story buildings:

- **Ground-floor and drive-up units** usually rent for more. They are easier to load.
- **Upper-floor units** are usually cheaper and are reached by elevator and cart.

This is a good tool for price-sensitive customers: "The same 10x10 on the second floor is less per month, and we have carts and an elevator."

## Parking Spaces

Greens rents 1,038 vehicle parking spaces across its five sites, with most at Temecula (481), Fairfield (303) and Murrieta (161) (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Parking comes in three common kinds:

| Kind | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Uncovered** | An open, marked space on a paved or gravel lot |
| **Covered** | A space under a canopy roof that shades the vehicle |
| **Enclosed** | A garage-style unit with a door, for a car, boat or small RV |

What decides the space is the vehicle's **total length** (including the trailer tongue, hitch, ladder or motor), its **height** (for covered and enclosed spaces) and the **turning room** to get in. The RV & Boat Storage course teaches all of this, including the paperwork and rules.

## Mailboxes

Escondido and Valley Center rent mailboxes: 77 at Escondido and 279 at Valley Center (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). A mailbox customer gets an address for mail and packages. Mailboxes have their own small charges, such as a $10 Mailbox Administration Fee at move-in and package plans (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026; see Greens Fees and Charges).

## Specialty Products

Some operators offer wine storage, business centers, small lockers and portable containers that the facility delivers. Not every Greens site has these. Learn what your site has; never promise what it does not.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** A caller says, "I'm moving out of a two-bedroom apartment. What do I need?" You ask about the largest items: a king bed, a sectional and a washer and dryer. You recommend a 10x15, mention a 10x20 if they want an aisle, and ask whether they have wood furniture they care about, which leads to climate control.

**Valley Center.** A customer wants "the smallest unit you have" for 10 boxes of documents. You suggest a 5x5 and point out that paper and photos do better in a climate-controlled space if one is available.

**Temecula.** A caller asks for "a 10x10 for my boat." A boat does not go in a standard storage unit. You switch to parking, ask the boat's length on its trailer, and quote a covered or uncovered space.

**Fairfield.** The site has only 4 enclosed units and 303 parking spaces (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). A caller wanting a 10x10 might be better served by another Greens site or by a parking space if what they really have is a trailer. Be honest about what the site offers.

**A Texas site.** A customer storing a home office in July asks if a drive-up is fine for a desktop computer and printer. You recommend climate control, because Texas summer heat is hard on electronics.

## Common Mistakes

- **Selling the biggest unit.** Over-selling leads to an early move-out; under-selling leads to a bad move-in day. Size from the largest item.
- **Promising humidity control or a temperature** you have not confirmed.
- **Forgetting height.** Tall items and stacking change what fits.
- **Quoting a price from memory.** Rates change. Quote the system's current rate.
- **Putting a vehicle in a storage unit** where the site does not allow it.

## Key Points

- Sizes are width x depth in feet; square feet = width times depth. A 10x10 is 100 square feet.
- Know the guide: 5x5 closet, 5x10 walk-in closet, 10x10 one bedroom (most popular), 10x15 two bedrooms, 10x20 three bedrooms or a car, 10x30 four to five bedrooms.
- Size from the largest item and the number of rooms, then think about access and an aisle.
- Drive-up is easy loading; interior is more protected; climate-controlled is for things that heat and moisture damage. Never over-promise climate control.
- Ground-floor and drive-up units usually cost more than upper floors.
- Greens also rents 1,038 parking spaces and 356 mailboxes (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
