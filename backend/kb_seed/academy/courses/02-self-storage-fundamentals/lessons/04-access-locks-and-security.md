## The Legal Heart of Self-Storage: Exclusive Control

Self-storage works on one idea: **the tenant has exclusive control of the unit.** The tenant supplies their own lock and keeps the only key. Greens does not hold a key, does not go into a rented unit, and does not know what is inside.

That is why the rental agreement says it creates **no bailment**. A bailment is a legal relationship where one party takes custody of another person's goods, like a valet who takes your car keys or a dry cleaner who holds your coat. A bailee is responsible for the goods. A storage operator never takes custody; it rents space. That is a big part of why Greens' liability is limited and why the tenant needs their own protection (the Tenant Protection and Insurance lesson).

What exclusive control means for you every day:

- **Never ask for a copy of a tenant's key.** Never keep one, even if they offer.
- **Never go into a rented unit casually,** even to "check on something" for the tenant.
- **Never ask a tenant to list everything inside.** Greens does not take an inventory of what tenants store.
- **Never open a unit for anyone who is not the tenant** or an authorized person on the account (the Privacy and Compliance lesson).

> [!TIP]
> When a customer asks "Can you keep an eye on my stuff?", the honest answer is: "We watch the property with cameras and gate controls, and only you have the key to your unit. That's why we recommend protection for your belongings."

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## Gates and Gate Codes

Most storage sites, Greens' included, have a perimeter fence and a gate with a **keypad**. Every tenant gets their own personal **gate code**, kept on their lease in the management system.

Rules for gate codes:

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| One code per tenant, never shared or reused | The access log ties each entry to one account |
| Never give a code to anyone not on the account | A code is the key to the property |
| Never read a code to an unverified caller | Gate codes are passwords |
| Never write a code on a receipt in front of others | Someone could see it |
| Never let a tenant or a vendor in on your own code | Your code is logged as you |

When a tenant leaves or is replaced, the old code goes away with the lease. When an employee leaves, codes and master codes they knew are changed.

### Gate Hours Versus Office Hours

There are two sets of hours, and customers mix them up:

- **Gate hours** (access hours): when tenants can get through the gate to their unit or vehicle. Usually longer, often early morning to evening.
- **Office hours:** when staff are in the office to rent, take payments and help.

Some sites offer longer access for a fee. Each Greens site's hours are on Greens Site Hours and Access. Tell every new tenant both sets of hours before they sign.

### The Access Log

The gate system records each code use: which code, when, and whether it was allowed. At Greens, Fairfield's gate reports to Cubby today, and Cubby records each try as allowed, master code, unknown code or outside of hours (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). The other four sites' gates do not report to Cubby yet.

The access log is evidence. It answers questions like "When was the last time this tenant came in?" after a break-in, a dispute or a police request. Never edit, delete or share it except through the right process (the Privacy and Compliance lesson).

### When Gate Access Changes for a Delinquent Tenant

The management system can change or suspend a delinquent tenant's gate code. In California, the law ties denying access to the lien attaching, which happens after the preliminary lien notice period (Bus. & Prof. Code 21705(a)). In Texas, access is denied on seizure under the notice of claim, as Greens' policy and counsel set it.

> [!NOTE]
> At Greens today, Cubby changes a delinquent tenant's gate code at 12:00 AM on the 11th, the same night the late fee posts (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). This is what Cubby does today and is under review with counsel. Follow Cubby, never explain or defend the timing to a tenant as the law, and send a tenant who disputes it to the manager. The Delinquency and the Lien Process lesson covers the lien process in full.

## Locks

### Who Supplies the Lock

The tenant supplies and owns the lock, and keeps the only key. Many sites sell locks in the office, and a lock sale at move-in is both a sale and a security step.

### The Three Common Lock Types

| Lock | What it looks like | Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Disc lock** | A round body that hides most of the shackle | Hard to cut with bolt cutters | The industry's standard recommendation. A common item to sell. |
| **Cylinder lock** | Built into the door latch, installed by the facility; the tenant gets a unique keyed core | Strong; nothing exposed to cut | Often removed and re-keyed between tenants. Some systems also allow a padlock as a tamper seal. |
| **Padlock** | The classic lock with an exposed U-shaped shackle | Weakest; easy to cut or pry | Cheapest. Fine for very low-value storage, but recommend a disc lock. |

How to recommend: "Most tenants use a disc lock. The shackle is covered, so bolt cutters can't get a grip on it. We sell them here." It is honest, it helps the tenant and it is a sale.

### Overlocks

An **overlock** is the facility's own lock, usually a brightly colored heavy lock or a device on the hasp, placed next to the tenant's lock so the door cannot be opened. It is used:

- On a **delinquent** unit, at the step the management system flags, where law and policy allow.
- On a **vacant** unit, so no one moves in without renting.
- On a rented unit **found unlocked**, to secure it until the tenant comes and puts their own lock on.

Overlock rules:

1. **Delinquent overlocks only where the system flags them.** Never overlock a delinquent unit by hand outside what the management system flags.
2. **Record every overlock** in the system, the same day.
3. **Never overlock a paid-up unit.** Check the account first.
4. **Remove it promptly when the tenant pays.** A paid tenant who arrives to find an overlock is one of the top complaints in the industry.
5. **The tenant never removes an overlock.** Staff do.

> [!NOTE]
> At Greens today, Cubby flags a delinquent unit Overlock Needed at 12:00 AM on the 20th, and staff usually put the overlock on the next day (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). This is what Cubby does today and is under review with counsel. Follow Cubby and send any dispute to the manager.

### Cutting a Lock

Cutting a tenant's lock is serious. It happens only under a documented, authorized process:

- A step in the lien process that allows it.
- A court order or search warrant.
- A tenant who lost their key, proves their identity on the account, and signs an authorization (a fee may apply; Greens' Lock Removal/Inventory Fee is $75.00, From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026, see Greens Fees and Charges).
- A real emergency, such as a fire or a leak, handled by the manager.

Every cut lock follows the same pattern: the right authorization, a witness, photos before and after, an inventory where the process requires one, and a new facility lock afterward. Your manager decides; you never cut a lock on your own.

## Smart Entry

Some sites use **smart entry**: Bluetooth or app-based locks on the gate and sometimes on each unit. The tenant opens the gate and their unit with a phone. The operator gets a per-unit record of every open and close, and can lock a unit out remotely. Smart entry changes the tools, not the rules: the tenant still has exclusive control, and every lockout still follows the lawful process.

## Cameras

Cameras usually cover the gate, the office, hallways and drive aisles.

- **Review footage after an incident** (a break-in, damage, an accident) with your manager.
- **Preserve it.** Once there is an incident, a claim or a subpoena, footage must not be deleted by the system's automatic cleanup. Tell your manager right away so it is saved.
- **Never share footage** with the public, another tenant or a customer. Police get it through the law enforcement process (the Privacy and Compliance lesson).

## Putting It Together: A Security Walk

When you walk the site (the Running the Site: Open to Close lesson has the full routine), you are checking the security system you just learned:

- Every rented unit has a tenant lock.
- Every vacant unit has a facility lock or overlock and shows vacant in the system.
- Every unit flagged for overlock has one; no paid unit has one.
- No cut or damaged locks. If you find one, take photos, report it, and check the camera and gate log with your manager.
- The gate closes fully and the keypad works.

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido.** On your walk you find unit 1142 with its door closed but no lock. The system shows it rented and paid. You put a facility lock on it, note it in the system, and call the tenant to come put their own lock on. You do not open the door to look inside.

**Fairfield.** A tenant says the gate refused his code at 11:00 PM. You check the access log in Cubby: "outside of hours." His code is fine; the gate was closed. You tell him the gate hours.

**Valley Center.** A tenant pays her full balance at 10:00 AM. Her unit has an overlock. You remove it that morning and record it, and you check that her gate code works before she leaves.

**Temecula.** A man says he is a tenant's brother and needs the gate code to get "his brother's tools." He is not on the account. You do not give the code, and you do not confirm that his brother rents at Greens.

**A Texas site.** A tenant lost her key and wants the lock cut. You confirm her identity against the lease, and your manager handles the signed authorization, the witness, the photos and the new lock.

## Common Mistakes

- **Keeping a spare key** "to help" a tenant.
- **Reading a gate code over the phone** to someone you have not verified.
- **Leaving an overlock on after payment.**
- **Overlocking the wrong unit.** Check the unit number against the system twice.
- **Cutting a lock** without the authorized process, a witness and photos.
- **Letting footage be deleted** after an incident.

## Key Points

- The tenant has exclusive control and the only key; storage creates no bailment.
- One gate code per tenant; gate codes are passwords and the access log is evidence.
- Gate hours and office hours are different; tell new tenants both.
- Recommend a disc lock; cylinder locks are strong; padlocks are the weakest.
- Overlocks go on only where the system flags them, are always recorded, never go on a paid unit and come off promptly when paid.
- Cubby's day-11 gate code change and day-20 overlock are what Cubby does today and are under review with counsel (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026).
- A lock is cut only under an authorized, documented process with a witness and photos.
