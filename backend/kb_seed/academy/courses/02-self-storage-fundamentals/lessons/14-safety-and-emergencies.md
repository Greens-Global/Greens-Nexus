## Someone Is Cutting a Lock

It is 7:00 AM at Temecula and you see a man inside the fence cutting a lock. Every instinct says go stop him. Do not. No unit, vehicle or payment is worth getting hurt, and you are not a guard, a police officer or a firefighter. This lesson teaches how to stay safe, when to call 911, and how to write the incident up so Greens and the tenant have what they need.

> [!SAFETY]
> If anyone may be in danger, call 911 first. Then call your manager.

## Working Alone

- Your manager knows when you open and close.
- Keep your phone charged and on you, not on the desk.
- Lock the office when you step out to walk the site.
- Trust your instincts: if a visitor makes you uneasy, stay near the office and call your manager.
- **Never step into a unit or closed space with a stranger.** Show a unit from the doorway: open it, step back, and let the customer go in while you stay outside by the aisle.
- **Never confront a trespasser or a thief.** Get somewhere safe, lock the office, call 911, call your manager, and note the time, a description and the plate if you can see it safely.

## Everyday Hazards

| Hazard | What to do |
|---|---|
| Trip hazards, potholes, wet floors | Mark with a cone or clean it; report for repair |
| A broken roll-up door or spring | Report it. Never repair a spring without training |
| An elevator that stops unevenly | Take it out of service and report |
| Carts with bad wheels | Pull them and report |
| Missing or out-of-date extinguishers | Report for inspection |
| Blocked exits | Clear them |
| Summer heat during outdoor work | Water, shade, breaks |

## Call 911 First

For a medical emergency, a fire or smoke from a unit, a gas or strong chemical smell, a person who may be living in a unit and in danger, a suspected drug lab, or a crime in progress. Never open a unit to investigate a smell or smoke; move people away and let emergency services decide.

## California Risks

**Wildfire.** Valley Center, Escondido, Temecula and Murrieta sit in wildfire-prone inland country. Know your evacuation route. On a red flag day keep brush and trash cleared and watch for smoke. If officials order an evacuation, **leave**; follow the site plan for the gate and office, and never let tenants back in until officials and your manager clear it.

**Earthquake.** Drop, cover and hold on, away from shelving and windows. Afterward check people, leave and call 911 if you smell gas, do not use elevators, and keep tenants out of a damaged building.

**Heat and power shutoffs.** The utility may cut power to lower fire risk. Gates, keypads, elevators and cameras can stop. Follow the site plan for the gate and tell tenants. Protect yourself from heat first.

**Texas** stores are run by CubeSmart, but if you visit one: severe storms, hail and tornadoes (interior room at a warning), flash floods around Austin and Georgetown (never drive through water on a road), and freezes.

## After Any Emergency

1. People first: everyone safe and accounted for.
2. No tenants into a damaged building until officials and your manager say so.
3. **Photograph damage before any cleanup,** inside and out.
4. Tell your manager everything you saw.
5. Talk to tenants as your manager directs; never promise what will be covered.

## Injuries

If a customer, vendor or coworker is hurt: call 911 if it is serious; help only as far as you are trained, and never move someone with a possible back or neck injury; do not admit fault or promise payment; get names and phone numbers of the injured person and witnesses; photograph the scene; report the same day. If **you** are hurt at work, tell your manager right away, even if it seems minor.

## Documenting an Incident With Nexus Forms

Every incident is written up the same day: a break-in, an injury, damage, a person living in a unit, a fire, a flood. In Nexus, open **Fill a Form** (from Workday, the bolt in the header or the Quick Actions dock) and choose the incident report your site uses.

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| What happened | "Found unit 1142 lock cut, door half open." |
| When | The date as MM/DD/YYYY and the time with AM or PM |
| Where | Site, building, unit or space |
| Who | People involved and witnesses, with phone numbers |
| What you did | "Put facility lock on, called tenant at 9:15 AM, called manager." |
| Photos | Taken before anything is moved or cleaned |
| Follow-up | "Manager asked to save camera footage from 2:00 AM to 4:00 AM." |

Write facts, not guesses or blame: "Tenant said the roof leaked" is a fact; "the roof is bad" is a guess. A form saved with no signal is kept on your phone and sent when you are back online.

## At Greens

**Temecula.** You go into the office, lock the door, call 911 and then your manager, and note his truck's color and make.

**Escondido.** A customer slips on a wet hallway floor. You help her sit, ask if she wants 911, get her details, photograph the floor and file the report. You do not say "It's our fault."

**Murrieta.** You hear voices and see an extension cord under a unit door. You step away and call your manager, and 911 if someone may be in danger. You do not knock or open it.

## Common Mistakes

- **Chasing a thief** or confronting a trespasser.
- **Opening a unit** to check a smell or smoke.
- **Cleaning up before photographing.**
- **Not reporting** a small injury of your own.

## Key Points

- Your safety comes first: 911 first when anyone may be in danger, then your manager.
- Never enter a unit with a stranger; never confront a trespasser or thief.
- Wildfire, earthquake, heat and power shutoffs are the California risks; evacuate when told.
- Photograph before cleanup; no tenants into damaged buildings.
- Document every incident the same day in Fill a Form: what, when, where, who, what you did, photos, follow-up.
