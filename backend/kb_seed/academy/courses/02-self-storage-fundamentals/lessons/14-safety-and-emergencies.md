## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Work alone on a site safely.
- Spot and report the everyday hazards of a storage site.
- Know when to call 911 first and your manager second.
- Respond to the emergencies most likely at Greens' California sites and at a Texas site.
- Document an incident completely, with photos, through Nexus Forms.

These are typical industry practices and common-sense safety rules. Greens' own emergency plan and your site manager's direction come first; ask your manager for the plan for your site.

## Your Safety Comes First

No unit, vehicle, payment or customer is worth getting hurt. You are not a security guard, a police officer or a firefighter. Your job in a dangerous situation is to get safe, call for help and report.

> [!SAFETY]
> If anyone may be in danger, call 911 first. Then call your manager.

## Working Alone

Many storage employees work alone for part of the day. Habits that keep you safe:

- **Tell someone your schedule.** Your manager should know when you open and close.
- **Keep your phone charged and on you,** not on the desk.
- **Lock the office** when you step out to walk the site.
- **Trust your instincts.** If a visitor makes you uneasy, stay in a public area, stay near the office and call your manager.
- **Never walk a customer into a building or to a remote row alone if something feels wrong.** Point the way, or ask them to come back when another team member is there.

### Never Enter a Unit With a Stranger

Never step inside a unit, or into a closed space, with someone you do not know. Show units from the doorway. If a customer wants to see inside, open the door and step back so they go first and you stay outside, near an open aisle.

### Never Confront Trespassers or Suspected Thieves

If you see someone cutting a lock, climbing the fence, or acting like they are breaking in:

1. **Do not approach them.**
2. **Go somewhere safe** and lock the office if you are in it.
3. **Call 911.**
4. **Call your manager.**
5. **Note what you saw:** time, description, vehicle and plate if you can see it safely.
6. Later, help your manager preserve camera footage and the gate log.

## Everyday Hazards

Walk the site with safety eyes, not just security eyes:

| Hazard | What to do |
|---|---|
| Trip hazards, potholes, uneven pavement | Report for repair; mark with a cone if needed |
| Wet floors in hallways or the office | Clean or mark it at once |
| **Broken roll-up doors and springs** | Report. Never repair a roll-up door spring without training: springs are under heavy tension and can cause serious injury. |
| An elevator that stops unevenly or makes noise | Take it out of service and report |
| Carts with bad wheels | Pull them and report |
| Fire extinguishers out of date or missing | Report for inspection |
| Blocked exits | Clear them |
| Heat during outdoor work in summer | Drink water, take shade breaks, and follow Greens' heat safety guidance |

## Call 911 First

Call 911 first, then your manager, for:

- A **medical emergency:** someone collapsed, injured badly, not breathing.
- A **fire,** including smoke coming from a unit.
- A **gas smell** or a strong chemical smell.
- A **person who may be living in a unit** and may be in danger.
- A **suspected drug lab** or chemical setup.
- A **crime in progress.**

Do not open a unit to investigate a smell or smoke. Move people away and let emergency services decide.

## California Emergencies

Greens' five California sites face a few big risks.

### Wildfire

Inland San Diego and Riverside counties, including **Valley Center, Escondido, Temecula and Murrieta,** are wildfire-prone.

- Know your site's evacuation route and your manager's phone number.
- During a fire watch or red flag warning, keep dry brush and trash cleared and watch for smoke.
- If officials order an evacuation, **leave.** Do not stay to protect the site.
- Follow Greens' plan for securing the gate and the office, and for telling tenants.
- Never let tenants back in until the site is cleared by officials and your manager.

### Earthquake

- During shaking: drop, cover and hold on. Stay away from shelving and windows.
- After: check yourself and others; if you smell gas, leave and call 911; do not use elevators.
- Report damage. Do not let tenants into a damaged building until it is cleared.

### Extreme Heat and Power Loss

- Inland California summers bring extreme heat and sometimes **public safety power shutoffs**, when the utility turns off power to lower fire risk.
- Without power, gates, keypads, elevators and cameras may stop working. Follow your site's plan for the gate (manual operation or keeping it secured) and tell tenants.
- Protect yourself from heat first.

## Texas Emergencies

A Texas site faces a different set:

| Risk | What to know |
|---|---|
| **Severe storms, hail and tornadoes** | Go to an interior room away from windows at a tornado warning. Report hail damage to roofs and doors afterward. |
| **Flash flooding** | Central Texas around Austin and Georgetown is known as "flash flood alley." Never drive through water over the road ("Turn around, don't drown"). Low drive-up rows can flood. |
| **Winter freeze** | The freeze of 02/2021 showed how fast pipes burst and power fails. Follow your site's plan for pipes and the gate. |
| **Heat** | Same as inland California: water, shade, breaks. |

## After Any Emergency

1. **People first.** Make sure everyone is safe and accounted for.
2. **Do not let tenants into a damaged building** until officials and your manager say it is safe.
3. **Photograph damage before any cleanup,** inside and out, with dates. Photos are evidence for Greens' insurance and for tenants' protection claims.
4. **Tell your manager** everything you saw.
5. **Communicate with tenants** the way your manager directs. Do not promise what will be covered (lesson 7).

## Injuries on Site

If a customer, a vendor or a coworker is hurt:

1. **Call 911** if it is serious.
2. **Help as far as you are trained.** Do not move someone with a possible back or neck injury.
3. **Do not admit fault or promise payment.** Be kind; stick to facts.
4. **Get names and phone numbers** of the injured person and any witnesses.
5. **Photograph the scene:** the floor, the step, the door, the lighting.
6. **Report** to your manager the same day and document it (below).

If **you** are hurt at work, tell your manager right away, even if it seems minor. Ask HR in Nexus can help with what comes next.

## Documenting an Incident With Nexus Forms

Every incident gets written down the same day: a break-in, an injury, damage, a person living in a unit, a fire, a flood, a confrontation.

In Nexus, open **Fill a Form** (from Workday, the + menu or the Quick Actions dock) and choose the incident report your site uses. Nexus Fundamentals covers the screens. A good report has:

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | "Found unit 1142 lock cut, door half open." |
| **When** | The date as MM/DD/YYYY and the time with AM or PM |
| **Where** | Site, building, unit or space number |
| **Who** | People involved and witnesses, with phone numbers |
| **What you did** | "Put facility lock on, called tenant at 9:15 AM, called manager." |
| **Photos** | Before anything is moved or cleaned |
| **Follow-up** | "Manager asked to save camera footage from 2:00 AM to 4:00 AM." |

Write facts, not guesses or blame. "Tenant said the roof leaked" is a fact. "The roof is bad" is a guess. A form saved with no signal is kept on your phone and sent when you are back online.

## Greens Scenarios

**Valley Center.** A red flag warning is in effect and smoke is visible to the east. Your manager calls and says the area is under an evacuation warning. You follow the site plan to secure the office and the gate, and you leave. You do not stay to answer tenant calls.

**Escondido.** A customer slips on a wet hallway floor and hurts her wrist. You help her sit, ask if she wants 911, get her name and number, photograph the floor and the area, and file the incident report in Nexus. You do not say "It's our fault."

**Temecula.** At 7:00 AM you see a man inside the fence cutting a lock. You go into the office, lock the door, call 911 and then your manager. You note his truck's color and make.

**Murrieta.** On your walk you hear voices and see an extension cord running under a unit door. You step away, call your manager, and call 911 if you believe someone may be in danger. You do not knock or open the unit.

**Fairfield.** A summer power shutoff stops the gate. You follow the site's plan for the gate and tell tenants calling in when access will be restored.

**A Texas site.** Heavy rain floods the low drive-up row. After the water goes down, you photograph each affected door before anything is opened or cleaned, and report to your manager.

## Common Mistakes

- **Chasing a thief or confronting a trespasser.**
- **Opening a unit** to check a smell or smoke.
- **Fixing a roll-up door spring** without training.
- **Cleaning up before photographing.**
- **Letting tenants into a damaged building** too early.
- **Admitting fault** after an injury.
- **Not reporting** a small injury of your own.

## Key Points

- Your safety comes first. Call 911 first when anyone may be in danger, then your manager.
- Never enter a unit with a stranger and never confront a trespasser or thief.
- Never repair a roll-up door spring without training.
- California: wildfire (Valley Center, Escondido, Temecula, Murrieta), earthquake, heat and power shutoffs. Texas: storms, hail, tornadoes, flash floods around Austin and Georgetown, freezes and heat.
- After an emergency: people first, no tenants into damaged buildings, photograph before cleanup.
- Document every incident the same day in Nexus Forms: what, when, where, who, what you did, photos and follow-up.
