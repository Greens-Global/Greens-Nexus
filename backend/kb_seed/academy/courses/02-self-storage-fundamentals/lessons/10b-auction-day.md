## The Morning of the Sale

It is sale day at Valley Center. Two units are listed in the online auction, a bidder is asking when he can see his unit, and the phone rings: one of the tenants wants to pay. This lesson covers what you do on the day of a lien sale, and the things you never do however the day goes.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## The Last Checks

A unit is sold only if every step before it holds. On the morning of the sale your manager confirms, for each unit:

- **No payment in full** has come in. Check the ledger right before the sale.
- **No Declaration in Opposition** has arrived (California).
- **No bankruptcy notice,** no report of death, no written dispute.
- **The military status check** is done and kept with the file, and shows no active duty.
- **Every notice went out on time** to every address it had to reach.

If anything is missing or unclear, the unit comes out of the sale. Pulling a unit is always safe; selling one that should not be sold is not.

## When the Tenant Pays

Up to the sale, a tenant who pays the full lien gets their unit back. Take the payment in Cubby, tell your manager at once so the unit is pulled from the auction, and remove the overlock when Cubby raises the task. A partial payment does not stop the sale (see the lesson Delinquency and the Lien Process); tell the tenant the full amount.

## What Staff Do

- **Follow your manager's plan** for the sale: in California an in-person auction or a public auction website; at Greens, the sale steps run through Cubby's lien workflow.
- **Open the unit only as the process requires,** with your manager: an authorized lock cut, a witness, photos before and after, and the inventory the process calls for (see the lesson Access, Locks and Security).
- **Set aside personal papers and photographs** as your manager directs.
- **Treat the buyer's removal by the sale's terms.** The buyer's time to empty the unit, any cleanup deposit and the rest of the buyer rules: your manager has Greens' terms.
- **Record everything:** who bought, for how much, when the unit was emptied. The sale fees post at the sale step in Cubby.

## What You Never Do

- **Never bid,** and never let a friend or family member bid.
- **Never take, keep, give away or throw out anything** from the unit, before or after the sale, outside the process. Not "junk," not a box of magazines.
- **Never discuss the tenant or the account** with bidders or onlookers.
- **Never promise anyone they can "get their stuff back after the auction."** Once lawfully sold, the goods belong to the buyer.
- **Never go ahead** after any stop event, even with the auction already open.

## After the Sale

The proceeds pay the tenant's balance and the costs of the sale. Any money left over is held for the tenant as the law requires (see the lesson Lien Law: California and Texas). A lien sale is not a profit center.

## At Greens

**Valley Center.** The tenant on the phone wants to pay half. You tell her the full amount that stops the sale and that half does not. She pays in full; you tell your manager, the unit is pulled, and the overlock comes off.

**Escondido.** A coworker suggests clearing "obvious trash" before buyers look, to get a better price. You say no and tell your manager.

**A bidder** asks who the tenant was. You do not say.

## Common Mistakes

- **Not checking the ledger** one last time before the sale.
- **Helping a friend bid.**
- **Tidying a unit** before or after the sale outside the process.

## Key Points

- On sale day, re-check payment, declarations, bankruptcy, death, disputes and military status for every unit.
- A tenant who pays in full before the sale gets the unit back; pull it at once.
- Staff never bid, never take anything and never discuss the tenant.
- Proceeds pay the balance owed; any surplus is held for the tenant.
