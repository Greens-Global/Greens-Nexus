## "You Have Cameras, So I'm Covered"

A retired couple renting a 10x15 at Valley Center wave off protection: "The facility is insured, and you have cameras." They are about to store a lifetime of furniture with nothing behind it. This lesson teaches how to correct that belief kindly, explain the two kinds of protection, and record the choice on every lease.

## The Myth to Kill First

Greens' property insurance covers Greens' buildings and Greens' liability. It does **not** cover the tenant's belongings. The rental agreement also has a **release of liability**: the tenant agrees Greens is not responsible for loss or damage to stored goods, except where the law says otherwise. So when a roof leaks, a unit is broken into or rodents get in, the tenant's own coverage is what pays, if they have any.

That is why every Greens move-in leaves with protection: Greens' plan or the tenant's own policy.

> [!TIP]
> "Our insurance covers our building, not your belongings. That's why every customer has protection: our plan or your own policy."

## Two Ways to Protect Belongings

| | **Protection plan** | **Tenant insurance** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | For a monthly charge, the operator waives its release and pays covered losses up to a limit | A real insurance policy from an insurance company |
| Is it insurance? | No. California's Supreme Court has held a protection plan is not insurance | Yes |
| Who decides a claim | The plan's claims administrator | The insurer |

A tenant's own **homeowners or renters policy** may cover belongings stored away from home, sometimes up to a limited amount. A tenant who has coverage shows proof of it instead of buying the plan.

## Why It Matters

To the tenant: a fire, a leak or a break-in can destroy years of belongings, and without protection there is nobody to claim from. To Greens: a tenant with protection has a clear path after a loss and is far less likely to blame the site, and the plan is steady ancillary income.

## Greens' Protection in Cubby

Cubby calls protection **Coverage** and records one of three choices on each lease: Greens' plan, the tenant's own policy (policy number and expiration date), or none. Greens' plan has two levels, **$2,500** and **$5,000** of protection. New tenants pay today's price; some older leases brought over from the previous system keep a lower price. Both prices are on Greens by the Numbers, and the details are on Greens Tenant Protection. For an existing tenant, quote what Cubby shows on their lease; never change a lease's price on your own.

Storage-unit tenants at Escondido and Valley Center are mostly on Greens' plan. **Vehicles, RVs and boats are covered by the owner's own vehicle or boat insurance,** not a storage plan; record their policy.

Cubby keeps protection current with two tasks:

- **Remind Tenant of POI Obligation:** proof of insurance is missing or expired. Call for the current policy number and expiration date.
- **Enroll Tenant in Protection Plan:** the tenant's own policy lapsed. Call and offer Greens' plan.

## How to Offer It

1. **Ask:** "Do you have insurance that covers your things in storage? Some homeowners and renters policies do."
2. **If yes:** record the policy number and expiration date in Cubby.
3. **If no:** offer the $2,500 and $5,000 levels at today's price for a new customer.
4. **One line of value:** "It covers losses like fire, theft and water damage up to that amount, subject to the plan's terms."
5. **Record the choice** on the lease.

### Words You Never Use

| Never say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Our insurance" (about the plan) | "Our protection plan" |
| "You're covered for that" | "You can file a claim, and the plan's administrator reviews it" |
| "Everything is covered" | "It covers common losses, subject to the terms" |
| "You have to buy our plan" | "Every rental needs protection: our plan or your own policy" |

Every plan has **exclusions**. Never describe one you have not read in the plan document; say "Let me get you the plan details."

## After a Loss

1. Be calm and kind; this is upsetting.
2. Make the scene safe and secure an open unit with a facility lock.
3. Report it to your manager the same day and file the incident report (see the lesson Safety and Emergencies).
4. Photograph everything and ask your manager to save the camera footage and gate log.
5. Point the tenant to their coverage: the plan's claim steps or their own insurer.
6. Never admit fault or promise payment.

## At Greens

**Valley Center.** You tell the couple cameras help find out what happened but do not pay for damage, and Greens' insurance covers the building, not their furniture. They choose the $5,000 level.

**Escondido.** Cubby raises Remind Tenant of POI Obligation two months after a move-in; her renters policy expired. You call for the renewed details.

**Murrieta.** After a storm a tenant finds water damage. You secure the unit, photograph it, report it and give him the claim steps. You do not say "this will be covered."

## Common Mistakes

- **Calling the plan "insurance,"** or promising a claim will be paid.
- **Quoting an old price** to a new customer.
- **Skipping the offer** because a customer seems rushed.
- **Ignoring** the POI and enrollment tasks.

## Key Points

- Greens' insurance covers the facility, not the tenant's belongings.
- A protection plan is not insurance; tenant insurance is.
- Every lease has protection: Greens' plan ($2,500 or $5,000) or the tenant's own policy with number and expiration date.
- New tenants pay today's price (Greens by the Numbers); vehicle tenants carry their own vehicle insurance.
- Never call the plan insurance and never promise a claim.
