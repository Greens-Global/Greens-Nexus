## What You'll Be Able to Do

- Explain why the facility's insurance does not cover what a tenant stores.
- Tell a tenant protection plan from tenant insurance.
- Explain why protection matters to the tenant and to Greens.
- Describe Greens' protection levels and prices, and the own-policy option.
- Offer protection at every move-in, honestly and without pressure.
- Avoid the words and promises that get staff and Greens into trouble.

## The Myth to Kill First

Almost every new customer believes one thing that is not true: **"The facility is insured, so my stuff is covered."**

It is not. Greens' property insurance covers Greens' buildings and Greens' liability. It does not cover the tenant's belongings. On top of that, the rental agreement usually contains a **release of liability** (often called an exculpatory clause): the tenant agrees that the operator is not responsible for loss or damage to stored goods, except where the law says the operator's own negligence cannot be excused.

So if a tenant's things are damaged by a roof leak, stolen in a break-in or ruined by rodents, the tenant's own coverage is what pays, if they have any. That is why every move-in at Greens leaves with protection: Greens' plan or the tenant's own policy.

> [!TIP]
> Say it plainly at every move-in: "Our insurance covers our building, not your belongings. That's why we ask every customer to have protection: either our plan or your own policy."

## Two Ways to Protect Belongings

There are two products in the industry. They sound alike but are legally different.

| | **Tenant protection plan** | **Tenant insurance** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The operator agrees, for a monthly fee, to waive its release of liability and pay for covered losses up to a limit | A real insurance policy from an insurance company covering the tenant's goods |
| Who stands behind it | The operator, often backed by the operator's own contractual liability insurance | An insurance company |
| Is it insurance? | No. In California, the state Supreme Court held in 2018 (*Heckart v. A-1 Self Storage*) that a protection plan is not insurance and is not regulated as insurance | Yes |
| Who sells it | Site staff, as part of the rental | Site staff in many states under a limited insurance license, or the tenant's own agent |

The tenant's own **homeowners or renters policy** may also cover belongings stored away from home, sometimes for a limited amount. A tenant who already has coverage can show proof of it instead of buying a plan.

## Why It Matters

### To the Tenant

- A break-in, a fire, a leak or pests can destroy things that took years to collect.
- Without protection, the tenant has no one to claim from.
- Monthly protection is small compared to the value of a household of furniture.

### To Greens

- **Fewer disputes.** After a break-in or a leak, a tenant with protection has a clear path to a claim. A tenant without it is more likely to blame the site and threaten to sue.
- **Fewer claims against Greens.** Protection reduces lawsuits and complaints.
- **Revenue.** Protection is a steady part of ancillary income (lesson 6).

## How to Offer It Without Pressure

Protection is offered at every move-in, but it is never pushed with fear or false claims. A simple, honest script:

1. **Ask.** "Do you have insurance that covers your things in storage? Some homeowners and renters policies do."
2. **If yes:** "Great. I'll need the policy number and the expiration date for your file."
3. **If no or not sure:** "We have a protection plan. $2,500 of protection is $9 a month, and $5,000 is $20 a month." (Quote the price Cubby shows for this lease.)
4. **Explain the value in one line:** "It covers things like fire, theft and water damage up to that amount, subject to the plan's terms."
5. **Record the choice.** The tenant's acceptance or the own-policy details go on the lease and the agreement.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

### Words You Never Use

| Never say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Our insurance" (about the protection plan) | A protection plan is not insurance | "Our protection plan" |
| "You're covered for that" | Only the claims administrator decides what a claim pays | "You can file a claim, and the plan's administrator reviews it" |
| "Everything is covered" | Every plan has exclusions | "It covers common losses like fire and theft, subject to the terms" |
| "You have to buy our plan" | The tenant may use their own policy | "We need protection on every rental: our plan or your own policy" |
| "The facility is insured, so you're fine" | The facility's insurance does not cover tenants' goods | "Our insurance covers our building, not your belongings" |

### What the Plan Does Not Cover

Every plan has **exclusions**: things it does not pay for. Typical examples in the industry include cash, jewelry and irreplaceable items, and damage from things that are prohibited to store. Greens' own exclusions and claim steps are on the Greens Tenant Protection page once the owners fill them in. Until then, never describe an exclusion you have not read; say "Let me get you the plan details."

## After a Loss

When a tenant reports a loss (a break-in, a leak, pests):

1. **Stay calm and kind.** This is upsetting.
2. **Make the scene safe** and secure the unit if it is open (a facility lock, lesson 4).
3. **Report it** to your manager the same day and write it up through Nexus Forms or the site's incident process (lesson 14).
4. **Preserve evidence:** photos, and ask your manager to save camera footage and the gate log.
5. **Point the tenant to their coverage:** Greens' plan's claim steps, or their own insurer.
6. **Do not admit fault or promise payment.** Facts only.

## Common Mistakes

- **Calling a protection plan "insurance."**
- **Promising a claim will be paid.**
- **Skipping the offer** because a customer seems rushed.
- **Not recording** the tenant's choice or policy details.
- **Ignoring the POI and enrollment tasks** in Cubby.
- **Pushing with fear.** Honest facts sell better than scare stories.

## Key Points

- The facility's insurance covers the facility, not the tenant's belongings.
- A protection plan is not insurance (Heckart v. A-1 Self Storage, California, 2018); tenant insurance is.
- Protection helps the tenant, cuts disputes and claims, and is real revenue for Greens.
- Greens' plan: $2,500 at $9.00 or $14.00 a month; $5,000 at $20.00 or $25.00 a month (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). Or the tenant's own policy, with number and expiration date recorded.
- Vehicle tenants carry their own vehicle insurance.
- Never call the plan insurance, never promise a claim, always record the choice.
