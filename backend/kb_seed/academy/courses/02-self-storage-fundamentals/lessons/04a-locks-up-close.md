## The Lost Key

A tenant at a cylinder-lock building has lost her only key and asks you to "just cut it." On that kind of door there is nothing to cut, and there is a better answer. This lesson shows how a storage door actually locks, so you can put an overlock in the right hole and handle a lost key the right way.

## The Hasp and the Staple

```figure
{"kind": "drawing", "title": "A Hasp", "draw": "hasp", "label": "The hinged plate, the staple and the shackle through it", "note": "A lock through the staple holds the plate down, and a closed plate covers its own screws."}
```

A **hasp** is a hinged, slotted plate that swings over a **staple** (a steel loop). The lock's shackle goes through the staple and holds the plate down. A good hasp covers its own screws when closed; one whose screws show can be unscrewed, lock and all.

## The Latch With Two Holes

On a roll-up door the hasp is built into the **latch** at the bottom corner. Pushed home, its slide bolt goes into a hole in the door guide so the curtain cannot lift. The bolt and plate carry matching holes, and the lock's shackle goes through them so the bolt cannot slide back.

```figure
{"kind": "drawing", "title": "Two Holes", "draw": "hasp-overlock", "label": "The tenant's lock in one hole, the overlock in the other", "note": "Know which hole is which on your site."}
```

Most latches have **two holes**: one for the tenant's lock and one for the facility's **overlock**, so the facility can lock a unit without touching the tenant's lock. Learn which is which at your site, so an overlock never ends up in the tenant's hole with their lock left off. A bolt that will not slide home, or a latch plate hanging by one bolt, is a unit that is not secure: report it.

## The Cylinder Lock

```figure
{"kind": "drawing", "title": "A Cylinder Lock, Apart", "draw": "lock-cylinder-core", "label": "The housing, the core, the key", "note": "Changing the core changes which key opens the door, with no locksmith and no new lock."}
```

A **cylinder lock** has no shackle. A steel barrel (the cylinder) fits into a round opening in a latch built for it. Inside it is the **core**, the keyed part a key turns. Because the whole lock sits inside the latch, there is nothing for bolt cutters or a pry bar to work on.

Sites run cylinder locks one of two ways:

| How | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Tenant-owned** | The tenant buys one that fits, pushes it into the latch and takes it at move-out |
| **Facility-managed** | The housing stays on the door. The facility holds a **control key** that pulls the core and drops in a fresh one, and gives the tenant that core's keys at move-in |

On a facility-managed site:

- **A core change takes a minute** with the control key and a fresh core from stock. That is the "reset the cylinder core" step when a unit is turned.
- **Every core and key is inventoried.** A missing core is a missing key to a unit.
- **The control key is the most sensitive key on the site.** It opens every cylinder-lock door. It is signed out, never lent, never copied and never on a ring with office keys. If it is lost, the manager treats it as a site-wide re-key.
- **A lost tenant key is a core change, not a lock cut.** The tenant proves who they are on the account, the manager pulls the core and issues a new one, and a fee may apply (see Greens Fees and Charges).
- **An overlock still goes in the second hole,** or as a lockout plug; the tenant's core stays.
- **A jammed or hammered housing is a door company job.** Never drill it.

Which Greens buildings use cylinder locks: your manager has this until each Site Profile page lists it. Ask before you tell a tenant what lock to bring.

## At Greens

**The lost key.** You confirm the tenant's identity against the lease and bring in your manager, who changes the core and issues new keys. Nothing is cut.

**An overlock walk.** Cubby flags a unit Overlock Needed. You check the unit number twice, put the overlock in the facility's hole beside the tenant's lock, and record it in Cubby.

## Common Mistakes

- **An overlock in the tenant's hole**, or a tenant's lock removed to make room.
- **Carrying the control key** on your everyday key ring, or lending it.
- **Cutting or drilling a cylinder lock** when a core change is the answer.

## Key Points

- The lock goes through the latch's hasp and staple; most latches have a second hole for the overlock.
- A cylinder lock sits inside the latch; its keyed core is changed between tenants.
- The control key opens every cylinder door: signed out, never lent or copied.
- A lost key on a cylinder site is a core change after an identity check, never a cut.
