## What the Agreement Is

The **rental agreement** is the contract between Greens and the tenant. It controls the rent, the fees, the notices, the rules and the lien. When a customer asks "Can you do that?", the answer starts with "What does the agreement say?"

Three things to understand about it:

1. **It is month-to-month.** It renews every month until one side ends it with the notice the agreement requires. That is why rates can change with notice (the How a Storage Site Makes Money lesson).
2. **It rents space; it is not a bailment.** The agreement says Greens never takes custody of the goods. The tenant has exclusive control and the only key (the Access, Locks and Security lesson).
3. **It is not a home lease.** Residential landlord-tenant law does not apply. Storage has its own laws: in California, the **California Self-Service Storage Facility Act** (Bus. & Prof. Code 21700-21716); in Texas, **Texas Property Code chapter 59**.

## Key Clauses

A typical storage agreement covers these. Learn where each one is in Greens' agreement so you can point to it.

| Clause | What it says | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|---|
| **Parties and space** | Who rents, which unit or space, its size | The unit number must be right; overlocks and notices depend on it |
| **Rent and due date** | The monthly rent and the day it is due | At Greens, rent is due on the 1st (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026) |
| **Fees** | Admin, late, lien, lock removal and others | Only fees in the agreement may be charged |
| **Lien statement** | The tenant's property is subject to a lien and may be sold if unpaid | Required by law; without it the lien process fails |
| **Contact and change of address** | The tenant must keep their address, phone and email current | Lien notices go to the last known address |
| **Alternate contact (California)** | A second person and address for lien notices | Required to be requested in California |
| **Value limit** | A cap on the total value the tenant may store unless Greens agrees in writing | Limits liability; California law allows it (21713) |
| **Release of liability and protection** | Greens is not responsible for the goods; the tenant needs protection | The Tenant Protection and Insurance lesson |
| **Use rules** | What may and may not be stored or done | Prohibited items and uses below |
| **Owner's right to enter** | When Greens may enter (emergencies, repairs, legal orders, prohibited items, lien enforcement) with the notice the agreement states | Entry follows the agreement, never curiosity |
| **Access and authorized users** | Who may enter and get information | Only the tenant and people listed on the account |
| **Military status** | Whether the tenant or spouse is on active duty | Federal and Texas protections (the Lines You Never Cross lesson) |
| **Termination and move-out** | Notice to leave, unit left empty and broom-clean, lock removed | The Running the Site: Open to Close lesson covers move-out checks |
| **Rules and regulations** | Gate hours, speed, no smoking, no dumping | The house rules |

## Lien Language: California and Texas Side by Side

The lien process in the Delinquency and the Lien Process lesson only works if the agreement says the right things. Here is what each state requires.

| Requirement | California | Texas |
|---|---|---|
| Law | Bus. & Prof. Code 21712 | Property Code 59.041 and 59.043 |
| Form | Written agreement | Written agreement |
| Lien statement | The tenant's property will be subject to a claim of lien and may be sold if rent or other charges are unpaid for **14 consecutive days**, and the Act authorizes this | The lien must be **underlined or in conspicuous bold print**, or the facility cannot sell without a court judgment |
| Alternate contact | The agreement must **request** an alternate name and mailing or email address for lien notices; if given, notices go to both | Not required by chapter 59 |
| Email notices | Allowed only if the agreement says lien notices may be emailed and the tenant signs consenting; delivery must be proven | Allowed only if the agreement has **underlined or bold** language that notice may be given by email |
| Towing vehicles | A separate statutory path (the Delinquency and the Lien Process lesson and the RV course) | Towing a vehicle, boat or trailer requires express authorization in the agreement (SB 1181, 2021) |

Email lien notices in California came from **AB 1108**, effective 01/01/2018, which also allowed sale through an auction website. It was made permanent by **AB 2960** in 2022.

What this means at the counter:

- **Collect complete contact information** at every move-in: address, phone and email.
- **In California, ask for an alternate contact** every time, and record it.
- **Never tell a tenant "we'll just email you"** unless the agreement and the signed consent are in place.
- **Ask about military status** and record it.

Missing contact details, a missing alternate contact or missing military status at move-in breaks the lien process months later.

## California SB 709: The First-Page Disclosures

For California rental agreements signed **on or after 01/01/2026**, SB 709 (Bus. & Prof. Code 21715.2) requires the first page, in larger and contrasting type, to disclose:

| Disclosure | What it means |
|---|---|
| The initial term and the renewal term | For example, month-to-month |
| Whether the move-in rate is promotional or discounted, and for how long | A $1 first month must say it is a promotion and how long it lasts |
| Whether the rate can change, and **the maximum rent the owner could charge during the first 12 months** | The tenant sees the ceiling up front |
| Every step needed to end the agreement, including removing all property | No hidden move-out rules |
| The owner's contact information | Who to call |

SB 709 started as a cap on rent increases and became a disclosure law instead.

What you do:

- **Be able to point to and explain each first-page disclosure** before the tenant signs.
- **Never promise "your rate will never go up."** Point to the maximum disclosed for the first 12 months.
- **Never describe a promotional rate as permanent.**

## California Late-Fee Rules

California's Bus. & Prof. Code 21713.5 sets four rules for late fees:

1. **No late fee until rent is unpaid at least 10 days** after the due date.
2. **The fee must be stated in the rental agreement.**
3. **Only one late fee per missed payment.**
4. **A cap by rent:** $10 if monthly rent is $60 or less; $15 if over $60 and under $100; the greater of $20 or 15% of monthly rent if rent is $100 or more.

At Greens, rent is due on the 1st and Cubby posts the late fee at 12:00 AM on the 11th, after 10 full days unpaid, at the cap for the rent (From Greens' Cubby, 11/2026). The amounts are on Greens Fees and Charges.

**Texas:** chapter 59 contains no specific late-fee cap (as of 2026). Fees are set by the rental agreement and should be reasonable. Never teach or quote a Texas late-fee number; Greens' Texas amounts come when the Texas sites join Cubby.

## Prohibited Items and Uses

Every storage agreement lists things that may not be stored and things that may not be done. Greens' agreement is the final word; these are the typical lists.

### Never Allowed

| Prohibited | Why |
|---|---|
| **Living in a unit**, even "just one night" | Units have no exits, ventilation or sanitation; people have died. It violates building and fire codes. Texas law forbids it outright (59.009). |
| **Hazardous materials:** gasoline, propane, fuel cans, paint thinners, solvents, fertilizer, chemicals, compressed gas, asbestos | Fire, explosion and poisoning risk to everyone on the property |
| **Explosives, ammunition and fireworks** | Fire and explosion |
| **Food, animal feed and perishables** | They draw rodents and insects to every unit nearby |
| **Live animals or plants; anything wet or smelly** | Cruelty, mold, odor, pests |
| **Illegal items:** stolen goods, drugs, counterfeit goods | Crime |

Firearms are prohibited in many agreements and allowed in some; follow Greens' agreement and ask your manager. Vehicles and equipment with fuel are usually allowed only in parking spaces or with limits set by the agreement.

### Business Use: Store Yes, Operate No

Storing business goods is normally allowed. Running a business **out of** the unit is not:

| Usually fine | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Inventory for an online store | Customers coming to the unit to buy |
| A contractor's tools and materials | Employees working in the unit |
| A sales rep's samples | Manufacturing, repairs or auto work |
| Office records | Band practice, a workshop, running equipment off unit power |

### Things to Discourage

Cash, jewelry, heirlooms and important documents are better kept somewhere else, and protection plans often exclude them.

### If You See a Prohibited Item or Use

Do not confront the tenant about it on your own and never enter the unit. Report it to your manager. The agreement usually lets the owner enter to remove prohibited items and charge the tenant, but that is a manager's decision with the right notice. Suspected living in a unit, a gas smell or a chemical smell is reported at once (call 911 if anyone may be in danger; the Safety and Emergencies lesson).

## What Greens Can and Cannot Do

| Greens can | Greens cannot |
|---|---|
| Set and change rent with proper notice | Promise a rate will never change |
| Charge the fees in the agreement, within the law | Charge a fee that is not in the agreement |
| Deny access or overlock through the lawful process | Lock out or overlock outside the lawful process |
| Enter as the agreement allows (emergencies, repairs, legal orders, prohibited items, lien enforcement) | Hold a key or go into a rented unit casually |
| Enforce a lien by the statutory process | Skip, shorten or back-date any lien step |
| Refuse to rent, applying the same rules to everyone | Discriminate (the Privacy and Compliance lesson) |
| Enforce the rules equally | Open a unit for someone not on the account |
| | Keep, take, give away or throw out anything from a unit outside the statutory process, even "obvious junk" |
| | Ignore military status or a bankruptcy notice |

## Greens Scenarios

**Escondido, 01/2026.** A new tenant signs on 01/15/2026. Because the agreement is signed on or after 01/01/2026, its first page shows the term, the promotional rate and its length, the maximum rent in the first 12 months, the steps to end it and Greens' contact information. You walk her through each one.

**Valley Center.** A tenant asks, "Can I just keep my camping stove and a gas can in the unit?" You explain that fuel and propane are prohibited because of fire risk, and suggest he keep them at home.

**Murrieta.** A tenant asks if he can work on his motorcycle in his unit on weekends. You explain that storing it may be fine under the agreement, but repairs in the unit are not allowed.

**Temecula.** A move-in customer gives only a phone number. You collect a mailing address and email too, and ask for an alternate contact, explaining that California law asks for one for important notices.

**A Texas site.** A tenant asks why part of the agreement is in bold. You explain that it describes the facility's lien, and Texas requires that language to be underlined or in bold.

## Common Mistakes

- **Rushing the signing** without explaining the first page.
- **Skipping the alternate contact** in California or the military question anywhere.
- **Promising a rate will never change** or that a promotional rate is permanent.
- **Letting someone sleep in a unit** "just tonight."
- **Ignoring a fuel can** or a smell.
- **Quoting a Texas late-fee number.**

## Key Points

- The agreement is a month-to-month rental of space, not a home lease and not a bailment. California: Bus. & Prof. Code 21700-21716. Texas: Property Code ch. 59.
- California requires a 14-consecutive-day lien statement, a request for an alternate contact and signed consent for email notices (21712). Texas requires the lien in underlined or bold print (59.041) and bold or underlined email language.
- Email notices and online sales in California date from AB 1108, effective 01/01/2018, made permanent by AB 2960 in 2022.
- SB 709: California agreements signed on or after 01/01/2026 disclose on page one the term, any promotion and its length, the 12-month maximum rent, how to end the agreement and the owner's contact information.
- California late fees: none before 10 days unpaid, stated in the agreement, one per missed payment, capped by rent (21713.5). Texas: no cap in ch. 59 (as of 2026).
- Never live in a unit, never store hazardous materials, food or explosives; store business goods but never run a business from the unit.
