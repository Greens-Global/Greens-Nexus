## The Moment

A customer at Escondido wants to store a project car that has not run in two years. Its registration sticker is long out of date, and he says, "It's on PNO, so it's legal." He is partly right. Whether it may come in, what you record, and how it may one day leave all depend on two rules: Greens' rule on vehicles that do not run, and the DMV's rule on PNO.

## Greens' Rule: Drivable, Registered, Insured

A stored vehicle must be drivable (no flat tires, broken windows or fluid leaks) and registered and insured in the tenant's name. A vehicle that runs moves under its own power when the tenant needs it to, and it is less likely to leak or be abandoned.

A vehicle that **does not run** is stored only when your manager approves it. When the manager approves one, the **Non-Operational Vehicle Deposit** applies (the amount is on Greens by the Numbers). You never approve one yourself, and you never quote the deposit as a way around the rule.

## What PNO Is

In California, an owner who will not drive, tow, store or park a vehicle **on a public road or highway for a whole registration year** can file **Planned Non-Operation (PNO)** with the DMV instead of paying full registration. Owners use it for project cars, a second car, or an RV parked for a year.

Our lot is private property, not a public road, so a PNO vehicle may sit here. Three things follow:

- **Its sticker will look expired.** That is the point of PNO. On a lot walk an expired sticker on a PNO vehicle is not, by itself, a sign of abandonment. The Cubby note tells you.
- **It cannot leave on a public road until it is registered again.** The DMV's rule covers driving and towing. A tenant who plans to "tow it out Saturday" renews the registration first. Never tell a tenant a PNO vehicle may go out on the road.
- **It still needs insurance.** Proof of insurance applies as for any vehicle.

## What You Ask For

- [ ] Photo ID, as for any move-in.
- [ ] **Proof from the DMV that PNO is on file,** in the tenant's name.
- [ ] Proof of insurance.
- [ ] The VIN, plate and lienholder, as for any vehicle.
- [ ] Your manager's OK, because the house rule asks for current registration. If it also does not run, the deposit applies.

Then write **"PNO" and the registration year** in the Cubby note on the lease, so the next person on a lot walk knows why the sticker is old.

> [!TIP]
> Never advise on a DMV filing. "The DMV can tell you how to file or renew" is the whole answer.

## When It Leaves

When the tenant is ready to take a PNO vehicle out, ask whether it is registered again. If it is, update Cubby. If it is not, tell the tenant the DMV needs to renew it before it goes on the road, and let your manager know. Staff never move it, whatever its status.

## At Greens

**Escondido.** The project car: you take the DMV's PNO proof, ID and insurance, and ask your manager. The car does not run, so if the manager approves it the deposit applies. You write "PNO" and the year in Cubby.

**Fairfield.** On a walk you find a motorhome with a sticker a year out of date. The Cubby note says PNO, and the account is current. You move on.

**Temecula.** A tenant says he will tow his PNO trailer home on Saturday. You explain that it must be registered again before it goes on the road, and suggest he check with the DMV first.

## Common Mistakes

- **Calling every old sticker abandonment.** Check for a PNO note and the account first.
- **Treating PNO as permission to tow on the road.** It is the opposite.
- **Approving a non-running vehicle yourself,** or skipping the Cubby note.

## Key Points

- Greens stores vehicles that run, registered and insured; a non-running vehicle needs your manager's approval and the deposit.
- PNO means the vehicle stays off public roads for the year; our private lot is fine.
- Ask for the DMV's PNO proof, ID and insurance, and note "PNO" and the year in Cubby.
- A PNO vehicle is registered again before it leaves on a public road.
