## The Moment

An older customer at Murrieta has backed his 40 ft trailer into a space twice and still sits crooked, with his coupler at the fence. He is not a bad driver; he is in the wrong shape of space, parked the wrong way round. The shape of a space decides whether a tenant backs up, which way the rig faces, and how hard the next hook-up is.

Cubby names Greens' spaces by shape, which is why a pricing group reads "12' x 32' Parking - Gravel, Drive-In."

## Drive-In Spaces

A **drive-in** space is open at **one end only**. The other end is a fence, a wall, a curb, a wheel stop, or the tail of the space in the next row. A vehicle goes in and comes out through the same end. This is Greens' ordinary space, and the Site Profiles call it **back-in (drive-in)**: one shape, two names.

| Vehicle | Goes in | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **A trailer** (travel trailer, fifth wheel, boat, cargo) | **Backed in**, tongue to the aisle | Next time the tow vehicle backs to the coupler in the aisle, hooks up and pulls straight out. Nosed in, the owner hooks up in the dead end and backs the whole rig out blind |
| **A motorhome, van or truck camper** | **Backed in**, windshield to the aisle | Driving out forward is safer than backing into an aisle full of people |
| **A car or small utility trailer** | Either way | Short enough that it hardly matters |

The closed end catches new staff three ways:

- **A wheel stop marks the tires, not the vehicle.** A Class A's rear overhang (the body behind the rear wheels) can be 8 to 12 ft. Fine against a painted line; a damage claim against a fence.
- **The tongue sits at the open end.** It is part of the measured length and stays inside the line, never in the aisle.
- **Neighbors.** A middle space leaves a mirror's width each side. An **end space** against a fence has one neighbor and is easy to ask for. A **corner space**, where two rows meet, is the hardest turn on the lot; do not give it to a 40 ft rig.

## Pull-Through Spaces

A **pull-through** is open at **both ends**, with an aisle at each. Nobody backs more than a truck length:

1. Arriving, the tow vehicle pulls the trailer straight in and unhooks, leaving it facing the exit.
2. Leaving, the tow vehicle comes in behind, backs the few feet to the coupler, hooks up and drives out the far end.
3. A motorhome simply drives in, and later drives out the other end.

That is why pull-throughs are the premium product, and why there are few: each uses two aisle frontages, so the lot gives up spaces to make one. Murrieta and Temecula have them; Murrieta's 12x50s are all pull-through.

- **The lane has a direction.** Rows usually run one way. Show the tenant which end is in and which is out the first time.
- **Both ends stay clear.** A rig parked with its tail in the far aisle blocks a lane.
- **Length still rules.** Hitch to bumper plus the margin, inside the lines at both ends.

## Angled Spaces

An **angled** space sits at 45 or 60 degrees to a **one-way** aisle. Turning in from the right direction is a gentle curve, so a long rig needs less aisle and a nervous driver gets in without a spotter. The one rule: **come from the right direction.** Against the arrows, every space is a jackknife turn. Faded arrows on an angled row are worth a same-day report.

## Other Shapes

- **Tandem (double-length):** two vehicles nose to tail for one tenant. The front one moves for the back one.
- **Covered rows:** drive-in spaces with posts at the ends of each bay. The post, not the line, is what gets hit.
- **Enclosed:** always drive-in; a garage has one door. Backing a trailer between door jambs is the hardest parking on the site.

## Who Gets Which Shape

| The customer | Offer first |
|---|---|
| A big motorhome, or "backing is hard for me" | A pull-through that fits; else the longest drive-in on an easy aisle |
| A trailer towed often | A drive-in near the gate, backed in |
| A dawn boat launch | A drive-in with a straight run to the gate |
| A long rig, a nervous driver, no pull-through open | An end space on a wide aisle, plus the pull-through waitlist |
| A car, jet ski trailer or pop-up | Any drive-in that fits |

> [!TIP]
> Ask every vehicle customer: "How do you usually park it?" The answer tells you whether to lead with a pull-through before you quote a price.

## At Greens

**Murrieta.** For the customer who keeps sitting crooked, you check Cubby for an open 12x50 pull-through and quote it. If none is open, you offer an end space and the pull-through waitlist.

**Temecula.** A tenant nosed a boat trailer into a drive-in and now must back his truck into the dead end to hook up. You show him the tongue-to-the-aisle way for next time.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating the wheel stop as the end of the vehicle.** The overhang goes past it.
- **Letting a trailer nose in.** Every later hook-up becomes a blind reverse.
- **Sending a long rig to a corner space.** It is the hardest turn on the lot.

## Key Points

- Drive-in: open at one end. Back trailers and motorhomes in, facing the aisle.
- Pull-through: an aisle at each end, one-way, the premium product.
- Angled: easy from the right direction, a jackknife from the wrong one.
- Ask how they park before you quote.
