## The Moment

A coworker at Escondido says, "That car in row B is two months behind. Let's add it to next week's auction and save time." It would be a serious mistake: a buyer could never register that car, and the lender on its title was never told. A titled vehicle or boat has its own path. You never run it yourself, but you need to know its shape, collect the right facts at move-in, and stop anyone who skips a step.

> [!WARNING]
> Training only, not legal advice. When in doubt, stop and ask your manager.

## Why Vehicles Are Different

- **The state holds the title.** A buyer cannot register a vehicle unless the sale followed the state's title process.
- **A lender is often on the title** (the legal owner), and its lien usually ranks ahead of the storage lien. A sale may pay the lender first and Greens little or nothing.
- **The state process takes longer** and must reach the owners and lienholders on the state's record.

So the goal is often to get a defaulted vehicle off the lot, lawfully, before the rent owed and the risk grow.

### The Law Behind It

California's storage law (Bus. & Prof. Code 21702.5) sends a vehicle or vessel that must be registered down a different path from the unit sale:

- **A vehicle** goes through the DMV lien sale, **Civil Code 3071**, whatever its value. The DMV's shorter process for vehicles worth $4,000 or less (Civil Code 3072) cannot be used for a storage lien. The facility applies to the DMV (form REG 656); the DMV notifies the registered owner and the legal owner; an owner who opposes stops the sale and the facility must go to court; otherwise a Notice of Pending Lien Sale (REG 280) is sent and published, and the sale is certified on REG 168.
- **A vessel** goes through **Harbors and Navigation Code 503**. Over $1,500 the DMV must authorize the sale; the owner gets certified-mail notice; the boat is open for inspection before the sale; the owner may redeem it within 10 days after the sale. A boat's trailer may need its own handling: your manager's call.
- **The lien may not include rent or charges accruing more than 60 days** after it attaches.
- **The tow option:** instead of a sale, a vehicle, boat or trailer may be towed once rent is 60 days unpaid and the preliminary lien notice was sent, with at least 10 days' notice by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing naming the tow company, its address and phone.

## Texas, Side by Side

Greens' Texas sites are run by CubeSmart on CubeSmart's own system and rules, so you will not work a Texas account in Cubby. The Texas law differs in ways worth knowing:

| | California | Texas |
|---|---|---|
| The law | 21702.5 sends vehicles to Civil Code 3071 and vessels to H&N Code 503 | Property Code 59.0445 adds steps for titled vehicles, boats and outboard motors |
| Who is told | Registered and legal owners, through the DMV | The last known owner and every recorded lienholder, within 30 days after taking possession |
| Owner's way out | Pay, or oppose (court); a vessel owner may redeem within 10 days after the sale | Pay before the 31st day |
| Tow option | 60 days unpaid, preliminary notice sent, 10 days' mailed notice | The lease must authorize towing, no payment by the 14th day after notice, and a licensed vehicle storage facility agrees in writing; the lien then ends |

## When the 61st Day Task Comes Up

- It goes to your manager. You do not start a DMV filing, a tow or a notice.
- Gather what your manager will need: the lease, the vehicle details, VIN or HIN, plate and plate state, title holder and lienholders, insurance, and the move-in photos.
- Photograph the vehicle's condition now, before any tow.

## Never Do This

- Never put a vehicle or boat in a unit auction.
- Never tow, boot, lock or move a vehicle, or call a tow company on your own.
- Never promise a tenant a date it will or will not be sold or towed.
- Never discuss the account with a lender or anyone else unless your manager says they are entitled to it.
- Never accept "just keep it, I don't want it" as permission. Write down exactly what was said, when, and pass it on.
- Never take a lien step against someone who is or may be in the military; Self-Storage Fundamentals covers those protections.

## At Greens

**Temecula.** A trailer account reaches the 61st day. You photograph the trailer, pull the lease, VIN, plate and lienholder, and hand them to your manager. You do not call a tow company.

**Fairfield.** A tenant 50 days behind calls: "Just keep the boat, I'm done." You thank him, write down his words and the time, and pass them to your manager.

**Murrieta.** A lender's representative asks about a motorhome. You take a name and number and pass it on without confirming anything.

## Common Mistakes

- **Sending a vehicle to the unit auction** to "save time."
- **Treating "keep it" as a signed release.**
- **No photos before a tow.**

## Key Points

- A titled vehicle or boat never goes through the unit auction.
- Until the 61st day the ladder is the unit ladder; from there the vehicle path is the DMV lien sale, the vessel lien sale or the tow option.
- The 61st day task goes to your manager with the lease, the vehicle facts and fresh photos.
- Never tow, move or lock a vehicle yourself.
