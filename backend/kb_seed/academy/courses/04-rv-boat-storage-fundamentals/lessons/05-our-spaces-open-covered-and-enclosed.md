## The Moment

A customer at Temecula wants "the covered one" for a fifth wheel with three roof air conditioners. A canopy protects that roof from sun and hail, and its lowest beam can tear the air conditioners off. Knowing what each kind of space protects against, and where its limit is, is how you sell it safely.

| Product | Protects against | Its limit | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** | Nothing overhead | None for height | The lowest |
| **Covered (canopy)** | Sun, UV and hail | The lowest beam, and the posts | The middle |
| **Enclosed (garage)** | Weather and most theft | The door's real width and height | The highest |

### Open

A marked space on a paved or gravel lot. Anything fits, because there is nothing to hit. It is the right choice for most trailers, work vehicles and boats under their own cover. Be honest: sun, rain and wind reach the vehicle. A fitted cover, tied down, helps a lot.

### Covered

A roof on posts, open on the sides. In a common design the posts stand about every 24 ft, so a bay holds two 12 ft spaces. Two limits:

- **Height.** Clear height is to the **lowest** point under the roof: the beam, not the roof sheet. A rig that is too tall loses its air conditioner or antenna on the beam, which means a damage claim and a building repair.
- **Posts.** Tenants back into them, and end and corner posts take the most hits.

A canopy does not stop blowing rain or dust, and low afternoon sun still reaches the west side of a rig.

### Enclosed

A garage with a roll-up door. A common design is 14 ft wide and 30 to 45 ft deep, with a door 12 ft wide and 14 ft high. **The door is the limit, and the real door is smaller than its label.**

> [!WARNING]
> A door sold as 14 ft high may measure about 13 ft 8 in once it is hung, and a raised rain lip can take another inch. A wake boat with its tower or a pontoon with its bimini can top 13 ft on the trailer. Measure the actual opening, and post it at the door.

## Space Sizes

Sizes are **width x depth in feet**, from about 10x20 for a car up to 12x50 for the longest motorhomes.

| Size range | Typical vehicles |
|---|---|
| 10x15 to 10x20 | Cars, motorcycles, jet ski trailers, pop-ups |
| 10x25 to 11x30 | Long pickups, Class B vans, short Class Cs, bass boats, cargo trailers |
| 11x30 to 12x35 | Most travel trailers, Class Cs, pontoons and wake boats |
| 12x38 to 12x45 | Long travel trailers, most fifth wheels, shorter Class As, toy haulers |
| 12x45 to 12x50 | The longest Class As and fifth wheels |

These ranges are a starting point; the measured length decides the space. Width matters too: a 10 ft wide space is tight for wide mirrors, an awning or a slide that sticks out. A 12 ft space leaves room to open doors and walk around.

## Gravel and Paved

- **Paved** is easy to drive and keep clean, but softens in summer heat. Jacks sink unless they sit on pads.
- **Gravel** drains well and keeps rates down. It is dusty, can rut, and heavy tongue jacks sink into it when wet.

Fairfield is all gravel, Murrieta all paved, Escondido and Temecula have both.

## Aisles and Gates

A 40 ft rig needs room to swing, so vehicle lots have wide aisles, stacking room at the gate so a waiting rig does not block the street, gate timers long enough for a long combination to clear, bollards at corners and canopy ends, and painted space numbers that match Cubby. You do not design lots, but you notice when something stops working: a gate that closes early, a faded line, a missing number, a bent post. Report it (see Walking the Lot).

So most of what you sell is open space: sell it well, with the gate, the walks and an honest word about covers. Covered and enclosed spaces are scarce and usually have a waitlist. Some sites also have large storage units that are not set up for vehicles; never offer one for a vehicle unless the Site Profile says it takes one.

Cubby records no heights. Clear heights come from your manager's measurements; your manager has them until each Site Profile is filled in. The next lesson, The Shape of a Space, covers drive-in, pull-through and angled spaces, then come the five Site Profiles.

**Temecula.** Before you look at covered spaces for the fifth wheel, you ask the height to the top of the air conditioners and get the covered rows' clear height from your manager.

**Valley Center.** A collector wants a garage for a classic car. Both are leased. You take a full waitlist entry and offer open space at Escondido meanwhile.

## Common Mistakes

- **Reading the clear height off the roof sheet.** The beam is lower.
- **Trusting a door's label.** A "14 ft" door is less once hung. Measure.
- **Offering a big storage unit for a car** without checking that the site allows vehicles in it.

## Key Points

- Open: cheapest, any height. Covered: sun and hail protection, with a beam and posts to hit. Enclosed: the best protection, limited by the door.
- Clear height is to the lowest beam or the measured door opening.
- Sizes are width x depth; most parking runs 10x20 to 12x50.
- Covered space is only at Temecula; enclosed vehicle space only at Valley Center.
