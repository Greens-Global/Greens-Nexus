## The Moment

A customer at Temecula has the boat registration in hand, the pontoon on the trailer behind his truck, and wants to be gone in ten minutes. The trailer registration is at home. If you let it in "just for today," you have a vehicle on the lot that the lien process, the insurer and the gate list cannot fully reach. Every item below comes back later; collect it now, because you will not get it later.

## Why Vehicles Need More

- **The state knows who owns it.** A vehicle or boat has a title and a registration. If the tenant stops paying, the lien steps must reach the owner and the lender on the state's record.
- **A lender may own part of it,** and its lien usually ranks ahead of the storage lien.
- **It can be driven away,** so who may take it out matters as much as who may get in.
- **It can leak, be damaged or be stolen,** and the tenant's own insurance is what pays.

Greens' checklist and house rules are on Greens Vehicle Storage Rules, later in this course. This lesson is the why.

## The Documents

**1. Photo ID.** Compare the name to the registration and the insurance card.

**2. Current registration for every vehicle,** in the tenant's name or a business they sign for: the RV or car, the boat **and** its trailer (registered separately), each jet ski, every cargo or utility trailer. Documents in someone else's name stop the move-in until your manager decides.

**3. Proof of insurance.** The tenant's own policy covers the vehicle. Greens' property insurance does not, and the tenant protection plan does not cover vehicles (Greens Tenant Protection). Record the policy number and expiration date in Cubby, and work the "Remind Tenant of POI Obligation" task when proof is missing or expired.

> [!WARNING]
> Never say "we're insured" or "you're covered here." The answer to "Do you cover damage?" is: "Your own vehicle insurance covers your RV. We need proof of it at move-in."

**4. VIN or HIN and the details.** VIN (or the HIN on a boat's transom), make, model, year, color, plate and plate state, and Cubby's vehicle type. **The VIN or HIN must match** on the registration and the insurance card. A mismatch is a stop: nothing goes in until it matches.

**5. Title holder and lienholder.** Ask: "Is there a loan on it? Who is the lender?" Record the legal owner and every lienholder. If the account ever defaults, the vehicle lien steps must reach them (see Vehicle and Boat Liens).

**6. Out-of-state plates.** Note them clearly; a later lien lookup goes through another state's agency.

**7. Which vehicle, exactly.** Write the specific vehicle into the lease. A tenant who wants to swap vehicles updates the lease and Cubby first.

A vehicle that does not run, or that is on Planned Non-Operation with the DMV, needs your manager's OK first; the next lesson, Vehicles That Do Not Run, and PNO, covers both.

## Move-In Photos

Photos are the baseline for every later talk about damage, leaks or abandonment.

- [ ] All four sides of the vehicle and the trailer
- [ ] Close-ups of existing damage: dents, scratches, cracked fiberglass, torn covers
- [ ] The plate and the VIN or HIN
- [ ] The space, before and after the vehicle goes in
- [ ] Anything unusual: a leak stain, a flat, an open hatch

## Who May Drive It Out

- **List every authorized driver** on the lease, and give gate access only to them.
- **Staff never drive or move a tenant's vehicle.** An operator who moves vehicles takes on their "care, custody and control" and needs insurance to match. The tenant moves it, or your manager decides.
- Someone not on the lease arrives to take a vehicle: stop and check with your manager, and never confirm to them that anyone is a tenant.

## At Greens

**Temecula.** The pontoon owner emails a photo of the trailer registration within the hour; you record both, then complete the move-in.

**Murrieta.** The insurance card shows a different VIN: the customer swapped trailers and never updated the policy. The move-in waits until the numbers match.

## Common Mistakes

- **One registration for a boat and its trailer.** It takes two.
- **Skipping the lender question.** It is the hardest thing to find later.
- **Letting it in "just for today"** before every document is in.
- **Moving a vehicle "just a few feet."** Never.

## Key Points

- ID, current registration for every vehicle, and the tenant's own insurance.
- The VIN or HIN matches on every document, or nothing goes in.
- Record the lienholder and authorized drivers at signing.
- Staff never move a tenant's vehicle.
