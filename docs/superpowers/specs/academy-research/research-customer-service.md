# Research: Customer Service & Sales Fundamentals (Greens Storage)

Research notes for a front-line course at a self-storage and RV/boat storage operator in California and Texas that runs on Cubby. This is source material, not the course.

**How to read this file**
- Each claim carries its source URL inline. **[DATED]** marks material more than about 5 years old or tied to old pricing or technology. **[UNVERIFIED]** marks a secondhand claim I could not trace to its primary source. **[LEGAL - confirm with counsel]** marks anything that touches state law.

## 1. Who the customer is and what they feel

### 1.1 Why people rent
- **Life events.** The industry shorthand is the "Ds": death, divorce, disaster, dislocation (moving). The fourth D changes with who is speaking. SSA blog: https://www.selfstorage.org/blog/articleid/44/the-four-d-39-s . A widely repeated claim says the Ds drive about half of rentals, with space shortage about a third: https://gcn.com/?p=22951 **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Moving is the top reason.**
  - StorageCafe survey of more than 1,700 U.S. respondents: moving 37%, lack of space at home 22%. https://www.storagecafe.com/blog/?p=1656
  - Storable Spring 2024 Tenant Insights: moving drove 40% of bookings and downsizing 18%. https://info.storable.com/hubfs/eBooks/Storable%202024%20Spring%20Tenant%20Insights%20Report.pdf
  - Storable 2025 Outlook: 37% of Americans were considering or planning a move within 6 to 12 months, up from 25%. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/suppliers-products/storable-releases-2025-self-storage-industry-outlook-report
- **Serious disruption.** 26.5% of users cite at least one serious disruption, such as divorce, eviction, death or money trouble. https://www.hireahelper.com/advice/self-storage-statistics/
- **UK data, as a pointer only.** SSA UK (2021) found that a death in the family was the most common life event behind storage, and that divorced or separated people were twice as likely to use it. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/associations/self-storage-association-of-the-united-kingdom-releases-annual-industry-report
- **Length of stay.** The SSA 2023 Demand Study (secondhand) puts the average stay at about 20 months, up from 16.5 in 2020, and penetration at about 11.1% of U.S. households. https://rebusinessonline.com/?p=429928 , https://digital.modernstoragemedia.com/almanac/self-storage-almanac-2025ok/section-1-industry-data . Customers usually underestimate how long they will stay (DeFazio 2026, below).

### 1.2 What they feel (useful for the empathy module)
- **They are stressed.** Many are mid-move, mid-divorce, grieving, or handling a parent's estate. Spartan Investment Group's CEO: be compassionate, keep paperwork right so you do not add to a family's grief, and keep a calm environment. Scott Lewis, Jun 2020. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/customer-service/self-storage-and-3-ds-supporting-tenants-times-death-divorce-or-displacement-0
- **They are new to storage.** Most shoppers have never rented. They do not know how keypads, hasps or disc locks work, what size they need, or how to pack. They see their belongings as personal history, and they can tell within about 10 seconds whether you sound scripted or caring. Sarah Beth DeFazio, Universal Storage Group, Jun 16, 2026. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/sales/stepping-over-the-very-low-bar-frank-sales-and-service-advice-for-self-storage-facility-managers . In some markets up to 50% of residents have never rented (Ballard and Maxwell, 2011 **[DATED]**, below).
- **They price-shop and research first.** They check rates, reviews and Google photos online, then call to verify, then visit. They have usually compared three competitors before they call (DeFazio 2026).
- **They decide fast and loyally.**
  - "58% of people will rent from the first self-storage facility that they call," attributed to the SSA 2023 Demand Study by a call-center vendor. https://listselfstorage.com/us/industry-insights/how-much-revenue-do-self-storage-facilities-lose-from-missed-calls/ **[UNVERIFIED, vendor-sponsored]**
  - StoragePug attributes "57% rented from the first facility they contacted" to the SSA 2025 Demand Study. https://www.storagepug.com/blog/how-to-rate-your-self-storage-facility **[UNVERIFIED]**
  - Course takeaway: the first good conversation usually wins the rental.
- **They distrust the industry.** Common pain points are rate increases after a low move-in special, fees, and lien sales. Consumer press runs stories on repeated price hikes (e.g., https://wmar2news.com/matterformallory/customers-fed-up-with-continual-price-hikes-at-self-storage-facilities). Assume the customer has read a bad review of the industry before.

### 1.3 What drives choice
- **StorageCafe 2025** (2,824 renters): price was the top single factor at 27%, with security close behind (about 17% per a secondary summary). https://www.storagecafe.com/blog/self-storage-demand-and-trends-2025/
- **StorageCafe 2026** (1,430 respondents): price 28%, location 17%; 54% chose climate-controlled units. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/suppliers-products/storagecafe-2026-survey-shows-more-renters-using-self-storage-as-extension-of-their-homes
- **Storable 2025 Outlook** (multi-select): price 83%, convenient or accessible location 68%. https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/news/storable-releases-2025-self-storage-industry-outlook
- **Takeaway:** price leads, but location, security, convenience (access hours, drive-up) and reviews decide close calls. I found no survey that measures cleanliness, but every operator source treats it as part of the "security and trust" impression.

### 1.4 RV and boat customers (different from household storage)
- They rent year after year, often seasonally. They care about security, 24-hour access, wash bays, dump stations and air. Many rent "for emotional rather than economic reasons," so they are less price-driven. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/running-a-profitable-boat-rv-storage-operation-service-and-amenities-drive-success , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-storage/how-effectively-operate-boatrv-storage-side-your-self-storage-business
- Discovery questions to add for this customer:
  - Overall length including tongue or hitch, and height. Covered, enclosed or open?
  - How often do you take it out? Do you need early or late access?
  - Is it registered and insured? Protection plans for vehicles often differ; there is a boat/RV-specific product, for example: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/tenant-insurance-protection/tenant-property-protection-launches-tpp-plus-for-boat-rv-self-storage-customers
  - Who else will drive it in and out?
- Reviews praise staff who are "knowledgeable" about the vehicles and who communicate (U-Haul review pages, low-quality source).

## 2. Service standards and frameworks

### 2.1 Service-recovery frameworks
- **LAST: Listen, Apologize, Solve, Thank.** Widely taught. Order matters: solving before listening produces a fix that does not match the problem. On public channels, move quickly from Listen to Apologize. https://www.customerservicemanager.com/what-is-the-last-method-for-customer-service , https://centeredgesoftware.com/blog/5-effective-ways-to-resolve-complaints-with-last/ . It is usually credited to Disney; one widely cited source is https://www.mouseplanet.com/6982/Providing_Service_that_LASTs
- **LEAST** adds Empathize: Listen, Empathize, Apologize, Solve, Thank. https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ahlei-cgsp/service-recovery/least-model
- **HEARD** (Disney): Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Resolve, Diagnose. "Diagnose" means fixing the process, not blaming a person. https://wp.customerservicegroup.com/2025/05/15/service-recovery-the-disney-way/ , https://www.dentalmanagers.com/blog/distinctioncast-disneys-service-recovery-the-art-of-de-escalation-techniques-for-the-om-and-team/
- **Ritz-Carlton empowerment.** Front-line staff may spend up to $2,000 per guest to fix a problem without asking. https://customerservicemanager.com/?p=52823 . For storage, the lesson is to give staff a defined authority, for example "may waive one late fee per tenant per year" or "may grant up to $X credit." Greens sets the number.
- **Chick-fil-A "Second Mile Service."** Every customer on every visit gets at least one element beyond the expected. "My pleasure" in place of "you're welcome" began around 2001 to 2003. https://www.familybusinessmagazine.com/chick-fil-second-generation-aims-go-second-mile-0?page=1 , https://www.mentalfloss.com/food/fast-food/why-chick-fil-a-employees-say-my-pleasure . Storage versions of the second mile:
  - carry the lock and boxes to the car;
  - a handwritten thank-you card;
  - a call a few days after move-in (DeFazio 2026);
  - a condolence card in the unit for a bereaved family (Lewis 2020);
  - Jordheim's "do something unexpected" (below).
- **Active listening** (general practice, no single source needed):
  - Let the customer finish.
  - Paraphrase ("So the movers come on the 15th and you need somewhere for a two-bedroom until the house closes. Did I get that right?").
  - Label the emotion ("That sounds like a stressful week").
  - Take notes and use the customer's own words back to them.
  - Ballard: tie every product benefit to a remark the caller made (below).

### 2.2 Storage-specific service habits
Source: Tron Jordheim, PhoneSmart (StorageMart's call center), "10 Tried-and-True Tips," Jan 2, 2012 **[DATED but timeless]**. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/retail-sales/10-tried-and-true-tips-for-becoming-a-master-of-self-storage-sales-and-customer-service

1. Keep studying the craft.
2. Smile; callers hear it.
3. Natural eye contact.
4. Listen.
5. Say you are there to help: "Sure, I can help you with that."
6. Ask good questions.
7. Do something unexpected.
8. Share a laugh. His "Chuckle Rule": each shared laugh raises your odds of the sale, but three to four is the limit.
9. Ask for the sale.
10. Ask for a referral.

His training tip: track yourself against the list for a week, and teach a few tips at a time.

### 2.3 Tone, appearance and office
- **Before you pick up** (Ballard and Maxwell, 2011): remove gum, finish food or drink, smile, focus.
- **On the phone** (Weinman, 2010): callers can hear attitude.
- **Presentation** (DeFazio 2026): keep the office, drive aisles, keypads, paint and restrooms clean.
- **Mystery-shop item:** "employee stood up to greet the shopper" (Mixon 2020, below).
- **Posting:** post the rent due date and time in the office. https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/msm-exclusives/top-10-customer-complaints (Nov 2018)
- **Gates:** post clear gate instructions at both the entrance and exit gates, since most "my code doesn't work" problems are users not knowing the steps (same source).

### 2.5 Language access (CA and TX)
- **California:** 28.8% of residents aged 5 and older speak Spanish at home, the highest share of any state (2024 ACS via USAFacts). Of U.S. Spanish speakers, 58.9% say they speak English "very well," so roughly 4 in 10 do not. https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-speak-spanish-at-home/country/united-states

## 3. The phone

### 3.1 Why the phone still matters
- **The 35% rule of thumb.** "The average conversion rate of calls to sales in this industry is 35 percent." Sue Weinman, Apr 15, 2010 **[DATED]**. Her example: 600 calls a year at 35% is 210 rentals and about $273K in revenue; missing 15% of calls costs about $41K. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/conversions-renovations/increasing-your-conversion-rate-helping-self-storage-managers-turn-callers-into-renters . Storeganise repeats the figure: https://storeganise.com/blog/self-storage-conversion-rate
- **Newer numbers, all weak:**
  - "Phone conversion fell from 65% to 57%," attributed to an SSA 2025 report by Stashbee. https://stashbee.com/blog/self-storage/how-to-increase-self-storage-sales-conversion-rates **[UNVERIFIED - likely confused with the "57% rent from the first facility contacted" stat]**
  - Swivl (AI phone vendor), Sep 28, 2026: about 1 in 7 sales calls became a rental (38 rentals from 252 sales calls, about 15%), but about 4% when every inbound touchpoint is counted. Voice was credited with 88 to 91% of rentals. The lesson is that the denominator decides the rate: count only sales calls when measuring staff. https://www.tryswivl.com/blog/self-storage-caller-intent-conversion
  - StoragePug frames about 20% sales conversion as something to improve. https://www.storagepug.com/blog/how-to-rate-your-self-storage-facility
  - Public Storage reported its "highest ever lead conversion" in Q3 2025, with no percentage given.
  - **Suggested course benchmark:** measure call-to-reservation and reservation-to-move-in separately; aim for at least 35% of new-rental calls ending in a rental or reservation, and set Greens' own target from Cubby data.
- **Caller mix.** Up to 80% of incoming calls may come from existing tenants (vendor, unsourced). About 15 to 20% of calls arrive after hours. https://listselfstorage.com/us/industry-insights/how-much-revenue-do-self-storage-facilities-lose-from-missed-calls/ **[UNVERIFIED]** . Missing a call cuts conversion of that lead by 52% ("some experts," unsourced).
- **What shoppers actually find:**
  - **IntelliShop study** (Ron Welty, Dec 2004 **[DATED]**; about 1,000 calls to 500+ facilities). Staff were friendly: 92% answered professionally, and 94.9% asked about needs. They did not sell:
    - only 25.4% asked the caller to rent;
    - 98% invited a visit, but only 33% asked to set an appointment;
    - 21% offered to send follow-up information;
    - 22% gave no information about features;
    - 39.8% used the property name at the close;
    - 87% of shoppers said they would rent from the person they spoke to, based on personal comfort.
    https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-industry-news/telephone-mystery-shopping-a-study
  - **DeFazio, 2026:** many managers do not answer at all; roughly 95% of those who do fail to capture contact details; reservations are almost never offered.
  - **Jordheim, Oct 2015:** about 90% of the time, an "How late are you open?" call gets the hours and no "Why did you call?" https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-industry-news/act-like-you-care-and-sell-like-you-mean-it-engage-and-capture-self-storage-customers
  - **Teaching point:** friendliness is common; asking for the rental, capturing contact details and following up are rare. That is where Greens wins.

### 3.2 Structure of a storage sales call
Combined from Ballard and Maxwell 2011, Weinman 2010, DeFazio 2026, Mixon 2020 and the ISS script articles. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/retail-sales/why-self-storage-operators-still-need-sales-scripts-and-what-they-should-include , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/retail-sales/your-self-storage-sales-blueprint-creating-an-effective-script-for-online-phone-and-in-person-interactions

1. **Greeting.** Company and property name, your name, an offer to help. Scripts should set the tone rather than force word-for-word recital.
2. **Find out why they called.** Even for "What are your hours?" (Jordheim).
3. **Discovery.** The four Ballard questions:
   - "Have you ever rented self-storage before?"
   - "What do you plan to store?" (also screens for prohibited items such as tires, batteries, perishables, hazardous materials and livestock, and spots commercial users)
   - "When do you need to move in?" (creates urgency)
   - "How long do you think you'll need storage?" (drives unit location and how long a discount runs)
   DeFazio adds: any climate-sensitive items? Get the name and phone number early.
4. **Recommend a size and unit.** Do not ask "What size do you need?" (Ballard; DeFazio). See section 4.
5. **Build value.** Tie features to what they said: gate and cameras, lighting, carts, access hours, climate control, drive-up access, truck, on-site staff, cleanliness. State one clear thing that sets the property apart. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/retail-sales/sales-fail-how-to-avoid-pushing-your-self-storage-walk-ins-out-the-door (Mees 2020)
6. **Price.** Give it after value. Explain the move-in special and when it ends, plus the admin fee, the protection plan, and the lock if one is needed. Disclose rate increases honestly (see 6.2).
7. **Close.** Ask for the rental or reservation, or an appointment with a set time. Assumptive close: "Great, let's get you set up in the 10-by-10. When do you want to move in? Does Saturday work?" (DeFazio). Option close: offer one unit below and one above the target. https://www.radiusplus.com/post/closing-walk-in-sales-at-your-self-storage-facility
8. **Objections** (section 7).
9. **Capture and follow up.** Name, phone, email, move date and the unit quoted. Promise a specific callback time and keep it (Weinman). Thank them, using the property name.

### 3.3 Sample scripts (assembled from the sources above, for adaptation)

**Greeting (new caller)**
> "Thank you for calling Greens Storage [Location], this is Maria. How can I help you today?"

**Turning an hours question into discovery** (after Jordheim)
> Caller: "How late are you open?"
> "Our office is open until 6 today, and gate access runs until 9. Are you looking at renting a unit, or can I help you with your account?"

**Discovery**
> "Happy to help you find the right space. Have you rented storage before? ... What are you planning to store? ... Is that from an apartment or a house, and about how many rooms? ... When are you moving? ... And how long do you think you'll need it?"

**Recommendation and value, tied to their words**
> "Since your movers come on the 15th and the new place isn't ready until spring, I'd put you in a 10-by-15 on the ground floor. It's drive-up, so you can back the truck right to the door, and with a sofa, a queen bed and about 30 boxes you'll still have a walkway. You mentioned your mom's dresser. If it's solid wood, our climate-controlled building keeps it out of the summer heat."

**Price and close**
> "That unit is $X a month, and this month the first month is $1. There's a one-time $X admin fee, and protection starts at a few dollars a month. I can reserve it right now with no charge so it's held for the 15th. What's the best phone number and email for the confirmation?"

**Ballard-style urgency** (2011)
> "Since your movers won't be coming until the first of next month, you have a unique opportunity to make this the most stress-free move of your lifetime."

**Callback promise**
> "Let me check that and call you back by 3 o'clock. What's the best number?" Then call at 3.

**Answering for a call center or another location.** Use the same structure, plus the property name and directions.

### 3.4 Voicemail and callback rules
These are compiled practices; set Greens' own numbers.
- Return every voicemail and missed call within one business hour during open hours, and first thing the next business morning for after-hours calls. This follows Byrd's 30-minute "mandatory" target for web leads and the 52% missed-call penalty.
- **Outgoing message:** property name, office hours and gate hours, the website for online rental, and a promise of a callback.
- **Message for a prospect:** first name, property, the unit and price they asked about, your direct number, and a time you will try again. Example from Byrd 2021: "Hi, Joe. This is Garrett with XYZ Self-Storage in Smithville."
- **Never** leave account balance or collections details on voicemail or with a third party (section 6.4).
- Log every attempt in Cubby against the lead.

- Many operators roll overflow and after-hours calls to a dedicated storage call center:
  - INSOMNIAC Live! (OpenTech Alliance). StorageMart merged PhoneSmart into it and PhoneSmart left the third-party business. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/suppliers-products/opentech-alliance-and-storagemart-to-merge-self-storage-call-centers
  - CSM's C3.
  - White Label Storage.
  - AI voice vendors (Swivl, Storiq).
  - CallPotential (call tracking, lead manager, collections dialer). Its case studies claim conversion gains from integrated follow-up, e.g., StorQuest +66%. https://www.casestudies.com/company/callpotential/case-study/storquest-self-storage-increases-call-conversion-rate-by-more-than-66-by-integrating-calls-lead-capture-and-follow-up-into-their-management-software-with-callpotential **[vendor claims]**
- I found no evidence of storage vendors named "StorageAnswers" or "Ledgemark." Do not cite them.
- For the course: if Greens uses rollover, staff need to know how to pick up a call-center reservation and follow up on it.

### 3.6 Recording and self-review
- Review five of your own recorded calls each week, track outcomes per 10 calls or walk-ins, and run 5 to 10 minute microlearning sessions weekly (DeFazio 2026).
- **[LEGAL - confirm with counsel]** California is an all-party-consent state for recording calls (Penal Code 632), so recorded lines need a "this call may be recorded" notice. Texas is one-party consent.

### 3.7 What mystery shoppers grade
- **Carol Mixon** (SkilCheck Services), Oct 8, 2020. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/staffing/how-to-fine-tune-your-self-storage-staff-and-performance-through-mystery-shopping
  - **Objective items:** stood up to greet; asked qualifying questions; named several features and tied them to benefits; gave office and access hours; invited the shopper to visit and reserve.
  - **Subjective items:** took control of the presentation; tried to sell features and benefits; professional; created urgency to visit; organized and efficient; built rapport.
  - **Thresholds:** at least 70% on a phone shop; close at least 90% of walk-ins who come in to rent; 90 or above earns a certificate.
  - **Standards:** mention a feature and benefit on every call (free truck, specials, door alarms); ask for the sale and invite a visit on every call; offer packing and moving supplies at every rental.
- **Other items** from the IntelliShop study and DeFazio: used the property name at the close; offered to send information; asked for name, phone and email; offered a reservation; set an appointment time; quoted the move-in special accurately, including when it ends; recommended a size after discovery; mentioned the protection plan; thanked the caller sincerely.
- **Vendors offering storage shops:** Shoppers Critique (https://shopperscritique.com/industries-served/storage-mystery-shopping/), SkilCheck, and KnowYourMarket (UK SSA approved).
- **Shopping competitors:** https://www.insideselfstorage.com/staffing/using-secret-shopping-to-learn-about-your-self-storage-business-and-its-competitors

## 4. Size recommendation and upselling without pressure

### 4.1 Size guide (industry consensus; room counts vary by brand)
Sources: https://www.sparefoot.com/blog/how-to-pick-the-right-size-storage-unit (Jan 2026), https://www.cubesmart.com/size-guide/ , https://www.uhaul.com/Tips/Storage/What-Size-Storage-Unit-Do-I-Need-31025/

| Size | Sq ft | Compare to | Typical contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5x5 | 25 | Hall closet | Boxes, seasonal items, a small dresser |
| 5x10 | 50 | Walk-in closet | A studio's furniture, a mattress set, boxes |
| 10x10 | 100 | Half a one-car garage (the most-rented size) | A 1-bedroom apartment; about 20 to 30 medium boxes plus furniture |
| 10x15 | 150 | Small one-car garage | A 2-bedroom |
| 10x20 | 200 | One-car garage | A 3-bedroom house, or a car |
| 10x30 | 300 | Large garage | A 4 to 5 bedroom house |
| Parking | Varies | - | Measure the RV or boat's overall length including tongue and trailer, plus height for covered or enclosed spots |

Most units have about 8-foot ceilings, so stacking counts. Start from the largest item and make sure it fits.

### 4.2 Technique
- Ask "What are you storing?", never "What size do you need?" (Ballard; DeFazio).
- Follow up with: "From what size home?", "How many rooms?", "Any big pieces such as a sectional, piano or king bed?", "Will you need to get to things often?" (frequent access means room for a walkway and a ground floor), "Anything that hates heat?"
- Use visual aids: the website size guide, a sized-box display in the office, or showing an empty unit and placing items verbally. "Your couch goes along this wall standing on end" (DeFazio).
- When unsure, go up a size. Customers who doubt it will fit go and shop one more place (DeFazio). If the wanted size is not available, consider the next size up at the smaller unit's price. https://www.radiusplus.com/post/closing-walk-in-sales-at-your-self-storage-facility
- On a tour, show the smaller size first and work up (ISS tour tips, older).

### 4.3 Upselling that helps
DeFazio's test: "Would the customer be upset later to learn you hadn't offered something that could have helped?"
- **Climate control:** ask about wood furniture, electronics, documents, photos, instruments, mattresses and artwork. Inland CA and TX summer heat makes this a real need, not a pitch.
- **Protection plan or tenant insurance:** lead with real scenarios such as a leak from a neighboring unit or a fire nearby. "Stories close; disclosures don't" (DeFazio). Facilities rarely cover tenant losses, and most leases say so. https://10federalstorage.com/blog/what-to-do-if-your-storage-unit-is-broken-into
  - Industry guidance is to have tenants initial or sign when they decline coverage, so the choice is on record (MSM, Nov 2018).
  - **Attach-rate benchmarks:** optional programs reach 5 to 30%; lease-compliant (proof of coverage required) programs 55 to 80% at move-in. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-profit-centers/making-money-with-tenant-insurance-and-property-protection **[DATED]** . Deans and Homer: operators without auto-enroll should average at least 65%. https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/msm-exclusives/self-storage-protection-chris-betteridge . Storelocal reported 70% under auto-enrollment.
  - **[LEGAL - confirm with counsel]** Tenant insurance is an insurance product, and selling it can require a license (California has a specific self-service storage limited license). A protection plan is a contractual waiver. Staff should describe the product with the approved wording only.
- **Locks:** recommend a disc or cylinder lock built for storage, and show why it is hard to cut. https://www.radiusplus.com/post/closing-walk-in-sales-at-your-self-storage-facility
- **Boxes and supplies:** offer them at every rental (Mixon standard). Bundle a starter kit and offer to carry it to the car (DeFazio).
- **Autopay:** "This way you never have to think about it, and you'll never get hit with a late fee" (DeFazio). One operator waives the admin fee for autopay sign-up (Williams 2013).
- **Larger unit:** suggest it when goods will likely outgrow the smaller one or the stay is long.
- **What to avoid:** stacking add-ons before the customer has agreed to rent, implying insurance is required when it is not, and pushing climate control for items that do not need it.

## 5. Walk-ins, tours, move-in

### 5.1 Walk-ins
- Walk-ins are the easiest customers to close. Mixon's standard is to close 90% of walk-ins who come in to rent.
- Stand up and greet within seconds. Ask whether they have called before, since that decides which script you use (ISS script article).
- **Common failures from a walk-in shopping report** (Eric Mees, Jun 2020; https://www.insideselfstorage.com/retail-sales/sales-fail-how-to-avoid-pushing-your-self-storage-walk-ins-out-the-door):
  - no urgency;
  - assuming the customer knows storage;
  - no stated difference from competitors;
  - no written quote;
  - no contact capture.
- **Fixes from the same report:**
  - Hand-write a quote sheet for the units shown, with the property's contact details.
  - Capture contact details "to lock in the rate."
  - Offer to email the move-in incentive.
  - Ask whether they want a heads-up if their size runs low.
  - Prorate the first month for a move-in before month-end.

### 5.2 The tour
Sources: DeFazio 2026; https://www.insideselfstorage.com/retail-sales/building-relationships-with-prospective-self-storage-customers ; Jordheim practicum topics (body language, guiding the demo).
1. **Before:** pick the route and the unit; make sure it is clean, swept and lit. Bring the cart if it is close.
2. **At the gate:** have the prospect practice the keypad code. Mention cameras, gate logs, lighting and on-site presence. Point out the hours.
3. **On the way:** point out the carts, elevator, wash bay or dump station (RV), dumpster rules, and where to park the truck.
4. **At the unit:** repeat the unit number and size, show how the hasp closes and let them try it, and explain disc locks versus padlocks.
   - Step into the unit first so they follow you in.
   - Describe where their things will go.
5. **Safety:** never enter a unit with a stranger. Let the prospect step in while you stay at the door, or use the unit-door demonstration. Self-Storage Talk operators list "never enter a unit with a tenant or prospect" as rule one. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/site-maintenance-safety/watch-your-back-self-storage-operators-share-their-top-tips-for-protecting-personal-safety-when-on-site
   - There is a tension here: sales sources (older) suggest walking into the unit so the customer follows. The course should resolve it as: stay in the doorway, never enter a closed space alone with someone.
6. **Close at the unit:** "This unit will work great for you. Let's go back to the office and take care of the paperwork." https://www.radiusplus.com/post/closing-walk-in-sales-at-your-self-storage-facility . Ask directly ("Would you like to rent this one today?", Jordheim). Garrett Byrd claims a customer who visits will rent "nine times out of 10" if the presentation is handled properly.

### 5.3 Completing the lease and move-in orientation
Topics compiled from the MSM top-10 complaints (Nov 2018), Williams (May 2022) and Aycha Williams (Jan 2013).
- **Verify identity:** photo ID; capture name, phone, email, address and an alternate contact. Cubby's flow should drive this.
- **Explain the lease terms that cause most complaints**, and have the tenant initial them:
  - due date, grace period, late-fee date and amount (e.g., "due on the 1st, late fee on the 6th");
  - that rates can change, and the notice period;
  - the lien process, in plain words;
  - prohibited items, and no living or working in the unit;
  - access hours and the security purpose of the gate;
  - "whoever has your gate code and key can come and go" (see 8.5);
  - dumpster and trash rules ("take out what you bring in");
  - move-out notice requirements;
  - insurance or protection: accepted, or declined and signed.
- **Show** how to pay online and set up autopay. Program the gate code. Make sure they have a lock.
- **After move-in:**
  - a thank-you (handwritten or text);
  - a call a few days later: "How did the move go? Everything working with your code?";
  - ask for a review once they are settled (section 6.3);
  - mention the referral program (Jordheim).

### 5.4 Online / contactless renters (no human contact)
- Online rental and contactless move-in through the management software have been standard since 2020. https://www.sparefoot.com/self-storage/news/9394-storable-announces-fully-integrated-contactless-move-in-capabilities/ . I found no verified 2024 or 2025 U.S. share of online rentals; Gitnux's "35% of new rentals" for 2023 is **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **What staff still owe the online renter:**
  - Confirm their gate code works.
  - Check that the unit is clean and unlocked or has the right lock.
  - Send a welcome message with hours, rules and how to reach the office.
  - Make a short welcome call within a day of move-in. That call is the orientation they skipped: due date, autopay, protection, no living in units.
- **Online reservation follow-up:** call within 5 to 15 minutes during open hours (Byrd), confirm the size fits using the discovery questions, confirm the move-in date, and call the day before to confirm arrival.

## 6. Digital channels and existing tenants

### 6.1 Email, text and chat etiquette (compiled practice)
- Same response targets as the phone (section 2.4). Reply to web and chat inquiries as fast as you would someone at the counter (DeFazio).
- **Text:** identify yourself and the property in the first message. Keep texts short. Use texts only with consent and only during reasonable hours.
  - **[LEGAL - confirm with counsel]** Marketing texts need prior express written consent under the TCPA. Honor STOP right away.
- **Email:** a clear subject ("Your 10x10 reservation at Greens Storage [Location], 11/15/2026"), name the unit, price, special and end date, and a direct number. Personalize with a detail from the conversation (DeFazio).
- **Chat:** answer, ask one discovery question, and move to a reservation or a phone call. Do not paste walls of policy text.
- **Never** put full balance or lien details in an email or text unless policy says so (privacy; section 6.4).

### 6.2 Rate-increase conversations
Sources:
- MSM top 10 complaints, Nov 2018 (complaint #1 is rent increases and expiring specials). https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/msm-exclusives/top-10-customer-complaints
- ISS "Proceed With Care." https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/proceed-with-care-how-to-raise-your-self-storage-rates-and-collect-past-due-rent-in-a-time-of-inflation
- Tocarro Williams, 2022.

**Practices:**
- Disclose at move-in that the special is introductory and rates are reviewed.
- Know the reasons behind an increase (taxes, insurance, improvements, market). Get them from management so you can answer with confidence.
- Before an increase goes out, flag tenants who recently had a leak, break-in or hardship.
- If true, tell the tenant their rate is still below the current street rate. One operator says they "never raise existing tenant rates to or beyond current street rates."
- Know the save options and your authority: downsize, a partial concession for long-term good payers, autopay. Concession authority is set by management.

**Example wording** (Williams 2022):
> "I understand this increase is hard to hear. Here's what's driving it, and I'm glad to walk through your options."

**[LEGAL - confirm with counsel]** Notice periods come from the lease and state law. I did not confirm a statutory notice period for storage rate changes in either CA or TX. Many operators give 30 days or more. Counsel should state Greens' rule.

### 6.3 Reviews
- **Asking:** ask every customer the same neutral way, ideally after a good move-in or a solved problem. Send the direct Google link by text or email.
  - Do not "gate" (asking only happy customers).
  - Do not offer an incentive tied to a positive review.
  - The FTC rule (final August 2024) bans buying sentiment-conditioned reviews and suppressing negative ones; neutral incentives are allowed but risky. https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews , https://bhfs.com/insights/alerts-articles/2024/ftc-finalizes-rule-to-combat-deceptive-online-reviews
  - Google's own policy reportedly bars selectively soliciting positive reviews (verify on Google's help pages). Recommend no incentives at all.
  - Staff must never write reviews for the property or about competitors.
- **Responding:**
  - Reply to every review within 48 hours (DeFazio); some say the same day.
  - Thank positive reviewers by name and mention a specific detail.
  - On negative reviews, stay calm and own real mistakes. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-management-operation/bad-review-how-to-react-when-customers-talk-trash-about-your-self-storage-business-online
  - Say who you are and your role. https://support.sparefoot.com/sparefoot-marketplace/sparefoot-marketplace-product-guides/reviews/best-practices-reply-to-your-online-reviews
  - Keep it short (about 70 words) and take it offline. https://www.shiftflow.app/playbook/how-to-respond-to-a-bad-google-review
  - Never disclose account details, balances, lien status or the person's unit in a public reply. That is a privacy problem and may break collections law.
  - Fix the process behind the review. https://kinnovis.com/how-self-storage-operators-should-handle-negative-reviews/
- **Template, negative (service):**
  > "Hi [Name], I'm [Your name], the manager at Greens Storage [Location]. I'm sorry your visit on [day] didn't go the way it should have. That's not the experience we want for anyone. I'd like to make it right. Please call me directly at [number] or reply here, and I'll take care of it personally."
- **Template, negative (rate increase or fee):**
  > "Hi [Name], thank you for the feedback, and I understand how frustrating a rate change is. I'd like to go over your options with you, including other unit sizes. Please call me at [number] at your convenience."
- **Template, negative (lien or auction):** no account details at all:
  > "Hi [Name], I'm sorry for how difficult this situation has been. I'm not able to discuss account details here, but I'd welcome a call at [number] to talk it through."
- **Template, positive:**
  > "Thank you, [Name]! We're so glad the move went smoothly and that [detail, e.g., the cart and the drive-up access] helped. We're here whenever you need us. - [Your name], Greens Storage [Location]"

### 6.4 Payments, collections and the law

**Process and tone**
- **Aycha Williams, "9 Steps,"** Jan 18, 2013 **[DATED]**. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/9-steps-to-help-self-storage-managers-make-effective-collections-calls
  1. Explain fees at move-in.
  2. Be consistent with every tenant.
  3. Collect emails for reminders.
  4. Call early and often: twice before and twice after the late date, plus a final call before month-end.
  5. Be professional: speak slowly, lower pitch, smile, offer solutions such as splitting across two cards.
  6. "Blame the system." The course should drop this one: telling customers a system forbids something it does not is a misrepresentation, and under CA and TX law below it is a legal risk, not just an ethical one.
  7. Be organized: schedule the follow-up immediately.
  8. Know the psychological factors: positive tone, expect emotion, don't take it personally.
  9. Get a firm commitment, with a date.
- **StoragePug, Mar 2023:** go in with a plan (auction date, last payment date, lease terms), speak slowly, don't multitask. https://storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-collections-calls-guide . Sample responses:
  - "I sent the check": "Can I get the check number and date?"
  - "I've been busy": "I can take a payment right now," or "We can set up a payment plan."
  - "I never got the bill": "I'll send you a copy of the bill and your signed lease right now."
  - Empathy line: "I understand, that sounds very difficult."
- **Other ISS guidance:**
  - Frame early calls as help: "I'm calling to help you avoid the late fee on the 6th."
  - Empathy does not mean waiving everything; know the waiver policy.
  - Talk only to the tenant.
  - Never leave account-specific messages with anyone else or on an answering machine.
  - https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/i-hate-making-collections-calls-self-storage-managers-learn-to-be-good-at-rent-collection-even-when-they-don-t-like-it , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/customer-care-not-just-collections-strategies-for-self-storage-delinquency-prevention

**Legal frame [LEGAL - confirm with counsel]** (important for Greens)
- The federal FDCPA mostly covers third-party collectors, not a facility collecting its own rent. **But:**
  - **California's Rosenthal Act** (Civil Code 1788 et seq.) covers a creditor collecting its own consumer debts. Through Civ. Code 1788.17 it pulls in most FDCPA conduct rules. It bans false, deceptive or misleading representations and harassment. SB 1286 extended it to some small-business debts from July 2025. https://www.privacyrights.org/resources/rosenthal-fair-debt-collection-practices-act-california , https://www.swlaw.com/publication/californias-recent-expansion-of-the-rosenthal-fair-debt-collection-practices-act-to-commercial-debts/
  - **Texas Finance Code ch. 392** defines "debt collector" broadly enough to include creditors collecting their own consumer debts. 392.301 to 392.306 cover threats, harassment, unfair practices and misrepresentation (e.g., 392.302 harassment, 392.304 misleading representations). https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/docs/FI/htm/FI.392.htm , https://insidearm.com/news/00047102-are-early-out-services-banned-texas-exami
  - Whether a storage rent balance is a "consumer debt" under each law, and how the lien statute interacts, is for counsel. The course should simply train FDCPA-style conduct everywhere.
- **Conduct rules to teach as Greens policy:**
  - Call only between 8 AM and 9 PM in the tenant's local time, not at work if they say they can't take calls there, and not repeatedly to annoy. Reg F presumes more than 7 calls in 7 days is harassment for third-party collectors; use it as a ceiling.
  - No threats, profanity or shaming. Never threaten action Greens does not intend or is not allowed to take, e.g., arrest or "selling tomorrow" when the legal sale date is weeks away.
  - No false statements about amounts, fees or the law. No fake urgency.
  - Speak only to the tenant (or a verified authorized representative or attorney). With a third party, give only your name and a callback number, never the reason.
  - Voicemail: name, company and callback number only; do not mention the debt.
  - Stop calling at work, or stop altogether, if the tenant asks in writing, and escalate.
  - Log every contact in Cubby.
  - Lien-sale steps and notices follow the statute and Cubby's lien workflow exactly. Staff never "pull" a unit or cut a lock outside the process.
- **Lien law pointers [LEGAL - confirm with counsel]:**
  - California: Self-Service Storage Facility Act, B&P 21700 to 21716. The preliminary lien notice may go out after 14 consecutive days unpaid (21703 and 21704); the lien attaches 14 days after the termination date in that notice (21705); the sale must be advertised; the tenant can redeem up to the sale. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2011/bpc/division-8/21700-21716/21703/ (older code text; check current leginfo).
  - Texas: Property Code ch. 59 (not researched in this pass).
  - Lien conversations should be empathetic and factual: the amount needed to stop the process, the deadline, and the ways to pay. Fee complaints at auction are a top-10 complaint; explain the process accurately and apply it equally (MSM 2018).

### 6.5 Move-outs and save attempts
- Call within 24 hours of a move-out notice, live and not automated, to learn the reason and offer a save (ISS tenant retention article, above).
- Save options: downsize to a smaller unit at a preferred rate; waive late fees with an autopay conversion; fix the service problem behind the move. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/tenant-insurance-protection/5-ways-to-keep-tenants-from-leaving-your-self-storage-facility
- Reality check: one operator's October 2025 report showed "no longer needed" as by far the top move-out reason, with one dissatisfaction move-out. https://coda.io/@ryan-dayhoff/storage-depot-meeting-notes/ssd-october-2025-yoy-14 . Most move-outs are happy endings. Treat them that way: thank them, ask for a review and a referral, and confirm the unit is broom-clean and the lock removed. https://www.storagepug.com/blog/happy-tenant-move-out
- Monthly churn of 5% or less is "generally considered good" (vendor claim, unsourced).
- **Script:**
  > "Thanks for letting us know. Can I ask what's changing? ... If it helps, we have a 5x10 for $X that would hold what you're keeping, or if it's the rate, let me see what I can do."
  If they are leaving anyway: "We've loved having you. Is there anything we could have done better?"

## 8. Difficult situations

### 8.1 Angry customer de-escalation
Steps from Tocarro Williams, May 2022 (https://www.insideselfstorage.com/customer-service-experience/how-to-keep-the-peace-with-your-self-storage-customers-even-when-they-re-being-difficult), combined with LAST/HEARD:
1. **Stay calm and lower your voice.** Let them finish (Hear).
2. **Empathize and paraphrase:** "So you came to move your things and your code didn't work, after a long drive. I'd be upset too."
3. **Apologize** for the experience, without admitting legal fault on damage claims (MSM 2018).
4. **Solve** within your authority. Explain the policy using the signed agreement: "Your signed agreement covers this. Let's look at that section together."
5. **Set limits if needed:** "I'm happy to keep talking, but I can't continue if we move to yelling or insults."
6. **Escalate with a time:** "I'm going to share this with my supervisor and get back to you within 24 hours." Give the supervisor's name and number if they ask (MSM 2018).
7. **Thank them and follow up.** Then Diagnose: log it and fix the cause.
8. **Threats, harassment or any safety concern:** call law enforcement. Do not argue.

### 8.2 Break-in or theft report
Compiled from https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-management-operation/first-thought-best-thought-self-storage-operators-share-their-immediate-response-to-crisis-situations and https://10federalstorage.com/blog/what-to-do-if-your-storage-unit-is-broken-into
- Take it seriously and show empathy. The tenant may have lost irreplaceable items.
- Secure the unit with a facility lock. Ask the tenant to file a police report and get the report number. Write an internal incident report. Notify your supervisor.
- Preserve camera footage and gate logs. Do not promise what they show, and do not share footage directly with the tenant; follow police and policy.
- Check neighboring units for cut locks and notify those tenants.
- Explain the claim path for their protection plan or insurance: the claim form, the police report, and the tenant's own list and photos of items.
- Do not admit facility liability or speculate. Facilities generally don't cover losses, and leases say so; say it gently. Do not blame the tenant (e.g., "you should have bought a better lock").
- Keep the tenant updated. A news story shows the failure: tenants complained that nobody followed up. https://wdrb.com/news/security-concerns-grow-at-louisville-storage-facilities-as-more-break-in-victims-come-forward/article_84bbabb1-6cc2-463f-ade0-455f1823bcb9.html
- Review responses: no details of the incident or investigation.

### 8.3 Damaged goods (leak, pests, heat)
- Show empathy without admitting liability. Explain the lease and the protection or insurance path.
- For leaks: move the tenant to another unit, with a concession if authorized.
- For pests: explain the treatment schedule and commit to handling it.
- Document with photos and report to maintenance. (MSM 2018)

### 8.4 Lockouts and lost keys or codes
- Verify identity with photo ID against the account before resetting a code. https://www.shurgard.com/en-nl/self-storage/why-shurgard/faqs/security
- A tenant who lost their key: cut the lock only for the verified tenant, with policy steps and any fee, and record it.
- Overlocked for nonpayment: explain the balance needed to remove the overlock (section 6.4).
- Gate failures are often user error. Walk them through it kindly, and make sure people already inside can exit after hours (MSM 2018).

### 8.5 Someone asking for access to another person's unit
- The default answer is no. Only the tenant, or someone who has the tenant's code and key, gets in. The operator does not act as gatekeeper or cut locks for anyone but the verified tenant.
- Greenberger & Brewer (2014): avoid "authorized access" lists. A divorcing tenant forgot to remove her husband; he demanded the lock be cut and called police. Standard line: "Whoever has your gate code and key will be able to come and go at will until you change your code or lock." https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal-issues/why-self-storage-operators-should-avoid-the-danger-of-authorized-access-and-how-to-do-it
- Non-tenants need a court order. Express sympathy, explain, and refer them to civil court (MSM 2018). Don't confirm or deny that the person even rents there. That is a privacy point and my recommendation.
- **Police:** be cooperative, but follow Greens policy on warrants and subpoenas and escalate to management. I did not research specific guidance.

### 8.6 A family calling after a tenant died
- Lead with condolence. Do not discuss the account or give access until you have documentation:
  - the death certificate, plus letters testamentary or of administration (executor);
  - or a small-estate affidavit (CA Probate Code 13100; TX has its own small estate affidavit).
  (MSM 2018; Lewis 2020.) **[LEGAL - confirm with counsel]**
- Offer to pause late fees while the family gathers paperwork, if policy allows. Explain the steps clearly in writing.
- **Script:**
  > "I'm so sorry for your loss. I want to help you get his things. To protect his belongings, we need [documents]. Once we have those, I'll walk you through everything."

### 8.7 Domestic disputes
- Never take sides. Follow the lease and the code/key rule (8.5). Get consent from both joint tenants for changes. Keep contact information current for both (Lewis 2020).
- If someone is in danger, or a protective order is presented, call police and escalate. Do not disclose one party's unit or visits to the other.

### 8.8 Someone living in a unit
- It is a safety, health and legal problem: no ventilation, water or bathroom, plus code violations.
- **Never approach alone.** Call police or get backup. Be firm but don't embarrass the person, and treat them with dignity.
- Document everything: footage, gate logs, keypad activity. Follow the lease default and state process. https://storagepug.com/blog/what-you-should-do-if-a-tenant-is-living-in-a-unit , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/tenant-insurance-protection/this-is-not-a-home-preventing-self-storage-tenants-from-living-on-site
- Warning signs: cars parked overnight, people walking around after hours, a unit door partly open from inside, extension cords, the same gate code used late and early. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-talk-featured-thread-why-are-you-still-here-handling-tenants-who-hang-around-after-hours
- Prevention: state the rule at move-in; enforce loitering policies.
- Optional and compassionate: keep a local services card (shelters, 211) to offer.

### 8.9 Safety when alone on site
Sources: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/site-maintenance-safety/all-alone-using-your-spidey-senses-to-stay-safe-in-self-storage , the ISS Watch Your Back roundup (Dec 31, 2025), and https://www.insideselfstorage.com/site-maintenance-safety/workplace-safety-advice-for-self-storage-facility-managers
- Never enter a unit with a tenant or prospect.
- Never say you are alone. ("My manager's in back" or "My coworker will be right out.")
- Keep the counter between you and visitors. Know where the panic button and exits are. Keep little cash.
- Check that cameras record, alarms work and gate emergency codes function.
- Have a written safety plan and run drills. Have a check-in routine with a supervisor (phone or text at set times).
- Trust your instincts. You may end a tour or decline to walk the property and offer a video tour instead.
- In a robbery, comply and do not resist. Note descriptions afterward, then call 911.
- A Seattle manager was killed on duty, which shows this is a real risk. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-management-operation/self-storage-talk-members-discuss-slain-seattle-manager-facility-safety

## 10. Role-play scenarios (prompts and what "great" looks like)

1. **The price-only caller.**
   - *Prompt:* "How much is your 10x10? That's all I need."
   - *Great:* gives a range, asks what they're storing and when they're moving, finds out it is a 3-bedroom, and recommends a 10x20 with a reason. Quotes the special and when it ends, asks for a free reservation, and captures name, phone and email.
2. **The hours question.**
   - *Prompt:* "How late are you open today?"
   - *Great:* answers, then asks "Are you looking to rent, or can I help with your account?" and moves into discovery.
3. **First-time renter after a divorce.**
   - *Prompt:* the caller sounds overwhelmed and needs to move out this weekend with half a 2-bedroom.
   - *Great:* acknowledges the stress briefly without prying, uses simple language, recommends a 10x10, explains how access works, offers a truck or carts and a supply bundle, and closes on a Saturday move-in time. Does not make it about price.
4. **Daughter clearing her late father's home.**
   - *Prompt:* she needs storage for an estate. Separately, she asks for access to her father's existing unit with you.
   - *Great:* rents her a new unit. For the father's unit: condolence first, explains the documents needed, offers a fee pause if policy allows, gives written next steps, and does not open the unit without them.
5. **Competitor is $20 cheaper.**
   - *Prompt:* "Storage King has a 10x10 for $89."
   - *Great:* stays positive and never criticizes the competitor. Compares total cost (admin fee, required insurance, special length, lock) and the differences (drive-up, cameras, clean, staff on site). Matches only within authority and for the expected stay (Ballard), then asks for the rental.
6. **"I'll think about it."**
   - *Prompt:* a walk-in toured, liked the unit, and starts to leave.
   - *Great:* asks what's holding them back, addresses timing, writes a quote sheet, offers a free hold, captures contact details, and sets a specific follow-up time. Then follows up as promised.
7. **Web reservation, no answer.**
   - *Prompt:* a reservation came in 4 minutes ago, for move-in tomorrow.
   - *Great:* calls within 5 minutes, leaves a short voicemail with name, property and callback time, sends a text and email confirming the unit, size and price, calls again later, and confirms tomorrow's arrival.
8. **Contactless renter can't get through the gate.**
   - *Prompt:* the call comes at 7:40 PM; the office closes at 6, and they rented online.
   - *Great:* (if on duty or the on-call phone) verifies identity, walks them through the keypad, checks the code in Cubby, makes sure they can exit, and the next day calls with the move-in orientation they missed.
9. **Rate-increase anger.**
   - *Prompt:* "You raised me 12% again! I've been here four years."
   - *Great:* listens, thanks them for their loyalty, explains the drivers honestly, notes they are still below street rate (if true), and offers options (downsize, autopay, a concession within authority). Does not get defensive and does not promise what it can't deliver. Escalates with a time if asked.
10. **Collections call, hardship.**
    - *Prompt:* the tenant is 20 days late and says they lost their job.
    - *Great:* verifies identity before naming the balance. Empathy ("That sounds really hard"). States the amount and the key date factually. Offers a partial payment or plan per policy and splits cards if that helps. Gets a commitment date and logs it. No threats, no misleading urgency, no "the system won't let me" fiction.
11. **Wrong person answers a collections call.**
    - *Prompt:* the tenant's roommate picks up and asks what it's about.
    - *Great:* gives only first name, company and callback number, without the reason or the balance. Voicemail rules apply.
12. **Ex-spouse wants in.**
    - *Prompt:* "My ex rented that unit but half the stuff is mine. Cut the lock."
    - *Great:* calm sympathy. Does not confirm whether the ex is even a tenant. Explains that only the tenant controls access and that a court order is the path. Refers them to civil court, and calls police if threatened. Documents and alerts the manager.
13. **Break-in discovered by tenant.**
    - *Prompt:* "My lock is cut and my tools are gone!"
    - *Great:* empathy first. Secures the unit with a facility lock. Asks them to call police and get the report number. Writes the incident report, notifies the supervisor, preserves footage, checks nearby units, and explains the protection plan claim path. No liability admissions and no blame. Calls back with updates.
14. **Damage from a roof leak.**
    - *Prompt:* the tenant's boxes are wet and they want Greens to pay.
    - *Great:* apologizes for the experience, photographs it, offers a dry unit and a concession within authority, and explains the protection plan claim. Does not admit liability and escalates the claim per policy.
15. **Suspected live-in.**
    - *Prompt:* the same car has been parked overnight three nights, and there's a light in a unit at 6 AM.
    - *Great:* does not approach alone. Documents gate logs and footage, informs the supervisor, and plans a police-assisted contact. Firm, respectful message about the policy, with a resources card.
16. **Prospect wants to step inside the unit with you, alone on site.**
    - *Prompt:* you are alone; the prospect insists you come into the far unit to measure.
    - *Great:* stays in the doorway, hands them the tape, keeps the phone handy, and never says they are alone.
17. **Spanish-speaking walk-in.**
    - *Prompt:* the customer has limited English and wants a unit for a family move.
    - *Great:* greets warmly, uses a Spanish-speaking teammate or approved translation help, uses the Spanish materials, and covers the key lease points with understanding confirmed. Never relies on a child for lease terms.
18. **One-star review: "They sold my stuff!"**
    - *Prompt:* a public review after a lien sale.
    - *Great:* replies within 48 hours with a calm, short, empathetic message and no account or lien details, takes it offline, and tells management.
19. **Move-out notice.**
    - *Prompt:* the tenant emails that they are leaving at month-end.
    - *Great:* a live call within 24 hours. Asks why. If a save fits (a smaller unit, an autopay fee waiver), offers it. If not, thanks them, confirms move-out steps (broom-clean, lock removed, date), and asks for a review and a referral.
20. **RV owner shopping covered parking.**
    - *Prompt:* "Do you have a spot for a 34-foot fifth wheel?"
    - *Great:* asks overall length, height and access frequency. Confirms the spot fits, highlights the security, wash and dump station, and the access hours. Covers vehicle protection or insurance requirements, quotes, and reserves. Asks about seasonal use.
