## 2. Industry basics

### 2.1 History
- Modern self-storage is generally traced to **Odessa, Texas, 1964**: Russ Williams and stepson Bob Munn opened "A-1 U-Store-It U-Lock-It U-Carry-the-Key," a small cinder-block-and-steel building marketed to oil-field companies. One history claims an earlier facility of the same name in Fort Lauderdale, Florida (1958). **[VERIFY - competing claims; safest phrasing is "the industry grew up in Texas in the 1960s."]** Sources: https://www.storagefront.com/blogs/what-you-re-in-store-for-the-surprisingly-interesting-history-of-self-storage , https://www.sparefoot.com/self-storage/news/620-original-us-storage-facility-gets-makeover/
- Texas spread followed: a 300-unit facility in Midland (1966); "Stor-More" in Brownsville (1967). Same sources.
- **Public Storage** was founded **08/14/1972 in El Cajon, California** (San Diego County - near Greens' Escondido / Valley Center sites, a nice local hook) by B. Wayne Hughes and Kenneth Volk Jr., with about $50,000; Hughes got the idea from mini-storage success around Dallas and Houston. It became a REIT in 1995, bought Shurgard in 2006 for $5.5 billion, and had 3,073 owned facilities plus 307 managed for others at 12/31/2024. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Storage . Wikipedia also says Public Storage announced an HQ move to **Frisco, Texas** (Feb 2026) and an acquisition of **National Storage Affiliates** (July 2026). **[VERIFY both 2026 items before using.]**
- Facility count history: ~6,600 U.S. facilities in 1985 to 50,000+ today; roughly 3,000 new facilities a year were added 2000-2005 (California Senate Judiciary analysis of AB 498, July 2025, citing NYT / Governing). Source: https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-07/ab-498-michelle-rodriguez-sjud-analysis_0.pdf
- The reality-TV effect ("Storage Wars") made lien auctions famous; the same Senate analysis cites an estimate of ~155,000 units auctioned a year. **[VERIFY - old estimate.]**

### 2.2 Size of the industry (numbers conflict - present as ranges with year)
- **Facilities / space:** SSA-attributed figures: 50,000+ U.S. facilities and 2+ billion rentable square feet (https://storeganise.com/blog/self-storage-trends). A 2023-era figure: ~51,206 facilities, 2.04 billion sq ft (https://mozzidigital.com/self-storage-business/self-storage-statistics-latest-self-storage-insight-in-2023/). - **California:** about 3,500 facilities with ~213.3 million sq ft (CA Senate Judiciary, 2025, citing selfstorage.com). Same PDF as above.
- **Revenue:** wide disagreement - $19.6B (2022, ConsumerAffairs: https://www.consumeraffairs.com/movers/self-storage-industry-statistics.html), ~$29B (2023-era), $44.3B (CA Senate analysis 2025 citing SpareFoot). **[VERIFY - recommend saying "tens of billions of dollars a year" and citing one dated source.]**
- **Household penetration (best source: SSA Self Storage Demand Study):** 12.6% of U.S. households in 2024 (SSA 2025 Demand Study), up from 11.1% (2022) and 10.6% (2020); roughly 16.7 million renter households. Rule of thumb for learners: **"about 1 in 8 U.S. households rents storage."**
- **Occupancy:** national ~92% at the 2021-2022 peak, ~87% by 09/2023 (ConsumerAffairs), ~85.5% (08/2026, TractIQ). Post-pandemic normalization plus heavy new supply = more competition, which is why conversion and retention matter.
- **Consolidation:** the four big self-storage REITs plus U-Haul control ~35.5% of the market; the top 100 operators ~52% (RentCafe: https://www.rentcafe.com/blog/self-storage/the-100-largest-self-storage-companies-in-the-u-s/). Self Storage Almanac (cited in an SEC filing) says the top 100 operators hold ~63% of rentable square feet but only ~34% of facilities - i.e., big operators own bigger buildings and most facilities are still independent. ### 2.3 Who runs storage: REITs vs independents vs third-party management
- **REITs** (Real Estate Investment Trusts): publicly traded owners. 2025 ISS / Modern Storage Media top-operator lists: Extra Space Storage, Public Storage, CubeSmart, U-Haul, National Storage Affiliates (https://www.insideselfstorage.com/iss-brand/inside-self-storage-releases-2025-top-operators-lists-in-multiple-formats , https://digital.modernstoragemedia.com/messenger/issue/november-2025ob/2025-top-operators/). Rankings differ by measure (RentCafe puts Public Storage first at 11.3% of national inventory, Extra Space 8.6%). Extra Space merged with Life Storage in 2023 **[VERIFY date - from general knowledge]**.
- **Independents:** single-site and small-portfolio owners (mom-and-pop) and private regional operators. Greens is a private regional operator.
- **Third-party management (3PM):** an owner hires a manager (often a REIT - Extra Space, CubeSmart and Public Storage all run large 3PM platforms) to operate the site under the manager's brand, systems, pricing and staff. CubeSmart has the second-largest 3PM portfolio (~53.2 million sq ft managed). The owner keeps the asset; the manager takes a fee (commonly a percent of revenue - **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**). For Greens: some sites are third-party managed, so employees may meet the manager's staff, systems and rules. Teach the concept: who owns the building, who employs the staff, whose rental agreement and lien process applies.
- **Software / tech platforms:** large platforms like Storable report data across 30,000+ facilities (avg length of stay 19.3 months, Q1 2026 - https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Global+Self+Storage+Reports+First+Quarter+2026+Results/26461253.html cited in search). Greens uses Cubby.

### 2.4 Why people rent - the "4 Ds" and beyond
- **The 4 Ds:** Death, Divorce, Downsizing, Dislocation (moves, job loss, disasters). Public Storage leadership has used "Disaster" as the fourth D. Some add "Distribution" and "Decluttering" to make 6 Ds (remote work, e-commerce). Sources: https://www.neighbor.com/storage-blog/4-ds-of-self-storage-death-divorce-dislocation-downsizing/ , https://www.storagecafe.com/blog/the-6-ds-of-self-storage-where-demand-is-strongest-across-the-us/ , SSA blog https://www.selfstorage.org/Blog/ArticleID/44/The-Four-D-39-s
- 2024 survey: top reason = lack of space at home; second, at 25%, = moving (StorageCafe, cited in the AB 498 analysis).
- Life events beyond the Ds: home renovation, military deployment, college summers, new baby, combining households, staging a home for sale, hobby gear, seasonal items, vehicles / RVs / boats (HOA restrictions are a big driver in Southern California and Texas suburbs - link to the RV course).
- Teaching point: many customers arrive in a stressful life moment (a death, a divorce, an eviction). Empathy is part of the job (light touch; the customer service course owns this).

### 2.5 Customer segments
- **Residential:** the majority. Moves, life events, overflow.
- **Business / commercial:** contractors, pharma and sales reps (samples), e-commerce sellers, retailers (seasonal inventory), records storage. They stay longest - 47.5% of business renters stay over two years vs ~24% of all renters (SSA 2023 Demand Study via Self Storage Almanac 2024: https://digital.modernstoragemedia.com/almanac/self-storage-almanac-2024db/section-9-site-information/). Business use of a unit is normally allowed for storage; running a business *out of* the unit (customers, employees, retail, manufacturing) is not (see rental agreement).
- **Students:** summer and study-abroad storage; short stays; parent often pays. **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**
- **Military:** deployments and PCS moves. Fairfield, CA sits next to Travis Air Force Base; Austin-area sites are within reach of Fort Cavazos (formerly Fort Hood) - make the SCRA lesson concrete. Federal protections apply (section 9).
- **Vehicle / RV / boat:** separate course.
- Behavior facts (SSA 2023 study via Almanac): 10x10 is the most popular size, then 5x10; 10x20 and 10x15 rising; 55.7% of rentals are exterior drive-up; ~25% of tenants rent multiple units; 69%+ travel under 20 minutes to their unit (storage is hyper-local); monthly is the most common visit frequency; 41% buy boxes / tape / supplies; automatic card payment is the most preferred payment method.

## 3. The product

### 3.1 Standard unit sizes and what fits (sizes are width x depth in feet; capacity is approximate)
Sources: https://www.uhaul.com/Tips/Storage/What-Size-Storage-Unit-Do-I-Need-31025/ , https://www.rentcafe.com/storage-units/size-guide/ , https://www.kostorage.com/blog/self-storage-unit-sizes-guide
| Size | Sq ft | Comparable to | Typical contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5x5 | 25 | Hall / small closet | Boxes, seasonal clothes and decor, small furniture, files, a dresser or small mattress set |
| 5x10 | 50 | Walk-in closet | Studio or small one-bedroom apartment: full mattress set, dresser, boxes, small items |
| 10x10 | 100 | Half a one-car garage / large bedroom | One-bedroom apartment or 1-2 rooms incl. furniture and small appliances; 20-30 medium boxes plus furniture. Most popular size. |
| 10x15 | 150 | ~2/3 of a one-car garage | Two-bedroom home; "about 4 rooms" (U-Haul) |
| 10x20 | 200 | One-car garage | Three-bedroom home incl. washer, dryer, refrigerator; or a car (if allowed) |
| 10x30 | 300 | Two-car garage / large | Four- to five-bedroom home, major appliances, heavy outdoor gear |
- Selling tips (light touch): start from the largest item, ask about the move (rooms, appliances), remember ceiling height (most units ~8-10 ft; cube = sq ft x height) **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**, and that a unit packed to the door leaves no aisle.
- Odd sizes (5x15, 10x25, lockers, 4x4, 5x8) and wine / document lockers exist at some sites.

### 3.2 Unit types
- **Drive-up (exterior):** roll-up door faces a drive aisle; load straight from the vehicle; usually single-story ground level; not climate-controlled; 55.7% of rentals nationally.
- **Interior:** inside a building, reached through a hallway, often via elevator for upper floors; often climate-controlled; more protected from dust and pests.
- **Climate-controlled:** temperature (commonly cited ~55-80 F) and sometimes humidity regulated **[VERIFY range per Greens' spec]**. Recommend for wood furniture, electronics, photos, documents, musical instruments, mattresses, leather, art, wine. Hot inland California (Temecula, Murrieta, Valley Center, Fairfield) and Texas summers make this a real selling point. Do not over-promise ("climate-controlled" is not "humidity-controlled" unless it is).
- **Ground floor vs upper floor:** ground floor and drive-up units command higher rents; upper floors are cheaper and rely on elevators and carts.
- **Parking:** uncovered, covered (canopy) and enclosed spaces for cars, RVs, boats, trailers. Covered in the RV / boat course.
- **Specialty:** wine storage, business centers, lockers, portable containers (CA 21701.1 addresses containers transported by the facility).

### 3.3 Access control
- **Gate:** keypad entry with a unique personal **gate code** per tenant (never share or reuse; never give a code to anyone not on the account). The gate logs who entered and when (the access log is evidence in disputes and investigations).
- **Access hours** vs **office hours:** gate hours (e.g., 6 AM - 9 PM) are usually longer than office hours; some sites offer 24-hour access at a premium. **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**
- **Delinquent lockout:** the management system can suspend a delinquent tenant's gate code. **California caution:** the statute ties denial of access to the lien attaching after the preliminary lien notice (B&P 21705(a)). Whether and when to lock out is a company policy decision tied to the rental agreement and counsel - staff follow the system and policy, never improvise.
- **Smart entry:** Bluetooth / app-based gate and unit locks (e.g., Nokē). Tenants open with a phone; the operator gets a per-unit audit trail and can lock out remotely. Source: https://nationallocksupply.com/blog/selfstorage-facility-hardware-complete-guide/

### 3.4 Locks
Sources: https://www.storagecafe.com/blog/?p=5203 , https://radiusplus.com/post/which-self-storage-lock-is-right-for-you , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/products/setting-security-level-your-self-storage-site-choosing-lock-system
- **The tenant supplies and owns the lock and keeps the only key.** This is the legal heart of self-storage: the tenant has exclusive control; the facility does not hold a key and does not know what is inside.
- **Disc lock:** round body shrouds most of the shackle; hard to cut with bolt cutters. Industry-standard recommendation and a common merchandise item.
- **Padlock:** exposed shackle - easier to cut or pry. Cheapest, weakest.
- **Cylinder lock:** built into the door latch (installed by the facility, drilled); tenant gets a unique keyed core; some systems also allow a padlock as a tamper seal. Often removed and re-keyed between tenants.
- **Overlock:** the facility's own lock (often a brightly colored, typically red, heavy padlock or a hasp-mounted device) placed alongside the tenant's lock to block access - used on delinquent units (where the law and policy allow), on vacant units, or when a unit is found unlocked. Every overlock must be recorded in the management system; never overlock a paid-up unit by mistake.
- **Cutting a lock:** only under a documented authorized process - a lien step, a court order / warrant, a tenant who lost a key and proves identity on the account (signed authorization, ID, often a fee), or an emergency - with witnesses, photos, an inventory where required, and a new facility lock after. **[GENERAL PRACTICE - Greens to define its procedure.]**

## 4. Revenue mechanics

### 4.1 Rates
- **Street rate:** the current advertised / move-in price for a unit type (what a new customer pays today). Changes often (dynamic pricing by size, occupancy and competition).
- **In-place rate:** what an existing tenant actually pays now. In mature facilities in-place rates are often well above street rates: Public Storage's average in-place rate was 74% above its average move-in rate in Q4 2024. A 20-40% street-to-in-place spread is described as typical for operators who run rate increases. Sources: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/iss-blog-your-street-rates-and-ecri-must-work-hand-in-hand-my-playbook-for-self-storage-pricing , https://pulserevops.com/industry-kpis/ik0469
- **Web rate / online rate:** many operators price lower online than at the counter. **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**
- **ECRI (Existing Customer Rate Increase):** periodic increases to sitting tenants on month-to-month agreements. Timing varies: some models start 3-5 months after move-in and repeat every 6-9 months; others ~9-12 months; U-Haul cites a 12-month rate lock and 30 days' notice. Typical size cited 4-15% **[VERIFY - vendor blog]**. Sources: https://digital.modernstoragemedia.com/?p=237009 , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/the-ecri-evolution-modern-self-storage-rate-management-and-its-impact-on-asset-revenue-value-and-underwriting , https://www.uhaul.com/Tips/Storage/Do-Storage-Units-Raise-Your-Rent-Every-Year-37032
  - Why it works: moving out costs the tenant a truck, a day and labor, so many absorb a modest increase. Why it is risky: aggressive ECRIs drive move-outs, bad reviews and regulators' attention (https://modernstoragemedia.com/msm-exclusives/are-aggressive-ecris-leading-us-to-lower-noi-and-higher-customer-churn , Slate 01/2025 cited in AB 498 analysis).
  - Notice: rental agreements specify the notice period for a rate change (30 days is common) **[GENERAL PRACTICE - check Greens' agreement]**.
  - **California SB 709 (2025, B&P 21715.2, effective for agreements signed on or after 01/01/2026):** the rental agreement must disclose, on the first page in larger, contrasting type: the initial term and renewal term; whether the move-in rate is promotional / discounted and for how long; whether the rate can change and **the maximum rental fee the owner could charge during the first 12 months**; every step needed to terminate (including removing all property); and the owner's contact information. It started as a rent-increase cap and was amended into a disclosure law. Sources: https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/news/sb709-has-been-signed-into-law-ssa-webinar-scheduled , https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB709 . Front-line lesson: in California, staff must be able to explain the first-page disclosures and never promise "your rate will never go up."

### 4.2 Occupancy - three kinds (ISS: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/what-s-your-self-storage-facility-occupancy-understanding-and-using-the-3-types)
- **Unit occupancy** = units rented / total units.
- **Physical (square-foot) occupancy** = rented sq ft / total rentable sq ft. Most quoted; large units can make it look better than it is (100 of 200 units rented can be 80% physical occupancy if the rented ones are the big ones).
- **Economic occupancy** = rent actually achieved (collected) / gross potential rent at street rates. Discounts, free months, comp units and delinquency drag it down: ten 5x10s at $50 = $500 potential; one at $45 = 99%; add one free charity unit = 89%. ISS suggests keeping the unit-vs-economic gap at 3 points or less.
- Teaching point: "full" is not the goal; revenue is. A facility at 95% physical occupancy with heavy discounts and 10% delinquency can earn less than one at 88%.

### 4.3 Concessions / promotions
- First month free, $1 first month, 50% off 2-3 months, free truck rental with move-in. They drive move-ins but lower economic occupancy and anchor the customer to a low rate (which is why the first ECRI comes early at some operators). In California, SB 709 requires the promotional rate and its length to be disclosed on page one.

### 4.4 Fees
- **Admin / setup fee:** one-time at move-in; one ISS guide suggests $10-$15 (https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/generating-self-storage-income-through-fees-and-other-extras-). Many operators charge ~$20-$30 **[VERIFY - Greens' amount]**.
- **Security / cleaning deposit:** some operators, ~$20-$40 (same source).
- **Late fees - California (B&P 21713.5):** no late fee until rent is unpaid at least **10 days** after the due date; the fee must be stated in the rental agreement; only **one** late fee per missed payment; maximum: **$10** if monthly rent is $60 or less; **$15** if rent is over $60 and under $100; **the greater of $20 or 15% of monthly rent** if rent is $100 or more. Source: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_21713.5 (added by AB 2263, 1999). Example: $180/month unit -> max late fee $27 (15%); $120 unit -> $20.
- **Late fees - Texas:** Chapter 59 as fetched contains **no specific late-fee cap**; fees are set by the rental agreement and must be reasonable. A 2005 bill (HB 2050) proposing caps was not enacted. **[VERIFY with Greens counsel / TSSA - do not teach a Texas number.]** Source: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/79R/billtext/html/HB02050I.htm
- **Lien / pre-lien / auction fees:** charged to cover notice and sale costs; pre-lien charges ~$20-$35 and auction fees ~$25-$100 are cited (https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/charging-fair-responsible-fees-a-smart-profitable-practice-for-every-self-storage-operation). In California the lien may include "expenses necessary for the preservation, sale or disposition" of the property (B&P 21702); fees must be in the agreement and real, not penalties. **[VERIFY Greens' schedule]**
- Others: cut-lock fee, NSF / returned payment fee, transfer fee.

### 4.5 Tenant protection and tenant insurance (why it matters)
Sources: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/products/self-storage-tenant-insurance-vs-protection-plans-differences-advantages-and-risks , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/insurance/self-storage-tenant-protection-plans-are-they-insurance-and-why-does-it-matter
- **The owner's property insurance does not cover the tenant's belongings.** The rental agreement typically releases the owner from liability for loss or damage to stored goods (exculpatory clause) except for the owner's own negligence where law requires. Customers often assume "the facility is insured" - the course must kill this myth.
- **Tenant insurance:** a third-party insurance policy covering the tenant's goods (fire, theft, water, etc.) up to a chosen value for a monthly premium; sold by staff under a limited-lines license in many states.
- **Tenant protection plan:** not insurance; the operator agrees, for a monthly fee, to waive its liability-release clause and pay for covered losses up to a limit (often backed by the operator's contractual liability insurance). California Supreme Court, *Heckart v. A-1 Self Storage* (2018): protection plans are not insurance and not subject to the Insurance Code (https://www.insideselfstorage.com/profit-centers/california-supreme-court-rules-self-storage-tenant-protection-plans-aren-t-subject). Louisiana and New Mexico have restricted protection plans. **[VERIFY which product Greens sells in CA vs TX.]**
- Why it matters: protects the customer from loss, protects the facility from claims and lawsuits, reduces disputes after break-ins / leaks, and is a significant revenue line ("ancillary income"). Many operators require proof of coverage or enrollment at move-in **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**.
- Staff rules: describe the plan accurately, never call a protection plan "insurance" if it is not, never promise coverage for a specific loss ("the claims administrator decides"), and record acceptance or declination in the agreement.

### 4.6 Merchandise / retail
- Disc and cylinder locks, boxes (small / medium / large / wardrobe / dish-pack), tape, bubble wrap, mattress and furniture covers, moving blankets, dollies. 41% of renters buy supplies (SSA 2023). A lock sale at move-in is both revenue and security.

### 4.7 Revenue management concepts (awareness level for front-line staff)
- Price by unit type and size based on occupancy, demand, seasonality (spring / summer peak for moves) and competitor rates; push rates on units nearly sold out, discount on units with high vacancy.
- Balance street rates (to fill) and ECRIs (to grow revenue): https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/iss-blog-your-street-rates-and-ecri-must-work-hand-in-hand-my-playbook-for-self-storage-pricing
- Staff impact: quote the system's current rate, never invent discounts, upsell climate control / protection / locks honestly, keep unit status accurate (a unit wrongly marked occupied or "not rentable" is lost revenue).

## 5. The rental agreement

### 5.1 Nature of the deal
- **Month-to-month:** most agreements renew monthly; either side may end with the notice the agreement states. Average stays are much longer than a month: Extra Space ~16 months (stabilized stores, 2022); Storable platform 19.3 months (Q1 2026); Global Self Storage ~3.6 years (Q1 2026); ~25% of renters stay 1-2 years and ~24% over 2 years (SSA 2023). Sources: https://in.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/EXTRA-SPACE-STORAGE-INC-12540/news/EXTRA-SPACE-STORAGE-INC-MANAGEMENT-S-DISCUSSION-AND-ANALYSIS-OF-FINANCIAL-CONDITION-AND-RESULTS-OF-42212099/ , https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Global+Self+Storage+Reports+First+Quarter+2026+Results/26461253.html . Teach "about 1.5 years on average, varies a lot."
- **A license / rental of space, not a lease of a home and not a bailment.** Standard clauses say the agreement creates no bailment: the operator never takes custody of the goods; the tenant has exclusive control of the space and the only key. That is why the operator generally does not know (and should not ask to inventory) what is stored, and why its liability is limited. Sources: https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/use-of-storage-space , https://www.unittrac.com/Blog/elements-of-a-strong-rental-agreement
- Landlord-tenant (residential) law does not apply - storage has its own statutes (CA B&P 21700-21716; TX Property Code Ch. 59).

### 5.2 Key clauses (typical)
- Parties, unit number and size, rent, due date, fees (admin, late, lien, NSF, cut-lock).
- **Lien notice** - statutory requirements:
  - **California B&P 21712:** the agreement must be in writing and state that the occupant's property will be subject to a claim of lien and may be sold to satisfy it if rent or other charges remain unpaid for **14 consecutive days**, and that the Act authorizes this. It must **request an alternate contact** (name plus mailing or email address) to receive lien notices - if given, notices go to both. **Email notices** are allowed only if the agreement says lien notices may be emailed (to the occupant and the alternate) and the occupant signs consenting; the owner must prove actual delivery (see 8.2). Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-21712/
  - **Texas 59.041 / 59.043(d):** the contractual landlord's lien must be **underlined or in conspicuous bold print** in a written agreement, or the facility cannot sell without a court judgment. Email notice requires underlined or bold language in the agreement that notice may be given by email if the tenant provides an address. Towing of vehicles requires express authorization in the agreement (SB 1181, 2021). Sources: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_59.041 , https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_59.043
  - **California SB 709 first-page disclosures** (agreements on or after 01/01/2026) - section 4.1.
- **Value limit:** many agreements cap the total value the tenant may store (e.g., $5,000) unless the owner agrees in writing; this caps liability. CA B&P 21713 expressly lets parties agree to "limit the value of property the occupant may store." The $5,000 figure is common practice, not law. **[VERIFY Greens' figure.]**
- **Release / limitation of liability; insurance / protection requirement** (section 4.5).
- **Use restrictions** (5.3), **owner's right to enter** (inspection, repairs, emergencies, law-enforcement or government order, prohibited items, lien enforcement - with notice as the agreement states) **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**, **change of address / contact duty** (tenant must keep address, phone and email current - critical because lien notices go to the last known address), **termination / move-out** (notice, unit broom-clean, remove lock, no refund of partial month unless policy says), **rules and regulations** (gate hours, speed, no smoking, dumping), **access rights** (only tenant and authorized users on the account), **military status** disclosure, **dispute / jurisdiction** clauses.

### 5.3 Prohibited uses and items (typical lists)
- **Living in a unit** - never. Texas law prohibits residential use outright (Tex. Prop. Code 59.009: a tenant may not use or allow use of the facility as a residence). Agreements everywhere prohibit it and it violates building and fire codes. Units have no egress, ventilation or sanitation - people have died. Sources: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_59.009 , https://www.moverscorp.com/moving_guide/self_storage_restrictions
- **Hazardous materials:** gasoline, propane, fuel cans, paint thinners, solvents, fertilizer, chemicals, compressed gas, asbestos, toxic waste. (Vehicles / equipment with fuel are usually allowed only in parking or with limits - per agreement.)
- **Explosives, ammunition and fireworks; firearms** - many agreements prohibit firearms and ammunition **[VERIFY Greens' policy; Texas operators vary]**.
- **Perishables / food / animal feed** (attract pests), **live animals or plants**, **anything wet or smelly**.
- **Illegal items:** stolen goods, drugs, unregistered vehicles (per agreement), counterfeit goods.
- **Operating a business from the unit:** storing business inventory is fine; customers coming to the unit, employees working there, retail sales, manufacturing, band practice, auto repair, using power outlets for running equipment - not permitted. **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**
- **Irreplaceable items** (cash, jewelry, heirlooms, important documents) - discouraged or excluded from coverage.
- Owner may enter to remove prohibited items and charge the tenant (https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/use-of-storage-space).

### 5.4 What the operator can and cannot do (front-line summary)
- Can: set and change rent with proper notice; charge contract fees within law; deny gate access / overlock per the lawful process; enter per agreement terms; enforce a lien by the statutory process; refuse to rent; enforce rules equally.
- Cannot: hold a key or go into a rented unit casually; open a unit for a non-tenant (spouse, parent, police without legal process); skip or shortcut any lien step; sell or dispose of contents outside the statutory process (even "obvious junk"); keep or take items from a unit; charge fees not in the agreement; discriminate; ignore military status or a bankruptcy notice.

## 6. Delinquency and the lien process

### 6.1 General shape (both states)
1. Rent due -> 2. grace period -> 3. late fee (CA: not before day 10) -> 4. reminders (calls, texts, emails - **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**) -> 5. formal statutory notice -> 6. lien attaches / access denied -> 7. notice of sale -> 8. advertisement -> 9. public sale (often online) -> 10. proceeds applied, excess held for the tenant. At any point before the sale the tenant can usually stop it by paying in full. Most delinquencies resolve early - good collections calls prevent most auctions.

### 6.2 California - California Self-Service Storage Facility Act (Bus. & Prof. Code 21700-21716)
Statute index: https://law.onecle.com/california/business/division-8/chapter-10/index.html ; official: leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (section-by-section text via FindLaw links below). Plain-English timeline: https://www.norcaladvocates.com/selfstoragelawblog/how-storage-unit-auctions-work-in-california-and-how-to-stop-one (consumer-side law firm; useful because it shows how tenants will challenge a sale).
- **21702 - Lien:** the owner has a lien on all property stored for rent, labor, late fees and other charges, and expenses to preserve, sell or dispose of the property. Lien includes late fees (AB 2263, 1999).
- **21703 - Preliminary Lien Notice:** once any part of rent or other charges is unpaid **14 consecutive days**, the owner may terminate the right to use the space by sending a notice to the **last known address AND the alternate address**, by **certified mail**, **first-class mail with a certificate of mailing**, or **email** (if 21712(c) is satisfied). It must contain: an itemized statement of sums due and when they became due; a statement that the right to use the space terminates on a date **not less than 14 days after mailing** unless paid; a notice that access may then be denied and a lien imposed; and the owner's / agent's name, street address and phone. Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-21703/ . 21704 provides the statutory sample form.
- **21705(a) - Lien attaches:** if the full amount is still unpaid on the termination date, the lien attaches as of that date and the owner **may deny access, enter the space and remove the property to a place of safekeeping**.
- **21705(b) - Notice of Lien Sale:** sent to both addresses (same methods). It must state: the right to use the space has ended and the occupant no longer has access; the property is subject to a lien, the current amount, and that it grows with unpaid rent; the property may be sold on a date **not less than 14 days from mailing** unless the occupant returns a **Declaration in Opposition** by certified mail; the occupant can regain full use by paying the full lien before that date; excess proceeds will be held for the occupant (or other claimant) for **one year**, then go to the county. A **blank Declaration in Opposition** form must be enclosed. Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-21705/
- **21706 / 21710 - Declaration in Opposition:** if a valid declaration (signed under penalty of perjury, with a current physical address and phone, returned by certified mail before the sale date) is received, the owner **may not sell** and must instead sue (civil or small claims court) to enforce the lien. Staff must recognize a declaration the moment it arrives and escalate immediately - selling after one arrives is a serious violation.
- **21707 - Advertisement and sale:** before the sale, advertise either (a) **once a week for two consecutive weeks** in a newspaper of general circulation (public notice district or the facility's county), or (b) **once in a newspaper AND once on a publicly accessible website that customarily conducts or advertises online auctions**, where the online ad stays up **at least 7 days** before the sale (option (b) from AB 542, effective 01/01/2024). With no newspaper, post at least **10 days** before the sale in **six or more conspicuous places** near the sale. The ad must contain the account holder's name and the facility's name and location. The sale must be **commercially reasonable** - an in-person auction or a sale on a publicly accessible auction website. Excess proceeds (after the lien and sale costs) are held for **one year** for the occupant or a court-order holder, then paid to the **county treasury**. Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-21707/ , AB 542 coverage: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-industry-news/california-considers-change-to-self-storage-lien-sale-advertising-requirements
- **21708 / 21709:** holders of perfected security interests may claim; anyone may pay the lien before the sale (a third party with an interest can redeem - per the NorCal Advocates summary, the full lien plus one month in advance) **[VERIFY exact 21709 wording]**.
- **21711:** good-faith purchasers at a lien sale take the goods free of the occupant's rights.
- **21702.5 - Vehicles and vessels:** a self-storage lien on a registered vehicle or vessel is enforced under **Civil Code 3071** (vehicles; DMV lien-sale process) or **Harbors & Navigation Code 503** (vessels), not 21705-21711; recorded lienholders on the title come first; the lien cannot include charges accruing more than **60 days** after it attaches. Alternatively the owner may **tow** a vehicle / watercraft / trailer once rent is **60 days** unpaid and the 21703 notice has been sent, with at least **10 days'** notice by first-class mail with certificate of mailing giving the tow company's name, address and phone. Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-21702-5/ . (Detail belongs in the RV / boat course.)
- **Email notice history - correction to the brief:** the course outline mentions "2016 changes allowing email notice and online auctions." Research shows the California changes were **AB 1108 (Daly, Ch. 227, Stats. 2017), effective 01/01/2018**: email lien notices (with signed consent in the agreement and proof of delivery and receipt, else re-send by mail) and sale through an auction website as a commercially reasonable sale. It had a sunset (01/01/2021), extended by AB 3364 (2020) and made permanent by **AB 2960 (2022)**. **AB 542 (2023)** added the newspaper-plus-online advertising option (effective 01/01/2024). **AB 498 (2025)** changed proof of email delivery to allow evidence that the email was downloaded, printed, viewed, **opened**, or otherwise acknowledged (sponsored by CSSA and SSA; Senate Judiciary July 2025) - it reportedly took effect 01/01/2026 alongside SB 709 **[VERIFY AB 498 chaptered / effective]**. Earlier: **AB 655 (2010, effective 01/01/2011)** restructured the lien attachment / notice of lien sale steps. Sources: https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-07/ab-498-michelle-rodriguez-sjud-analysis_0.pdf (legislative history on p. 9-10), https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/msm-exclusives/legislative-victories . **The writer should NOT say 2016.**
- **Email proof of delivery (21712(c), pre-AB 498 version):** tenant e-signs an acknowledgment; or logs into a password-protected website / app and views / downloads the notice; or replies to the email. If delivery cannot be shown, re-send by mail using the method the notice section requires.
- **Minimum California timeline** (approximate, assuming no payment and no declaration): day 0 due -> day 10 late fee earliest -> day 14 preliminary lien notice earliest -> +14 days (about day 28) lien attaches, access denied -> notice of lien sale -> +14 days minimum -> advertising (2 weeks or newspaper plus 7 days online) -> sale. Commonly described as **at least about 6 weeks**, in practice often 45-60+ days.

### 6.3 Texas - Property Code Chapter 59 (Self-Service Storage Facility Liens)
Official: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.59.htm ; section-by-section: texas.public.law links below; practitioner summary: https://storageauctions.net/lien-laws/tx-lien-laws
- **59.006 / 59.021 - Lien:** the lessor has a lien on **all property in the facility** for charges due and unpaid; it attaches when the property is placed in the facility.
- **59.041 - Enforcement:** only by court judgment, **or** by seizure and sale without a court if the lien is a contractual landlord's lien **underlined or printed in conspicuous bold** in a written rental agreement and Chapter 59 is followed.
- **59.042 / 59.043 - Notice of claim:** before seizure and sale the lessor must deliver written notice of the claim **in person, by verified mail, or by email** to the tenant's last known postal or email address. Verified mail is delivered when deposited with USPS or a common carrier; email is delivered when sent (email only if the agreement has underlined / bold email-notice language). Contents: itemized account of the claim; lessor's / agent's name, address and phone; a statement that the contents have been seized under the contractual landlord's lien; a statement that if the claim is not satisfied by the **14th day after delivery**, the property may be sold at public auction (or a vehicle towed); and an **underlined or bold request that a tenant in military service notify the lessor of their status immediately** (the lessor may require written proof, e.g., DoD documentation). Sources: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_59.042 , https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_59.043 . Chapter 59 sets no minimum number of days of delinquency before the notice may be sent (it is driven by the agreement) **[VERIFY with counsel]**.
- **59.044 - Notice of sale (advertising):** if unpaid after 14 days, publish once in each of **two consecutive weeks** in a newspaper of general circulation in the county; if none, post at the facility and at least **five other conspicuous places** nearby. Contents: general description of the property; that it is sold to satisfy a landlord's lien; tenant's name; facility address; time, place and terms (place may be an internet website address for an online sale). Sale timing (59.042(c)): no earlier than the **15th day after first publication**, or after the **10th day after posting**. Source: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_59.044 . HB 2574 (2023) proposed an online-advertising alternative; it passed the House but its final outcome was not confirmed **[VERIFY - check whether Texas now allows online-only advertising]**.
- **59.045 - Conduct of sale:** public; at the facility, a reasonably near public place, **or through a publicly accessible internet website** (S.B. 952, 2017, effective 09/01/2017); follow the advertised terms; sell to the highest bidder.
- **59.008 - Redemption:** the tenant may redeem before sale by paying the lien plus the lessor's reasonable expenses.
- **59.046 - Excess proceeds:** written notice to the tenant's last known address; the lessor holds the excess and pays it if the tenant asks **within two years** of the sale; after that, the lessor owns it (differs from California's one year then county).
- **59.0445 - Vehicles, boats, outboard motors:** notice to the last known owner and every recorded lienholder within 30 days of taking possession (publication or verified mail), payable before the **31st day**; sale after the 31st day; false information is a Class B misdemeanor. **59.052 - Towing (SB 1181, effective 09/01/2021):** a vehicle / boat / trailer may instead be towed to a licensed vehicle storage facility if towing is authorized in the agreement, the tenant did not pay within the notice period, and the tow company agrees in writing; Texas DMV guidance cited as 15 days after the notice of claim. Sources: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/facility-operators/texas-governor-signs-optional-towing-bill-into-law-for-self-storage-operators
- **59.009 - No residential use.** **59.010 - Military:** Texas State Guard / Texas National Guard members on active duty get the same protection as federal servicemembers under the SCRA.
- **59.005 - Damages for violation** (lessor liability for violating the chapter) **[VERIFY amounts]**.
- History: SB 690 / HB 1259 (2011, effective 01/01/2012) allowed verified mail or email instead of certified mail, added the military notice, and moved vehicle / boat foreclosure into Chapter 59 (https://www.insideselfstorage.com/associations/modernized-texas-self-storage-lien-bill-signed-by-governor).
- **Late fees:** see 4.4 - no statutory cap found.
- **Minimum Texas timeline** (approximate): notice of claim delivered -> 14 days to pay -> first newspaper ad -> second ad the following week -> sale no earlier than day 15 after first publication. Roughly a month from notice to sale at the earliest, plus whatever delinquency period Greens' agreement and policy set before the notice.

### 6.5 What staff must NEVER do in the lien process
- Skip, shorten, re-order or back-date any notice or deadline; send a notice to only one address in California when an alternate is on file; "send it by email" without the signed consent and proof-of-receipt process.
- Accept a partial payment without knowing what it does to the lien timeline (company policy decides; partial payment generally does not stop a sale unless accepted under policy / in writing) **[VERIFY Greens' policy]**.
- Proceed after receiving a Declaration in Opposition (CA), a bankruptcy notice, or any indication the tenant is an active-duty servicemember - stop and escalate.
- Enter a unit, remove, keep, give away, throw out or buy anything from a liened unit; let friends or family bid; promise a customer they can "get their stuff back after the auction."
- Sell personal papers, photos and documents carelessly - many operators set aside personal papers and photographs for the tenant **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**.
- Discuss a tenant's delinquency with anyone not on the account.
- Ignore a deceased-tenant report (section 9.5).

## 7. Facility operations

Sources (general): https://www.insideselfstorage.com/self-storage-industry-news/a-self-storage-owner-s-guide-to-creating-a-policies-and-procedures-manual , https://www.storagepug.com/blog/daily-manager-checklist , https://www.storagepug.com/blog/facility-rounds-sheet , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/site-maintenance-safety/the-maintenance-toolbox , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/staffing/3-critical-lessons-i-learned-my-first-week-self-storage-manager . The specific items below are **[GENERAL PRACTICE]** compiled from these; Greens should overlay its own SOP.

### 7.1 Opening routine
- Check alarm, disarm, note any overnight alarm / gate events; review cameras for overnight incidents.
- Walk the gate and fence line; confirm gate operates and keypad works; check lighting left on / off.
- Open office: lights, computers, management system, phones (check voicemail / web leads and reservations), cash drawer count to the opening bank.
- Check the move-in / move-out list, reservations due, payments due, delinquency and lien tasks for the day.
- Make the office presentable (merchandise faced, brochures, restroom), flags and signs out.

### 7.2 Walk-throughs / lock checks (daily or as policy sets)
- Walk every aisle and hallway: every rented unit should have a lock; vacant units should be locked by the facility or overlocked (and shown vacant in the system); every delinquent unit to be overlocked should be.
- Look for: **unlocked rented units** (secure with a facility lock and notify the tenant to come re-lock - document); **cut or damaged locks** (possible break-in - photos, report, notify tenant, check camera and gate logs); unit doors not fully closed; signs of **people living in a unit** (bedding, cooking, voices, lights, extension cords); odors, leaks, liquids under doors, pests; dumped trash; damaged doors, latches, hallways, lights; anything blocking aisles or fire exits; vehicles parked where they should not be.
- Reconcile the walk to the system: a "vacant" unit with a lock on it, or an "occupied" unit with no lock, is an audit issue (possible unpaid use or a theft).

### 7.3 Vacant unit preparation (after move-out)
- Confirm the tenant removed their lock and everything; photograph; sweep / clean, remove debris and nails, check door operation and seals, check for damage, pests, stains or odors; repair or report; reset cylinder core if used; place facility lock; mark rent-ready in the system so it can be sold. Items left behind = follow the abandoned-property policy, never just keep them.

### 7.4 Overlocks
- Placed only on the system's list (delinquent per lawful step, vacant, unlocked-unit security); colored and logged; removed promptly when paid (a paid tenant arriving to find an overlock is a top complaint); never removed by the tenant.

### 7.5 Cleanliness and curb appeal
- Clean office and restrooms; trash picked up; landscaping; graffiti removal; cobwebs; clear signage; working lights; clean gate and keypad; door paint. Curb appeal is a sales tool (SSA CSSM has an "image and curb appeal" session). Online reviews mention cleanliness more than almost anything else **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**.

### 7.6 Pest control
- Scheduled professional service (bait stations around perimeter), no food in units rule, quick cleanup of spills, report droppings / bites / smells; seal gaps. Rodents and roaches are the main threats; in Texas and inland California scorpions, spiders and snakes also appear **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**.

### 7.7 Safety
- Watch for trip hazards, potholes, broken doors (roll-up door springs are dangerous - never repair without training), uneven elevator, wet floors; carts in good repair; fire extinguishers inspected; exits clear; heat illness precautions in summer (CA has a heat illness standard for outdoor work - **[VERIFY Greens HR policy]**); never enter a unit alone if something seems wrong; never confront trespassers or suspected thieves - call 911.

### 7.8 Abandoned items and dumping
- Items left in a vacated unit, in aisles or at the dumpster: photograph, log, follow policy; property in a unit that is still rented belongs to the tenant (only the lien process or the tenant's written abandonment / release lets the facility dispose of it). Some agreements include a signed "abandonment" or move-out release. **[GENERAL PRACTICE - legal review advised; when in doubt, treat as the tenant's property.]**

### 7.9 Security cameras and access logs
- Cameras cover gate, office, hallways and drive aisles; staff review footage after incidents and preserve it (see 9.3: once a subpoena or incident arises, stop automatic deletion). Gate logs show code use. Never share footage with the public or other tenants; law enforcement gets it under the law-enforcement process.

### 7.10 Key and code controls
- Master keys (cylinder-lock master, office, elevator, gate override) are tracked, signed out and never lent; staff do not give tenants or vendors access by using their own codes; change codes when staff leave; every gate-code change or override is logged. **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**

### 7.11 Cash handling
- Opening bank count, receipt for every payment through the system (never take cash "off the books"), no personal checks cashed, drop safe, two-person count / deposit where possible, daily deposit, close-out report reconciled to the system; never store cash in your car or desk. Push customers to autopay (the most preferred method per SSA). **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**

### 7.12 Emergencies
- California: earthquake, wildfire (inland San Diego / Riverside counties - Valley Center, Escondido, Temecula, Murrieta - are wildfire-prone), extreme heat, power outages / public safety power shutoffs. Texas: severe storms, hail, tornado, flash flooding (Central Texas / Hill Country around Austin and Georgetown is "flash flood alley"), winter freeze (Feb 2021), heat. Plan: evacuation, who to call, how to secure the gate, tenant communication, documenting damage with photos before cleanup, and never letting tenants into a damaged building until it is cleared. **[GENERAL PRACTICE + regional knowledge; VERIFY Greens' emergency plan.]**
- Medical emergency, fire in a unit, gas smell, suspected person living in a unit, suspected drug lab / chemical smell: call 911 first, then the manager.

### 7.13 Closing routine
- Last walk / drive of the property; confirm no one is left after gate hours (some systems sound a warning); confirm gate closed; close-out cash and reports; set alarm; lights on per policy; lock office.

## 8. Legal and compliance basics for front-line staff

### 8.1 Privacy of tenant information
- Tenant identity, address, phone, payment status, unit number and even the fact that a person rents at the facility are confidential. Confirm identity before discussing an account; only the tenant and people the tenant listed as authorized (on the account) get information or access. A spouse, parent, roommate, ex, landlord, process server or private investigator who is not on the account gets nothing - "I'm not able to share whether that person is a customer." **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**
- **Alternate contact (CA)** receives lien notices but is not automatically authorized for access or account information **[VERIFY Greens' policy]**.
- Gate codes are passwords; never read one out to an unverified caller.

### 8.2 Law enforcement requests (subpoenas and warrants)
Sources: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal-issues/investigative-access-how-self-storage-operators-should-respond-when-faced-with-a-search-warrant-or-subpoena , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal/when-police-want-access-your-self-storage-business-understanding-subpoenas-and-search-warrants , https://www.kts-law.com/government-agencies-and-private-individuals-rights-to-tenant-information-and-rights-to-entry/
- **Search warrant (signed by a judge):** ask to see it; check it names the facility and the unit / tenant; note the lead officer's name, agency, badge and warrant number; get a copy; provide access as needed (open gate; officers may cut the lock); do not argue validity, participate in the search, or obstruct; do not tip off the tenant or disable cameras; write an incident report; get the inventory of seized items.
- **No warrant:** politely explain company policy requires a warrant for unit entry and legal process for records; call the manager / designated contact. Exceptions (exigent circumstances, probation / parole searches, liened units) are management / counsel decisions, not front-line ones.
- **Subpoena (for records):** don't ignore it; note the deadline; route to management / counsel; produce only what is requested; preserve records including camera footage (stop auto-delete).
- **Tenant notification:** generally do not notify the tenant; some warrants include gag orders.
- **Blanket requests** (e.g., "list of all tenants"): management negotiates scope; front-line never hands over a rent roll.

### 8.3 Active-duty military - SCRA
Sources: https://www.servicememberscivilreliefact.com/blog/what-self-self-storage-owners-should-know-about-scra , DOJ enforcement (e.g., https://www.justice.gov/crt/case-document/file/1306176/dl and related consent orders), statute 50 U.S.C. 3958 (https://policyrisk.com/us-code/title/50/section/3958)
- **50 U.S.C. 3958:** a person holding a lien on a servicemember's property or effects (expressly including storage liens) **may not foreclose or enforce the lien during military service or for 90 days after, without a court order.** The court may stay the proceeding or adjust the obligation. DOJ treats it as **strict liability** - good intentions are no defense; knowingly violating it is a federal misdemeanor **[VERIFY penalty wording]**. DOJ has sued and settled with storage and towing companies that auctioned servicemembers' goods without court orders; consent orders require operators to check military status before every lien sale (e.g., via the Defense Manpower Data Center SCRA website) and to get a court order or a valid written waiver.
- **Waivers** must be a separate document (not buried in the lease) signed during or after the start of service **[VERIFY]**.
- Practice: ask at move-in "Are you or your spouse active-duty military?" and record it; Texas notices must contain the bold military-status request; before ANY lien sale, run a DMDC SCRA check and keep the certificate; if military, stop and escalate - no sale without a court order.
- Texas 59.010 extends the same protection to Texas State Guard / National Guard on active duty (more than 30 consecutive days per 59.001).
- Lease termination by servicemembers: the SCRA also lets servicemembers end certain leases on PCS / deployment orders; whether this applies to storage agreements is debated - handle per policy **[VERIFY]**.

### 8.4 Fair treatment and anti-discrimination
- Self-storage facilities are businesses open to the public: federal civil rights law and state law prohibit refusing service or treating customers differently based on protected characteristics. **California's Unruh Civil Rights Act** (Civil Code 51) covers all business establishments and protected characteristics including sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, sexual orientation, citizenship, primary language and immigration status **[VERIFY list against current Civil Code 51]**. Texas has narrower public-accommodation coverage but federal law (Civil Rights Act Title II for some places, ADA Title III for all) still applies **[VERIFY]**.
- Practical rule: same rates, same rules, same deposits, same lien treatment for everyone; never ask about immigration status or nationality; requirements (ID, application) applied uniformly. Fair Housing Act does not apply to storage (not housing), but people sometimes assume it does.

### 8.5 ADA basics
Sources: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal-issues/everything-self-storage-operators-need-to-know-about-the-americans-with-disabilities-act , 2010 ADA Standards 225.3 (https://www.corada.com/documents/2010ADAStandards/225-3), Janus summary https://www.janusintl.com/storage-disability-products/ada-requirements
- Accessible units required: **5% of units for facilities with 1-200 units (at least 1); 10 units + 2% of units over 200 for larger facilities** (180 units -> 9; 320 units -> 13), dispersed among unit classes. California's building code (CBC 11B-225.3) does not require dispersion among buildings in a multi-building facility.
- The route to accessible units, the office, restrooms, keypad height and parking must be accessible. "Drive-by" ADA lawsuits target storage sites - report any barrier (blocked accessible parking, broken ramp).
- Staff: offer an accessible unit when asked; never ask about the nature of a disability; service animals (dogs) are allowed in the office (ADA Title III); offer help reading or signing documents; make reasonable modifications to policies when asked (e.g., let an authorized helper sign or access) **[VERIFY against Greens policy]**.

### 8.6 Deceased tenant
Sources: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal-issues/tenant-death-in-self-storage-legal-options-for-facility-operators , https://www.storagecafe.com/blog/what-happens-to-a-self-storage-unit-when-the-tenant-dies/ , SSA course on deceased storer's unit
- Only the tenant (and listed authorized persons, per policy) has access. On a death, family members do not automatically get in. The facility generally requires a **death certificate** plus proof of authority over the estate: **letters testamentary** (executor named in a will), **letters of administration** (court-appointed administrator), or a **small-estate affidavit** where state law allows (California Probate Code 13100 small-estate procedure; Texas small estate affidavit) **[VERIFY thresholds; escalate]**.
- Rent keeps accruing; the estate is responsible. If the facility knows the tenant died, proceeding with a lien sale is legally sensitive - escalate; notices may need to go to the estate.
- Be compassionate - this is one of the "4 Ds."

### 8.7 Bankruptcy
Sources: https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal-issues/contending-with-tenant-bankruptcy-important-do-s-and-don-ts-for-self-storage-operators , https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal-issues/a-bankruptcy-primer-for-self-storage-operators-understanding-your-role-and-rights-in-this-legal-process , case *In re Ozenne* (https://hallapproved.com/ca/cases/federal/bankruptcy/2006/1868514/)
- Filing a bankruptcy petition creates an **automatic stay** (11 U.S.C. 362) the moment it is filed: stop the lien sale (even the day of the auction), stop collection calls / texts / emails, stop adding late fees, stop negotiating the old debt. Selling during the stay exposes the operator to liability. Get the case number and attorney information, verify the filing, escalate. The operator can ask the bankruptcy court for relief from stay, and ongoing rent after filing may be pursued under the court process.

### 8.8 Other compliance touch points
- **Living in units** - report immediately; never ignore (safety and liability).
- **Recording calls** - California is an all-party-consent state for call recording (Penal Code 632); Texas is one-party **[VERIFY Greens' phone system disclosures]**.
- **Collections calls / texts:** debt-collection and TCPA rules on texting / autodialing apply to some collections activity **[VERIFY - legal review]**.
- **Payment card data** - never write down card numbers; PCI basics **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**.

## 9. Key performance metrics (what a front-line employee should understand)

Sources: https://kinnovis.com/10-essential-self-storage-metrics-every-operator-should-track/ , https://insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/3-revenue-optimizing-kpis-every-self-storage-operator-should-be-tracking , https://monument.io/blog/third-party-self-storage-manager/
- **Occupancy** - unit, physical (sq ft), economic (section 4.2). Stabilized economic occupancy targets ~85-92% are cited **[VERIFY]**.
- **Move-ins / move-outs** - counts per day / week / month.
- **Net rentals** = move-ins - move-outs (positive = growing).
- **Rentable vs unrentable units** - units out of service for damage or cleaning are lost revenue; turn them fast.
- **Delinquency %** - share of units or rent past due (often measured at 30+ days, also 0-30, 31-60, 61-90 buckets); a single operator report showed ~10.6% **[VERIFY - not a benchmark]**. Collections calls early in the cycle matter most.
- **Lien / auction count** - auctions are a last resort; a high number usually signals weak collections or bad move-in screening.
- **Conversion rate** - inquiries (calls, web leads, walk-ins) to rentals; reservations to move-ins. Cited ranges vary widely (25-30% overall leads; 40-80% reservation-to-rental) **[VERIFY - operator reports only]**.
- **Average length of stay** ~16-19 months (section 5.1).
- **Rent per square foot** / average in-place rent; **street vs in-place spread**.
- **Protection / insurance penetration** - % of tenants enrolled (often a staff goal) **[GENERAL PRACTICE]**.
- **Ancillary / merchandise sales** per move-in.
- **Online reviews / ratings** - volume and score.
- **Autopay %** - drives lower delinquency.
- Teaching point: each metric links to a behavior (answer the phone, follow up on reservations, offer protection, chase past-dues on day 1-5, keep units rent-ready, keep the walk-through honest).

## 10. Common new-employee mistakes and myths (compiled; [GENERAL PRACTICE] unless cited)

**Myths to bust**
1. "The facility's insurance covers my stuff." No - tenant coverage or the protection plan does (4.5).
2. "Staff have a key / can check on my unit." No - the tenant has the only key and exclusive control (no bailment).
3. "Storage auctions are full of treasure." Most liened units contain low-value household goods; TV shows are staged entertainment.
4. "We can sell a unit as soon as they're late." No - strict statutory steps and minimum days (section 6).
5. "Partial payment stops the auction" / "We can just throw out junk from a delinquent unit." No - policy and statute govern (6.5).
6. "Rent is fixed while you rent." Month-to-month; rates can change with notice (CA requires first-page disclosure of the 12-month maximum).
7. "Police can always get in." Only with a warrant or other legal basis (8.2).
8. "Family can get into a deceased relative's unit." Only with proof of authority (8.6).
9. "Self-storage is just real estate - no customer service needed." Most renters are in a life transition; service drives reviews and retention.
10. "Bigger is always better to sell." Over-selling a size leads to a quick move-out or transfer; under-selling leads to a frustrated customer on move-in day.

**Common mistakes**
- Giving out account or gate information to someone not on the account (spouse, parent, police without process).
- Not collecting complete contact info, alternate contact (CA) and military status at move-in - which breaks the lien process later.
- Skipping or mis-dating a lien notice; not mailing to the alternate address; emailing without consent / proof.
- Forgetting to remove an overlock after payment; overlocking the wrong unit.
- Marking units vacant / rented incorrectly; not doing honest walk-throughs.
- Quoting rates or promising discounts the system doesn't show; calling a protection plan "insurance."
- Accepting cash without a system receipt; sloppy deposits.
- Letting tenants use unit power for appliances, letting someone "just sleep in the unit tonight," ignoring hazardous storage.
- Entering a unit or cutting a lock without the authorized procedure and witnesses.
- Repairing a roll-up door spring without training.
- Engaging with a suspected thief or trespasser instead of calling 911.
- Not escalating a Declaration in Opposition, a bankruptcy notice, an SCRA hit or a death report.

## 11. Glossary (54 terms)

- **Access control system:** gate keypad, codes and software that log who enters the property.
- **Access hours:** hours tenants can enter the gate; differs from office hours.
- **Admin fee:** one-time setup fee charged at move-in.
- **Alternate contact / alternate address (CA):** a second person / address (mail or email) the tenant names to receive lien notices (B&P 21712(b)).
- **Ancillary income:** revenue beyond rent - protection plans, merchandise, fees, truck rental.
- **Auction / lien sale:** public sale of a defaulted tenant's goods to satisfy the lien.
- **Autopay:** automatic recurring card or bank payment.
- **Bailment:** a legal relationship where one party takes custody of another's goods - self-storage agreements expressly create **no** bailment.
- **Climate-controlled unit:** a unit in a temperature-regulated (sometimes humidity-regulated) space.
- **Commercially reasonable (CA):** the standard a lien sale must meet (B&P 21707) - in person or on a public auction website.
- **Concession:** a discount or promotion (free month, reduced rate).
- **Conversion rate:** share of inquiries or reservations that become rentals.
- **Cylinder lock:** a lock built into the door latch, with a unique core per tenant.
- **Declaration in Opposition (CA):** a sworn form a tenant returns to stop a lien sale; owner must then go to court (B&P 21705, 21710).
- **Delinquency:** rent past due; often reported by aging bucket (0-30, 31-60...).
- **Disc lock:** round padlock with a shrouded shackle; hard to cut.
- **Drive-up unit:** exterior unit you can park in front of.
- **ECRI:** Existing Customer Rate Increase - rent increase to a current tenant.
- **Economic occupancy:** actual rent achieved / gross potential rent at street rates.
- **Exclusive control:** the tenant alone controls access to the unit and its contents.
- **Gate code:** a tenant's personal entry code.
- **Gross potential rent:** rent if every unit were rented at street rate.
- **In-place rate:** what an existing tenant currently pays.
- **Interior unit:** a unit reached from inside a building.
- **Late fee:** fee for late rent; capped in California (B&P 21713.5).
- **Lessor / owner / operator:** the facility side. **Lessee / occupant / tenant:** the renter. (CA statute says "owner" / "occupant"; TX says "lessor" / "tenant".)
- **Letters testamentary / letters of administration:** court documents showing who controls a deceased person's estate.
- **Lien:** the facility's legal claim on stored property for unpaid charges.
- **Lien notice / Preliminary Lien Notice (CA):** the first statutory notice after 14 days unpaid (B&P 21703).
- **Lockout:** suspending a tenant's gate access and / or overlocking the unit.
- **Management system (PMS / FMS):** the facility software (at Greens, Cubby) for rentals, payments, gate and reports.
- **Month-to-month:** a rental that renews each month until ended.
- **Move-in / move-out:** a new rental / a vacate.
- **Net rentable square feet (NRSF):** total square footage available to rent.
- **Net rentals:** move-ins minus move-outs.
- **Notice of Claim (TX):** the statutory notice starting the Texas lien sale clock (59.043).
- **Notice of Lien Sale (CA):** second notice giving the sale date, at least 14 days out (B&P 21705(b)).
- **Overlock:** a facility lock placed on a unit to block access.
- **Physical occupancy:** rented square feet / total rentable square feet.
- **Protection plan / tenant protection:** operator program (not insurance) that waives the liability release and covers losses up to a limit.
- **Rate lock:** a promise not to raise rent for a period.
- **REIT:** Real Estate Investment Trust - publicly traded real estate owner (e.g., Public Storage, Extra Space, CubeSmart).
- **Rent roll:** list of all rented units, tenants and rents.
- **Rentable / unrentable unit:** ready to sell vs out of service.
- **Reservation:** a held unit for a future move-in.
- **SCRA:** Servicemembers Civil Relief Act - federal law requiring a court order before enforcing a storage lien on an active-duty servicemember (or within 90 days after) (50 U.S.C. 3958).
- **Smart lock / smart entry:** Bluetooth / app-based gate and unit access.
- **Stabilized:** a facility that has reached its target occupancy (often ~85-90%+) after lease-up.
- **Street rate:** current advertised move-in price.
- **Tenant insurance:** a third-party insurance policy covering the tenant's goods.
- **Third-party management (3PM):** an outside company operating a facility for its owner.
- **Unit mix:** the combination of unit sizes and types at a facility.
- **Unit occupancy:** units rented / total units.
- **Value limit:** a contractual cap on the total value a tenant may store.
- **Vacate / vacant unit prep:** steps to clean, inspect and make a unit rent-ready.
- **Verified mail (TX):** USPS or common-carrier mail with proof of mailing; replaced certified mail for Texas notices in 2012.
- **Walk-through / lock check:** routine inspection of all units and locks.
