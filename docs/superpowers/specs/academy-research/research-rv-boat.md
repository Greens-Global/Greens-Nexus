## 1. Market: why RV and boat storage exists

### 1.1 Ownership numbers
- **RVs, 2025:** 8.1 million US households own an RV (Go RVing / RVIA 2025 RV Owner Demographic Profile, Ipsos). The median owner age is 49, down from 53 in 2021. 46% of owners are 35-54. Median use is 30 days a year, up from 20 in 2021. 16.9 million households say they intend to buy within five years, and 4 million are former owners. https://rvia.org/2025-go-rving-rv-owner-demographic-profile ; https://www.rvtravel.com/2025-rv-demographics-1199/ **[Sourced]**
  - **Caution:** the widely quoted "11.2 million RV households" is the **2021** study. The 2025 study changed its method and says the two numbers are not comparable. Do not write "RV ownership fell 28%." **[Sourced]**
  - Older figure, useful only for context: 8.9 million RV households in a 2011 study, typical owner about 48 years old (Inside Self Storage, 2012). https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/maximizing-revenue-from-boat-and-rv-storage-attracting-tenants-with-the-right-pricing-amenities-and-marketing **[Dated]**
- **Boats:** about 11.8 million registered or documented recreational boats in the US in 2024, plus an estimated 3.6 million unregistered (NMMA via secondary report). The 2023 figure was 11.6 million, down 1.9%. California is a top-5 registration state (with FL, MI, MN, OH, 2023). https://marinefabricatormag.com/2024/10/23/2023-total-boat-registrations-report-shows-downward-boating-trends/ ; https://www.marinadockage.com/?p=22334 **[Verify]** The 11.8M figure comes from one secondary source. USCG "Recreational Boating Statistics 2024" has the official registration table: https://mail.uscgboating.org/library/accident-statistics/Recreational-Boating-Statistics-2024.pdf
  - 2024 sales: 238,117 new boats (141,590 outboard boats), 858,798 pre-owned sales, $55.6 billion retail spend (NMMA). https://marinefabricatormag.com/2025/10/02/nmma-releases-annual-total-industry-sales-by-category-and-state-report/ **[Sourced]**
- **Supply gap claim:** Toy Storage Nation says the market needs about **5x** the current RV/boat storage supply, based on about 25 million RV- or boat-owning households. Yardi Matrix data (via RV PRO) reportedly shows fewer than 70 RV/boat storage developments underway in 2025. https://programminginsider.com/rv-boat-storage-reaches-crisis-levels-signaling-entrepreneurs-to-pounce-on-booming-opportunity/ **[Verify]** Toy Storage Nation sells workshops, so this is a promotional source. Use it as "industry groups estimate," not as fact.

### 1.2 Why people cannot park at home
- **HOAs:** a growing number of HOAs bar oversized vehicles on members' lots (RV PRO). In Texas, operators near Rockwall/Heath tie steady demand to deed-restricted communities. https://www.recnationstorage.com/blog/how-much-does-rv-and-boat-storage-cost-in-rockwall/ **[Sourced]** Much of Temecula/Murrieta and the newer Georgetown/Leander/Round Rock subdivisions are HOA communities **[Verify, general knowledge]**.
- **City ordinances (our own cities):**
  - **Escondido:** Municipal Code Ch. 33, Art. 25 "Parking of Recreational Vehicles in Residential Zones." The driveway is the main allowed spot. Front-yard parking off the driveway is allowed only if conforming driveway parking is not available. Never on landscaping. The city further restricted RV parking on streets and city lots in 2023-2024 (Ordinances 2023-02, 2024-09, 2024-10); there is an **RV Parallel Parking Permit** for short loading windows. https://ecode360.com/43265913 ; https://www.escondido.gov/DocumentCenter/View/6340/ADOPTION-OF-ORDINANCE-NO-2024-09-REGULATION-OF-RECREATIONAL-VEHICLE-PARKING-PDF ; https://www.escondido.gov/DocumentCenter/View/597/RV-Parallel-Parking-Permit-PDF **[Sourced; details Verify]**
  - **Murrieta:** a city info bulletin (IB-229) says RV access is permitted only through a city-permitted driveway opening, with width standards. https://murrietaca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/132/RV-Access-and-Storage-IB-229 **[Sourced]**
  - **Temecula, Valley Center (unincorporated San Diego County), Fairfield, Georgetown/Austin:** rules not confirmed in this research. **[Verify]** A common pattern is a 72-hour limit on any vehicle parked on a public street (California Vehicle Code 22651(k) lets cities tow a vehicle parked 72+ hours) **[Verify]**. Third-party city guide: https://caniparkmyrv.com/california-rv-parking-rules/
  - Training point: staff should not quote city law to customers. They can say "many cities and HOAs here limit driveway and street parking; check with yours," and know that this is the number-one reason people call.
- **Other reasons:** lots too small or no side-yard access, RV too tall for the garage, theft risk at home, neighbor complaints, and renters who have no driveway at all.

### 1.3 Customer profiles
Industry descriptions (Inside Self Storage, 2007 and 2012) **[Dated, still directionally true]**:
- Tenants know their vehicle's value and usually carry insurance. Incomes run higher than a typical apartment renter's. Many still owe a loan (lienholder), which gives them a reason to keep paying. They shop and pay online and travel. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/market-trends-for-rv-and-boat-storage
- They stay a long time once they find a site that works. Churn and late pays run lower than in unit storage (same source).
- Covered and enclosed tenants behave like a different customer group from open-lot tenants (2012 article above).

Profiles to teach (synthesized; **[Verify against Greens' own Cubby tenant mix]**):
| Profile | What they store | What they care about |
|---|---|---|
| Retirees / snowbirds | Class A or fifth wheel, often $150k+ | Covered or enclosed, security, a 24/7 gate, being able to prep and load on site |
| Weekend warriors / families | Travel trailer, toy hauler, ski/wake boat | Price, easy access on Friday nights and early mornings, near the freeway or lake |
| Full-timers | Rarely store; sometimes park between trips or during a house sale | Short-term flexibility. **Cannot live on site.** |
| Anglers | Bass or center-console boat on a trailer | Early-morning access (pre-dawn launch), close to Lake Skinner, Diamond Valley, Dixon, Wohlford (CA) or Lake Travis / Lake Georgetown (TX) **[Verify the lakes are the draw for each site]** |
| Contractors / small business | Enclosed cargo or utility trailer, work truck, equipment trailer | Price, access hours, room to hitch. Watch for working on site and storing materials or fuel. |
| Car collectors / second cars | Classic or exotic car, project car | Enclosed only, security, battery tender power. Project cars may be inoperable, which is a policy question. |
| Powersports | Jet skis (PWC) on a double trailer, ATVs/UTVs, dirt bikes in a toy hauler | Short spaces, price |

### 1.4 Seasonality
- **Southern California:** use runs year-round. Peaks are spring break through Labor Day for boats and summer/holidays for RVs. Some desert snowbirds go the other way and take the RV to the desert in winter. One 2012 operator saw RV owners cancel in summer when they went to the desert, so watch for "seasonal move-outs." **[Dated]**
- **Texas (Austin/Georgetown):** boat season roughly March-October. **Hail season (spring) drives demand for covered spaces.** Summer heat and UV push people to want covers. https://www.recnationstorage.com/boat-storage/tx/Houston ; https://www.recnationstorage.com/blog/how-much-does-rv-and-boat-storage-cost-in-frisco/ **[Sourced, marketing pages]**
- **Fairfield (Solano County):** boating on the Delta/Lake Berryessa is mostly summer. **[Verify]**
- Unlike unit storage, RV/boat tenants are **in and out often**, so gate traffic peaks Friday afternoon, Saturday dawn and Sunday evening. Staff schedules and gate/camera checks should reflect that. **[Verify with Cubby/gate logs]**
- No hard national data on monthly move-in seasonality was found. **[Gap]**

## 2. Vehicle literacy for staff

### 2.1 Legal road limits (why vehicles top out where they do)
- Typical limits without an oversize permit: about 45 ft for a single vehicle (bus/motorhome), **102 in (8.5 ft) wide**, **13.5-14 ft high**. California caps a motorhome towing a boat at 65 ft combined (Inside Self Storage, 2007). https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/market-trends-for-rv-and-boat-storage **[Dated]**
- California Vehicle Code 35250: no vehicle or load may exceed **14 ft** high. A SEMA summary lists CA general limits of 65 ft combined length, 14 ft height, 102 in width. https://www.semasan.com/files/pdf/motorsports_trailers/California.pdf **[Verify against leginfo]**
- Training point: almost nothing legal on the road is wider than 8.5 ft (mirrors and awnings excepted) or taller than 14 ft. Length is the variable that matters most for space fit, and height matters only for covered and enclosed spaces.

### 2.2 RV classes
Ranges below are approximate and vary a lot by model and source. **[Verify; sources aggregated]** https://www.neighbor.com/storage-blog/rv-dimensions-length-width-and-height-explained/ ; https://camperreport.com/how-tall-is-a-camper-trailer/ ; https://www.outdoorsy.com/types-of-rvs

| Type | What it is | Typical length | Typical height | Notes for staff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Class A** | Bus-shaped motorhome on a truck/bus chassis. Gas (front engine) or diesel pusher (rear engine). | 25-45 ft | 11-13.5 ft | The biggest and most valuable. Often tows a car ("toad"). Has slide-outs, leveling jacks, often roof AC x2-3. |
| **Class B** | Camper van (Sprinter, Transit, ProMaster base) | 17-24 ft | 8-11 ft | Often fits a 25 ft space. High-roof vans may be about 9.5-10 ft. |
| **Class C** | Truck-cab motorhome with an over-cab bunk | 21-35 ft (often about 28) | 10-12 ft | "Super C" on a heavy-truck chassis can be 35-40+ ft. |
| **Travel trailer** | Towed by bumper/ball hitch | Body 12-35 ft, plus 3-5 ft tongue | 9-11.5 ft | Advertised length often includes the hitch. Ask. |
| **Fifth wheel** | Towed by a hitch in a pickup bed; raised front "nose" | 22-45 ft (most common 32-36) | 12-13.5 ft | **The tallest towable.** The kingpin overhang (3-5 ft) may not be in the stated length. Needs the truck to move it. |
| **Toy hauler** | Travel trailer or fifth wheel with a rear ramp door and garage for ATVs/motorcycles | 25-45 ft | Same as its base type | The ramp door adds no length closed, but the tenant needs room behind it if loading on site (which should not happen in an aisle). |
| **Pop-up / tent trailer** | Folding canvas camper | 8-16 ft folded | about 4-5 ft folded | Cheapest RV; short space. |
| **Truck camper** | Slide-in camper on a pickup | Truck length + overhang | 10-12 ft | Sometimes stored off the truck on jacks. **[Greens to decide whether allowed]** |
| **Teardrop / small trailer** | Tiny sleeping trailer | 10-16 ft | 4-7 ft | |

Height add-ons: **rooftop AC (about 10-14 in), antennas, satellite domes, solar panels, roof ladders, vent covers, and cargo boxes**. Measure to the top of the tallest item, not the roof line. https://www.neighbor.com/storage-blog/?p=14623 **[Sourced]**

Rule of thumb from an operator: a **38 ft RV needs at least a 42 ft space**, because ladders, mirrors and bumpers add length. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/market-trends-for-rv-and-boat-storage **[Dated, still a good rule]**

### 2.3 Boats
| Type | Typical hull length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bass boat** | 17-21 ft | Low profile. Big outboard and often a trolling motor; trailer adds about 4-7 ft. **[Verify]** |
| **Pontoon** | 18-26 ft | Wide (8-8.5 ft beam). A bimini top up can push height past 13 ft on a trailer. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/market-trends-for-rv-and-boat-storage |
| **Ski / wake boat** | 20-25 ft | **Wake tower** is the height problem. Example: Arc Sport (23 ft boat) is 31 ft long on its trailer with the tongue out and 28 ft 2 in with it folded, and 121 in (about 10 ft) tall on the trailer with the tower down. https://arcboats.com/faq/article/arc-sport-dimensions **[Sourced]** |
| **Bowrider / deck boat** | 18-26 ft | General family boat |
| **Center console** | 18-30+ ft | Fishing; big outboards (often twin), T-top raises height |
| **Cuddy cabin / cruiser** | 20-35 ft | Small cabin; taller |
| **Sailboat (trailerable)** | 16-26 ft | **The mast** is often stored lying on the boat and can stick out past the bow or stern. Keel or swing keel raises height on the trailer. |
| **Go-fast / performance** | 30-45 ft | 8.5 ft beam, up to 11,000 lb dry (2007). https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/market-trends-for-rv-and-boat-storage |
| **PWC (jet ski)** | 10-13 ft each | Often 2 on one trailer, about 15-20 ft total |
| **Kayaks/canoes on a trailer** | 10-20 ft | Small trailer |

**Boat length vs. storage length:**
- The advertised length (LOA, length overall) runs bow to stern on the hull. It **does not include** the swim platform, outboard motor, bow pulpit, or trailer. https://aceboater.com/en-us/length-of-a-boat **[Sourced]**
- For storage, the figure that matters is the **total footprint on the trailer: tip of the trailer tongue (coupler) to the rearmost point** (usually the outboard lower unit or swim platform). A 16 ft boat on its trailer measured 22 ft with a 72 in tongue (forum example). https://www.iboats.com/threads/trailer-length.689009 **[Sourced, anecdotal]**
- **Swing (folding) tongues** shorten storage length by a few feet. Manufacturers may list both a towing length and a storage length.
- Outboard trimmed up vs. down changes length and height.

### 2.4 Other vehicles
- **Cars/SUVs/pickups:** most fit 10x20. A crew-cab long-bed pickup can be about 20-22 ft; dually trucks are wider than 8 ft at the mirrors. **[Verify]**
- **Work/cargo trailers:** enclosed cargo trailers commonly 6x12 to 8.5x24 ft box plus 3-4 ft V-nose/tongue. Equipment/flatbed and dump trailers are heavy, which matters for asphalt and tongue jacks sinking. **[Verify]**
- **Motorcycles:** fit small spaces or units. Insurance and registration still apply.
- **Car haulers / toy hauler trailers / utility trailers:** treat like any trailer: registration (trailer plate) required.

## 3. Space types and standards

### 3.1 The three main products
| Product | What it is | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open / uncovered** | Striped space on paved or gravel lot | Lowest price; any height fits | No sun/hail protection; least security |
| **Covered (canopy)** | Roof only, open sides. Posts often every **24 ft** (two 12 ft stalls); **clearance about 14 ft**; thru-fastened roof so leaks are easy to fix. Not secure. | Sun, UV and hail protection; middle price | Height limit; posts to hit; blowing rain and dust still reach |
| **Enclosed (garage)** | Individual building unit with a roll-up door. Typical **14 ft wide x 30-45 ft deep**, door **12 ft wide x 14 ft high**. Options: motorized door, metered power. | Best security and protection; the highest rent | Door width and height limit; hardest to back into |

Source for the canopy and enclosed figures: Caesar Wright (Mako Steel), Inside Self Storage, 10/20/2015. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/building-boat-rv-storage-site-selection-design-types-and-more **[Dated but standard]**

Other variants: **condo storage** (owned garages, often with mezzanines/clubhouse), **climate-controlled enclosed**, and **indoor parking in a warehouse** (operator-parked/valet).

### 3.2 Sizes and dimensions
- Typical self-storage parking sizes: **10x20, 10x25, 10x30, 10x35, 10x40, 12x40, 12x45, 12x50** (car up to large Class A). Unit naming is **width x length**. **[Verify with Greens' own Cubby unit types]**
- Minimum unit width is typically **12 ft**; most enclosed units are 14 or 15 ft wide and 45-50 ft deep, with eave height about 16 ft (Inside Self Storage). **[Sourced; numbers vary between articles]**
- An operator wished he had built **35, 45 and 55 ft** spaces (measured inside), and said to plan more 30-40 ft spaces than 50+ ft. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/market-trends-for-rv-and-boat-storage **[Dated]**
- **Door clearance reality:** a door sold as 14 ft may measure about **13 ft 8 in** hung in its frame, and a raised rain lip can cost another inch. Ski boats with towers and pontoons with biminis can exceed 13 ft on a trailer. (Same source.) **Teach: always measure the actual door, and post the clear height at the door.**

### 3.3 Aisles, turning and layout
- Drive aisle recommendations differ by source **[Verify]**:
  - 50 ft for enclosed, 45 ft for angled canopies (Wright, 2015).
  - 38 ft for canopy with 60-degree angled spaces, 50 ft for enclosed (other IIS article).
  - 35 ft is workable with 30-45 degree angled spaces.
  - 35 ft covered / 60 ft enclosed at one operator (2012).
- **Angled spaces** make backing in easier and allow narrower aisles. **Pull-through** spaces (drive in one end, out the other) are the easiest and are premium. **Back-in** spaces are the norm.
- Site size: boat/RV sites typically need **7-10 acres** vs. 3-5 for unit storage, mostly for aisles. Customers will drive farther than the 3-5 miles typical for unit storage. (Wright, 2015.)
- **Stacking (queue) room at the gate** so a 60 ft rig waiting for the gate does not block the street. (Same source.)
- Damage prevention: **crash posts (bollards) at building corners**, wide aisles, **gate sensors that hold the gate open longer** for long rigs, distinct painted lines and **sequential space numbers recorded in the management software**. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/risk-proofing-your-boat-rv-storage-operation-insurance-and-more (Bortman, 2009) **[Dated, still standard]**

### 3.4 Pricing
- Usually priced **per space by size** (length band), sometimes quoted as a **rate per linear foot**. A national rule of thumb is **$3-$8 per foot per month** depending on market and product (that is about $75-$200 for a 25 ft RV). https://www.thedonutwhole.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-store-a-25-foot-rv/ **[Verify; weak source]**
- Local directory averages vary wildly: Temecula RV storage averages cited from about $130 to $311/month; San Diego about $370/month. https://www.storagecafe.com/rv-storage/us/ca/temecula/ ; https://www.rentcafe.com/rv-storage-units/us/ca/san-diego/ **[Verify; directory mix skews numbers]**. Texas examples: Frisco about $130-$240+ (RecNation); Houston about $31-$609 (wide mix).
- **Covered and enclosed** command higher rents but cost more to build and maintain. Canopy build cost was about $20-$25 per sq ft turnkey in 2015. **[Dated]**
- Rate increases: one operator raises by size code at 80-90% occupancy; another raised at 6 months then about every 9 months by 4-6% (2012). **[Dated]**
- Prepay discounts (a year up front) are common. **[Greens to decide]**
- **For the course: use Greens' own rate card per site from Cubby, not industry averages.**

## 4. Measuring and fitting a vehicle

**Length**
- Ask for **overall length as it will sit in the space**, not the model name. "A 30-foot trailer" may be 30 ft box, 34 ft hitch to bumper.
- Travel trailer: **coupler (hitch ball socket) to rear bumper**, including the spare tire or bike rack on the back.
- Fifth wheel: **front of the nose/pin box to rear bumper** (kingpin overhang often not in the brochure).
- Motorhome: **front bumper to rear-most item**: ladder, spare, bike rack, tow bar (a towed car is a separate vehicle and usually a separate space).
- Boat: **tip of the trailer tongue to the rearmost point** (outboard/swim platform/mast overhang). Ask whether the tongue folds.
- Add a buffer: about **3-4 ft** over the measured length for walking room and margin (the "38 ft needs 42 ft" rule). **[Verify / Greens to decide]**

**Height** (covered and enclosed only)
- Ground to the **highest point**: AC shroud, antenna, satellite dome, solar panel, wake tower, bimini, T-top, mast step, kayak on the roof.
- Tower down / bimini down / antenna lowered numbers are what matters, but the tenant must actually lower them every time.
- Tires low or air-bag suspension changes height by inches.

**Width**
- Body 8-8.5 ft. **Mirrors, awnings, and slide-outs** are wider. **Slides must stay in** while stored (otherwise they block neighbors and covers drip onto them). **[Greens to decide; common rule]**

**Why it matters**
- Too long: the tail sticks into the aisle, gets hit by other tenants, blocks fire lanes, and creates code issues. Too tall: roof AC torn off on the canopy beam or door header (a large damage claim, and a building repair). Wrong size rented: the tenant has to move, which creates a bad first experience, and a lost discount or rate dispute.

**A fit-check conversation (sample flow for the course writer)**
1. "What are you storing?" (type, then year/make/model).
2. "What's the overall length hitch to bumper, including anything on the back like a ladder, spare or motor?"
3. "For covered/enclosed: what's the height to the top of the AC or tower?"
4. "Does it have slides? Do they stay in?" / "Does the tongue fold?"
5. "How do you usually park it: back in? Do you have someone to spot you?"
6. "How often will you take it out, and what time of day?" (gate hours, early launches).
7. Offer the next size up if it is within 2-3 ft. Note measurements in Cubby. **Verify on move-in with a tape** and photos. **[Greens to decide whether staff measure every vehicle]**

## 5. Rental requirements (what to collect and check)

From Inside Self Storage legal articles (Greenberger 2014; Zucker 2011; Bortman 2009). **[Dated but still standard practice]**
https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/the-legalities-of-storing-vehicles-insight-for-self-storage-operators
https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/legal-considerations-for-titled-property-in-self-storage-understanding-liability-and-lien-sales-for-boats-rvs-and-other-vehicles
https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/risk-proofing-your-boat-rv-storage-operation-insurance-and-more

- **Photo ID** checked against the registration/title and insurance card.
- **Current registration** for **every** vehicle, trailer and boat: the RV or car, the boat **and** its trailer (separate registrations), PWCs. **Reject documents not in the tenant's name** (or a business they sign for), and set up a renewal collection system.
- **Proof of insurance**. Many self-storage insurers do not cover vehicles, and the facility's policy does not cover the tenant's vehicle. Make insurance a lease condition. If coverage lapses, the tenant bears the risk. Ask to be notified of cancellation (this is not the same as being an additional insured).
  - Tenant protection / storage protection plans usually **exclude motor vehicles, boats and trailers**. **[Verify with Greens' plan provider]**
- **Record VIN / hull number (HIN), make, model, year, color, plate/registration number** in the lease and in Cubby. The VIN/HIN should **match** on the registration, title and insurance card.
- **Title holder and lienholder names**, captured at signing: many states require lien notices to lienholders on default, and a lender's lien usually ranks ahead of the storage lien.
- Some operators rent only to the title holder (theft and creditor risk). **[Greens to decide]**
- **Ask directly whether a vehicle will be stored**, and whether it may be swapped. "Vehicle" means different things to different people. Write the specific vehicle into the lease or addendum.
- **Value limit**: many leases cap stored value (commonly $5,000 for units). Boats/RVs may get a higher cap with proof of insurance.
- **Operable vehicle policy**: common rule is the vehicle must start, move under its own power (or be towable on inflated tires), and have current tags. No vehicles on blocks. **[Verify; Greens to decide]**
- **Photos at move-in**: all four sides plus close-ups of existing damage, the plate/VIN/HIN, and the space. Keep them in the tenant file. (Bortman 2009; Good Sam.) **[Sourced]**
- **Who may drive it out**: list authorized users on the lease and gate code. Staff never move tenant vehicles without written permission and proper insurance (Zucker 2011 notes the operator who parks vehicles takes on care, custody and control). **[Sourced]**
- **Out-of-state plates** make later lien lookups harder. Note them. **[Sourced]**
- **Texas specific:** for a lien on a boat to work under TPWD's statutory lien foreclosure process, there must be a **written, signed rental agreement containing the self-service storage lien**. https://tpwd.texas.gov/publications/pwdforms/media/pwd_309b_affidavit_of_statutory_lien_foreclosure_sale_by_self-service_storage_facility.pdf **[Sourced]** Texas also requires the lease to authorize towing for the towing option (see 7.3).
- Texas title/registration: all motorized vessels must be titled regardless of length, and outboard motors are titled too; sailboats 14 ft+ need registration; boat **trailers** are titled and registered through TxDMV (county tax office), not TPWD. https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/boating/title-registration-id-requirements/title-requirements ; https://galvestoncountytx.gov/our-county/tax-assessor-collector/tpwd-boat-registration-title **[Sourced]**
- California: vessels and their trailers are registered with the **CA DMV** (CF numbers on the hull, vessel registration sticker). **[Verify, general knowledge]**

## 6. Rules and risks

### 6.1 House rules found in industry practice (compose Greens' list from these)
- **No living or sleeping in vehicles.** California's self-storage act bars residential use of a self-service storage facility (B&P 21701 definition). https://law.onecle.com/california/business/division-8/chapter-10/index.html **[Verify exact subsection]** In practice also: no overnight stays, no generators running, no hooking up "to camp."
- **No maintenance, repairs, oil/fluid changes, or bodywork** on site. **[Common rule; Greens to decide]** Light prep (loading, tire pressure, battery swap) is usually OK.
- **No washing in the space** (only in a wash bay if offered). Washing in spaces sends soap/oil into storm drains (California stormwater rules) **[Verify]**.
- **Hazardous materials:** lease should restrict hazardous fluids, gases and spare tires (Bortman 2009). Extra fuel cans, propane cylinders outside the RV's mounted tanks, fireworks: not allowed. **[Greens to decide]**
- **Propane:** common rules require the tank **valve closed at the source** whenever stored; some require tanks disconnected or even empty. https://belvoir.armymwr.com/download_file/view/5d880ef3-6af6-4450-8c17-35bc5dd58ea6/7849 ; https://parachute.municipal.codes/PMC/15.10.060 **[Sourced]** Enclosed units may face stricter fire-code rules on LP-gas. **[Verify with fire marshal]**
- **Batteries:** recommend disconnecting/removing house and chassis batteries; idle systems drain them. https://blog.goodsam.com/rv-storage-issues/ **[Sourced]** Owners plugging in heaters or chargers without approved power are a fire hazard and a liability (same source).
- **Drip pans / drip protection** under engines and generators: one recommendation is to make it mandatory in the lease (Bortman 2009). **[Sourced]**
- **Slides in, awnings in, stabilizer jacks on pads** (jacks sink into hot asphalt). **[Common; Verify]**
- **Covers** must be tied down (a loose cover becomes a sail in Santa Ana winds and damages neighbors). **[Greens to decide]**
- **Only the rented vehicle in the space**, nothing stored beside or under it (no extra trailers, tires, furniture, canopies). **[Common rule]**
- **No dumping** gray/black water anywhere but an approved dump station. Illegal dumping is a health and stormwater violation. **[Verify]**
- **Speed limit and one-way traffic**; spotter recommended when backing.

### 6.2 Risks staff watch for
| Risk | Why it matters | What staff do |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluid leaks** (oil, coolant/antifreeze, transmission, fuel, hydraulic, black/gray water) | Cleanup cost, pollution liability, neighbor damage; antifreeze is toxic to animals | Absorbent, photo, notify tenant, document. Insurance: ask the agent about **pollution coverage for fuel leaks** (Bortman 2009). |
| **Fire** | Batteries, chargers, rodent-chewed wiring, propane, solar systems, lithium battery packs | No unauthorized cords; report smoke/smell to 911 first |
| **Rodents** (mice, pack rats) | Chew wiring (fire), nest in engine bays/compartments; a dime-size gap is enough | Pest control around perimeter; tell tenants to remove food and check once or twice a month. https://www.rvtravel.com/rodents-rv-storage/ ; https://blog.goodsam.com/pesky-rv-critters-2/amp/ |
| **Tires** | Lose pressure in storage; UV and heat cause dry rot; flats leave a vehicle unmovable | Note flats on site walks, notify tenant. Good Sam suggests over-inflating about 10% and a barrier on concrete. |
| **Sun/heat** | Southern CA and TX summer heat damages roofs, seals, covers, tires; covered spaces still get low-angle sun | Sell covered/enclosed honestly as protection |
| **Wind** | Santa Ana winds (SoCal) and Fairfield's Delta winds lift covers, awnings, canopy roofing | Wind checks after storms; loose cover policy |
| **Hail** (Texas especially) | Dents and cracks roofs and fiberglass; key driver for covered demand | Post-storm walk with photos; do not admit liability, report to manager |
| **Collision** | Tenants back into posts, buildings, neighbors | Bollards; incident report; tenant's insurance handles |
| **Theft** | Catalytic converters, batteries, solar panels, generators, outboard motors, whole trailers (hitch locks help) | Gate logs, cameras, report suspicious activity |
| **Abandonment** | Rent stops but the rig stays; dead tags; flat tires | Lien process (section 7), early outreach |
| **Water/mold** | Roof leaks in stored RVs; standing water in boats breeds mosquitoes | Note open hatches, full bilges, tarps sagging with water |

## 7. Vehicle liens: how they differ (training level, NOT legal advice)

**Why vehicles differ:** a registered vehicle or vessel has a **title** held by a state agency. A buyer at an auction cannot register it unless the sale follows the vehicle/vessel title process, and there is usually a **lender (legal owner) whose lien ranks ahead of the storage lien**, so selling often does not pay the operator. Zucker (2011) and Greenberger (2014), Inside Self Storage. **[Sourced]** Greenberger's practical advice: the goal is often to **get the vehicle off the lot quickly** to stop liability and unpaid rent growing.

### 7.1 California
- **B&P 21702.5** (Self-Service Storage Facility Act): a lien on a vehicle or vessel that must be registered under the Vehicle Code is enforced under **Civil Code 3071 (vehicles)** or **Harbors and Navigation Code 503 (vessels)**, **not** under the Act's own sale steps (21705-21711). The lien **may not include rent or other charges accruing more than 60 days** (as of the 2010 text). The current text also mentions removal by a towing company that complies with Vehicle Code 12520 and acts under Vehicle Code 22658. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-8/chapter-10/section-21702-5 ; https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB655/2009 ; https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/13-14/bill/asm/ab_0951-1000/ab_983_bill_20140903_enrolled.htm **[Sourced; Verify current text, and confirm whether and how the towing language applies]**
- **Vehicles (DMV):** a vehicle stored at a self-service storage facility is sold under **Civil Code 3071** whatever its value. The **$4,000-or-less short process (Civil Code 3072, REG 668) cannot be used** for a self-storage lien. https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/titles/lien-sales-for-vehicles/conduct-a-lien-sale-for-a-vehicle-valued-at-4000-or-less/ ; https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/titles/lien-sales-for-vehicles/how-to-conduct-a-lien-sale-for-a-vehicle-valued-over-4000/ **[Sourced]**
  - Forms: **REG 656** Application for Authorization to Conduct a Lien Sale (filed with DMV, with a fee); DMV notifies registered and legal owners and interested parties, who may file a **Declaration of Opposition** (REG 3068 is the opposition form under Civil Code 3068) **[Verify form number]**; if opposed, the lienholder must go to court; **REG 280** Notice of Pending Lien Sale (sent by certified mail about 20 days before sale and published); **REG 168** Certification of Lien Sale; **REG 343** to retitle. Excess proceeds go to DMV with a copy of the REG 168 **within 15 days** of the sale. https://qr.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/notice-of-pending-lien-sale-reg-280-pdf ; https://dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/certification-of-lien-sale-reg-168-pdf **[Sourced; timelines Verify]**
- **Vessels (Boaters Lien Law, H&N 500-509):** DMV authorization is required for a vessel valued over **$1,500** (BOAT 152 application). Notice by certified mail **20 days** before the sale (BOAT 166); newspaper ad **10-30 days** before the sale; an owner's opposition stops the sale unless the lienholder wins in court; the vessel must be available for public inspection at least **1 hour** before the sale and **no sealed bids**; the owner may **redeem within 10 days after** the sale (H&N 506.5). Certification: BOAT 178. https://california.public.law/codes/harbors_and_navigation_code_section_503 ; https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/notice-of-pending-lien-sale-for-vessels-or-vessel-trailers-valued-at-over-1500-boat-166-pdf ; https://california.public.law/codes/harbors_and_navigation_code_section_506.5 **[Sourced; Verify the boat's trailer is handled under the same process]**
- **Pending change:** AB 498 (2025) amended the Self-Service Storage Facility Act (electronic notices, among others). https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-07/ab-498-michelle-rodriguez-sjud-analysis_0.pdf **[Verify what passed and whether it touched 21702.5]**
- **Training message:** for California staff the lesson is: "A vehicle or boat is never sold through our normal unit auction. It goes to the manager and the vehicle lien process (DMV), which takes longer and needs the owner and lender notified." Unregistered items inside a space (e.g., tools in a toy hauler) are a separate question for the manager. **[Greens to decide escalation path]**

### 7.2 Texas
- **Property Code ch. 59** is the self-storage lien law. **59.0445** adds steps for **titled motor vehicles (Transportation Code ch. 501) and titled motorboats, vessels and outboard motors (Parks & Wildlife Code ch. 31)**, and for vehicles/vessels titled out of state:
  - Within **30 days after the lessor takes possession**, send written notice to the **last known owner and each recorded lienholder** (look them up with TxDMV/TPWD), stating the charges and that the property may be sold if not paid before the **31st day** after mailing/publication.
  - Notice by verified mail; publication only in narrow cases (e.g., no response from the state agency within 21 days, owner unidentifiable, no address).
  - Owner or lienholder may redeem by paying before the 31st day.
  - Then a **public sale** (59.045: at the facility or a reasonably near public place). For a motor vehicle, the lessor may instead dispose of it under **Transportation Code ch. 683 subch. D** if it is worth only parts/scrap or a sale is uneconomical.
  - Knowingly false info in the notice is a Class B misdemeanor.
  https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_59.0445 ; https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-5/subtitle-b/chapter-59/subchapter-c/section-59-0445/ ; https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_59.045 **[Sourced]**
- **Boats:** TPWD form **PWD 309B** Affidavit of Statutory Lien Foreclosure Sale by Self-Service Storage Facility; requires a written signed lease containing the lien. https://tpwd.texas.gov/publications/pwdforms/media/pwd_309b_affidavit_of_statutory_lien_foreclosure_sale_by_self-service_storage_facility.pdf **[Sourced]** Vehicles retitle through TxDMV with a "Self-Service Storage Facility Lien Foreclosure" form. **[Verify current TxDMV form]**
- **Towing option (SB 1181, effective 09/01/2021; Property Code 59.051-59.055):** if the tenant is in default and the property is a **motor vehicle, trailer, semitrailer, motorboat, vessel or outboard motor**, the lessor may have it towed to a licensed **vehicle storage facility (VSF)** instead of a lien sale, if: (1) towing is **expressly authorized in the written rental agreement**; (2) the required lien notice was delivered and the tenant **did not pay by the 14th day** after delivery; (3) the VSF **agrees in writing** to accept it. The storage lien is **extinguished** once towed (59.053) (so the unpaid rent is not recovered from the vehicle), and the lessor is not liable for damage during or after the tow (59.055). TxDMV paperwork: copy of the signed lease authorizing the VSF to take possession, proof of notice, signed lien foreclosure form. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal-issues/when-a-vehicle-storage-tenant-defaults-on-rent-can-you-tow-rather-than-go-to-lien-sale- ; https://www.insideselfstorage.com/facility-operators/texas-governor-signs-optional-towing-bill-into-law-for-self-storage-operators ; https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/87R/analysis/html/SB01181F.htm **[Sourced]**
- More than 40 states now have some towing option (Zucker 2022). Best practice: **photograph the vehicle's condition before the tow**. **[Sourced]**

### 7.3 Abandoned vehicles (general practice, Zucker 2011)
Certified notice to owner that the property will be deemed abandoned in 30 days; request owner/lienholder list from the state; apply to the agency or court; notify all; if no objection, abandonment confirmed and a new title/court order issues. **[Dated; state-specific; Verify]** Distinguish **trespass vehicles** (dumped on the lot with no lease) from **tenant vehicles in default**: California private-property towing (Vehicle Code 22658, signage required) applies to the first. **[Verify]**

## 8. Operations

### 8.1 Gate and lot
- **Gate sensors / timers** long enough for a 60-65 ft combination to clear; **stacking room** outside the gate; wide gates and turning radius at the entrance. (Bortman 2009; Wright 2015.) **[Sourced]**
- One code per tenant; tailgating by a second rig is a common security gap. **[Verify]**
- **24/7 or extended hours** are a major selling point (anglers launching pre-dawn). One operator gave 24-hour access only to covered/enclosed tenants (2012). **[Dated; Greens to decide]**
- Staff driving the lot: in a cart or company vehicle at walking speed; never drive or move a tenant's vehicle; watch for people backing (mirrors cannot see you). **[General safety; Verify]**

### 8.2 Site walk checklist (synthesized; Greens to adopt)
- Every occupied space matches the Cubby record (vehicle type, plate/VIN). Flag **unauthorized vehicles** (rig in wrong space, extra vehicle, car parked in an RV space while the RV is out).
- Vehicle sticking past the line or into the aisle; vehicle in a fire lane.
- **Expired tags** (registration sticker month/year) - leading indicator of abandonment.
- **Flat or low tires**, jacks sinking, vehicle listing.
- **Leaks / stains** under vehicles (photo, absorbent).
- **Covers** loose, torn, flapping; tarps holding water.
- Open windows, hatches, doors; slides out; awnings out.
- Extension cords run to outlets not part of the rental; generators.
- Signs of occupancy: lights at night, pets, trash, people sleeping.
- Items stored beside vehicles (tires, fuel cans, furniture).
- Building damage: hit posts, bent canopy beams, door damage.
- Photos with date/time for every exception; log in Cubby/task system.

### 8.3 Amenities that win and retain tenants
From Inside Self Storage operator interviews (2007-2012) **[Dated, directionally right]**:
https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/running-a-profitable-boat-rv-storage-operation-service-and-amenities-drive-success ; https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/more-than-space-boat-rv-storage-operators-drive-demand-with-amenities-security
- **Dump station** (sanitary/sewer for black and gray tanks): most requested by RV tenants; many operators keep it free.
- **Wash bay**: helps retention and marketing even if not profitable; controls water use (one operator used an ionized wash rack).
- **Potable water fill**, **air compressor** for tires, **propane fill** (some sites).
- **Electric hookups**, often 20-amp outlets in covered/enclosed spaces for **battery tenders/trickle charging** (maintenance task: keep outlets working).
- **Wide aisles, pull-throughs, 24-hour access, good lighting, cameras** (one site: alarmed garages and 16 remotely monitored cameras).
- Restrooms/showers at lake-destination sites; ice machine; RV supplies sold in the office.
- Premium: valet parking, on-site mechanic or mobile detailing partners, concierge prep (RV SuperStorage, Chandler AZ).
- Operators split on charging: free for covered/enclosed vs. pay-per-use for open; the article says amenities "do not have to be free." **[Greens to decide]**

### 8.4 Damage incidents and documentation
- Move-in photos are the baseline (section 5).
- On an incident (tenant hits a post, rig hits a neighbor, hail, wind, theft): safety first; photos of everything, both vehicles, plates, the space and building; names and insurance info; incident report same day; **do not admit fault or promise payment**; the tenant's own insurance handles their vehicle; tell the manager, who decides about notifying Greens' insurer. (Synthesized from Zucker 2011 and Bortman 2009.) **[Greens to decide on form/route]**
- If Greens offers parking, fueling or washing services, the operator takes on **care, custody and control** of the vehicle and needs matching insurance (Bortman 2009). **[Sourced]**

### 8.5 Weather
- **Wind:** loose covers, awnings out, canopy roofing panels. Walk the lot after Santa Ana or Delta wind events. **[Verify local wind patterns]**
- **Hail (TX):** covered sells; after hail, photograph site and canopy damage. https://www.recnationstorage.com/blog/how-much-does-rv-and-boat-storage-cost-in-frisco/
- **Heat:** tires, roofs, seals, batteries degrade faster; covered spaces still get low-angle sun on the west face. Heat also makes asphalt soft, so jack stands sink (use pads). **[Verify]**
- **Rain/flooding:** check drainage at low spaces; boat covers pooling water; open bilges.
- **Freeze:** rare for these sites, but Fairfield and Georgetown can freeze; RVs need winterizing (Good Sam). **[Verify]**

## 9. Selling and retaining

### 9.1 Discovery questions
- What are you storing (type, length hitch to bumper, height for covered)?
- How often do you use it, and when (weekdays at 5 AM? weekends? months at a time)?
- Where do you take it (lake, desert, coast)? Is this site on your way?
- What happens today (driveway, HOA letters, another facility, a friend's lot)?
- What matters most: price, protection from sun/hail, security, access hours, amenities?
- Is it financed / insured / registered in your name? (sets up the paperwork step)
- Any other toys (boat + RV, second trailer)?

### 9.2 Objections (RV/boat specific; responses for the course writer to shape)
| Objection | Angle |
|---|---|
| **"Why do you charge by the foot / why is the bigger space so much more?"** | Space is priced by the land and aisle it uses; a longer rig needs a longer space and more turning room. Fitting the right size prevents damage to their rig. |
| **"It's too expensive for a parking spot."** | Compare to the cost of HOA fines, a sun-damaged roof or a hail claim deductible, theft of a battery or catalytic converter, or a cover replacement. Offer open as the budget option or a prepay discount. **[Greens pricing]** |
| **"Is it secure?"** | Gate access by code, cameras, lighting, site walks, enclosed option. Do not promise "nothing will happen"; insurance is still required. |
| **"The place down the road is cheaper."** | Ask what they get there: aisle width, access hours, dump station, paved vs. dirt, covered height, distance to the lake. Never disparage the competitor. |
| **"I'll just park at home."** | Acknowledge; ask about HOA/city rules, the neighbors, theft. Leave the door open: "If that changes, here's my card." |
| **"Will my 13 ft 6 in fifth wheel fit covered?"** | Never guess. Measure, check the posted clear height, offer open while waiting for a taller spot. |
| **"I only use it in summer; can I pause?"** | Explain month-to-month; the space may not be there when they come back (waitlist). **[Greens policy]** |
| **"Do you cover damage?"** | No; the tenant's own vehicle insurance covers their RV/boat; tenant protection plans usually exclude vehicles. **[Verify plan]** |

### 9.3 Waitlists and upgrades
- Covered and enclosed spaces often run full; operators keep **waitlists**. https://www.insideselfstorage.com/vehicle-boat-rv-storage/maximizing-revenue-from-boat-and-rv-storage-attracting-tenants-with-the-right-pricing-amenities-and-marketing **[Sourced, 2012]**
- Common path: rent **open now**, join the **covered/enclosed waitlist**, upgrade when one opens (keeps the customer, raises revenue). Capture length/height on the waitlist so the offer fits. **[Greens to decide on Cubby waitlist handling]**
- Referral partners: RV dealers, repair shops, marinas, boat dealers. One operator got about 10% of rentals from RV shops/dealers and paid the partner 50% of the first month after a 3-month stay (2012). **[Dated]**
- Retention: tenants stay long when access, cleanliness and fit work; the dump station and wash bay build loyalty. Seasonal cancellations are the main churn risk.

## 10. Glossary (about 50 terms)

**RV terms**
1. **Class A** - bus-style motorhome, 25-45 ft.
2. **Class B** - camper van.
3. **Class C** - motorhome with a truck cab and over-cab bunk.
4. **Super C** - Class C on a heavy-duty truck chassis; often 35-40+ ft.
5. **Diesel pusher** - Class A with a rear diesel engine.
6. **Travel trailer** - RV towed by a ball hitch on a bumper/frame receiver.
7. **Fifth wheel** - trailer with a raised front that hitches in a pickup bed.
8. **Toy hauler** - trailer or fifth wheel with a rear ramp garage for ATVs/motorcycles.
9. **Pop-up (tent trailer)** - folding camper.
10. **Truck camper** - camper that slides into a pickup bed.
11. **Toad / dinghy** - car towed behind a motorhome.
12. **Tow vehicle** - truck or SUV pulling a trailer.
13. **Coupler** - the front of a trailer tongue that fits over the hitch ball.
14. **Kingpin / pin box** - the fifth wheel's hitch point.
15. **Tongue jack** - crank or electric jack holding up a trailer's front.
16. **Stabilizer jacks / leveling jacks** - legs that steady or level a parked RV.
17. **Slide-out (slide)** - room section that extends out to widen the RV.
18. **Awning** - roll-out shade on the side.
19. **Holding tanks** - fresh, **gray** (sink/shower) and **black** (toilet) water tanks.
20. **Dump station** - sewer inlet where RVs empty gray/black tanks.
21. **Shore power** - plugging an RV into an outside electric outlet (30 or 50 amp RV plugs; 20-amp standard household).
22. **Battery tender / trickle charger** - small charger keeping batteries topped up.
23. **House batteries vs. chassis batteries** - the RV's living-area batteries vs. the engine's starting battery.
24. **LP / propane tanks** - fuel for stove, fridge, furnace; ASME tanks on motorhomes, DOT cylinders on trailers.
25. **Generator (genset)** - built-in or portable power.
26. **Boondocking / dry camping** - camping with no hookups.
27. **Full-timer** - person who lives in an RV year-round.
28. **Snowbird** - travels south/to the desert for winter.
29. **Winterize** - drain water lines / add RV antifreeze before freezing weather.
30. **GVWR** - gross vehicle weight rating (max loaded weight).
31. **Wheelbase / tail swing** - distance between axles / how far the rear swings out in a turn (why long rigs need wide aisles).
32. **Hitch-to-bumper length** - the true storage length of a trailer.

**Boat terms**
33. **LOA (length overall)** - hull length bow to stern, excluding motor, platform and trailer.
34. **Beam** - width of the hull at its widest.
35. **Bow / stern / port / starboard** - front / back / left / right.
36. **Outboard** - motor hung on the stern; **inboard** inside the hull; **I/O (sterndrive)** engine inside with a drive leg outside.
37. **Lower unit** - the bottom of an outboard or sterndrive (often the rearmost point on a trailer).
38. **Trim / tilt** - raising the motor; changes length and height.
39. **Swim platform** - step at the stern.
40. **Bimini top** - fabric sun top on a frame.
41. **Wake tower** - tall frame on ski/wake boats; often folds.
42. **T-top / hardtop** - fixed roof on a center console.
43. **Mast** - sailboat spar; stored down on the boat when trailered.
44. **Keel / swing keel** - sailboat fin under the hull.
45. **Bilge / bilge plug** - lowest inside part of the hull / drain plug (pulled for storage so rain drains).
46. **Pontoon** - flat-deck boat on aluminum tubes.
47. **PWC (personal watercraft)** - jet ski, WaveRunner, Sea-Doo.
48. **HIN (hull identification number)** - the boat's VIN, on the transom.
49. **CF number (California) / TX number (Texas)** - the state registration number shown on the boat's bow.
50. **Swing tongue / folding tongue** - trailer tongue that folds to shorten storage length.
51. **Bunks vs. rollers** - carpeted boards or rollers the boat sits on in the trailer.

**Storage/ops terms**
52. **Pull-through vs. back-in space**; **angled space**; **clear height** (the real measured height under a canopy beam or door header); **drive aisle**; **stacking lane**; **bollard (crash post)**; **legal owner vs. registered owner** (lender vs. user on a California title); **lienholder**; **VSF (Texas vehicle storage facility)**.
